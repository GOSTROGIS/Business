Online research and source boundaries

Research snapshot: 23 October 2026. This note explains the sources used alongside the user-supplied SkillPlan guide to develop the original ACCESS IS4S learner textbook and practitioner guide. The portable source ledger records titles, publishers, exact URLs, retrieval dates, supported claims, limitations and source hashes. It includes 15 online source records: four federal pages and 11 BC institutional resources, of which two Douglas PDFs received selective inspection only. Retrieval does not mean every linked publication or course was read.

The research supports practical teaching decisions: recognize adults’ existing knowledge, make the skill and evidence clear, preserve learner choice, respect distinct Indigenous identities and establish local relationships before incorporating cultural contributions. It does not establish community approval, institutional endorsement or validated proficiency scores for the new activities.

A short source note for the books

This edition is informed by SkillPlan’s Skills for Success Get Started Guide for Practitioners, revised March 2024, and current official online sources checked on 23 October 2026. Federal pages confirm the nine-skill framework and provide current component and proficiency information. Verified Douglas College, BCIT and JIBC resources inform learner agency, practitioner development and respectful local engagement. The explanations, practice cases, answer guidance and teaching tools are independently authored. Linked resources retain their own rights and conditions. Bibliographic references and blocked pages are identified as leads, not publications we claim to have read.

BC Indigenous adult-learning context

Douglas College’s Indigenous Skills for Success Journey, Planting the Seeds for Growth is a directly relevant train-the-trainer resource. The landing page links the course, nine learning tools, a facilitator guide, workbook and course materials. The Facilitation Guide, printed pp.7–10 / PDF pp.8–11, discusses practitioner audiences, participants’ contexts and values, individual journeys and flexible delivery. Its printed p.4 / PDF p.5 credits Carlene Quock of ACCESS on the advisory committee; printed p.6 / PDF p.7 dedicates the project to former ACCESS CEO John Webster. These are documented acknowledgements in that project, not endorsement of this edition. Selected front matter and delivery guidance were read; the whole course and videos were not reviewed.

Douglas’s Indigenous Student Services describes self-directed independent learners, varied adult student circumstances, individualized supports and voluntary confidential self-identification. These institutional practices informed our emphasis on learner agency and private support conversations. They do not create ACCESS eligibility rules or promises of particular services.

BCIT’s Indigenous Guides verifies the provenance of the BCcampus Pulling Together series. It identifies a steering committee including Indigenous post-secondary leaders, FNESC, IAHLA and Métis Nation BC, and Indigenous and ally writers. The linked books were inaccessible here, so their content and licences remain unverified. BCIT’s Indigenous Modules page also identifies a professional-learning pathway; the course itself was not reviewed.

BCIT’s Protocol Welcome and Land Acknowledgement distinguish the practices and emphasize local context and differing protocols. These sources support confirming appropriate relationships and local authority. A campus territory statement, booking procedure, honorarium amount or ceremony should not be copied as a universal ACCESS practice.

JIBC’s Indigenization page connects curriculum with policies, services, relationships and community engagement. Its Living Indigenization: Engagement Protocols & Guidelines, ©2025, credits Heather Saranczak and Jason La Rochelle for content. Printed pp.12–17 / PDF pp.7–9 discuss dignity, self-determination, unique community protocols, distinct First Nations, Inuit and Métis identities, migration, urbanization and reciprocal relationships. The books use concise attributed paraphrases. No claim is made that JIBC speaks for every Nation or has reviewed our material.

These findings support ordinary training, work and community practice cases with meaningful choice. They do not justify invented pan-Indigenous stories, compulsory cultural disclosure or a generic ceremony. Any local cultural contribution needs agreement about purpose, authority, attribution, review, distribution and compensation. No such consultation or cultural review was completed by this research task.

Search attempts and access limits

Official sources were read directly and through institutional navigation. Actual search queries were also submitted. The full query URLs, timestamps and outcomes are retained in sources.json.

Search route and query Actual outcome
JIBC advanced search: First Peoples Principles Returned actual broad search results, including institutional Indigenous pages. The returned results did not verify the First Peoples Principles of Learning text. Relevant institutional pages were then read directly.
BCIT search: Indigenous education HTTP 200 returned a JavaScript/robot challenge rather than results. No challenge was bypassed.
BCIT site search: First Peoples Principles of Learning HTTP 503; no result content retrieved.
BCIT LibGuides search: First Peoples Principles of Learning Returned a loading shell, not accessible result content.
Douglas search: First Peoples Principles Query appeared in the page, but client-rendered result content was unavailable.
Douglas search: Indigenous Adult Higher Learning Same client-rendered limitation; no search snippets verified.
Google, Bing and DuckDuckGo queries Proxy CONNECT 403 blocked retrieval. These were attempted searches, not successful general-search coverage.

The ledger also retains two initial institutional query URLs that did not perform the intended targeted search; corrected form URLs were used afterwards. An HTTP success status alone was never treated as proof of useful source content.

Original FNESC/First Peoples Principles of Learning, IAHLA, ACCESS, BCcampus and OpenTextBC pages were blocked. Consequently, the books do not reproduce unverified FPPL text, claim a completed FPPL alignment, infer IAHLA teaching positions from its name, or describe current ACCESS services based on an inaccessible website. BCIT’s verified series provenance is a separate, narrower finding. This is a curated research snapshot with explicit access gaps, not an exhaustive literature review.
