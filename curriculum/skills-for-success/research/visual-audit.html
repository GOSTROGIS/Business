Visual audit for the textbook redesign

Inspection date: 23 October 2026. Scope: the supplied 148-page SkillPlan guide and the pre-redesign ACCESS learner textbook (104 pages) and practitioner guide (155 pages). This records the starting condition for the visual redesign, not its finished result.

What was actually inspected

The original PDF was rendered into eight contact sheets covering every page. Representative pages 18, 126 and 133 were also inspected at a larger scale to confirm the framework arrows, fishbone structure and sample email interface. A second reviewer rendered and inspected twelve representative pages from each new book, including covers, chapter openings, cases, examples and handouts. Raster-image and vector-drawing inventories supplemented this visual inspection; counts were not used as substitutes for viewing pages.

The original has 27 raster image objects distributed across 22 pages. Many important graphics are vector shapes, lines, icons and text and do not appear in that count. The two new PDFs have no raster image references in the inspected versions. They contain vector content, chiefly the cover mark, rules, shaded panels and table grids.

The original remains text-heavy, especially its strategy and bibliography pages. Its useful advantage is a consistent mix of human scenes, symbols, diagrams and document specimens. Its dense rotated grab-and-go tables on pp. 53, 61, 70, 77, 84, 91, 98, 105 and 113 are not a model for readable learner-facing print design.

What the new books currently lack

The rewritten prose has substantially more learner practice, but the sampled pages rely almost entirely on paragraphs, tables and coloured boxes. Covers have large unused fields. Chapter openings do not visually establish a situation or learning purpose. Numerical worked examples explain calculations without showing the quantities. Digital tasks lack a visible file/table specimen. Interaction skills lack an illustrated sequence showing how a conversation or handover changes. The handouts provide writing space but few diagrams that learners can examine, label or use to reason.

For practitioners, add a nine-skills map, a six-approach teaching path, an assessment-and-retry cycle, fraction-ruler teaching graphic, evidence-led fishbone and annotated email. Place figures beside the relevant explanation and make captions state what the reader should notice or do.

Useful source visuals to reuse or adapt

The user has expressly authorized using and transforming the supplied PDF. These five candidates can complement the new original illustrations; they do not need to be excluded because they came from the supplied document. Credit the guide to SkillPlan, identify its March 2024 edition and source page, and distinguish Government of Canada funding from authorship. When a figure carries its own attribution, preserve that attribution as well.

Source candidate Page Recommended treatment
Nine-skills ring 10 Crop the central figure at adequate resolution for a source-framework inset, or adapt its nine labelled relationships into an ACCESS visual. The page attributes the overview to Government of Canada (2021). Keep every skill label legible.
Historical-to-current transition chart 18 Use a clearly labelled historical reference inset in the practitioner framework chapter. Add the current explanation that Document Use is embedded and continuous learning remains transversal; the arrows should not imply that the two frameworks are exact one-for-one equivalents.
Fishbone template 126 Reuse as an optional blank template or adapt to the actual packet-case evidence. Retain room to write, distinguish observations from hypotheses, and avoid claiming that the diagram proves a cause.
Five Whys cascade 127 Adapt the stepping structure into the supported why-chain activity. Relabel the end as a possible explanation and next check; make clear that inquiry need not use exactly five questions or find one cause.
Composed email specimen 133 Reuse as a labelled source example or adapt its visible interface to the new fictional training-room message. Preserve the useful distinction between recipient, subject and message body; the practice task requires no actual send.

The measurement handouts on pp. 120–121 contain only sparse object outlines, not a labelled fraction-ruler teaching diagram. A new mathematically exact ruler, measured object and endpoint annotation will teach more than enlarging those outlines. The user clarified that SVGs and diagrams should lead the visual design. The finished edition therefore emphasizes authored diagrams and uses four contemporary scenes for human context; the historical source chart supplies continuity.

Print-quality direction

Combine original ACCESS artwork and diagrams with useful, attributed visuals from the supplied guide. Use the cream, ink, red and gold palette with consistent symbols and strong silhouettes. Cultural specificity should come from grounded contemporary B.C. learning and community contexts, not invented sacred designs or generic cultural decoration.

Use vector graphics for exact scales, arrows, labels and data; use original illustration for people, place and narrative. Keep diagram text selectable or accessible through a meaningful alternative description. At final print size, labels must remain readable, strokes must survive photocopying, and meaning must survive greyscale. Do not depend on colour alone. Keep a figure, caption and associated question together and preserve space for learner responses. Check the actual PDF at page size after placement; an attractive standalone image can still become unreadable when reduced.

Implemented visual edition

The redesign adds 38 unique SVG assets and four fictional generated illustrations. Every learner skill chapter contains a six-component overview and two precise case diagrams, with matching visuals in the practitioner companion. Project, portfolio and teaching diagrams extend the approach. The original p.18 transition chart is reused as an attributed vector inset. There are 67 SVG placements across both books including their covers, and four illustration placements. See the visual index, preview and validation record.
