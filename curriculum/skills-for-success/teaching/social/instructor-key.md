# Instructor key · Fresh social-skill cases and evidence anchors

**Use after preparation, separately from the learner role cards.** This key supports [CK-CM, CK-CL, CK-AD and CK-CI](learner-fresh-cases.md). It contains original local teaching criteria, not standardized assessment items, official Skills for Success levels or validated scoring rules. Local instructor moderation and learner piloting have **not been performed**. The exemplars below are fictional illustrations, not observations of ACCESS learners.

Distribute only the needed sender, receiver, change or user card. Do not give this key before an unfamiliar check. A learner may lawfully have read the published packet or answer material; ask about exposure and record it without blame. If the relevant facts/model are already known, call the attempt rehearsal or familiar practice. A genuinely new-message check requires a different checked source and an updated key. Do not turn a publication layout into a claim that facts were unseen.

## Record evidence at the criterion, not as a personality score

Use the existing four local descriptions: **Not observed** means the opportunity/evidence is absent or unusable; **Not yet demonstrated** means an accessible opportunity occurred but the stated action was not demonstrated; **Demonstrated with support** identifies the task-solving assistance needed; **Demonstrated independently** identifies the action completed without another person supplying the relevant solution under the recorded conditions. A self-correction before a solution prompt can be independent evidence. None of these labels measures attitude, cultural identity, intelligence or a national proficiency level.

Separate **routine access support** from **task-solving support**. Larger print, an agreed language, learner-controlled AAC, an interpreter conveying rather than supplying meaning, or a scribe recording a learner-directed response do not automatically turn the learner's reasoning into someone else's. Record them nonetheless. “Which room is on line one?” may direct a solution to a missed fact; supplying “Cedar” supplies the answer. Agree the criterion first. A learner may independently choose to request tool help while needing support to complete the tool action. Those are different observations.

Use the following reusable row for each selected criterion; duplicate it rather than compressing all components into a single score.

| Field | Record before or during the opportunity |
|---|---|
| Opportunity | Learner code; date; case/form/version; observer; criterion ID. |
| Evidence type | Case analysis / rehearsal with model / observed attempt / observed application in a new task; prior exposure. |
| Conditions | Mode/language; source access; agreed time; tool; access arrangement; who actually authored/operated. |
| Direct evidence | Action, exact phrase or artifact feature; relevant source fact; absence of opportunity where applicable. |
| Assistance | Routine access support separately from the precise task-solving prompt; who provided it and when. |
| Judgement | One local status for this criterion, with reason and limits. |
| Next opportunity | Feedback, chosen retry, observer/date or booking still needed; learner comment or disagreement. |

Do not infer a missing interaction from a correct product. Do not mark an inaccessible or interrupted opportunity “Not yet demonstrated” merely because there is no finished response. A learner's account can inform the record; label it as self-report until the relevant action is observed or otherwise evidenced.

## CK-CM · Expected content and criterion anchors

For each message, **essential details** are the activity/purpose, current room, start and finish times, and required learner action. The old room/time explain the correction. Materials supplied and indoor status are useful supporting details. Several correct minor details cannot compensate for an unresolved wrong room or time.

| Source | Essential information | Appropriate unresolved-question route |
|---|---|---|
| A2 | Label-making; Room Cedar; 14:10–14:45; bring classroom practice folder. | Jo, fictional classroom coordinator; parking/travel/food/childcare are not supplied. |
| B3 | Map-reading; Room Spruce; 09:35–10:05; bring one blank practice card. | Rae, fictional classroom coordinator; transport/refreshments/out-of-hours access are not supplied. |
| C2 retry | Display-review; Room Alder; 11:20–11:50; bring draft display title card. | Lee, fictional classroom coordinator; actual address/travel/meals are not supplied. |

Accept a receiver checking details over several turns rather than in one flawless speech. A sender can reread the source. The relevant question may concern a supplied fact or a genuine unknown; do not insist on an unknown if the exchange already produced a useful clarification. Neither role may invent an arrangement or make a real-world promise.

| Criterion and component | Observable anchor | Limit or support to record |
|---|---|---|
| CK-CM.1 · Listen with intention | Receiver establishes a usable mode/turn method and seeks the information needed to attend. | Quietness, eye contact and stillness are not evidence requirements. |
| CK-CM.2 · Listen to understand | Receiver checks the essential details, identifies a discrepancy or missing point, and reaches a confirmed or explicitly unresolved understanding. | Count minor details separately; record which essential detail remains wrong. Identify auditory, signed, written or other reception actually observed. |
| CK-CM.3 · Speak with clarity | Sender organizes current details in understandable groups and makes a reference clear without relying solely on pointing/colour. | An accent or AAC pace is not an error. Extended written grammar is not the reception criterion. |
| CK-CM.4 · Speak with purpose | Sender identifies the update and needed action; either role handles a genuine unknown through the named contact. | “Coordinator will provide transport” is unsupported; “ask Jo to check” preserves the limit. |
| CK-CM.5 · Adapt to audience and contexts | Learner asks what will help this recipient and uses the answer in the exchange. | Do not infer preference from identity or require a disclosure. If no adaptation opportunity occurred, record it. |
| CK-CM.6 · Adapt to other people's modes and tools | Learner uses the agreed channel, leaves a workable response turn and checks meaning after a mode change if one occurs. | A functioning device alone does not establish understanding. Record any channel interruption and recovery. |

**Fictional moderation example, essential details.** A receiver says “Cedar at 14:00,” then checks their note and asks “Did you say 14:10?” The sender confirms. No one supplied the correction first. This can support independent CK-CM.2 under the stated conditions. A second receiver correctly names folder, pens, cards and indoors but retains Room Birch after an accessible check: the four supporting facts do not meet CK-CM.2. A third receives the prompt “Look at the new room in line one” and then corrects the room: record that assistance against the affected criterion. If captions failed and the exchange stopped, reschedule the unobserved part; do not invent a judgement about reception ability.

For the original **CM-04**, essential details in Case A are the current Room 2, 10:00–10:45, the classroom card activity and the relevant bring/do instructions. The requirement to find four relevant details should be applied alongside these action-critical facts, not as a substitute for accuracy. Record a correction and the help involved. These local anchors clarify teaching use; they do not create an official pass mark.

## CK-CL · Expected product and individual evidence

Version 1 must preserve **8 maps / Bag A / ready**, **6 pencils / Box B / ready**, **2 clipboards / Desk C / awaiting instructor check**, the **13:10 check-in**, and the fictional practice label. The next group's 13:20 start is useful context. There are 16 items altogether, but only 14 are ready; the incorrect draft confuses both status and location. A learner need not calculate totals to demonstrate the handover if each correct row is usable. No one can declare the two clipboards checked.

The Version 2 retry changes the clipboards to **1 ready and 1 pending**, both at Desk C; maps, pencils and check-in are unchanged. If totals are used, 15 items are ready and 1 pending. Check labels distinguish the two clipboard statuses. Layouts may differ; their usefulness is tested through the same reader questions.

| Criterion and component | Observable anchor | Limit or support to record |
|---|---|---|
| CK-CL.1 · Work well with other people | Learner agrees a role, contributes its work and gives/receives a clear handover. | A group signature does not show the individual action. |
| CK-CL.2 · Value diversity and inclusivity | Learner invites an available contribution route and actually considers the resulting idea or check. | “Any ideas?” alone shows an invitation, not that an unheard contribution influenced the decision. No cultural disclosure is required. |
| CK-CL.3 · Manage difficult interactions | Learner separates a factual discrepancy from a layout preference, hears another reason and proposes a relevant check. | Record Not observed if no disagreement opportunity occurred; do not stage harmful conduct. A boundary/support request can be appropriate. |
| CK-CL.4 · Facilitate collaboration | During the learner's turn: states purpose; invites both members; recognizes one dependency; summarizes next actions; checks a receiver's understanding. | Record each action separately in the mini-record below. Talking for five minutes is insufficient. |
| CK-CL.5 · Achieve a common goal | Learner coordinates their contribution and participates in verifying the shared product against the source and reader task. | Correct card quality does not establish everyone's contribution. |
| CK-CL.6 · Reflect and improve teamwork | Learner identifies an observed process issue or useful practice, tries the chosen improvement and compares the result. | A future plan alone is not an enacted improvement. A successful original product need not be changed. |

For **CK-CL.4 and the original CL-12**, use five separate observation lines: purpose; invitations and accessible routes; contribution received/responded to; dependency and assigned action; summary/check-back. On each, write the action or Not observed, the assistance and a next step. The invitation can be demonstrated even if another member declines; the declined contribution cannot be recorded as received or used. Allow processing time and an agreed alternative rather than treating a brief pause as refusal.

**Fictional moderation example, invitation and result.** A facilitator asks both members for a source check, waits for a written reply and uses “clipboards still pending” in the handover. This evidences more than issuing an invitation. Another asks “everyone okay?” and closes before a member can respond: record the invitation but not an accessible contribution/response. If the instructor then says “ask each person and wait for their answer,” the revised facilitation is supported on that action. If an interpreter or AAC tool conveys a learner-authored invitation, record the access condition without assuming someone else chose the action.

For **CL-14**, accept either a useful product revision **or a successful check with a reason to retain the product**. For example: “The reader found the five-stage order, Town of Willow Reach, 10:45 finish, two larger-print copies and the spoken review within 10:10–10:30; we retained the current card.” This is an evidence-based no-change outcome. “It looked good” without the reader evidence is insufficient.

## CK-AD · Expected revised pack and enacted workflow

Kit R Version 1 contains **1 WELCOME + 2 EXAMPLE + 2 BLANK RESPONSE = 5 cards**. Version 2 contains **1 + 3 + 2 = 6 cards**, with the Version 2 label and a retained Version 1 record. The printer change allows either hand-prepared paper or a saved on-screen pack; printing must not be claimed. The actual pack, count/check, retrieval and status report supply action evidence.

The Kit T retry starts **1 WELCOME + 1 EXAMPLE + 3 BLANK RESPONSE = 5** and adds **1 REFERENCE**, making **6 cards** in Version 2. The three original counts do not change. The reference text is supplied in the learner brief. Version names may vary if they unambiguously identify the intended pack and revision; do not impose an unannounced filename test.

| Criterion and component | Observable anchor | Limit or support to record |
|---|---|---|
| CK-AD.1 · Demonstrate responsibility | Learner identifies the commitment, checks the current source and reports actual ready/pending status. | Agreement to every request is not required. An honest unfinished item is useful evidence. |
| CK-AD.2 · Persist and persevere | Learner chooses and performs a purposeful next step despite the printer barrier; retries or verifies after feedback. | Repeating an unusable step indefinitely is not the goal. Record the actual action, not a fictional person's behaviour. |
| CK-AD.3 · Regulate emotions when appropriate | If useful and chosen, learner uses a task-focused pause, next-action marker or support strategy and comments on its effect on the task. | Do not score emotional appearance or demand a strategy when none is needed. A rehearsed strategy is labelled rehearsal. |
| CK-AD.4 · Adjust goals and expectations | Learner preserves the pack's purpose while revising the count/version and selecting an accepted output format. | An unsupported promise to print later does not resolve the stated requirement. |
| CK-AD.5 · Plan and prioritize | Learner orders revision, checking and retrieval; performs their own workflow turn and distinguishes dependencies/support needs. | If another person saves/retrieves, record who did it. Adaptability evidence is not automatically Digital evidence. |
| CK-AD.6 · Seek self-improvement | Learner asks for a specific check, uses actual feedback and compares the next check/attempt. | If the first result was correct, repeated retrieval and a reasoned confirmation are acceptable. Do not manufacture a failure. |

**Fictional moderation example, support is criterion-specific.** A learner independently says, “The printer is unavailable; I can use the paper route. Please show me how you want the version stored.” They prepare six correct slips and direct the Version 2 label; the instructor demonstrates the storage convention. The independent selection of a feasible response can support CK-AD.4. The assisted storage step is recorded against CK-AD.5 as appropriate to its criterion, rather than changing every Adaptability judgement to supported. If a partner chooses the revised card count, that is task-solving help for the revision/check. If the learner never touches or directs the file operation, do not claim independent Digital saving. Choosing support can itself be responsible action, as in original **AD-05**.

An observer may read the brief aloud without supplying a plan when reading is an unrelated barrier. A learner-directed scribe may write the chosen order. Record these arrangements. If the observation is interrupted before retrieval, mark retrieval Not observed and use a booked recovery turn. Completing a worksheet describing the intended process is case analysis, not enacted retrieval.

## CK-CI · Valid designs, fair trials and limited conclusions

The initial display must preserve **MAP Ready**, **WORD Being checked**, **NUMBER Ready**. The first user's correct route is to leave WORD, choose MAP for today and ask the instructor about WORD. In the later change, **MAP Being checked**, **WORD Ready**, **NUMBER Ready**; the new first choice is MAP and the allowed alternative is NUMBER. The corresponding route is to leave MAP, choose NUMBER and ask the instructor about MAP. This is the permitted fictional task, not an actual booking policy.

There is no single required layout. Three distinct options might be a separate labelled card at each pack, a central status/action board, or a fold-out decision path with words and symbols. Merely changing three colours is not three meaningfully different approaches. None of these model options should be supplied before an independent idea-generation attempt. Text, original simple shapes and learner-directed marks are all acceptable; beauty and cultural symbolism are not criteria.

| Criterion and component | Observable anchor | Limit or support to record |
|---|---|---|
| CK-CI.1 · Imagination and curiosity | Learner asks a useful user-task question and explores a possibility before settling. | Do not infer curiosity from enthusiasm or a finished artifact. |
| CK-CI.2 · Identify innovation opportunities | Learner specifies a useful information change tied to choosing a ready pack. | Inventing a real community need or a personal deficit is unnecessary. |
| CK-CI.3 · Generate ideas | Learner supplies at least three different information approaches and explains a difference. | Record if the instructor supplied an option; novelty here means new to this attempt/person, not globally original. |
| CK-CI.4 · Develop ideas | Learner selects using the brief and makes a usable prototype containing the required statuses/actions. | A scribe making learner-directed marks is different from another person designing the system. |
| CK-CI.5 · Apply ideas | Learner runs a bounded trial, records actual user actions/prompts, responds to the evidence and retests or confirms. | Separate help to the designer from help to the user; record familiarity and avoid claims about all users. |
| CK-CI.6 · Facilitate a creative environment | Learner invites feedback, checks before changing another person's work and gives a partner a concrete observation while leaving ownership with them. | Taking over a partner's prototype does not demonstrate support for their authorship. |

**Fictional moderation example, two support records.** A designer prepares the display with an instructor prompt to add the missing WORD status. The test user then correctly selects MAP without a prompt. Record designer support on development, and an unprompted user result under the trial conditions; the user's success does not erase the designer's prompt or establish independent evidence for all six components. In a different trial the designer independently develops the display but tells the user “choose MAP” during the test: record the user's prompted outcome, not independent first-use success. The designer may still show useful application by noticing that the test was coached, stating the limit and planning another fair trial. This same distinction applies to original **CI-15**.

### CK-CI trial record · Keep the two kinds of help separate

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Prototype | Learner code, version, approach and authoring support. |
| User conditions | Willing user code/role; task card; prior familiarity; agreed mode; routine access arrangement. |
| Designer assistance | Exact idea, wording or construction help given to the learner-designer and by whom. |
| User assistance | Exact prompt/help during the user task and when it happened; or none observed. |
| Result | What the user selected, said or did; where confusion occurred; no invented success. |
| Response | Change made or evidence-based reason to retain; next trial and its familiarity limit. |

## Rehearse moderation without pretending it happened

Before a local pilot, two practitioners may independently judge the fictional examples above and compare their reasons. Label this **practice using fictional exemplars**, not completed moderation of learner evidence. For actual moderation, use appropriately consented or anonymized real evidence, the exact version/criterion, independently recorded initial judgements, the support conditions, a discussion of differences and the agreed revision or unresolved disagreement. Record the event only after it occurs using the [delivery and local review templates](delivery-and-local-review.md).

A small local agreement between practitioners does not establish reliability, national proficiency, effectiveness for all Indigenous adults, or community endorsement. Keep unobserved components visible and arrange another opportunity using the [teaching plans and observer rota](teaching-plans.md). The [route cards](route-cards.md) support core, supported and retry pathways without assigning learners fixed ability identities.
