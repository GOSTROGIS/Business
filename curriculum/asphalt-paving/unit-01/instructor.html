ACCESS IS4S classroom teaching • Three 60-minute sessions • 180 minutes

Pair with the learner workbook and supporting materials. The workbook supplies the readings, equipment cards, data and questions; the separate change card is in the supporting materials. No unavailable provider worksheet or live website is required to complete this opening sequence. A device and spreadsheet or accessible table tool are needed to observe Digital; a later individual tool turn is required if devices are unavailable.

Scope and source status

The occupational anchor is the user-supplied Asphalt Paving/Laydown Technician Program Outline, Line A, competency A1 Introduction to the paving industry and paving equipment, PDF page 22 / printed page 20, footer 12/12. Its objective is understanding roads-construction history and the relationship between governing bodies and contractors. Its nine learning tasks are nine occupational-content items, distinct from the nine Skills for Success.

Learner question IDs A1-Q01 through A1-Q18 identify original workbook questions. They do not reproduce the outline's learning-task numbers; use the coverage table below to connect them.

The supplied outline is a historical source, not evidence of current regulations or this ACCESS cohort's approved syllabus. The packet includes later-dated sections; record page-specific footers rather than assigning one date to all content. A1's “EPS, MMCD” wording is retained only as a source-evaluation prompt. Do not expand EPS by guesswork or tell learners that every listed item is a statute. Identify full titles, issuers, current versions, legal/contractual status and applicability before teaching specific requirements.

Time boundary: These are 180 minutes of ACCESS IS4S classroom teaching. They are not an official A1 allocation, a substitute for the partner's technical timetable or evidence of qualification. The supplied outline's PDF page 17 / printed page 15 allocates 3% to all of Line A, A1–A6, and marks it 100% theory. Applied to the supplied profile's 120 technical-training hours, that is 3.6 hours for the entire line. Plan these classroom sessions alongside the provider’s technical delivery.

Continue into the authored A2 history unit and A3 technology unit, then the A4–A6 units on workers, road documents and trends. The six-unit Line A sequence contains 37 activities. This opening workbook develops A1’s basic history, equipment purposes and pit-to-finished-product overview; operation, mix design, machine settings and compaction procedures belong in supervised technical training.

All place names, companies, fund names, employment counts, money and comparison distances in the workbook are invented and labelled. The materials/process/history readings are original introductory teaching summaries, not quotations or provider-issued operating documents. Before delivery, the asphalt instructor should review terminology and relevance. Use the practical teaching sequence: task → useful document → worked steps → your turn → check → explain → changed condition.

Observable outcomes and A1 coverage

By the end, learners can explain a basic process and equipment functions, connect roads with community and trade needs, read a small industry table and project brief, distinguish project roles, and identify what evidence an environmental or requirements claim still needs. Assess these as introductory understanding with recorded supports.

Prepare the room and materials

  • Print the learner workbook and response sheet. Prepare the separate change card for distribution after the first draft in Session 3; keep it out of the learner packet until then. Optional stage/equipment cards support matching and sequencing.
  • Supply pencils, calculators, large-print copies and glossary space. Display the five-stage process. No machines or equipment demonstrations are required.
  • Prepare one accessible device per pair where possible. Open a local spreadsheet or table tool, demonstrate saving to a learner-accessible folder and use learning IDs rather than personal records. Internet access and accounts are unnecessary if an offline tool is available.
  • Set up pairs and allow an instructor partnership where needed. Ask about preferred supports privately; do not require disclosure of learning diagnoses or personal/cultural histories.
  • Provide all three focus-skill goals before each session. Observe each learner, not only the pair's product. Schedule a short individual follow-up where a shared task hides individual evidence.
  • For learners who need longer reading or device instruction, provide extra practice time. A partly completed source-verification table can support A1-Q10; record that scaffold. The timings below are a pilot proposal, not a reason to rush or reclassify incomplete work as completed.

Session 1 • 60 minutes

Focus Skills for Success: Reading, Communication, Collaboration.

Minutes Instructor action Learner action / evidence
0–5 (5) State purpose and three focus skills. Invite one familiar road use or allow a supplied example. Ask learners to predict a machine's purpose. Baseline response; preferred support recorded.
5–17 (12) Read/model the first paragraph, showing how to locate evidence and paraphrase. Explain infrastructure. Support A1-Q01–A1-Q02 without supplying every answer. Annotated reading and short history account.
17–30 (13) Model one flow connection and one equipment/function match. Explain the distinction between purpose and operation. A1-Q03 map and A1-Q04 six matches; pairs check then learners retain their own answers.
30–42 (12) Read the resource and road-type tables. Model “could serve” rather than a universal use claim. A1-Q05 resource question and A1-Q06 type/user connections.
42–55 (13) Explicitly model explain–listen–restate–clarify–confirm. Model inviting a contribution and disagreeing by referring to the document. Observe both turns. A1-Q07 explanation, clarification and joint map improvement with individual contributions noted.
55–60 (5) Give specific feedback and identify a next reading/support step. A1-Q08 exit note; collect the process map and observations.

Total: 5 + 12 + 13 + 12 + 13 + 5 = 60 minutes.

Session 2 • 60 minutes

Focus Skills for Success: Numeracy, Digital, Writing.

Minutes Instructor action Learner action / evidence
0–5 (5) Retrieve equipment/process vocabulary from Session 1; preview the three new focus skills. Show the difference between a count and a dollar amount. State a known word and a check they can use.
5–15 (10) Read the fictional brief. Model owner versus contractor versus funder. Use EPS as a demonstration of an unresolved label, not a current legal rule. A1-Q09 roles/funding calculation and A1-Q10 three verification entries.
15–25 (10) Think aloud through the East/West example: heading, whole, operation, units, check. Give a fraction aid showing two of five counters if helpful. Follow and explain one worked step.
25–35 (10) Assign independent A1-Q11; give just-in-time scaffolds, then allow peer checking. Individual calculations, interpretation and a recorded check.
35–50 (15) Demonstrate entry, a total, saving and reopening. Let each learner control the tool; observe individually. A1-Q12 digital table, verified totals, saved file and reopen demonstration.
50–60 (10) Model a concise opening and the proofreading checklist: role, number, source status, reader. A1-Q13 note, partner interpretation and one useful edit.

Total: 5 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 15 + 10 = 60 minutes. Device beginners may need an additional supported turn outside this core session. Keep Digital “not observed” until evidence is actually collected.

Session 3 • 60 minutes

Focus Skills for Success: Problem solving, Adaptability, Creativity and innovation.

Minutes Instructor action Learner action / evidence
0–8 (8) Retrieve one source-checking habit. Teach the four-step claim routine and model a narrow, conditional conclusion. Identify the claim, evidence, gap and possible next step in a short oral example.
8–22 (14) Read C1–C3. Prompt learners to compare like with like and identify missing information before deciding. A1-Q14 four-part response, including a revised claim.
22–34 (12) Present version 1 of the communication task. Model two different organizational ideas without designing the learners' entire sheet. A1-Q15 two sketches, choice reason and first draft, up to 150 words.
34–43 (9) Release the change card only after a first draft exists. Name a practical response: pause, identify what changed, preserve essential information, reprioritize. A1-Q16 change note and revision plan.
43–55 (12) Observe revisions and paired usability checks. Ask the reader to locate each answer rather than merely praise the layout. Revised sheet of no more than 90 words; A1-Q17 feedback and justified improvement; both drafts retained.
55–60 (5) Ask learners to point to evidence for the three focus skills and identify a transfer habit. A1-Q18 reflection with evidence references and next practice goal.

Total: 8 + 14 + 12 + 9 + 12 + 5 = 60 minutes. Overall: 180 minutes.

Answer key and acceptable responses

A1-Q01: Infrastructure is the shared physical systems supporting a community. Daily-life examples include reaching school, services or recreation; trade examples include deliveries and goods transport. Accept other sensible examples tied to the reading. Learner must locate relevant wording, not only repeat an instructor's answer.

A1-Q02: Some routes began as paths formed by repeated travel; people cleared/shaped routes and added stone/gravel or other materials; different road types serve different purposes and contexts. No dates are required. Do not insist this is the history of every road or community.

A1-Q03: Pit/quarry → plant → haul → place → compact. Purposes: obtain/process mineral material; prepare specified mixture; transport it; place it; compact it. Accept accurate paraphrases and note that real projects have more steps.

A1-Q04: 1 E4; 2 E5; 3 E2; 4 E6; 5 E1; 6 E3. Paver places/shapes the mat; roller compacts. Answers should not invent operating settings.

A1-Q05: A suitable question asks whether the material meets the project's specification, whether supply is approved/permitted for the project, or what properties/availability need checking. Examples include gravel, sand and crushed stone; source types include pits and quarries. The response must reject “nearby means suitable” as an unsupported inference.

A1-Q06: Accept two accurately described types and plausible conditional uses. For example, a gravel road could provide access to a work area; an asphalt-surfaced road could serve a town route. An ice road could support seasonal access under responsible-authority controls; do not accept learner-created safe-ice thresholds. User/access questions might concern a clear pedestrian route, access for a mobility device, delivery timing, bus access or emergency access. The task asks questions, not a learner-designed traffic-management plan.

A1-Q07: Observe accurate explanation, listener restatement, a useful clarification and a confirmation. Collaboration requires both contributions and a jointly agreed improvement; sitting in a pair alone is insufficient. Accept different culturally appropriate listening behaviours; eye contact is not a criterion.

A1-Q08: A road connects people/places or supports movement; equipment performs a particular production/placement task. Accept another accurate distinction and a genuine next vocabulary goal.

A1-Q09: Owner: fictional Town of Willow Reach. Contractor: fictional Pathway Paving Company. Contributors: Town $60,000; Regional Access Fund $40,000; Community Route Fund $20,000. Total $60,000 + $40,000 + $20,000 = $120,000. Funding does not by itself make an organization the road owner or the contractor; the brief assigns those roles separately. Do not generalize this fictional arrangement to all projects.

A1-Q10: Accept entries requesting full title/issuer/current version, legal versus contractual status and applicability for EPS and MMCD; ask the provider, responsible project authority, current official issuer or authorized contract documents. Funding entry should request the actual agreement, approved contribution and conditions from the funder/owner's authorized project records. “Search it online” alone is incomplete: name what evidence would establish the answer. No acronym expansion or legal conclusion is required.

A1-Q11: (1) Employers 4 + 3 + 3 = 10; workers 120 + 90 + 90 = 300. (2) Central has 3/10 of the employers, with ten as the whole. (3) Contract value $6,000,000 + $4,000,000 + $5,000,000 = $15,000,000; North exceeds Central by $2,000,000. (4) Supported comparison: North has one more employer than Central; Central and South have equal employer/worker counts; South's contract value is $1,000,000 greater than Central's. The table is fictional and limited to its three invented areas, so it cannot establish B.C. totals. (5) A valid different check could group 3 + 3 = 6, then 6 + 4 = 10, or verify $15m − $6m − $4m = $5m. Optional: 3/10 = 30%; profit requires costs and other accounting information not provided.

A1-Q12: Digital table has the three correct rows, totals 10, 300 and 15,000,000 with appropriate headings. Formula route uses B2:B4, C2:C4 and D2:D4 for B5, C5 and D5. Observe the learner enter/check at least one value, use the save workflow and reopen/find the record. A correctly printed table created entirely by someone else does not establish that learner's Digital evidence.

A1-Q13 — Example answer, not required wording: “In this practice project, the Town of Willow Reach owns the road. Pathway Paving Company carries out the contracted work. Fictional funding totals $120,000, including $60,000 from the Town. The invented industry table contains 10 employers. We still need the full title, current version and applicability of the EPS reference before using it to describe a real requirement.” Look for a useful edit after feedback; grammar feedback should serve clarity and the intended reader.

A1-Q14: (1) “Always.” (2) The listed one-way source distance is 20 km for the asphalt option and 50 km for the concrete option; the former is 30 km shorter. This does not establish actual total travel/emissions. (3) Any two relevant gaps: equal design/service requirements; material quantities; trips/loads; production data; vehicle/fuel assumptions; design life; maintenance; end-of-life; local environmental impacts. (4) Example: “The asphalt source is nearer in this fictional example. That may affect transport, but it does not prove which option has the lower total environmental impact. What comparable production, transport and maintenance evidence does the project reviewer need?” Do not reward an unsupported asphalt-versus-concrete winner.

A1-Q15: Two genuinely different organizations, such as a five-stage flow plus role boxes versus a three-question layout; a choice linked to the visitor's needs; a first draft before the change. Artistic polish is not the criterion.

A1-Q16: Learner identifies one-minute review, black-and-white print and 90-word limit; keeps the three factual answers and useful labels; revises prioritization/layout/wording without shrinking type. Record the strategy used, not a judgement of personality or willingness to comply.

A1-Q17: Reader can locate the three answers, identifies a specific point of clarity/confusion, and author uses the feedback to make or justify a change. Revised sheet is at most 90 words. Example of a valid short sheet:

Where does material come from? Mineral materials can come from pits or quarries. The basic flow is pit/quarry → plant → haul → place → compact.

Who owns this road? In our fictional project, the Town of Willow Reach owns it. Pathway Paving carries out the contracted work.

Does nearer mean greener? A shorter source route alone cannot rank total environmental impact. Comparable project requirements, quantities, transport, production and maintenance information are still needed.

A1-Q18: Accept specific references to A1-Q14, the before/after plan, the two layout ideas and tested revision. A generic “I used all the skills” is insufficient; ask the learner to point to an observable action or artifact.

Feedback rubric and evidence record

Use not observed / not yet demonstrated / demonstrated with support / demonstrated independently as local feedback descriptions, not official SFS proficiency levels or trade competency grades. Use “not observed” when evidence has not been collected; use “not yet demonstrated” when an observed attempt still needs practice. Record application in a new situation separately as a transfer note. A learner can show different descriptions for different skills. Record the support, the actual evidence and the next step; permit another attempt after feedback.

Communication A1-Q07: accurate explanation, listener restatement and clarification/check-back. One-way speech without checking understanding → practise “Have I understood…?” and swap roles.

Accessibility and extension

  • Offer large print, uncluttered copies, optional reading aloud/text-to-speech, glossary cards, manipulatives and calculator use. Record reading support so a read-aloud response is not reported as independent unaided reading. Check comprehension through the learner locating and using information with their chosen support.
  • For Writing, allow keyboard, speech-to-text or a scribe with learner control over wording and revision. Preserve the learner's authored text and record the method. A purely oral answer without a text product does not demonstrate this writing outcome.
  • For Communication, accept an accessible interactive mode, including AAC. Assess message, listening/interpretation and clarification rather than accent, speaking speed or eye contact.
  • Keep pair tasks predictable and allow instructor pairing. Invite community experience without requiring anyone to represent a Nation or disclose personal histories. Local cultural knowledge should be used only with appropriate local direction and permission.
  • For Digital, paper is a temporary alternate for the content; schedule actual tool use and leave Digital unobserved until then. A partner's completed file is insufficient evidence for both learners.
  • Start: use a partly ordered process map, highlighted headings, fraction counters and a preformatted spreadsheet. Practise: remove some prompts. Extend: use the optional percentage, compare two supported statistics, or test the information sheet with a second reader. These labels are instructional choices, not validated SFS complexity levels.
  • If an instructor changes the fictional data, regenerate the answer key and check totals before delivery. Do not mix a real statistic into the fictional table without a source, period, geography and clear label.

After the pilot

Record actual timings, unclear vocabulary, data errors, device barriers, individual evidence gaps and learner feedback. Obtain qualified technical review of A1 terminology and the coverage map. Continue with Industry evidence and decisions, then the remaining Line A units. Check the retained source dates and any changes before delivery; use the technical handoff record for reviewer decisions and required revisions. Keep the fictional practice set available as an entry task and retain separate evidence from the public-source activities. Classroom participation alone does not establish technical competency.
