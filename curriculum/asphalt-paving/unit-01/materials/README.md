## Data labels and spreadsheet layout

**Both datasets are invented teaching data. They are not B.C. industry statistics, company accounts or evidence of actual funding.** Areas are fictional; each employer and worker is counted once within its table. Contract value is in dollars for a fictional practice year and is not profit. Keep the datasets separate.

Each CSV has its header in row 1 and these columns: **A Area; B Employers; C Workers; D Contract value (dollars)**. The independent data occupy rows 2–4, matching the workbook's `SUM(B2:B4)`, `SUM(C2:C4)` and `SUM(D2:D4)` examples. The worked example contains only two data rows. Do not apply the independent table's row ranges to it without checking.

The independent CSV deliberately contains no answer totals. The instructor key supplies them. Import/open the CSV as a teaching aid when appropriate, but A1-Q12 requires each learner to control the tool, check an entry, save and find the record. A prefilled file alone is not evidence of those actions. A saved spreadsheet format can retain formulas and formatting; exporting back to CSV usually retains values only.

## Observe the learner's work

Make a separate observation-record copy for each learning ID. Session and skill are prefilled; observation date, evidence, support, feedback status, next step, recheck date and transfer note are blank. Use these local feedback statuses:

- Not observed: sufficient evidence has not yet been collected.
- Not yet demonstrated: an attempt was observed, but the task criterion has not yet been met.
- Demonstrated with support
- Demonstrated independently

Record application in a new situation separately in the **Transfer note** column, including the new context and the evidence. These are local learning descriptions, not official Skills for Success proficiency levels or trade competency grades. Note actual support and evidence rather than inferring skill from attendance. Paper work can preserve the task when a device is unavailable; Digital remains **not observed** until the learner uses the tool.

The response sheet and cards are original classroom materials. They explain purposes and support source literacy; they provide no equipment-operating authorization or current legal requirements. The instructor guide records source citations and the pending current-source supplements for A1 task 6 industry statistics, task 8 regulations/funding and task 9 environmental comparison.
