Unit 1 · Answer and teaching guidance

Use 10 minutes for source/measure vocabulary, 20 for LA1-01–02, 25 for LA1-03–04, 25 for LA1-05–06, and 10 for revision. Prepare I1–I5 on paper and an actual device turn for each learner. The public-source register and retained extracts make the lesson workable offline; online retrieval remains unobserved until performed.

Task Expected evidence and useful feedback
LA1-01 Employment is 246,750 people in B.C. construction in 2024. Full-time share is 12 percentage points above the all-industry figure. 87,280 is forecast construction openings over 2025–2035, not guaranteed paving jobs. I1b's categories sum to 427 statistical locations; 175 − 63 = 112 locations; 175 ÷ 427 × 100 = 40.983606…%, or 40.98%. The road-construction classification includes highways, streets and bridges. Locations are not unique companies or workers; size ranges must not be used to estimate total employees. Comparing this location count with all-construction employment mixes measure, scope and period.
LA1-02 A retained note identifies title, WorkBC/Province of B.C., URL, access date, metric/year and any change. Observe locating, saving and reopening independently of the accuracy of the paper answer.
LA1-03 I2 supports selected orientation requirements. I3 explains the document system, but the actual signed contract and relevant amendments/special provisions are needed to establish its applicable edition. I4 identifies the public funders and contractor. I3b confirms End Product Specification in the 2006 clause; it does not establish every old note’s intended usage. Profit and a separate unexplained note still require further evidence. A register must not silently label a specification “law.”
LA1-04 215.19 + 385.58 = $600.77 million. Federal fraction 215.19 ÷ 600.77 × 100 = 35.819032…%, or 35.82%. The provincial share is 64.18% after rounding. The total exceeds $600 million by $0.77 million. Province is the recorded contracting authority; Canada and B.C. are reported funders; Kicking Horse Canyon Constructors is the awarded contractor. The source does not identify profit.
LA1-05 Both recovery pathways are supported. No matched environmental winner is established. A valid grid marks missing quantities/designs, production factors, distances/modes, construction impacts, maintenance schedules, use-stage assumptions and recovery/disposal scenarios. The proposed common service is the 50 m × 6 m route for 20 years; that is a comparison brief, not a verified design.
LA1-06 A per-tonne factory result has a different unit and boundary from a whole-route life-cycle result. Different required quantities may change the comparison. A useful request specifies equal function, service period, approved design quantities, included stages, current geography-relevant data, maintenance and end-of-life assumptions, uncertainty and reviewer. Do not reward an invented emissions calculation.

Worked conclusion: “Both asphalt and concrete have documented material-recovery pathways. The supplied information does not establish which option has lower whole-life emissions for the same 50 m by 6 m route over 20 years. A comparison needs approved pavement quantities, production and transport data, construction and maintenance assumptions, and end-of-life treatment. A factory result per tonne cannot establish the proposed whole-route claim. We can request comparable evidence before recommending an environmental preference.”

Unit 2 · Answer and teaching guidance

Suggested 75 minutes: 10 for purpose and vocabulary, 15 for chronology, 15 for materials/categories, 15 for paired labels, 15 for independent caption and 5 for reflection. Supply H1–H4 and paper or accessible drawing tools. Model observation versus interpretation before using the photograph description. No image or private archive is required.

Task Expected evidence
LA2-01 312 BCE Via Appia development begins; 1942 Alaska pioneer road completed; 1943 permanent location completed; 1946 Canadian portion responsibility transferred; 1948 civilian non-military opening; 1956 U.S. highway legislation. “Pioneer road” and “permanent location” are different milestones. Chronology alone cannot establish consultation, effects or all perspectives.
LA2-02 Materials: earth, cut stone, gravel, timber logs, concrete, asphalt mixture. Asphalt cement is the bituminous binder in asphalt; Portland cement is used in the cement-based binding system in concrete. Methods: closely laid crosswise logs; delivery/placement/compaction. Purposes: moving timber, military access, travel to a community facility. Accept an explanation that different designs/materials can serve the same purpose. A photo question might concern date, location, scale, layers, use or author.
LA2-03 Observable: logs across a route; truck in background; wet-looking appearance in the supplied description. Possible corduroy construction is a supported interpretation. Logging purpose, owner, actual moisture condition, date and location are not established. The truck does not prove logging.
LA2-04 H2 supports the specific wartime-access explanation. It does not support a universal war-only claim; H1 and H3 also identify trade and daily movement. 1948 − 1942 = 6 years. H2 records work/responsibility milestones within that interval.
LA2-05 One panel distinguishes timber/crosswise-log method from logging purpose. The other gives mixture delivery, paver/screed placement and roller compaction with preparation/checks acknowledged. Do not score drawing polish or accept an invented operating setting.
LA2-06 A valid caption integrates a correct date with a defined event, distinguishes method/material/purpose, gives evidence for demand and names a limitation. It may discuss public-record limits without asking learners to supply cultural knowledge.

Model caption: “The Alaska Highway's pioneer road was completed in 1942 as a wartime land-access project; its permanent location was completed in 1943. Roads can later support other uses, including civilian travel and trade. Material, method and purpose describe different things: timber logs are a material, corduroy is a construction form, and logging is a road use. Modern asphalt paving combines manufactured mixture, delivery, placement and compaction. These examples show changes in techniques and needs, but they do not establish a single history for every road or describe every community's experience. A local account needs its own evidence and appropriate permission.”

Unit 3 · Answer and teaching guidance

Use 15 minutes for ingredients and the flow visual, 15 for plant comparison, 15 for product clarification, 20 for recycling routes, 10 for the counter model and 15 for revision/digital note. Prepare 100 counters or a labelled paper grid; count categories, not a real mixture. Provide an actual device turn for LA3-07 if Digital is assessed.

Task Expected evidence
LA3-01 Asphalt cement and asphalt binder name the binding material in this introductory context. Aggregate is mineral material; asphalt binder binds the asphalt mixture; process fuel supplies energy and must not be assumed to be an ingredient. “Cement” may refer to asphalt cement or Portland cement. An additive may alter a specified property; its purpose and suitability require the actual product and approved mixture requirements. A pit commonly supplies unconsolidated material; a quarry commonly extracts rock that is processed. Neither source name guarantees suitable aggregate. Source suitability needs specification/material-property evidence.
LA3-02 B indicates batches; D indicates a continuous drum-mix process in the simplified case. Shared stages include supplied materials, proportioning/mixing, storage or loading and haul. A drying drum alone does not distinguish the two. Figure LA-4 shows separate weighed batches versus a continuing measured material stream; some continuous facilities use a separate dryer and continuous mixer.
LA3-03 Ask for full product name/grade, current approved design or project specification, supplier/product confirmation and relevant safety/handling information through the responsible person. The undefined “Group A / C” and “altered oil” labels require an explicit clarification; do not infer a grade or product from them. No grade is approved by the task.
LA3-04 Plant route: recovery/processing → suitable plant input → approved mixture/use. Hot-in-place route: specialist coordinated process works on existing pavement in place. Both need applicable approved process, suitability and quality evidence. Removal is not proof of later reuse.
LA3-05 80%, 15%, 5%; sum 100%. The recovered-mixture category includes more than one constituent, so the model does not isolate total binder content. The counters are not mass or a production recipe.
LA3-06 Remove the unsupported plant-haul label and show the in-place route; retain the idea of recovering/reusing existing material. Identify equipment/process details as pending. Observe each learner's correction and handover.
LA3-07 The learner controls the wording, saves and retrieves a versioned file and explains the distinctions. Paper composition supports Writing but does not demonstrate save/reopen. Figure LA-3: 7 Hopper (holds supplied material); 10 Screed Extension (extends working width); 11 End Gate (helps form the mat edge); 12 High-Speed Rubber Tracks (travel component, not a speed instruction). Figure LA-5 route: hopper receives → slat conveyors move rearward → augers distribute across width → screed places/shapes the mat. Conveying rearward and distributing laterally are distinct functions. Check two selected labels against the photo. Retain model 8680, manual 1033428-SOP, printed p. 3-7 / PDF p. 43, and serial scope 365152 and above. The image does not establish operating settings, current condition or applicability to another machine.

Unit 4 · Answer and teaching guidance

Suggested 90 minutes: role mapping 15, safety case 20, licences 15, handover 10, schedule 15 and development/enquiry 15. Allow spoken planning before authored writing. Keep every role-play in the classroom; provide no simulated machine signals. Evaluate observable actions and source distinctions rather than confidence, accent or posture.

Task Expected evidence
LA4-01 Broad functions and meaningful dependencies: haul supplies mixture; paver places; screed helps control the placed layer; raker supports handwork/finishing; roller compacts; milling removes/profiles existing material. A handover identifies status or information, not an invented signal. W1 authorizes no site access or operation.
LA4-02 Equipment-name knowledge is insufficient workplace-specific orientation. Learner asks for supervisor/contact, relevant hazard and emergency information, task instruction/demonstration and the appropriate communication arrangements. The employer's obligation is not transferred to the learner.
LA4-03 Admission: provider's current intake requirements. Licence: ICBC/applicable legal requirements for the actual vehicle/activity. Competence: appropriate training and assessment records. Assignment: responsible employer/site authorization. Class 5/7 in a provider prerequisite cannot authorize every truck or machine.
LA4-04 Example: “The role map is ready for review, but licensing is still pending. We can print the classroom explanation with that question marked open and ask the responsible person before adding an authorization claim.” Observe the receiver's check-back and each contribution.
LA4-05 18:00–24:00 is 6 h; 00:00–02:00 is 2 h; elapsed 8 h. Subtract 0.5 h = 7.5 paid hours in the fictional record. Add Wednesday 6 h = 13.5 h. Questions may concern overtime, cancellation/travel pay, breaks, fatigue controls or actual schedule arrangements. No legal entitlement follows from these invented inputs.
LA4-06 Collective bargaining is negotiation between an employer and workers represented by a union about employment terms; the applicable parties, agreement and dates must be checked. Forecast demand and attendance do not guarantee hiring, continued work or qualification. Questions identify the actual agreement/terms and role-specific pathway; the goal includes a task, evidence, support, check date and responsible reviewer. A saved/reopened file is separate from a paper enquiry. Do not require sending, personal employment disclosure or joining any organization.

Use 90 minutes: claim reading 15, material/lightweight-fill discussion 15, cost calculation 10, supplier enquiry 10, option table 20, changed condition 10 and brief/check 10. Supply N1–N8 and the retained public-source excerpts. Learners need no product samples, site data or external accounts. Task Expected evidence
LA6-01 Paving-stone permeability depends on the complete system; porous asphalt with a reservoir still needs site/drainage design and maintenance; recycling alone cannot establish total impact. Each revision names a condition and a question rather than simply adding “may” to a vague claim.
LA6-02 $1,145,000 − $633,000 = $512,000. Limits include historical 2016/2017 context, California/U.S. geography/currency context, project-specific quantities/design, missing installation/overhead/profit and no current B.C. supplier quotes. Technical suitability depends on site/design requirements, not the cheapest listed total. The example concerns lightweight fill; that role cannot be relabelled as approved pavement sub-base without design and material evidence.
LA6-03 Questions identify actual product/system, intended use, relevant tested performance, maintenance, compatibility and approval. Pattern questions include access/visibility and permission for any protected imagery. No universal epoxy, colour or pattern performance is accepted without evidence.
LA6-04 Porous asphalt relates to water management, subject to site/loading/drainage/maintenance study. Coloured asphalt may relate to wayfinding; it does not answer infiltration or structural needs by colour alone. EPS geofoam relates to embankment/load conditions that have not been established for this courtyard. Accept different investigation shortlists when evidence and limits are explicit. Appropriate review may include qualified pavement/civil/geotechnical/design professionals and the facility's maintenance decision-maker.
LA6-05 Lack of a specialized-maintenance commitment is relevant to clogging/maintenance risk in the porous-asphalt reference. A reasonable revision makes maintenance feasibility a condition for investigation or postpones that option. It does not establish an alternative's suitability.
LA6-06 The brief recommends an investigation, with a known mechanism, a case-specific unknown, source date and next decision. Reader can distinguish a recommendation to study from permission to construct. Record actual file actions separately.

Model brief: “For the fictional 600 m² courtyard, investigate whether a porous-asphalt/stone-reservoir system could meet the surface-water and loading requirements. FHWA's2015 brief describes the mechanism and identifies clogging and specialized maintenance as considerations. We have no infiltration/geotechnical results or maintenance commitment, so we cannot recommend construction. First obtain site/design review and a feasible maintenance plan. Keep environmental comparisons within a defined life-cycle boundary and use project-specific information before ranking options.”
