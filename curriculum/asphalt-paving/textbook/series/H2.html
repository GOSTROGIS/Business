Milling and reclaiming safety procedures

A milling plan must account for the cutting system, material discharge, truck movement, traffic and people on the ground. Agree on boundaries and communication before the rotor or travel system moves.

Learning step 1

Interpret and follow job plan

Read the path, pattern, depth, pass sequence, utilities, obstacles, discharge arrangement and planned stopping points. Confirm the current revision and who can authorize a change. Walk the area only when access is controlled. Resolve utility information and unidentified obstructions before cutting. Brief the plan with ground crew, truck drivers and traffic-control personnel, using a drawing and read-back.

Learning step 2

Awareness of machine-specific safety

Recognize rotor and conveyor entanglement, ejected material, silica-containing dust, exhaust, noise, hot surfaces, pressurized hydraulics, blind areas and raised components. Dust control is an engineered system requiring inspection and effective operation; a visible spray does not prove adequate exposure control. Block or support raised equipment using specified devices before access. Guard removal, clearing jams and service require the applicable isolation procedure.

Learning step 3

Traffic Control requirements

The traffic-control plan and site assessment determine TCP positions, numbers, signs, devices and communication. Milling traffic, public traffic and pedestrians can change relative positions as work advances. Keep a defined route and reassess driveways, intersections and service access. Only trained and authorized people perform assigned traffic-control roles. Stop if control or communication is lost.

Learning step 4

Proper PPE

Select PPE through the hazard assessment and current requirements, including suitable footwear, head, eye, hearing and high-visibility protection as applicable. Respiratory protection requires the employer’s program, correct selection, training and fit testing where required. WorkSafeBC Part 8 assigns workers specified basic clothing, general-purpose gloves, footwear and safety headgear, while employers provide other required PPE; agreements may provide more. The employer must ensure required PPE is used and maintained.

Learning step 5

Lockout procedures

Identify every hazardous energy source, including engine/fuel, electrical, hydraulic, gravity, rotating parts and stored pressure. Use the machine-specific written lockout procedure: stop, isolate, secure, dissipate or restrain stored energy and verify the safe state before work. A key removed or battery switch off does not alone establish full isolation. Each worker’s protection follows the applicable lockout system.

Learning step 6

Secure equipment for safety

Park in the designated stable location, lower or support components as instructed, apply parking controls and required chocks, and secure keys and access. Protect the public from the machine and residual hazards. Remove or secure detachable items without leaving guards missing. Record defects and communicate the machine’s status to the next shift.

Worked example · A changed discharge route

The planned truck position is blocked by a delivery vehicle. Someone proposes swinging the conveyor over a pedestrian route.

Think it through.

Stop the conflicting movements, preserve the pedestrian boundary and ask the lead to revise the arrangement with traffic control. Check overhead and swept-path hazards. Resume only after the new plan is communicated and controls are in place.
