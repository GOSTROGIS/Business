Screed work safety procedures

Screed work places people close to hot material, moving extensions and the roller path. Safe positions and clear communication must remain effective as the paving width and site conditions change.

Learning step 1

Interpret job plan

Review path, pattern, thickness, temperature requirements, crown, joints, obstacles and access. Confirm the current width and any planned changes before extending equipment. Agree on who controls each side, how adjustments are communicated and how the paver/roller crews respond to a stop.

Learning step 2

Machine-specific safety hazards

Identify hot plates and mix, heater energy, fumes, noise, hydraulic pressure, extension pinch zones and moving machinery. Keep feet and clothing clear of the mat and hot surfaces. Wet or unsuitable footwear can worsen burn risk. Plan heat exposure, hydration and breaks through the site program. Do not step into the roller path to inspect the edge; coordinate a safe observation opportunity.

Learning step 3

Traffic-control requirements

Use the traffic-control plan and assigned trained personnel. The required TCP arrangement depends on the site assessment, not a fixed number in a classroom example. Keep long tools, cables and extension movements inside the controlled work area. Reassess when the work passes an entrance or narrows a public route.

Learning step 4

Proper PPE

Use the selected footwear, head, eye, hearing, visibility and heat/chemical protection, with training in use and limitations. Check fit and condition, including wet or contaminated clothing. Follow current Part 8 responsibilities and employer arrangements. PPE cannot protect a person who enters an uncontrolled crush zone.

Learning step 5

Lockout procedures

Before service or hazardous access, apply the machine-specific isolation for electrical/heater, fuel where fitted, hydraulic, gravity and stored energy. Support raised components using specified devices and verify the safe state. Screed heating may be electric, propane or another system; identify the actual equipment rather than applying a generic fuel-isolation instruction.

Learning step 6

Secure machine for safety

At parking or interruption, secure the screed and paver together using the required supports/locks, parking controls and access protection. Prevent unintended extension movement and protect people from residual heat. Report defects and confirm the status with the responsible operator.

Worked example · Width changes beside a walkway

The plan calls for widening the mat, but the extension would enter a protected walking route.

Think it through.

Hold the extension movement, communicate the conflict and have the responsible lead resolve the work/access arrangement. Brief the change with traffic control and the crew before moving. Do not rely on a shouted warning as the only control.
