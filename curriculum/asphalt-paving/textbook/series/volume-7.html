Use this volume

This volume provides complete classroom readings, worked examples, named practice tasks and answer guidance for screed control and handwork. Follow the learning route in each chapter, study the labelled figures and explain your reasoning. The separate answer book contains the worked keys and observation records.

Care for people, place and the next person using the work runs through the cases. Local First Nations, Métis and Inuit learners and partners bring distinct knowledge and priorities. Participation does not require sharing personal stories or cultural teachings, and no diagram claims to represent a Nation’s protocol.

Classroom authoring is complete for the listed competencies. Qualified technical review, actual fleet/site matching, a local teaching pilot and observed practical qualification are separate requirements. The practical records are ready for an instructor to configure and use; they do not certify unobserved work.

Keep the term with its meaning

Aggregate
Mineral particles forming the solid skeleton of the mixture or granular layer.
Binder
The asphalt material that coats and binds the aggregate in the specified mixture.
Compacted thickness
Layer thickness after the required compaction process; distinct from loose depth behind the screed.
Density reference
The specified comparison value used as the denominator of a percent-density calculation.
Gradation
Distribution of aggregate particle sizes, commonly expressed as cumulative percent passing specified sieves.
Head of material
The quantity/distribution of mix immediately in front of the screed.
Job mix formula (JMF)
The accepted mixture proportions and properties under the applicable quality/contract process.
Lockout
Isolation, securing and verification of hazardous energy for work requiring protection.
Lot / sub-lot
Defined portions of work used for sampling, testing and acceptance under the contract.
OMM
Operation and maintenance manual matched to the actual machine, serial range and configuration.
RAP
Reclaimed asphalt pavement: recovered material containing aggregate and aged binder.
SDS

Screed
The paver assembly that forms the mat; its response depends on setup, material and movement.
Station / offset

TCP
Traffic control person with the required training and assigned role.
Traceability
A record connecting material, location, time, sample, result and authorized decision.

Study chapter · Outline J1

Paving screed and attachments

The screed forms the asphalt layer behind the tractor. Its geometry, material supply, temperature and movement interact. Knowing the parts helps the crew discuss a mat problem without making several unrelated adjustments at once.

Screed categories
Common attachments
Principal functions

Learning step 1

Screed categories

A rigid or fixed-width screed uses its assembled width and approved extensions. A variable-width screed changes working width through its designed extension system. Front- and rear-mounted extensions have different arrangements and setup needs. Identify the actual main screed, extensions, plates, heating system, vibration/tamper equipment and control options. Nominal paver size does not establish the fitted screed’s full capability.

Learning step 2

Common attachments

Attachments can include bolt-on extensions, hydraulic extensions, end gates, cutoff shoes, curb-forming equipment and grade/slope controls. A stringline, ski, averaging beam or other reference system supplies different information; a laser or sonic sensor is not an interchangeable reference for every job. Confirm compatibility, mounting, calibration and safe movement envelope. Narrowing a pass with a cutoff attachment affects material distribution and the approved setup.

Learning step 3

Principal functions

The tractor pulls the screed through tow arms; the feed system supplies material in front of it. A floating screed reaches a working balance influenced by tow-point position/angle of attack, material head, speed, mixture and screed condition. A control change takes travel distance to develop its full effect. Do not repeatedly adjust before observing the response. The main plate and extensions must work together to avoid lines, tearing or uneven thickness. Heat prevents sticking to cold plates; it is not a substitute for correct material temperature or setup.

Worked example · An extension line

A visible line follows the inner edge of an extension while the rest of the mat looks consistent.

Think it through.

Record side, station and conditions. Check the relevant extension setup, plate condition, heating and material distribution through the authorized procedure. A line is a clue; do not assume one control or universal adjustment will fix every model.

Study chapter · Outline J2

Screed work safety procedures

Screed work places people close to hot material, moving extensions and the roller path. Safe positions and clear communication must remain effective as the paving width and site conditions change.

Interpret job plan
Machine-specific safety hazards
Traffic-control requirements
Proper PPE
Lockout procedures
Secure machine for safety

Learning step 1

Interpret job plan

Review path, pattern, thickness, temperature requirements, crown, joints, obstacles and access. Confirm the current width and any planned changes before extending equipment. Agree on who controls each side, how adjustments are communicated and how the paver/roller crews respond to a stop.

Learning step 2

Machine-specific safety hazards

Identify hot plates and mix, heater energy, fumes, noise, hydraulic pressure, extension pinch zones and moving machinery. Keep feet and clothing clear of the mat and hot surfaces. Wet or unsuitable footwear can worsen burn risk. Plan heat exposure, hydration and breaks through the site program. Do not step into the roller path to inspect the edge; coordinate a safe observation opportunity.

Learning step 3

Traffic-control requirements

Use the traffic-control plan and assigned trained personnel. The required TCP arrangement depends on the site assessment, not a fixed number in a classroom example. Keep long tools, cables and extension movements inside the controlled work area. Reassess when the work passes an entrance or narrows a public route.

Learning step 4

Proper PPE

Use the selected footwear, head, eye, hearing, visibility and heat/chemical protection, with training in use and limitations. Check fit and condition, including wet or contaminated clothing. Follow current Part 8 responsibilities and employer arrangements. PPE cannot protect a person who enters an uncontrolled crush zone.

Learning step 5

Lockout procedures

Before service or hazardous access, apply the machine-specific isolation for electrical/heater, fuel where fitted, hydraulic, gravity and stored energy. Support raised components using specified devices and verify the safe state. Screed heating may be electric, propane or another system; identify the actual equipment rather than applying a generic fuel-isolation instruction.

Learning step 6

Secure machine for safety

At parking or interruption, secure the screed and paver together using the required supports/locks, parking controls and access protection. Prevent unintended extension movement and protect people from residual heat. Report defects and confirm the status with the responsible operator.

Worked example · Width changes beside a walkway

The plan calls for widening the mat, but the extension would enter a protected walking route.

Think it through.

Hold the extension movement, communicate the conflict and have the responsible lead resolve the work/access arrangement. Brief the change with traffic control and the crew before moving. Do not rely on a shouted warning as the only control.

Study chapter · Outline J3

Asphalt screed operator roles and responsibilities

The screed operator helps turn the plan into a consistent mat. The role combines observation, measured checks, timely communication and controlled adjustment.

Plan work procedures
Maintain mat thickness
Communication techniques
Problem solving strategies
The importance of teamwork
Importance of adhering to standards

Learning step 1

Plan work procedures

Read the accepted thickness, width, crown, joint and end-product requirements before setup. Identify where measurements will be made, who records them and who authorizes changes. Confirm the drawing, JMF and quality plan versions. Plan transitions and obstacles before they reach the screed.

Learning step 2

Maintain mat thickness

Monitor loose mat thickness and width using approved methods and the trial-based relationship to compacted thickness. Material volume depends on area and thickness; delivery and feed must support the intended rate. A thick area and thin area may average to the correct tonnage while both miss the geometry. Keep checks tied to locations.

Learning step 3

Communication techniques

Communicate with the paver operator and handwork crew using short, specific messages: side, station, observation, requested action and acknowledgement. Share changes in head of material, speed, temperature or width. Do not issue several conflicting adjustments through different people.

Learning step 4

Problem solving strategies

Investigate a symptom before adjusting. Compare current conditions with the stable setup, identify a plausible cause and make an authorized controlled change. Allow the response to develop over the machine’s relevant travel distance, then measure again. Document what changed. Repeated large corrections can produce waves and make the cause harder to identify.

Learning step 5

The importance of teamwork

The paver operator controls the steady supply/travel process; screed operators control assigned screed functions; rakers manage authorized handwork; quality personnel provide measurements and disposition. These roles depend on one another. Respectful challenge is useful when someone sees a discrepancy: ask for the observation and check it together.

Learning step 6

Importance of adhering to standards

Meet the specified geometry and quantity without treating either excess or shortage as automatically acceptable. Record departures and obtain authorized correction. End-product specifications also include properties beyond screed control, such as density and smoothness. Do not promise full acceptance from a loose-thickness check alone.

Worked example · One controlled change

A mat check is low after a speed change. One person wants more head, another wants a tow-point change and a third wants a different speed.

Think it through.

Pause the conflicting instructions, record the observation and changed condition, and have the responsible lead coordinate a controlled response. Recheck after the relevant response distance. Changing everything at once prevents a useful diagnosis.

Screed maintenance

Screed maintenance protects plate condition, movement and the quality of the next mat. Use the fitted screed’s service instructions and coordinate shared systems with the paver operator.

Service lubrication system (greasing)
Inspect and clean components as required
Housekeeping tasks
Maintain screed plate

Learning step 1

Service lubrication system (greasing)

Identify lubrication points, including thickness-control screws and extension mechanisms where specified. Confirm lubricant, interval, quantity and safe position. Clean fittings and load the grease gun correctly. Centralized systems and optional fittings change the route; do not assume grease at one point reaches every component. Report damaged fittings and protect the work area from excess lubricant.

Learning step 2

Inspect and clean components as required

Inspect accessible components and the paver systems serving the screed, including hydraulic lines, electrical/heating connections and cooling systems where applicable. Radiator, oil-cooler, battery, plug and dipstick checks may belong to the shared tractor maintenance schedule rather than a separate screed engine. Identify what is actually fitted. Use isolation, cooling and approved access; do not open hot pressure systems or diagnose electrical faults beyond authorization.

Learning step 3

Housekeeping tasks

Remove rubbish, buildup and excess grease from safe access surfaces, controls, steps, decks and any fitted cab/windows. Use compatible products and contain waste. Coordinate cleaning with the paver crew so no one restarts or moves a shared system unexpectedly. Restore guards and account for tools.

Learning step 4

Maintain screed plate

Inspect the screed plate and extension plates for wear, damage, distortion and buildup. Check adjustment and replacement criteria in the actual manual. A scuff or streak in the mat can indicate a plate/setup/material problem; record the pattern and investigate rather than assuming more heat will fix it. Plate replacement and alignment require authorized procedures, support and specified fasteners/settings. Record the condition before and after service.

Worked example · A recurring streak

A streak returns along the same line after surface buildup is removed.

Think it through.

Record its position and persistence. Have authorized personnel inspect plate wear/flatness, extension alignment, heating and relevant setup under safe conditions. Do not continue scraping a moving or hot hazard area or guess a replacement tolerance.

Study chapter · Outline J5

Operate asphalt screed

Screed operation combines careful setup with measured feedback. The learner practises on the selected machine under supervision, using its OMM for exact control actions and limits.

Set-up screed
Maintain proper thickness
Check depth of mat

Learning step 1

Set-up screed

Inspect the screed and surroundings, identify the fitted heating system and complete daily maintenance. Establish starting width, crown/contour, extension alignment and the trial-based loose-thickness setting. Lower onto the specified starting supports through the safe procedure. Operate heating only as instructed; protect against burns, fire and electrical hazards. The LeeBoy 8680 example identifies separate main/extension/end-gate heat controls and warns against overheating. Do not transfer its settings to another machine.

Learning step 2

Maintain proper thickness

Coordinate with the paver operator to maintain a suitable steady speed and material head. Monitor the mat, hydraulic/electrical indications and assigned controls. Make authorized small, deliberate changes to angle of attack, tow points or contour as appropriate, then allow the response to develop and recheck. Keep width changes coordinated with auger/feed distribution and safe clearance. Operate lights or other ancillary equipment only where fitted and included in the plan.

Learning step 3

Check depth of mat

Measure loose depth using the approved probe/tool and safe access method. Identify the base interface rather than pushing into soft support, record station/side and avoid contact with hot material or moving equipment. A probe reading is not a core result or proof of final compacted thickness. Compare repeated readings with the specified target and trial relationship, report departures and retain the record.

Worked example · Loose depth and final thickness

A verified trial relationship is loose/compacted = 1.25. Target compacted thickness is 40 mm. A loose-depth check reads 50 mm.

Think it through.

Estimated loose target = 40 × 1.25 = 50 mm, so the reading matches that exercise target. Final compacted thickness still requires the specified verification; one loose reading does not establish the whole mat’s acceptance.

Study chapter · Outline J6

Follow shut down procedures

Screed shutdown and cleaning are coordinated with the paver. The final state must protect people from heat, movement and unsupported components while keeping the equipment ready for inspection.

Shut down procedures
Clean screed after use

Learning step 1

Shut down procedures

Choose the approved parking position with service access and suitable ground. Follow the actual stopping, heater shutdown, component positioning/support and paver parking sequence. Inspect safely for leaks, damage and missing guards. Apply full lockout when the cleaning or service task requires it; normal shutdown alone is insufficient. Secure controls and access and record the final state.

Learning step 2

Clean screed after use

Clean buildup using the manufacturer-approved method, compatible product and SDS controls. Coordinate with the paver crew so shared systems cannot move unexpectedly. Control ignition sources and residual heat; never spray a flammable product onto a hot heater or open flame. A fixed “20-minute clean” is not a universal safe duration. Collect scrapings and wash/cleaning waste, protect drainage and restore guards. Record defects and any work still required before the next start.

Worked example · Cleaning is delayed

The screed remains too hot for the approved cleaning product and procedure when the scheduled shift ends.

Think it through.

Maintain the safe shutdown state and coordinate the required cooling and cleaning arrangements. Do not spray early to meet the schedule. Record the outstanding task and responsible person so the next shift does not assume cleaning is complete.

Study chapter · Outline K1

Raker roles and responsibilities

The handwork crew shapes and finishes areas the paver cannot complete on its own. A raker needs judgement about material, grade, joints and timing, as well as awareness of every machine moving nearby.

Typical raker duties

Learning step 1

Typical raker duties

Typical duties include preparing small areas, distributing mix around structures, finishing edges, correcting authorized local irregularities and preparing longitudinal or transverse joints. A lute commonly has a long handle and a broad head with straight and toothed edges; a rake, shovel, tamper and wheelbarrow serve different purposes. Inspect handle attachment, head/brace condition and working edges before use.

Work around manholes and other appurtenances to the approved elevation and drainage detail. Remove excess and square the end of a run through the planned method. Avoid scattering coarse material over a finished mat or repeatedly disturbing a surface until the mixture cools. A paver or screed problem that repeats needs communication and correction at its cause, not endless hand repair.

At joints, understand which face is prepared, which treatment is specified, what overlap/allowance is intended and how compaction will occur. Longitudinal joints run generally along the paving direction; transverse joints cross it. Their details and rolling sequence depend on the project and mixture. Do not infer a universal joint allowance from a sketch.

Coordinate with the paver, screed and roller operators before entering an area. Keep tools and handles out of traffic and machine paths. Preserve accessible routes and protect nearby drains. Careful handwork serves the finished road and the people using it; communicate a concern early rather than hiding it under a cosmetic finish.

Worked example · The same hollow returns

A small low area appears repeatedly behind the same screed location. The raker has filled it twice.

Think it through.

Report the repeated location and timing to the screed/quality lead. Complete only authorized hand correction while the underlying cause is investigated. Repeated filling without diagnosis can change texture and compaction and hide a continuing setup issue.

Raking safety procedures

Handwork places a person close to hot material and moving equipment while using long tools. Plan the work position and escape route before concentrating on the surface.

Interpret job planning
Raker-specific safety hazards
Traffic control requirements
Proper PPE

Learning step 1

Interpret job planning

Read the path, work pattern, thickness, temperature requirements, crown, obstacles and sequence. Agree when handwork can enter and when the roller needs the area. Identify the safe material-transfer route, including wheelbarrow movement. Protect public access and confirm how a changing condition will be communicated.

Learning step 2

Raker-specific safety hazards

Recognize burns, heat strain, fumes, noise, dust, pressurized systems, blind areas, pinch zones and traffic. Keep long handles inside the work area. Use manageable loads and a stable stance, move feet to turn rather than twisting under load, and vary tasks/breaks through the work plan. Do not overreach to save a step. Wet or unsuitable footwear can worsen hot-material exposure. Report fatigue, dizziness or damaged equipment early.

Learning step 3

Traffic control requirements

Coordinate with trained traffic-control personnel and the designated equipment signallers. A raker does not step into live traffic to retrieve a tool or direct a truck without the assigned role and controls. Obtain acknowledgement before approaching a machine’s working area. If visibility or communication is lost, stop the affected activity and move only through the established safe route.

Learning step 4

Proper PPE

Use task-selected footwear, head, eye, hearing, visibility and heat/chemical protection. Inspect condition, fit and contamination. Follow current Part 8 supply responsibilities and employer arrangements. PPE does not replace ergonomic planning or separation from equipment. The instructor observes actual safe tool handling, communication and movement, not only whether the learner can name PPE.

Worked example · A handle reaches into the lane

A worker turns with a long lute while watching the mat. Its handle crosses the work-zone boundary.

Think it through.

Stop and reposition through the safe route. Review the work space, tool orientation and traffic boundary with the lead. A safe stance must account for the whole tool, not only the worker’s feet.

Study chapter · Outline K3

Place asphalt hot mix

Hand placement aims for a consistent layer that can be compacted properly. Work promptly and deliberately, using the approved preparation, joint and compaction methods for the actual repair or small area.

Common hand work crew duties
Common techniques

Learning step 1

Common hand work crew duties

Prepare the road edge, driveway, intersection, trench patch or pothole according to the specified repair detail. Confirm sound boundaries, support, drainage, depth and the required bond treatment. Apply the specified tack/emulsion to prepared faces under its procedure; an inert simulation may be used in training. Use shovels to place manageable amounts close to their final position. Coordinate delivery without entering a truck’s uncontrolled discharge area.

A wheelbarrow can move small quantities where the route, load, footing and heat controls allow. Inspect wheel, handles and tray, keep the load manageable and use the designated transfer arrangement. Spread with the suitable toothed or straight lute edge for the material and task. Avoid excessive manipulation that separates particles or cools the mix. Grade changes need controlled placement to limit uneven compaction, not an arbitrary heap of loose material.

Learning step 2

Common techniques

“Bumping” in this teaching context means controlled local adjustment of the loose mat with the approved hand tool to bring material into the required line or shape. The instructor demonstrates the project’s technique and terminology; it is not permission to strike machinery or people. Ramping forms a planned transition, which may be temporary or permanent according to the detail. Work around manholes to the verified elevation and crossfall without trapping water.

Square and prepare the end of a run and joints using the accepted method. Coordinate tamping/compaction equipment and roller access while the mixture remains suitable. A tiger torch or other heating tool is used only where specifically authorized, with its own training, equipment instructions and fire controls. It is not a default way to restore cooled or unsuitable mix. Inspect the finished line, surface and drainage, and report defects for authorized correction.

Worked example · A small rectangular patch

A prepared patch is 1.2 m × 0.8 m with 0.06 m compacted depth.

Think it through.

Area = 0.96 m²; compacted volume = 0.0576 m³. Material quantity requires an appropriate density and justified allowance. Confirm the repair detail and compaction method; the volume calculation does not establish that a single lift or any particular tool is suitable.
