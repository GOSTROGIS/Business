Crushing process and paving aggregate production

Aggregate production changes a natural or reclaimed feed into identified products. Each stage controls a different feature: crushing changes particle size and shape; screening separates sizes; washing removes specified unwanted fines; handling preserves the product. A plant diagram is useful because a defect observed at the stockpile may have begun several stages earlier.

The paving crew normally receives a finished product, but understanding its route helps them ask precise questions about segregation, contamination and supply. The material record should preserve source, product, test identity and destination.

Learning step 1

Gravel pits, quarries

A gravel pit extracts deposits that can contain varying proportions of gravel, sand and fines. A quarry extracts rock that commonly needs breaking and crushing. Deposits vary within a site, so one attractive sample does not establish an entire source’s suitability. Source approval, extraction permission, environmental controls and safe access are separate requirements. Dredged or recovered material likewise needs its authorized source and product assessment.

Learning step 2

Crushing equipment

A jaw crusher reduces feed between crushing surfaces using compression. A cone crusher reduces material between a moving and a fixed crushing surface. An impact crusher uses impact to break material. Feed properties, desired product and the designed circuit influence selection and result. A learner identifies function and flow, not an operating gap or speed from a generic sketch. Wear and inappropriate feed can change shape, size distribution and equipment demand.

Learning step 3

The screen

A screen separates material through openings of stated sizes. Material that passes an opening follows one route; retained material follows another, which may return to crushing. A torn, blinded or incorrectly configured screen can change the product even when the crusher is operating normally. Read the screen/product relationship and the inspection/test evidence together. A screen opening description alone is not the full aggregate specification.

Learning step 4

Conveyors

A conveyor carries material between stages or to storage. Fixed, transfer and stockpiling arrangements have different purposes. Belt, idlers, pulleys, drive, guards and discharge point are parts to identify. Transfer height, direction and handling can affect segregation and dust. The carrying route must be distinguished from a safe pedestrian route; a stopped belt is not an authorized crossing or maintenance condition.

Learning step 5

Feeders

A feeder controls how material enters a process. Its job is more than moving the largest possible amount: a consistent, suitable feed helps the downstream circuit operate as intended. Irregular feed, bridging or incompatible material can disturb processing. Changes are handled through the plant’s procedure; workers do not enter a hopper or reach into a feeder to correct the flow.

Learning step 6

Explain stockpiling and handling

Stockpiles keep identified products separate before use. Place and recover material using the approved handling arrangement to limit intermingling, segregation and contamination from the ground or adjacent products. Large particles can roll farther during pile formation, creating a different gradation at different locations. Maintain signs and records so a loader does not supply the wrong pile. Moisture and recovered-material variability also matter to later plant control.

Learning step 7

Washing procedures

Washing separates unwanted fine material through a designed water-and-material process. It can improve cleanliness for the intended product, but also creates wash water and collected fines that need permitted management. Washing does not automatically make a product suitable for every use. Check the specified test results and the site’s water/settling or recycling arrangement; uncontrolled discharge is not part of a legitimate material-improvement process.

Learning step 8

Types of aggregate transportation

Trucks, rail and barges can move aggregate between source, storage and plant. The selected transport method affects logistics and handling points, not the required product identity. Loads must remain traceable and protected against unacceptable contamination or segregation. Receiving checks compare the supplier, product, quantity and relevant test record with the order. A change of transport route or transloading point may introduce a new handling risk that belongs in the supply plan.

Worked example · Find the process change

Fictional circuit: a screen sends its oversize back to a cone crusher. A damaged screen allows larger particles into the finished-product pile. The delivery ticket still carries the correct product name.

Think it through.

The name on the ticket has not verified the physical product. Identify affected production and stockpile records, isolate the questioned product through the quality process, inspect the screen safely and obtain the specified testing. Do not blend the questioned pile into accepted material to hide the difference. The process diagram points to where the change occurred and which records may be affected.
