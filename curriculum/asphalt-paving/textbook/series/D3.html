Asphalt plant and placement emissions

Emissions are releases from a process into the environment; worker exposure is contact with a hazard at the workplace. They are related but are not measured or regulated in exactly the same way. A stack result does not establish a worker’s breathing-zone exposure, and a workplace mask does not establish compliance with an environmental permit.

Learning step 1

Discuss role of Metro Vancouver (formerly GVRD) in enforcement and regulation of emissions

Metro Vancouver has a regional air-quality regulatory program. Determine whether the plant or activity is within that jurisdiction and which permit, bylaw or authorization applies. The retained B.C. Asphalt Plant Regulation explicitly states that it does not apply in the Metro Vancouver Regional District. Do not carry an outside-region authorization into the region by assumption.

The practical record identifies the actual site, operator, authorization, operating conditions, monitoring/reporting requirements and responsible contacts. A historical count of member communities is not a compliance test. Consult the current regional program and actual permit when preparing the work.

Learning step 2

Role of MOE in establishing regulations

Outside Metro Vancouver, the B.C. Asphalt Plant Regulation establishes requirements for the covered hot-mix and hot-in-place recycling plants, with applicable registration, emission, operating and reporting provisions. Its text also addresses additional municipal/regional requirements. Identify the actual activity and location before selecting the rule. Provincial ministry names and responsibilities can change; the legal instrument and current authorized contact must be recorded.

An environmental authorization is different from the workplace exposure-control program. Both can be relevant to the same plant. Keep monitoring data and reports associated with their method, location, interval and required interpretation.

Learning step 3

Use of Bag house

A baghouse is a fabric-filter dust collection system. In the MS-4 concept, dust-laden gas passes through the filtering arrangement, particles collect and cleaning releases collected material to a hopper. The gas and collected-solids paths are different. Primary collection may remove larger particles before secondary collection. The actual plant arrangement determines how fines are returned or removed.

Filter condition, seals, airflow and the designed cleaning/collection system affect performance. A visible change, alarm or damaged component needs the operator’s prescribed response. Do not assume a baghouse removes every gaseous pollutant: its principal dust-control function does not establish control of all emissions.

Learning step 4

Emission controls

Burner operation, aggregate moisture, production rate, material temperature, handling and dust-control performance can influence emissions. Heating asphalt excessively or using an unsuitable fuel can create problems; actual limits and operating settings come from the approved plant process and authorization. Placement work also needs exposure controls suited to the product and conditions.

Record observations without diagnosing the entire process from colour or odour alone. An unusual visible plume can prompt the required response and investigation, but it is not a measured concentration. Keep the operating interval and material/fuel identity so the responsible people can relate the observation to production and monitoring records.

Worked example · Two measurements, different questions

Fictional records show a compliant stack sample from last week and a worker complaint about fumes at today’s loadout. The supervisor proposes closing the complaint using the stack result.

Think it through.

The records differ in location, time and purpose. The stack result does not resolve today’s worker exposure. Apply the workplace reporting/assessment process, control the affected task as required and retain both records with their identities. Investigate the actual loadout conditions and any process change without inventing a concentration.
