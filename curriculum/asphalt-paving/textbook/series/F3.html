Final grade preparation

Final grade preparation is the last opportunity to correct the support and geometry before the mat covers them. Work from accepted levels, drainage and utility details. A neat edge and a clean base help the paving crew place the intended thickness.

Learning step 1

Check and raise utilities

Locate utility structures using the project information and required locating process. Confirm casting type, ownership, target elevation and approved adjustment detail. Raising a frame may require rings, approved bedding, structural support and curing time; it is not simply lifting the lid. Protect openings, coordinate access and traffic control, and prevent loose material entering the system. Only authorized workers make the adjustment. Recheck finished elevation and crossfall around the structure so the paving joint does not create a pond or abrupt bump.

Learning step 2

Fine grading

Fine grading removes high spots and fills low areas with the approved material and method. Check moisture and compaction as required, remove contamination and protect the surface from rutting. Do not conceal soft spots with a thin scatter of dry aggregate. Confirm that repairs are accepted and that the base is ready for the specified prime or tack treatment, where applicable. Record the inspection location and time because construction traffic and weather may change the surface after it was checked.

Learning step 3

Boning crown/cross-fall

Boning uses aligned sighting references to compare intermediate levels with a defined grade. All rods and reference heights must be consistent, and the method needs a verified starting and finishing reference. Check crown and crossfall with appropriate instruments and the drawing’s actual geometry. A crown is not always centred, and curves, transitions, intersections and accessibility features may have different requirements. Check machine slope calibration against an independent measure. Agreement between two displays using the same wrong reference is not independent verification.

Learning step 4

Limit of asphalt layout

Mark the finished mat limits from approved alignment, width, edge and tie-in details. Include tapers, entrances, curb offsets, construction joints and the paving sequence. Distinguish the paver’s nominal screed width from the specified finished width and any working allowance. Measure at identified stations and record changes. Discuss public access and emergency routes before changing the work boundary. If the layout conflicts with a utility or existing surface, request a resolved detail before placing material.

Worked example · Width before tonnage

A rectangular paving area is 60 m long. The approved width is 4.2 m, but the marked limits measure 4.0 m.

Think it through.

Approved area = 252 m²; marked area = 240 m². The shortfall is 12 m². Verify the drawing and correct the layout before ordering or placing to the wrong boundary. Extra mix later does not automatically restore the intended edge support and joint quality.
