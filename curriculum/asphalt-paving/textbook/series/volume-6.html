Use this volume

This volume provides complete classroom readings, worked examples, named practice tasks and answer guidance for paver operation. Follow the learning route in each chapter, study the labelled figures and explain your reasoning. The separate answer book contains the worked keys and observation records.

Care for people, place and the next person using the work runs through the cases. Local First Nations, Métis and Inuit learners and partners bring distinct knowledge and priorities. Participation does not require sharing personal stories or cultural teachings, and no diagram claims to represent a Nation’s protocol.

Classroom authoring is complete for the listed competencies. Qualified technical review, actual fleet/site matching, a local teaching pilot and observed practical qualification are separate requirements. The practical records are ready for an instructor to configure and use; they do not certify unobserved work.

Keep the term with its meaning

Aggregate
Mineral particles forming the solid skeleton of the mixture or granular layer.
Binder
The asphalt material that coats and binds the aggregate in the specified mixture.
Compacted thickness
Layer thickness after the required compaction process; distinct from loose depth behind the screed.
Density reference
The specified comparison value used as the denominator of a percent-density calculation.
Gradation
Distribution of aggregate particle sizes, commonly expressed as cumulative percent passing specified sieves.
Head of material
The quantity/distribution of mix immediately in front of the screed.
Job mix formula (JMF)
The accepted mixture proportions and properties under the applicable quality/contract process.
Lockout
Isolation, securing and verification of hazardous energy for work requiring protection.
Lot / sub-lot
Defined portions of work used for sampling, testing and acceptance under the contract.
OMM
Operation and maintenance manual matched to the actual machine, serial range and configuration.
RAP
Reclaimed asphalt pavement: recovered material containing aggregate and aged binder.
SDS
Safety data sheet for the actual product; read with its label and workplace procedure.
Screed
The paver assembly that forms the mat; its response depends on setup, material and movement.
Station / offset
Location along an alignment / distance and direction from a defined reference.
TCP
Traffic control person with the required training and assigned role.
Traceability
A record connecting material, location, time, sample, result and authorized decision.

Asphalt placement process

Asphalt placement links surface preparation, delivery, paver setup, screed control and rolling. A consistent process helps produce the intended thickness, bond and surface while keeping the whole crew informed.

Place prime and tack coats
Where to position the Paver
Ready the Paving Machine
Lower Screed on to blocks
Place Haul Truck Loads in Hopper
Place the asphalt product
Compact asphalt

Learning step 1

Place prime and tack coats

Prime and tack have different purposes. A prime treatment may prepare an approved absorbent granular surface; tack promotes bonding between specified pavement surfaces or lifts. Follow the actual product, surface preparation, application rate, curing/breaking and traffic restrictions in the project procedure. Inspect the label and SDS and protect drainage and public routes. Do not substitute one treatment for the other or pave over an unready surface because a truck has arrived.

Learning step 2

Where to position the Paver

Position the paver within the planned asphalt train: delivery, placement, handwork and breakdown/intermediate/finish rolling. Confirm line, width, starting joint, access and truck approach. Establish safe separation and communication using the site plan, machine instructions and risk assessment. A diagram of the train explains roles, not universal spacing.

Learning step 3

Ready the Paving Machine

Complete inspection, fluid checks, guards, controls, alarms and the required warm-up. Select the machine’s specified operating mode and engine conditions; “full power” is not a universal instruction for every function. Check the fitted feed and grade-control systems and agree who controls each adjustment. Resolve faults before production.

Learning step 4

Lower Screed on to blocks

Set the screed on the specified starting support/block arrangement and establish initial thickness, crown and extension alignment. Loose thickness must allow for the actual mixture’s compaction behaviour and project trial results. A fixed 20% allowance is not universal. Keep hands and feet out of the lowering zone; move or retrieve blocks only through the approved safe sequence.

Learning step 5

Place Haul Truck Loads in Hopper

Coordinate the haul truck through the designated signal system. Confirm alignment, clear the pinch zone and control the transfer without striking or disturbing the paver. Hopper-wing operation and load level follow the machine procedure and paving plan. Avoid uncontrolled emptying or repeated material surges. Watch overhead hazards when a truck body rises.

Learning step 6

Place the asphalt product

Maintain appropriate paving speed and a consistent head of material at the screed. Conveyors carry mix rearward; augers distribute it across the width. The floating screed responds to several interacting factors, so abrupt changes can affect thickness and texture. Plan longitudinal/transverse joints, overlap and tie-ins using the accepted procedure. Record emerging defects and communicate before repeated adjustments.

Learning step 7

Compact asphalt

Rolling follows the approved pattern while the mixture is within its effective compaction conditions. Breakdown establishes early compaction; intermediate rolling develops density as required; finish rolling removes marks and completes the surface. Temperature, mixture, support, roller type and weather matter. The paver and roller crews coordinate supply and pace; speed alone does not establish production quality.

Worked example · Convert a trial thickness relationship

A fictional trial produced 50 mm compacted thickness from 62 mm loose thickness. The same approved trial relationship is used to estimate a 45 mm compacted target.

Think it through.

Loose/compacted factor = 62/50 = 1.24. Estimated loose thickness = 45 × 1.24 = 55.8 mm. This is an exercise estimate tied to the stated trial, not a universal setting; confirm with actual placement and compaction checks.

Study chapter · Outline I2

Paving machine equipment and attachments

A paver consists of a tractor that receives and moves material and a screed that forms the mat. Equipment choice depends on the work, access, production and required result—not only the nominal paving width.

Types of paving machines
Major components
Attachments

Learning step 1

Types of paving machines

Pavers range from compact pathway or shoulder machines to highway-class equipment. Common nominal categories include 8 ft and 10 ft machines, but actual minimum/maximum widths, extensions and transport dimensions depend on the model. Compare work width, production, turning space, support, transport and fitted options. Light, medium and highway duty describe broad applications rather than interchangeable operating limits. Match the OMM to model, serial range and configuration.

Learning step 2

Major components

Identify the tractor’s hopper and wings, push rollers/truck interface, slat conveyors or other fitted feed system, augers, engine, hydraulics, undercarriage, operator station and grade/slope controls. Wheeled, rubber-tracked and steel-tracked arrangements differ. Identify the screed’s tow arms/tow points, main plate, extensions, end gates, heating and vibration/tamper equipment where fitted. Conveyors move material toward the screed; augers spread it; the screed forms the layer. Do not confuse a feed sensor with a grade sensor.

Learning step 3

Attachments

A pickup machine can collect a windrow placed by an appropriate delivery system and feed the paver. A material-transfer vehicle or hopper insert may buffer supply or remix material, depending on design. These can reduce certain handling problems but do not guarantee freedom from segregation or a universal productivity increase. Belly-dump and end-dump arrangements require different work plans. Check compatibility, added weight, clearance and operating instructions for every attachment.

Worked example · Two sensor jobs

A diagram labels one sensor near the auger and another following a reference beside the machine.

Think it through.

The feed sensor can control the material supply in its assigned zone; a grade sensor follows a reference for elevation control. Confirm the actual model and function from the manual. Similar-looking sensors do not necessarily control the same system.

Paver work safety procedures

Safe paver work depends on a shared plan and controlled movement around hot material and moving machinery. The ground crew, truck driver and screed crew need the same understanding of the work boundary.

Interpret job plan
Awareness of machine-specific safety
Traffic control requirements
Use proper PPE
Lockout procedures
Secure machine for safety

Learning step 1

Interpret job plan

Brief the path, paving pattern, thickness, crown, joints, obstacles, utilities and public access. Identify the approved drawing and changes. Establish who directs truck movement, who controls the screed and how an emergency stop is communicated. Protect entrances and services according to the actual access plan.

Learning step 2

Awareness of machine-specific safety

Recognize hopper-wing and truck-interface pinch points, conveyor/auger entanglement, hot mix and heating systems, fumes, noise, hydraulic pressure, blind areas and moving rollers. Never enter a hopper or reach into a feed system because motion has paused. Keep clear of the machine’s swept path and raised components. Use specified blocking/support before work requiring access.

Learning step 3

Traffic control requirements

Use the traffic-control plan for signs, devices, trained personnel and communication. Public traffic control and guiding a delivery truck are distinct roles. Reassess as the paver advances and access points change. Stop affected movements when the route or signals become unclear; do not assume high-visibility clothing makes a worker visible to every operator.

Learning step 4

Use proper PPE

Use the required footwear, head, eye, hearing, visibility and heat/chemical protection selected for the task. Check condition and fit. Respiratory protection needs the applicable program and controls. The employer ensures required PPE is used and maintained; worker/employer supply responsibilities follow current Part 8 and any agreements. PPE does not remove a crush or entanglement hazard.

Learning step 5

Lockout procedures

Apply the machine-specific lockout procedure for service, clearing or hazardous access. Address engine/electrical, hydraulic, gravity, heat and stored energy. Secure and verify the safe state before work. A night switch, stopped engine or emergency-stop button alone is not proof of complete isolation. Reinstatement follows the authorized procedure with people clear and guards restored.

Learning step 6

Secure machine for safety

Park at the designated stable location, position and support the screed/attachments as instructed, apply parking controls and required chocks, and secure access. Record defects and communicate out-of-service status. Protect people who may approach after the crew leaves.

Worked example · A stopped auger

Material stops moving, and a worker reaches toward the auger to remove a lump.

Think it through.

Stop the approach and keep clear. A pause in automatic feed does not isolate the auger; it may restart. Use the approved stopping, lockout, stored-energy and clearing procedure with authorized personnel.

Asphalt paver operator roles and responsibilities

The paver operator maintains alignment and a steady process while coordinating the truck and screed crews. Good operation includes noticing a developing problem early and communicating it clearly.

Position Paver
Ready the Paver
Follow the line
The transition process
Maintain consistent “head”
Positioning of haul trucks
Signals/communication
Obstacles and overhead hazards

Learning step 1

Position Paver

Position the paver to the approved starting line and joint with sufficient working room. Check the first pass carefully because it may guide later passes. Avoid unnecessary manoeuvres and abrupt corrections. Confirm the ground support and the whole machine’s clearance, including extensions and attachments.

Learning step 2

Ready the Paver

Complete prechecks, appropriate warm-up and screed preparation. Heating prevents material sticking to cold plates; overheating can damage the screed. Follow the fitted heating system’s procedure and verify ready status. Set starting supports and thickness through the agreed screed setup; never use an assumed generic block size.

Learning step 3

Follow the line

Follow the authorized reference, which may be stringline, a suitable edge, laser or other grade/alignment system. Look ahead and make smooth steering changes. Check that the reference remains valid and protected. Automatic controls can follow an incorrect or disturbed reference faithfully, so independent checks remain necessary.

Learning step 4

The transition process

Coordinate truck changes before the hopper starves or receives a sudden surge. The delivery rate, available buffer, paver speed and planned stopping method must work together. Communicate delays early. Follow the machine’s hopper-wing and feed procedure rather than repeatedly cycling wings as a universal habit.

Learning step 5

Maintain consistent “head”

A consistent head of material in front of the screed supports stable operation. It is the controlled quantity/distribution at the screed, not simply a completely full hopper. Monitor feed sensors, conveyors and augers as fitted. Changes in head, speed and mixture conditions can alter the mat. Coordinate corrective action with the screed operator.

Learning step 6

Positioning of haul trucks

Guide haul trucks into the approved alignment using the designated signalling arrangement. Keep people clear of the truck/paver interface and verify clearance before unloading. Avoid impacts that disturb the paver. Raised bodies change overhead clearance and stability considerations; the driver follows the vehicle procedure and site plan.

Learning step 7

Signals/communication

Use agreed, deliberate signals that the intended driver can see through the appropriate mirror or communication system. Obtain acknowledgement. A horn’s meaning must be agreed, not guessed. One designated person directs the movement; anyone can call an emergency stop. Lost or conflicting signals mean stop.

Learning step 8

Obstacles and overhead hazards

Scan the route and overhead envelope for utilities, structures, branches, attachments, other machines and people. Account for the truck body, hopper wings and screed extensions as they move. Electrical approach limits depend on the actual system and required controls; do not substitute a guessed distance. Escalate an unresolved obstacle before entering its zone.

Worked example · Hopper level is not the whole story

The hopper contains mix, but the right side of the screed has a visibly reduced material head and texture changes.

Think it through.

Report the side and location, coordinate with the screed operator and inspect the feed-system status from safe positions. A full hopper does not prove uniform delivery across the screed. Use authorized adjustments and record the response; do not reach into the feed system.

Paving machine maintenance

A clean, correctly serviced paver feeds material consistently and gives the crew safe access. Daily maintenance follows the selected machine’s schedule and safe conditions, with defects recorded for the next shift.

Service lubrication system (greasing)
Service air intake system as required (air filters/pre cleaners)
Drain fuel tank sump, water separator fuel filters if equipped
Add fluids as required
Inspect and clean components as required
Housekeeping tasks

Learning step 1

Service lubrication system (greasing)

Locate lubrication points on the actual chart, including fitted centralized systems. Confirm grease type, interval, quantity and safe position. Clean fittings and use a properly loaded grease gun. The LeeBoy 8680 example includes optional systems that combine several points; do not assume every machine has them. Record service and report damaged or blocked fittings instead of forcing lubricant through an unknown obstruction.

Learning step 2

Service air intake system as required (air filters/pre cleaners)

Read the intake restriction indicator, inspect the pre-cleaner and check housing/seal condition. Follow the manufacturer’s cleaning/replacement policy and keep dirt out of the clean side. Some elements must be replaced rather than cleaned. Do not use compressed air unless the actual procedure permits it and specifies the method and limits.

Learning step 3

Drain fuel tank sump, water separator fuel filters if equipped

Service the fuel sump or separator only where fitted and under its prescribed conditions. Collect drainage, note water/debris and dispose of it properly. Keep ignition sources controlled. Routine separator service does not authorize opening high-pressure fuel components. Repeated contamination requires investigation.

Learning step 4

Add fluids as required

Match fluid to the labelled system and specification. Check ground level, temperature and stopped/running condition before interpreting the dipstick or sight gauge. Use the correct side of any dual-scale dipstick. Clean fill points, avoid cross-contamination and overfilling, and record unusual consumption. Coolant, hydraulic fluid, engine oil and release agent are not interchangeable.

Learning step 5

Inspect and clean components as required

Inspect radiator/oil cooler, accessible plugs, dipsticks, battery condition and hoses using safe access and isolation. Protect cooling fins and electrical parts during cleaning. Never open a hot pressurized system or touch a suspected hydraulic leak. Restore guards and refer repairs to authorized personnel.

Learning step 6

Housekeeping tasks

Clean hopper, conveyors, augers, screed decks, extensions, steps and commonly missed ledges using the approved procedure. Coordinate with the screed crew, control heat and ignition sources, and prevent contact with moving systems. Use a compatible release/cleaning product with its SDS and containment requirements. “Biodegradable” does not mean harmless or permitted to drain onto the ground. Collect waste, account for tools and document the final condition.

Worked example · A repeated low level

Hydraulic oil is low again after yesterday’s top-up. A fresh wet area is visible beneath the machine.

Think it through.

Record the repeated loss and safe visual observation, secure/hold the machine as required and notify the responsible person. Do not treat repeated top-up as a repair or touch the suspected leak. Confirm authorized correction before release.

Markers, grades and stakes

A paver operator uses survey information to maintain the approved line and geometry. The marks must be interpreted before they become machine instructions.

Abbreviations used on stakes

Learning step 1

Abbreviations used on stakes

Read common abbreviations with the project legend: BM benchmark; C cut; CB catch basin; SMH sanitary manhole; EL elevation; FG finished grade; PL property line; RW right of way; SAN/S sanitary; Mon monument; G gas; PVC polyvinyl chloride. A label identifies a feature or value, not necessarily its complete location or safe clearance. Keep units and datum with every elevation.

Learning step 2

Interpret stakes

Identify station, side, offset, reference mark, cut/fill, slope and target work. Preserve property pins, benchmarks and protected monuments. Distinguish an assumed local datum from geodetic control. Cross-heads and offset stakes transfer information away from the work area; they are not automatically the paving edge. If a mark is moved or conflicts with the current drawing, stop using it and request verification.

Learning step 3

Fundamentals of paver operation

Follow a verified line with a stable sighting reference and smooth steering. Look sufficiently ahead while maintaining awareness of trucks, crew and obstacles. Guide trucks into the planned straight approach rather than forcing the paver out of alignment to receive them. Experience improves precision through feedback, not by ignoring measured checks. Coordinate alignment with the screed operator’s width and grade controls.

Think it through.

Use the project legend and stated offset direction to locate the actual edge; the stake is a reference away from it. Verify the relation with the grade lead before setting the paver line. Do not shift the whole mat to the stake.

Study chapter · Outline I7

Operate asphalt paver

Paver operation is learned through observed, supervised control of the actual machine. In-school practical work in the outline is on flat surfaces; workplace performance adds the approved grades, slopes and conditions under competent supervision.

Standard moves
Maneuvering the paver
Position paver
Simulate paving laying down a mat using 5mm crush and sand mixture or similar compact quality material or asphalt

Learning step 1

Standard moves

Complete pre-start checks and daily maintenance, identify controls and warnings, and enter using the designated access and three-point contact. Follow the OMM start and travel sequence. Practise forward–stop–reverse–stop on the cleared flat training area, checking surroundings before each movement. Apply the specified parking and transmission controls. Transmission neutral or a control lock is not a substitute for maintenance lockout.

Learning step 2

Maneuvering the paver

Manoeuvre smoothly within the machine’s operating limits and the instructor’s plan. Maintain awareness of space, position, clearance and ground support. In-school learners stay on the approved flat area. Hills, slopes and varied conditions require a separate workplace plan, machine limits and supervised assessment; do not infer a safe slope from a generic diagram. Stop when visibility or support is uncertain.

Learning step 3

Position paver

Position the paver for different starting scenarios, confirming line, width, joint, truck approach and screed readiness. Coordinate hopper opening/closing, material-flow settings and truck guidance through the actual controls and agreed signals. Observe the whole movement envelope. The instructor sets time and quantity appropriate to conditions; speed is not rewarded at the expense of control.

Learning step 4

Simulate paving laying down a mat using 5mm crush and sand mixture or similar compact quality material or asphalt

For a simulated mat, use only an instructor- and manufacturer-approved training material and method; a sand/crushed-material exercise does not reproduce every hot-mix behaviour. For asphalt, follow the approved mixture, temperature, placement and rolling plan. Maintain the line and steady process, communicate with screed and ground crew, monitor performance, and clean/shut down using the safe procedure. Record classroom and workplace observations separately.

Study chapter · Outline I8

Follow shut down procedures

A controlled shutdown preserves the paver’s condition and leaves a clear handover. The process includes parking, heat and energy controls, inspection and security—not simply switching off the engine.

Shut-down procedures

Learning step 1

Shut-down procedures

Select the designated stable parking location with service access and clear public/emergency routes. Consider the possibility that the machine will not restart. Use level ground where required for the procedure and fluid checks. Coordinate completion of material handling and screed work before the final stop.

Follow the matching OMM sequence for stopping working functions, positioning the screed and attachments, parking controls, required cooling and engine shutdown. Do not transfer a cooldown time or control sequence from a different model. Use specified supports, locks and chocks. A normal shutdown does not by itself authorize maintenance access: apply the full lockout procedure when the next task requires it.

Inspect from safe access areas for leaks, damage, buildup and missing guards. Keep away from hot or pressurized systems and moving/unsupported parts. Record machine ID, meter, parking location, defects and required service. Secure keys, panels and detachable items, then communicate the final status to the responsible person and next shift.

The practical checklist uses the actual machine procedure. Safety-critical parking, support and energy-control items must all pass. The outline’s 80% checklist threshold cannot compensate for an unsafe final state. Repeat the required practice after feedback and record the new attempt separately.

Study chapter · Outline I9

Transport paver

Moving a paver between sites requires a verified transport configuration and a coordinated loading plan. The fitted screed and attachments can change width, mass and securement requirements.

Compliance with wide load regulations
Roles and responsibilities
Prepare machine for transport
Loading and unloading procedures

Learning step 1

Compliance with wide load regulations

Measure actual transport dimensions and confirm the load, route and applicable BC commercial transport requirements. Signs, lighting, permits, pilot cars and travel restrictions depend on the actual movement; they are not triggered by a single classroom rule. Removing extensions may change width but requires the approved handling and securement method. The carrier confirms the current requirements before departure.

Learning step 2

Roles and responsibilities

Assign a competent machine operator, carrier/driver and ground guide as required. The driver/carrier manages transport compliance and securement within their responsibility; the designated operator follows the machine loading procedure. Do not assume the paver operator is always included or excluded. Everyone must know the stop signal, communication method and final release responsibility.

Learning step 3

Prepare machine for transport

Follow the OMM transport configuration for screed, extensions, augers, cut-offs, guards and loose equipment. Use approved lifting/rigging for removed components and designated tie-down points. The LeeBoy 8680 example gives specific preparation and loading instructions in printed pages 4-64–4-65; those instructions apply to the stated model/serial scope, not every paver. Read the complete procedure before a practical demonstration.

Learning step 4

Loading and unloading procedures

Check carrier/ramp capacity, alignment, support, traction and the clear movement envelope. Use the specified loading direction and controlled speed, with a guide positioned safely. Stop if alignment or communication is lost. After loading, position/support the machine, apply required parking controls and secure using rated components and the applicable National Safety Code Standard 10/BC requirements. Control the machine before releasing restraints for unloading. A time target never justifies rushing a critical step.
