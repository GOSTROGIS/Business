Study chapter · Outline F1

Drainage, grade and base

A pavement is a layered structure that must carry loads and manage water. A smooth new surface will not correct an unstable foundation or a blocked drainage route. Reading the whole road helps the crew protect access to homes, services and community spaces.

Proper road drainage
Sub grade
Sub-base
Base

Learning step 1

Proper road drainage

Surface water follows crossfall, longitudinal grade and low points toward an approved collection route. Subsurface water needs its own drainage path. Inspect ditches, catch basins, culverts and outlets against the drawings; a visible inlet is not proof of a functioning outlet. Sediment, fuel and uncured materials must be kept out of drainage systems. Do not redirect water toward neighbouring land or a watercourse because that route looks convenient. Report ponding, erosion and blocked drainage before covering the evidence. Approved environmental controls and local access commitments belong in the work plan.

Learning step 2

Sub grade

Subgrade is the prepared soil foundation beneath the pavement structure. Soil type, moisture, density, frost susceptibility and soft areas influence performance. Rutting under equipment, pumping water and inconsistent support require evaluation; adding asphalt is not a substitute for correcting the cause. Geotextiles can separate materials or provide filtration, depending on the specified product and design. Geogrids have different functions. Check product identity, overlap, orientation and protection requirements before placement. Damaged or wrongly installed fabric cannot be assumed effective because it is hidden.

Learning step 3

Sub-base

Sub-base distributes loading and can provide a working platform or drainage function. Its specified grading, thickness, moisture and compaction requirements govern placement. Approved recycled material may be suitable, but availability alone does not establish compliance. Place in controlled lifts, protect against contamination and check each accepted layer before burying it. Segregation, excessive fines and trapped water can change how the layer behaves. Record the material source and test results so a later concern can be traced to an actual location and lift.

Learning step 4

Base

Base is the prepared layer directly supporting the asphalt in a typical flexible pavement. Crushed aggregate from a quarry or pit, approved dredged or river-derived material, and recycled products each require the project’s source authorization and material acceptance. Their names do not establish equal performance or permission to extract them. Check surface elevation, crossfall, density, cleanliness and drainage. A thin low spot in the base changes asphalt demand and may create a local thickness problem. Maintain the accepted base until paving starts; traffic or rain can alter it after inspection.

Worked example · Follow the water

A 120 m access road has a sound-looking base. A blocked catch basin sits at its low point, and water is pumping through one wheel path. The paving crew is ready.

Think it through.

Hold the affected work and identify the drainage and support problems. Protect public access and the inlet, document the location, and request the responsible person’s correction and acceptance. Do not treat extra asphalt as a drainage repair. Record the repaired outlet and accepted foundation before release.

Verify slopes, crowns and grades

Grade work turns a drawing into reliable elevations on the ground. Keep the benchmark, units, drawing revision and sign convention visible in every calculation. Another person should be able to repeat your result without guessing what a number means.

Confirm plans/blueprints
Define role of Surveyor
Define role of Grade Person
Typical calculations

Learning step 1

Confirm plans/blueprints

Read the title block, north arrow, legend, scale, revision and detail references before measuring. A plan is the overhead view; a profile follows an alignment; a cross-section cuts across it. Profiles often exaggerate the vertical scale, so the drawn angle is not the road’s actual slope. Use stated dimensions before scaling, especially on resized screens or prints. One inch is exactly 25.4 mm and one foot exactly 0.3048 m. Horizontal run is used for ordinary grade calculations; a tape stretched along a slope measures a longer distance.

Learning step 2

Define role of Surveyor

The surveyor establishes or verifies control and supplies elevations, alignment and construction information within their assigned role. Cut and fill at a profile station normally refer to the defined centreline, not automatically to the entire cross-section. Pipe invert is the inside-bottom elevation. Depth to invert and cover above the pipe crown are different quantities; calculating cover requires the appropriate pipe dimensions. Quantity work combines accepted geometry with a justified allowance for settlement or compaction. A material list includes identity, size, quantity, units and drawing reference, not just an unexplained total.

Learning step 3

Define role of Grade Person

The grade person transfers authorized control to usable offset stakes, marks and machine references. Protect the original control and record offsets so a stake displaced from the construction line is not mistaken for the line itself. Confirm benchmark identity, datum, instrument setup and backsight. If marks conflict or a stake moves, stop using it and obtain verification. Do not average conflicting elevations. Read back instructions with station, side, offset, target elevation and cut/fill direction.

Learning step 4

Typical calculations

For differential levelling, height of instrument (HI) = known elevation + backsight; point elevation = HI − foresight. For grade, rise/run × 100 gives percent when both use the same units. A 2% crossfall across a 3.5 m horizontal half-width gives 0.070 m or 70 mm drop. Use a spirit level, builder’s level, laser and automatic slope control only with their setup, checking and operating instructions. Check calibration and an independent reference. Instrument displays do not make an incorrect benchmark correct. Record tolerances from the project rather than inventing a universal pass band.

Worked example · A level-book entry

Benchmark EL 100.000 m; backsight 1.250 m; foresight to prepared base 1.670 m. Target base EL 99.650 m.

Think it through.

HI = 101.250 m. Existing base = 101.250 − 1.670 = 99.580 m. Target minus existing = +0.070 m: fill 70 mm at this point, subject to the specified material and compaction method. Check against the correct drawing revision and benchmark before directing work.

Study chapter · Outline F3

Final grade preparation

Final grade preparation is the last opportunity to correct the support and geometry before the mat covers them. Work from accepted levels, drainage and utility details. A neat edge and a clean base help the paving crew place the intended thickness.

Check and raise utilities
Fine grading
Boning crown/cross-fall
Limit of asphalt layout

Learning step 1

Check and raise utilities

Locate utility structures using the project information and required locating process. Confirm casting type, ownership, target elevation and approved adjustment detail. Raising a frame may require rings, approved bedding, structural support and curing time; it is not simply lifting the lid. Protect openings, coordinate access and traffic control, and prevent loose material entering the system. Only authorized workers make the adjustment. Recheck finished elevation and crossfall around the structure so the paving joint does not create a pond or abrupt bump.

Learning step 2

Fine grading

Fine grading removes high spots and fills low areas with the approved material and method. Check moisture and compaction as required, remove contamination and protect the surface from rutting. Do not conceal soft spots with a thin scatter of dry aggregate. Confirm that repairs are accepted and that the base is ready for the specified prime or tack treatment, where applicable. Record the inspection location and time because construction traffic and weather may change the surface after it was checked.

Learning step 3

Boning crown/cross-fall

Boning uses aligned sighting references to compare intermediate levels with a defined grade. All rods and reference heights must be consistent, and the method needs a verified starting and finishing reference. Check crown and crossfall with appropriate instruments and the drawing’s actual geometry. A crown is not always centred, and curves, transitions, intersections and accessibility features may have different requirements. Check machine slope calibration against an independent measure. Agreement between two displays using the same wrong reference is not independent verification.

Learning step 4

Limit of asphalt layout

Mark the finished mat limits from approved alignment, width, edge and tie-in details. Include tapers, entrances, curb offsets, construction joints and the paving sequence. Distinguish the paver’s nominal screed width from the specified finished width and any working allowance. Measure at identified stations and record changes. Discuss public access and emergency routes before changing the work boundary. If the layout conflicts with a utility or existing surface, request a resolved detail before placing material.

Worked example · Width before tonnage

A rectangular paving area is 60 m long. The approved width is 4.2 m, but the marked limits measure 4.0 m.

Think it through.

Approved area = 252 m²; marked area = 240 m². The shortfall is 12 m². Verify the drawing and correct the layout before ordering or placing to the wrong boundary. Extra mix later does not automatically restore the intended edge support and joint quality.

Gradation

Quality control connects the planned pavement with evidence from production and placement. The crew needs to know which document governs, which tests apply and who can release or reject work. This chapter uses the supplied BC Ministry of Transportation DB 2006 Section 502 for a dated contract-reading exercise.

MOT Specs
The audit process
Penalty/bonus
Limit of asphalt layout

Learning step 1

MOT Specs

A quality management plan identifies materials, accepted mix design, sampling and testing, production controls, placement checks, records and responsibilities. Quality control is the producer/contractor’s process control; quality assurance provides the owner’s confidence and verification through the specified system. Neither term means that a crew can self-approve a departure. In the 2006 example, a mix design becomes the job mix formula (JMF) through the required written acceptance process. Identify the actual contract edition, special provisions and amendments before using any limit on a real job.

Learning step 2

The audit process

An audit follows evidence: approved procedure, actual activity, dated record, result and corrective action. A missing record and a failed result are different findings, and both require a response. Trace a sample from station and lot through laboratory report to disposition. Check instrument identification and calibration records. Record what was observed without inventing motives. Close a finding only when the responsible authority accepts the evidence of correction. A tidy binder with untraceable sample numbers does not demonstrate control.

Learning step 3

Penalty/bonus

Payment adjustments are contract calculations tied to defined lots, tests and acceptance rules. A bonus for one property does not cancel rejection under another. The 2006 tables separately address binder content, gradation, application rate, segregation, density and smoothness. Always state parameter, units, applicable lift, table and edition. Crowns and crossfalls remain geometric requirements even where a payment table does not express them. Learners calculate an isolated adjustment first, then check all other acceptance conditions before discussing overall payment.

Learning step 4

Limit of asphalt layout

Define the paving limits, lift, station range, lane and measured area before assigning tickets or samples to a lot. Mark intersections, letdowns and other geometry according to the contract’s measurement method. A layout change can affect quantity, sample locations and the meaning of a payment calculation. For the 2006 gradation exercise, Table 502-G compares departures from the JMF on specified sieves; it is not a width table. Keep layout verification and the appropriate quality-table lookup linked through the same lot identifier.

Worked example · One good result is not full acceptance

A fictional 600 t lot qualifies for a +$0.75/t gradation adjustment under the complete applicable table conditions. A separate density result is under review.

Think it through.

The isolated gradation adjustment is 600 × $0.75 = +$450. Record it as provisional for this parameter. Do not declare the lot accepted or payable until all applicable requirements and the authorized disposition are resolved.

Study chapter · Outline G2

Oil content

In asphalt quality work, “oil content” commonly refers to asphalt binder content. It does not mean engine oil. Binder content affects the mixture, but its reported percentage is meaningless unless the mass basis and test method are known.

Define Section 502

Learning step 1

Define Section 502

Section 502.12 of the supplied 2006 specification evaluates actual asphalt content against the approved content. Table 502-F expresses deviation in kilograms of binder per 100 kg of dry aggregate. This is a different basis from binder as a percentage of total mixture. For example, 5 kg binder with 100 kg dry aggregate is 5.00% on aggregate mass and 5/105 × 100 = 4.76% on total mixture mass. Never subtract results reported on different bases.

The historical procedure uses the sample mean from the specified sub-lot samples. Preserve sample identity, test method, lot and lift. Compute actual minus approved content, then use the magnitude of the deviation where the table applies to either above or below. Follow the contract’s rounding and reporting rules rather than rounding early to reach a favourable band.

In Table 502-F, a deviation of 0–0.15 earns +$0.50/t, 0.16–0.30 has no adjustment, and 0.31–0.35 gives −$1/t. Larger deviations have larger deductions and different top/lower-lift rejection boundaries. The full table and associated clauses govern; these are historical exercise values, not current project permission. A top-lift deviation greater than 0.50 triggers the stated rejection/remedial provisions. An operator does not fix a field result by independently changing the approved JMF.

Possible causes of an unexpected result include actual production variation, sampling problems, incorrect identities, test issues or a mistaken reporting basis. Preserve the original evidence and refer the issue to the quality lead. A single favourable retest is not automatic authority to discard an unfavourable result.

Worked example · Read the basis before the band

Approved binder content is 5.20 kg/100 kg dry aggregate. The three fictional sub-lot results are 5.45, 5.50 and 5.49 on that same basis. Lot mass is 500 t.

Think it through.

Mean = (5.45 + 5.50 + 5.49)/3 = 5.48. Absolute deviation = 0.28. Under the dated Table 502-F exercise, 0.16–0.30 gives $0/t, so this isolated adjustment is $0. This says nothing about other acceptance parameters.

Study chapter · Outline G3

Application

Application rate describes how much asphalt mixture was placed over a measured area. It is a mass-per-area quantity. It can help identify a quantity or thickness concern, but does not replace density, level or compacted-thickness checks.

Define Section 502

Learning step 1

Define Section 502

Section 502.14 of the 2006 specification determines application rate from the delivered tonnage assigned to the lot and its covered area, with the specified treatment of entrance letdowns and intersections. Reconcile weigh tickets, returned material, location and lift according to the contract. Counting an entire truck twice or including a ticket from another lane changes the result even when the arithmetic is correct.

Use consistent units: 1 tonne = 1,000 kg. Actual application rate in kg/m² = accepted assigned mass in kg ÷ measured area in m². The ratio used by Table 502-H is actual rate ÷ specified rate × 100. This ratio is a percentage of the specified rate, not a binder percentage and not percent density.

The dated table distinguishes bottom/single lifts from the top lift of multiple lifts. For the uncomplicated 96.0–103.9% band, both columns show +$0.50/t. Other bands include different deductions, restrictions on payment for excess material and rejection/remedial provisions. Read the full row and correct lift column. Do not treat over-application as a desirable way to earn payment: it can change grade, tie-ins and material demand.

If a calculated rate seems wrong, check area boundaries, units, tickets, lift identity and authorized changes first. Then compare with thickness and survey evidence. A rate alone cannot prove a uniform mat; a thick patch and a thin patch can average to the specified rate.

Worked example · Mass over area

A fictional lot receives 288 t over 2,400 m². Specified application is 120 kg/m².

Think it through.

288 t = 288,000 kg. Actual = 288,000/2,400 = 120 kg/m². Ratio = 120/120 × 100 = 100%. In the dated 96.0–103.9% band, isolated adjustment = 288 × $0.50 = +$144, subject to all applicable conditions.

Segregation

Segregation is separation within the mixture that produces an uneven distribution of aggregate sizes or other mixture characteristics. A visibly coarse patch can be a clue, but a crew needs agreed inspection criteria and a traceable location before deciding its cause or treatment.

Define Section 502

Learning step 1

Define Section 502

Section 502.15 of the 2006 specification calls for a homogeneous mixture and uniform surface texture. Its visual classification distinguishes slight, moderate and severe segregation using the amount of stone and remaining binder/fine-aggregate matrix. The project’s agreed comparison areas or photographs support consistent judgement. Temperature differences can also affect paving behaviour; thermal segregation and aggregate-size segregation are related quality concerns but are not interchangeable descriptions.

Track where a pattern occurs: at truck changes, the end of loads, edges, behind a particular part of the screed or at repeated stops. Material handling, stockpiles, truck loading/unloading, hopper management and the feed system all deserve investigation. Do not claim a single cause from a photograph alone. Record station, lane, lift, time, load and observed texture, then notify the responsible lead while the crew can still investigate the process.

The historical top-lift tables 502-I-1 and I-2 address remediation and payment. They include different remedies by severity and counts per lane kilometre. These dated repair descriptions are an exercise in reading a contract, not a field instruction to apply slurry or overlay without approval. Current project specifications and the authorized repair design govern actual work. A payment deduction does not automatically remove the requirement for remediation.

Prevention is a coordinated task. Maintain the specified material-handling and paving process, communicate supply changes early, and avoid scattering coarse material onto a finished surface. When a defect is suspected, preserve evidence and obtain direction before an improvised repair hides the cause.

Worked example · Describe before diagnosing

A coarse-looking strip appears near the right edge after three truck changes. The surrounding surface has more fine matrix.

Think it through.

Record the repeated location and timing, photograph from a safe position with a scale and location label, and notify the paving/quality lead. Compare with agreed criteria and inspect the process under authorized conditions. Do not declare severe segregation or choose a repair from appearance alone.

Study chapter · Outline G5

Density

Percent density compares measured in-place density with a defined reference density. The reference is part of the result. Saying only “the density is 94%” leaves out information needed to interpret it.

Define Section 502

Learning step 1

Define Section 502

Section 502.11 of the supplied 2006 document uses different references for Marshall and Superpave mixtures. The Marshall expression divides in-place density by the density of the plant briquette. The Superpave expression divides in-place density by maximum theoretical density determined by the specified procedure. Use compatible units, the applicable method and representative results from the correct lot.

A core and a nuclear gauge do not produce evidence through the same procedure. Follow the approved sampling, testing, calibration/correlation and reporting requirements. Nuclear gauge operation requires the applicable CNSC licensing, training and controls; reading this chapter does not authorize it. Record location, lift, sample/gauge identification, method, density units and reference result. Avoid substituting a different day’s reference because it produces a more favourable percentage.

The dated tables E-1 and E-2 use different acceptance/payment bands. For example, a Marshall lot average of 97.6–98.0% has a +$0.50/t adjustment, while the Superpave +$0.50/t band is 93.0–93.9%. These bands cannot be exchanged. Sub-lot limits, rejection provisions and the other acceptance requirements also apply. A satisfactory lot mean can conceal an unacceptable individual area.

Compaction effort, temperature, mixture behaviour, support and rolling pattern affect the outcome. More passes are not automatically better: they may be ineffective after cooling or damage an unstable mat. Use the approved pattern and quality feedback to guide authorized changes.

Worked example · Two legitimate references, two different percentages

In-place density is 2.340 Mg/m³. A fictional plant briquette result is 2.400 Mg/m³; maximum theoretical density is 2.500 Mg/m³.

Think it through.

Marshall-style comparison: 2.340/2.400 × 100 = 97.50%. Maximum-theoretical comparison: 2.340/2.500 × 100 = 93.60%. Both calculations are arithmetic examples; use only the reference required for the actual mixture and contract.

Study chapter · Outline G6

Smoothness

Smoothness concerns the longitudinal profile experienced by a moving vehicle. It differs from crossfall, compacted thickness and density. A mat can meet one of those requirements while failing another.

Define Section 502

Learning step 1

Define Section 502

Section 502.16 of the dated 2006 specification measures longitudinal profile with a specified precision rolling instrument and computes the International Roughness Index (IRI). IRI is commonly expressed in m/km; lower values indicate a smoother profile under the model. It is not a direct measurement of crossfall or a single bump’s height. Straightedge checks and localized-defect requirements answer different questions and may apply in addition to IRI.

Identify the lane, direction, station limits, lift and applicable lot/sub-lot rules. The historical clauses treat auxiliary lanes and short lengths specifically, and use the applicable Table 502-J or J-2 when adopted. Acceptance limits and payment schedules depend on the road classification and contract. Do not import an IRI threshold from another job. Preserve the instrument record and the quality lead’s interpretation.

Smoothness begins before the profiler arrives: prepared base, correct setup, steady paving speed and material head, coordinated truck changes, careful joints and suitable rolling practice all matter. Abrupt control changes, stops and poorly matched joints can leave profile defects. A roller cannot reliably erase every paver setup error. Report an emerging pattern early enough for the lead to investigate the process.

The 2006 auxiliary-lane example prorates a qualifying adjustment by segment length relative to a 1 km lot. This is a contract rule, not a general conversion from IRI to dollars. Repairs and remeasurement require authorized procedures. Keep the original and subsequent profiles linked so the correction can be evaluated transparently.

Worked example · Prorating an authorized historical adjustment

For a fictional exercise, the applicable table and acceptance checks establish a +$800 adjustment for a full 1 km lot. The qualifying auxiliary segment is 565 m.

Think it through.

Length factor = 565/1,000 = 0.565. Prorated adjustment = 0.565 × $800 = +$452. The $800 is an explicitly supplied exercise value, not a quoted Table 502-J value or a current project entitlement.
