Use this volume

This volume provides complete classroom readings, worked examples, named practice tasks and answer guidance for materials, plants and quality evidence. Follow the learning route in each chapter, study the labelled figures and explain your reasoning. The separate answer book contains the worked keys and observation records.

Care for people, place and the next person using the work runs through the cases. Local First Nations, Métis and Inuit learners and partners bring distinct knowledge and priorities. Participation does not require sharing personal stories or cultural teachings, and no diagram claims to represent a Nation’s protocol.

Classroom authoring is complete for the listed competencies. Qualified technical review, actual fleet/site matching, a local teaching pilot and observed practical qualification are separate requirements. The practical records are ready for an instructor to configure and use; they do not certify unobserved work.

Study chapter · Outline C1

Crushing process and paving aggregate production

Aggregate production changes a natural or reclaimed feed into identified products. Each stage controls a different feature: crushing changes particle size and shape; screening separates sizes; washing removes specified unwanted fines; handling preserves the product. A plant diagram is useful because a defect observed at the stockpile may have begun several stages earlier.

The paving crew normally receives a finished product, but understanding its route helps them ask precise questions about segregation, contamination and supply. The material record should preserve source, product, test identity and destination.

Learning step 1

Gravel pits, quarries

A gravel pit extracts deposits that can contain varying proportions of gravel, sand and fines. A quarry extracts rock that commonly needs breaking and crushing. Deposits vary within a site, so one attractive sample does not establish an entire source’s suitability. Source approval, extraction permission, environmental controls and safe access are separate requirements. Dredged or recovered material likewise needs its authorized source and product assessment.

Learning step 2

Crushing equipment

A jaw crusher reduces feed between crushing surfaces using compression. A cone crusher reduces material between a moving and a fixed crushing surface. An impact crusher uses impact to break material. Feed properties, desired product and the designed circuit influence selection and result. A learner identifies function and flow, not an operating gap or speed from a generic sketch. Wear and inappropriate feed can change shape, size distribution and equipment demand.

Learning step 3

The screen

A screen separates material through openings of stated sizes. Material that passes an opening follows one route; retained material follows another, which may return to crushing. A torn, blinded or incorrectly configured screen can change the product even when the crusher is operating normally. Read the screen/product relationship and the inspection/test evidence together. A screen opening description alone is not the full aggregate specification.

Learning step 4

Conveyors

A conveyor carries material between stages or to storage. Fixed, transfer and stockpiling arrangements have different purposes. Belt, idlers, pulleys, drive, guards and discharge point are parts to identify. Transfer height, direction and handling can affect segregation and dust. The carrying route must be distinguished from a safe pedestrian route; a stopped belt is not an authorized crossing or maintenance condition.

Learning step 5

Feeders

A feeder controls how material enters a process. Its job is more than moving the largest possible amount: a consistent, suitable feed helps the downstream circuit operate as intended. Irregular feed, bridging or incompatible material can disturb processing. Changes are handled through the plant’s procedure; workers do not enter a hopper or reach into a feeder to correct the flow.

Learning step 6

Explain stockpiling and handling

Stockpiles keep identified products separate before use. Place and recover material using the approved handling arrangement to limit intermingling, segregation and contamination from the ground or adjacent products. Large particles can roll farther during pile formation, creating a different gradation at different locations. Maintain signs and records so a loader does not supply the wrong pile. Moisture and recovered-material variability also matter to later plant control.

Learning step 7

Washing procedures

Washing separates unwanted fine material through a designed water-and-material process. It can improve cleanliness for the intended product, but also creates wash water and collected fines that need permitted management. Washing does not automatically make a product suitable for every use. Check the specified test results and the site’s water/settling or recycling arrangement; uncontrolled discharge is not part of a legitimate material-improvement process.

Learning step 8

Types of aggregate transportation

Trucks, rail and barges can move aggregate between source, storage and plant. The selected transport method affects logistics and handling points, not the required product identity. Loads must remain traceable and protected against unacceptable contamination or segregation. Receiving checks compare the supplier, product, quantity and relevant test record with the order. A change of transport route or transloading point may introduce a new handling risk that belongs in the supply plan.

Worked example · Find the process change

Fictional circuit: a screen sends its oversize back to a cone crusher. A damaged screen allows larger particles into the finished-product pile. The delivery ticket still carries the correct product name.

Think it through.

The name on the ticket has not verified the physical product. Identify affected production and stockpile records, isolate the questioned product through the quality process, inspect the screen safely and obtain the specified testing. Do not blend the questioned pile into accepted material to hide the difference. The process diagram points to where the change occurred and which records may be affected.

Study chapter · Outline C2

Types and specifications of aggregates

An aggregate name is the beginning of a specification, not its complete meaning. The useful questions are: What particles are present? How do they interact? How do water and loading affect them? Which measured properties must the product meet? Gradation is the distribution of particle sizes; the properties of the blend are different from the properties of a single stone.

Aggregate processing
Characteristics
Common products

Learning step 1

Aggregate processing

Crushing, screening and washing perform different jobs. Crushing can alter size and particle shape; screening separates sizes; washing removes specified fine material. A product may pass through more than one stage. The process name does not prove the final result: test the identified product using the required method and compare it with the applicable specification. A change in source, wear or handling can affect results even when the nominal process remains the same.

Learning step 2

Characteristics

Permeability describes the ability of a material arrangement to transmit water. Load-bearing response concerns how the structure supports loading. Resistance to shearing depends on particle interaction and other conditions. Particle shape, texture, grading, fines and moisture can influence these behaviours. An open-looking product is not automatically a designed drainage layer; the complete gradation, contamination and drainage arrangement matter.

Liquid limit and plastic limit characterize consistency of the applicable fine-grained material under specified tests. The plasticity index is liquid limit minus plastic limit. These are not moisture limits for every whole aggregate or a visual test of a pile. Gradation is normally recorded as cumulative percent passing identified sieves. Keep sieve size, units, test method and product/sample identity with the result.

Learning step 3

Common products

Pit run describes material taken from a deposit with limited processing; its properties can vary. Screened road base, including a nominal '3-inch minus' description, needs the specified grading and quality limits. Bedding sand is selected for the relevant pipe or bedding design. Crushed road mulch is a regional/product term that must be resolved through its actual specification. Asphalt aggregates are selected and blended for the approved mixture.

Drain rock serves an intended drainage use with specified grading/cleanliness. Chips and driveway chips describe particular aggregate products whose intended application and grading must be established. Reclaimed asphalt pavement (RAP) contains recovered aggregate and aged binder; it is not interchangeable with clean virgin stone. Concrete sand, including material specified under ASTM C33, has its own requirements. Stucco sand is selected for a different mortar/render application. A shared word 'sand' does not authorize substitution among them.

Worked example · Read a sieve result

Fictional dry sample mass is 1,000 g. The measured retained masses on three successive sieves are 150 g, 350 g and 300 g; the pan holds 200 g. Use these values only to practise the arithmetic.

Think it through.

The masses total 1,000 g. Cumulative retained percentages are 15%, 50% and 80%; cumulative passing values are 85%, 50% and 20%. The pan fraction is 20%. Whether the product conforms cannot be decided without sieve sizes, specified method, product identity and acceptance limits.

Study chapter · Outline C3

Asphalt plant operation

Both drum and batch plants turn specified inputs into a controlled mixture, but they organize the flow differently. Learn the path of aggregate, binder, recovered material where permitted, collected fines and finished mixture. A material-flow drawing is not a pipe-and-instrumentation diagram: it explains relationships without supplying the plant’s operating settings.

The operation of a drum plant
The operation of a batch plant

Learning step 1

The operation of a drum plant

In a continuous drum-mix arrangement, cold-feed bins supply identified aggregate fractions. Feeders and conveyors proportion and carry material through the designed drying/mixing system. Binder and other permitted inputs are introduced through controlled systems. Drum layouts differ, including where drying and mixing occur; do not infer one universal arrangement from the word 'drum'. Moisture in the aggregate affects drying demand and production control.

Finished mixture moves to the designed storage/surge system and loadout. A storage silo provides a controlled material-handling stage; it does not remove the need to preserve mix identity, temperature conditions and acceptable storage duration. The load record connects the product and time to the truck. Dust collection, burner performance and recovered-fines control interact with production and environmental requirements. A change in one feed must be managed through the approved mixture/plant process.

Learning step 2

The operation of a batch plant

In a conventional batch arrangement, cold feeds send aggregate to drying/heating. A hot stone elevator lifts heated aggregate to the screen deck, where fractions are separated into hot bins. Weigh bins measure the selected fractions for a batch. The pug mill mixes the measured materials with the specified binder and other permitted inputs. A slat conveyor or other designed transfer moves the product toward storage/loadout.

The batch identity helps relate proportioning to a production record. It does not guarantee that every batch meets the formula: incorrect bin contents, screen condition, scales, moisture assumptions or binder measurement can affect the result. Quality control checks the process and tested product. Operators follow the actual plant’s safe controls, interlocks and procedures; learners should be able to trace an input and explain what evidence would reveal a change.

Worked example · One product, two process records

Fictional batch card: 950 kg aggregate plus 50 kg binder, with no other mass included. Fictional continuous record: 19,000 kg aggregate plus 1,000 kg binder over an interval. Compare binder mass percentage on a total-mixture basis.

Think it through.

Batch total = 1,000 kg; binder = 5%. Continuous total = 20,000 kg; binder = 5%. The same arithmetic ratio does not establish matching gradation, moisture correction, binder grade, temperature or conformity. The records describe different production organizations; both require the approved formula and checks.

Asphalt products and uses

A road requirement has an issuer, edition, project adoption and decision authority. A contractor, designer, owner and regulator may each issue documents, but their documents answer different questions. Read the contract register before choosing a clause from a familiar book. The same surface material can appear on a highway, a municipal street and private property under different project requirements.

Learning step 1

The key elements of the MMCD

MMCD means Master Municipal Construction Documents. The published contract documents provide standardized municipal construction specifications, drawings and related contract material. A project may adopt an edition and add supplementary conditions or specifications. Obtain the actual adopted documents and changes; the title 'municipal work' does not establish every MMCD clause or edition. Record the governing project reference, not a guessed requirement from another job.

Learning step 2

The role of the MOT

The provincial transportation ministry’s role is established by the project and applicable law/contract. Distinguish contractor quality control from owner-side quality assurance, audit and acceptance activities as defined in the contract. A contractor’s passing internal check is not automatically the owner’s acceptance. Conversely, an audit sample does not remove the contractor’s duty to control its own process. Current ministry naming can change; identify the actual issuer and authorized representative.

Learning step 3

The role of the DOT

DOT is commonly used for a Department of Transportation in other jurisdictions. It does not identify one universal Canadian asphalt standard. The supplied 2006 B.C. Design Build Standard Specifications is a specific historical document, not a generic federal transport regulation. When an old note says 'DOT standard', identify its jurisdiction, full title, edition and contractual adoption before using it. This prevents a familiar acronym from becoming an unsupported requirement.

Learning step 4

Engineer Specs

An engineer’s project specifications translate the design into required materials, workmanship, inspection and acceptance criteria. Such specifications are used in public as well as private work. Drawings and specifications must be read together, using the project’s hierarchy and change process. A conflict is referred to the authorized design/contract role; the crew does not choose whichever version gives the easiest quantity.

Learning step 5

Role of Architect

An architect may coordinate the site and building interface, accessibility, appearance and other project requirements through the design team. The actual division of responsibility is project-specific. Architectural drawings can identify finishes or interfaces while civil drawings establish drainage and pavement details. A worker must know which record and responsible designer address the question, and retain an authorized response when the work changes.

Learning step 6

Other asphalt and paving materials

Paving blocks/stones, asphalt, concrete and permeable pavement systems have different structures, construction methods and maintenance needs. 'Green road' is a claim or design objective that needs defined performance and evidence. A substitution affects more than appearance: loading, drainage, access, joints, durability and approved quantities may change. Compare the required service and obtain the authorized design decision before substituting a product.

Worked example · Resolve the document conflict

Fictional register lists drawing P-04 revision C and specification SP-07 revision 2. A delivery note refers to an older project’s MMCD detail. The crew is asked to change the surface material at the entrance.

Think it through.

The delivery note does not establish the governing design. Identify the current project documents, describe the requested change and obtain the authorized design/contract response. Keep the reason and affected drawing/specification revisions together. Do not treat an acronym or a supplier recommendation as approval.

Study chapter · Outline D1

Environmentally friendly properties

An environmental claim is useful when it names the material, exposure conditions, service provided and evidence. Asphalt can be recovered and used again, but 'recyclable' is not the same as 'recycled here', and neither word proves the lowest overall impact. The learning goal is a defensible comparison that respects the people, land and water affected by a project.

Define Green properties of asphalt

Learning step 1

Define Green properties of asphalt

Reclaimed asphalt pavement (RAP) contains recovered aggregate and aged binder. Processing and an approved mix or other permitted use can recover value from those materials. The receiving process must account for variability, contamination, binder properties and the required final performance. A recycling pathway has collection, processing, transport and production effects of its own.

Avoid blanket claims that all installed asphalt is 'non-toxic' or 'does not leach' under every condition. Product composition, contamination, exposure, receiving environment and applicable criteria determine what evidence is needed. An intact pavement, hot production process, milling dust and a stored pile are different exposure situations. A claim about one does not automatically apply to the others.

A fair comparison defines the same required service, area/loading, service period and maintenance assumptions. Include production, transport, construction, use/maintenance and end-of-life within the stated boundary. A lower production impact per tonne can be outweighed by different quantities, travel or service life. Environmental decisions can also involve water, noise, land, access and local priorities that a single emissions number does not capture.

In B.C., meaningful local engagement identifies the appropriate people and decision processes; a class cannot supply a Nation’s permission or knowledge. Use public/fictional evidence for the exercise and invite contributions only by choice. A careful conclusion can acknowledge a documented reuse pathway while asking for the data needed to compare actual options.

Worked example · Recycled proportion is not a full impact score

Fictional material record: a 240 t proposed mix contains 48 t of approved RAP. A sales note calls it “20% recycled, therefore 20% lower whole-life emissions.” No emissions or service-life data is supplied.

Think it through.

48/240 × 100 = 20% by mass in this record. That establishes the stated proportion only. Whole-life emissions need comparable quantities, processing/production, transport, construction, maintenance and end-of-life evidence. The proposed reduction is not demonstrated by the recycled percentage.

Use of storage areas

Storage keeps the right material identifiable and available while controlling release, incompatibility and exposure. A storage plan includes the container/tank, surrounding area, access, inspection, emergency response and destination for waste. A tidy-looking tank is not proof that its containment or product identity is adequate.

Type 1 storage
Type 2 storage
Use of recycled materials in hot mix asphalt
Use of recycled materials in asphalt plant burners

Learning step 1

Type 1 storage

For this lesson, Type 1 describes a primary storage container with a separate spill-containment arrangement, such as a tank within a designed containment area. It is a course grouping, not a universal legal classification. Fuels, primers, emulsions such as an identified SS-1 product, asphalt binder and waste streams require their actual SDS, compatibility, heating/handling and regulatory arrangements.

Identify the product and hazards before deciding which area is suitable. Natural gas service has its own engineered supply and safety requirements; it is not handled as an ordinary open liquid tank. Containment must remain effective, including when rainwater or other material occupies space. Drainage and disposal of collected liquid follow the approved procedure; opening a drain to clear capacity can release contamination.

Learning step 2

Type 2 storage

Type 2 here describes a self-contained storage arrangement, such as equipment with integral secondary containment, where its design provides that function. The word self-contained does not mean leak-proof, maintenance-free or automatically suitable for every fuel or liquid asphalt product. Verify the actual design, product compatibility, capacity, inspection and permitted location.

A biodiesel blend can have different handling/storage considerations from another fuel. A heated binder storage system introduces thermal and other hazards. Labels, isolation, overfill prevention, transfer connections and emergency access must match the actual installation. Read the tank/assembly instructions and product records together.

Learning step 3

Use of recycled materials in hot mix asphalt

Using RAP in hot mix can reduce demand for some new material and may offer cost benefits, but the outcome depends on processing, transport, consistency, binder contribution and required performance. Mobile recycling changes where processing occurs and may change traffic, emissions and material-control arrangements. Identify what is being recycled and into which approved product; do not treat every recovered pile as acceptable feed.

Keep recovered material segregated by the required source/type and protect it from unacceptable contamination. The quality record follows it through processing and blending. A low purchase price alone is not the cost of a compliant finished mixture.

Learning step 4

Use of recycled materials in asphalt plant burners

A plant burner must use only fuel permitted by its design and applicable environmental authorization. Recovered fuel or other recycled material is not automatically acceptable because it burns. Composition, contamination, handling, burner suitability, combustion control and emissions requirements matter. Heating temperature is established by the approved process and equipment, not by a generic classroom number.

Storage/transfer for a permitted recovered fuel needs the actual SDS and emergency arrangements. A change in fuel can affect burner performance and emissions and must be controlled through the authorized process. Do not conduct a classroom trial with an unidentified waste fuel.

Asphalt plant and placement emissions

Emissions are releases from a process into the environment; worker exposure is contact with a hazard at the workplace. They are related but are not measured or regulated in exactly the same way. A stack result does not establish a worker’s breathing-zone exposure, and a workplace mask does not establish compliance with an environmental permit.

Role of MOE in establishing regulations
Use of Bag house
Emission controls

Learning step 1

Discuss role of Metro Vancouver (formerly GVRD) in enforcement and regulation of emissions

Metro Vancouver has a regional air-quality regulatory program. Determine whether the plant or activity is within that jurisdiction and which permit, bylaw or authorization applies. The retained B.C. Asphalt Plant Regulation explicitly states that it does not apply in the Metro Vancouver Regional District. Do not carry an outside-region authorization into the region by assumption.

The practical record identifies the actual site, operator, authorization, operating conditions, monitoring/reporting requirements and responsible contacts. A historical count of member communities is not a compliance test. Consult the current regional program and actual permit when preparing the work.

Learning step 2

Role of MOE in establishing regulations

Outside Metro Vancouver, the B.C. Asphalt Plant Regulation establishes requirements for the covered hot-mix and hot-in-place recycling plants, with applicable registration, emission, operating and reporting provisions. Its text also addresses additional municipal/regional requirements. Identify the actual activity and location before selecting the rule. Provincial ministry names and responsibilities can change; the legal instrument and current authorized contact must be recorded.

An environmental authorization is different from the workplace exposure-control program. Both can be relevant to the same plant. Keep monitoring data and reports associated with their method, location, interval and required interpretation.

Learning step 3

Use of Bag house

A baghouse is a fabric-filter dust collection system. In the MS-4 concept, dust-laden gas passes through the filtering arrangement, particles collect and cleaning releases collected material to a hopper. The gas and collected-solids paths are different. Primary collection may remove larger particles before secondary collection. The actual plant arrangement determines how fines are returned or removed.

Filter condition, seals, airflow and the designed cleaning/collection system affect performance. A visible change, alarm or damaged component needs the operator’s prescribed response. Do not assume a baghouse removes every gaseous pollutant: its principal dust-control function does not establish control of all emissions.

Learning step 4

Emission controls

Burner operation, aggregate moisture, production rate, material temperature, handling and dust-control performance can influence emissions. Heating asphalt excessively or using an unsuitable fuel can create problems; actual limits and operating settings come from the approved plant process and authorization. Placement work also needs exposure controls suited to the product and conditions.

Record observations without diagnosing the entire process from colour or odour alone. An unusual visible plume can prompt the required response and investigation, but it is not a measured concentration. Keep the operating interval and material/fuel identity so the responsible people can relate the observation to production and monitoring records.

Determine asphalt mix design

A mix design establishes a suitable combination of aggregate, binder and other permitted ingredients for the required pavement performance. The accepted job mix formula (JMF) then provides the production target under the contract. It is more than a recipe by eye: the materials, proportions, test basis and performance requirements belong together.

Asphalt/aggregate composition
Types of mix designs
Mix temperature characteristics

Learning step 1

Asphalt/aggregate composition

The aggregate skeleton, binder and air voids form a mixture whose response depends on both materials and compaction. Marshall design uses its specified specimen preparation and stability/flow and volumetric evaluations. Superpave design uses its specified material selection, gyratory compaction and volumetric framework, with additional tests where required. Their results and specimen methods are not interchangeable labels.

A field crew does not redesign a mixture by adding binder when the surface looks dry. Document the observation, identify the supplied JMF and refer the condition through quality control. Changes in aggregate source, binder grade or recovered material can affect the design and may require the specified approval process.

Learning step 2

Types of mix designs

Dense-graded, open-graded, gap-graded and other designed mixtures distribute aggregate sizes differently and may serve different functions. A mixture chosen for drainage is not automatically suitable for every structural or traffic demand. The specification identifies the required mixture type, nominal size, binder and other design properties; the accepted formula identifies the actual approved combination.

Read the formula’s product/source identities, target proportions, binder grade/content, recovered material where permitted, test basis and version. A production ticket with the wrong JMF identifier is a traceability issue even if the mixture looks familiar. Keep design approval separate from daily production conformity.

Learning step 3

Mix temperature characteristics

Hot-mix asphalt is produced and placed under its required elevated-temperature process. Warm-mix technologies permit specified production/placement approaches at reduced temperatures relative to the corresponding conventional process. Cold-mix products use a different material/process arrangement and are selected for the intended application. These categories do not supply one universal upper/lower temperature.

The approved mix, binder, equipment, haul, ambient conditions and placement/compaction procedure determine the usable temperature requirements. Read actual values from the governing documents and measure by the specified method. A surface infrared reading and an internal probe reading answer different questions. Temperature is recorded with location, time, method and instrument identity.

Worked example · Keep the percentage basis

Fictional teaching JMF uses 5.2% binder by total mixture mass. A 25.0 t batch is considered for arithmetic only. No production temperature or field design is prescribed.

Think it through.

Binder model mass = 25.0 × .052 = 1.30 t; aggregate/other stated remainder = 23.70 t if no other components are separately specified. If the percentage were instead based on aggregate mass, this calculation would be different. Confirm the stated basis before comparing records.

A test result belongs to a sample, and a sample must represent an identified material and interval. Sampling plan, increment, composite sample, sample reduction, container, chain of custody and test method are connected terms. Aggregate sampling

Learning step 1

Aggregate sampling

Aggregate sampling must follow the specified location, timing, number of increments and method. A convenient scoop from the visible top of a pile may not represent the material being supplied. Weigh-bin or other plant sampling requires the designed safe access and procedure; exposure to hot material, gravity and connected equipment must be controlled.

Preserve product/source identity and the relevant production interval. Combine or reduce samples only as the method permits. Record unusual conditions and contamination. A sample is not made representative by increasing its label detail after an unsuitable collection.

Learning step 2

Belt sampling

Belt sampling can characterize a material stream when performed under the approved method and safe arrangement. A worker must not reach across or into a live moving belt. The required isolation/access and sampling device/procedure determine how the sample is obtained. Include the full required stream/fraction rather than selecting the pieces that look most typical.

A drum-plant process record connects the sampled feed with its bin, proportioning and time. A stopped-belt method and an approved automatic sampling device have different controls; neither is replaced by a generic drawing. Keep the sample method and the safe-work method together.

Learning step 3

Lab tests

Laboratory tests can determine gradation, binder content and mixture volumetric or other specified properties. The result must identify the method, units, sample and applicable comparison. Method differences and corrections can affect interpretation. A result outside a limit prompts the defined quality process; it does not justify editing the result or replacing it with a more convenient sample.

Distinguish a JMF target, production variability and the actual contractual acceptance criteria. Quality-control results help manage the process, while acceptance/audit results serve their specified role. Retain original results, corrections and disposition records.

Learning step 4

The process of taking mix temperature readings

Select the temperature instrument and method for the required measurement. An infrared device senses a surface within its field of view; spot size, viewing conditions and surface properties affect the reading. A probe measures at its specified contact/insertion location. Neither automatically represents the entire load.

Verify instrument identity, condition and required check/calibration status. Record the time, location, product/load, units and method. If readings disagree, investigate the different measurement conditions before averaging them. A Celsius/Fahrenheit conversion changes units, not where the temperature was measured.

Study chapter · Outline E4

Determine compaction levels

Density relates mass to volume. Compaction changes the arrangement of particles and air voids, but the measured density must be compared with the correct reference and method. Two instruments can report numbers that look similar while representing different locations or measurement principles. Keep the instrument, sample, units and denominator visible.

Two methods to determine compaction

Learning step 1

Two methods to determine compaction

A nuclear density gauge uses a radioactive source and detector arrangement to infer a material property through its calibrated method. The actual instrument, mode, calibration/standardization, surface preparation and test method affect the result. In Canada, possession/use and transport require the applicable CNSC licensing, training, security and radiation-safety arrangements. Reading the Troxler 3430 manual is useful instrument study; it does not authorize source handling or operation.

A core is a physical specimen removed at an identified location. The approved coring and laboratory method establish sampling, handling, dimensions and density determination. Coring also requires utility/location checks, traffic/access controls and safe equipment/water management, followed by the specified repair of the hole. A damaged, contaminated or mislabelled core may not support the intended result.

Gauge and core results are compared only through the specified procedure and any required correlation. A percentage density must state its reference: maximum theoretical mixture density, laboratory compacted reference or another defined basis. Do not compare percentages that use different denominators. The result belongs to the sampled location and acceptance unit defined by the contract; one point does not automatically represent an entire project.

Report original readings, checks, location and any limitation. A low result prompts the approved investigation/disposition process. Additional rolling is not automatically appropriate after the material’s workable window or when the cause is unknown.

Worked example · The denominator changes the meaning

Fictional in-place density is 2.34 t/m³. Reference A, maximum theoretical density, is 2.50 t/m³. Reference B, a compacted laboratory reference in a different teaching system, is 2.40 t/m³.

Think it through.

Against A: 2.34/2.50 × 100 = 93.6%. Against B: 2.34/2.40 × 100 = 97.5%. Both calculations use the same numerator; they answer different defined questions. Neither establishes acceptance without the applicable method and criterion. Never relabel 97.5% as percent of maximum theoretical density.
