Asphalt plant operation

Both drum and batch plants turn specified inputs into a controlled mixture, but they organize the flow differently. Learn the path of aggregate, binder, recovered material where permitted, collected fines and finished mixture. A material-flow drawing is not a pipe-and-instrumentation diagram: it explains relationships without supplying the plant’s operating settings.

Learning step 1

The operation of a drum plant

In a continuous drum-mix arrangement, cold-feed bins supply identified aggregate fractions. Feeders and conveyors proportion and carry material through the designed drying/mixing system. Binder and other permitted inputs are introduced through controlled systems. Drum layouts differ, including where drying and mixing occur; do not infer one universal arrangement from the word 'drum'. Moisture in the aggregate affects drying demand and production control.

Finished mixture moves to the designed storage/surge system and loadout. A storage silo provides a controlled material-handling stage; it does not remove the need to preserve mix identity, temperature conditions and acceptable storage duration. The load record connects the product and time to the truck. Dust collection, burner performance and recovered-fines control interact with production and environmental requirements. A change in one feed must be managed through the approved mixture/plant process.

Learning step 2

The operation of a batch plant

In a conventional batch arrangement, cold feeds send aggregate to drying/heating. A hot stone elevator lifts heated aggregate to the screen deck, where fractions are separated into hot bins. Weigh bins measure the selected fractions for a batch. The pug mill mixes the measured materials with the specified binder and other permitted inputs. A slat conveyor or other designed transfer moves the product toward storage/loadout.

The batch identity helps relate proportioning to a production record. It does not guarantee that every batch meets the formula: incorrect bin contents, screen condition, scales, moisture assumptions or binder measurement can affect the result. Quality control checks the process and tested product. Operators follow the actual plant’s safe controls, interlocks and procedures; learners should be able to trace an input and explain what evidence would reveal a change.

Worked example · One product, two process records

Fictional batch card: 950 kg aggregate plus 50 kg binder, with no other mass included. Fictional continuous record: 19,000 kg aggregate plus 1,000 kg binder over an interval. Compare binder mass percentage on a total-mixture basis.

Think it through.

Batch total = 1,000 kg; binder = 5%. Continuous total = 20,000 kg; binder = 5%. The same arithmetic ratio does not establish matching gradation, moisture correction, binder grade, temperature or conformity. The records describe different production organizations; both require the approved formula and checks.
