Use this volume

This volume provides complete classroom readings, worked examples, named practice tasks and answer guidance for people, tools and safe work. Follow the learning route in each chapter, study the labelled figures and explain your reasoning. The separate answer book contains the worked keys and observation records.

Care for people, place and the next person using the work runs through the cases. Local First Nations, Métis and Inuit learners and partners bring distinct knowledge and priorities. Participation does not require sharing personal stories or cultural teachings, and no diagram claims to represent a Nation’s protocol.

Classroom authoring is complete for the listed competencies. Qualified technical review, actual fleet/site matching, a local teaching pilot and observed practical qualification are separate requirements. The practical records are ready for an instructor to configure and use; they do not certify unobserved work.

Keep the term with its meaning

Aggregate
Mineral particles forming the solid skeleton of the mixture or granular layer.
Binder
The asphalt material that coats and binds the aggregate in the specified mixture.
Compacted thickness
Layer thickness after the required compaction process; distinct from loose depth behind the screed.
Density reference
The specified comparison value used as the denominator of a percent-density calculation.
Gradation
Distribution of aggregate particle sizes, commonly expressed as cumulative percent passing specified sieves.
Head of material
The quantity/distribution of mix immediately in front of the screed.
Job mix formula (JMF)
The accepted mixture proportions and properties under the applicable quality/contract process.
Lockout
Isolation, securing and verification of hazardous energy for work requiring protection.
Lot / sub-lot
Defined portions of work used for sampling, testing and acceptance under the contract.
OMM
Operation and maintenance manual matched to the actual machine, serial range and configuration.
RAP
Reclaimed asphalt pavement: recovered material containing aggregate and aged binder.
SDS
Safety data sheet for the actual product; read with its label and workplace procedure.
Screed
The paver assembly that forms the mat; its response depends on setup, material and movement.
Station / offset
Location along an alignment / distance and direction from a defined reference.
TCP
Traffic control person with the required training and assigned role.
Traceability
A record connecting material, location, time, sample, result and authorized decision.
SVG
Scalable vector graphics: useful for clear diagrams whose labels and geometry must be checked.
DOCX / XLSX
Editable document / spreadsheet workbook; verify actual content and formulas after creation.
PDF / HTML
Page-based reading copy / browser document; inspect completeness, access and linked resources.
MP3 / MP4 / VTT
Audio file / video container / timed web captions; test playback and retain a transcript.

Working safely around haul trucks

Worked example · A missed acknowledgement

Fictional sequence: 08:10 the ground worker calls the truck identifier; 08:11 an unrelated driver replies; 08:12 the assigned driver begins backing. The assigned driver has not acknowledged the direction. A pedestrian also approaches the facility entrance.

The sequence contains no confirmed instruction between the assigned people. Stop the movement through the agreed procedure, protect the pedestrian route and re-establish the identified driver/signaller exchange. The three timestamps show order, not proof of a safe movement. The handover should record the wrong-driver reply and the access conflict; simply repeating the original call leaves the cause unresolved.

Explain and apply the idea

Annotate the truck/paver interaction diagram with blind-area, pinch-point, swept-path and overhead-hazard questions. Describe the location a ground worker must avoid and what must be confirmed before choosing a signalling position.

Answer guidance.

Identify the truck/paver gap, reversing/swept path and overhead envelope; choose a position only through the actual plan with visibility and escape. No drawing dimension establishes clearance.

Observe: Reading, Problem solving, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Calculate and check the result

Rewrite the three radio messages in the worked case so the intended driver and acknowledgement are unambiguous. Rehearse a lost-contact event with spoken classroom roles only.

Answer guidance.

Messages identify the vehicle/person, intended action/location and confirmation. Loss of contact invokes the agreed stop response; resume only after shared understanding and access control are restored.

Observe: Writing, Communication, Collaboration through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Create and save a one-page delivery brief for the fictional site

Create and save a one-page delivery brief for the fictional site. A partner adds a blocked entrance and dirty camera. Revise the brief, reopen the saved version and identify your change.

Answer guidance.

Brief includes participants, route, public access, visibility/inspection, signals and lost-contact response. The revision addresses both changes; record each learner’s contribution and actual file operations.

Safety working around and operating power brooms

Worked example · The clean strip and the dusty doorway

Fictional observation: a broom produces a visibly clean 80 m strip, but dust travels toward an occupied doorway and its fitted water system has stopped spraying. The operator proposes finishing the last 20 m first.

The achieved 80 m does not validate the exposure control. The planned run is 100 m; 20% remains, but that arithmetic is irrelevant to accepting an uncontrolled hazard. Stop the affected work under the procedure, protect the doorway and have the dust-control fault assessed. Resume when the task controls are established, not when the inconvenience feels small.

Explain and apply the idea

Identify brush, supports, operator position and any dust-control components visible in the RB50 manual image. Separate visible features from optional equipment that must be checked.

Answer guidance.

Use the original labels. Do not infer an option from another model or a brochure. Explain brush rotation, movement envelope and the purpose of dust suppression.

Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Calculate and check the result

Check the worked-case percentage and write a defect/exposure report that distinguishes work completed from safe conditions.

Answer guidance.

80 + 20 = 100 m; 20/100 × 100 = 20% remains. Record water-system failure, dust direction, affected people and interim response; no completion percentage overrides controls.

Observe: Numeracy, Writing, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Use a device to locate the manual’s component, safety and sweeping sections

Use a device to locate the manual’s component, safety and sweeping sections. Save three page references, then redesign your brief when the route crosses a public entrance.

Answer guidance.

The record names manual/revision/serial scope and actual page references. Revised brief includes the public route and communication; the learner demonstrates retrieval, save and reopen.

Observe: Digital, Adaptability, Creativity and innovation, Collaboration through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Safety working around and operating air hammers

Worked example · Count exposure honestly

Fictional log: 09:00–09:20 setup; 09:20–09:35 tool in use; 09:35–09:45 pause; 09:45–10:05 tool in use. The worker reports “65 minutes of hammer operation.”

Elapsed time is 65 minutes, while recorded tool-use intervals total 15 + 20 = 35 minutes. The remaining 30 minutes are setup/pause. Keep both values because different exposures may continue during non-trigger time. The log does not calculate a permissible vibration dose; actual assessment needs the relevant exposure data and method.

Label the supply-to-tool diagram, including hose, coupling, retainer and tool

Label the supply-to-tool diagram, including hose, coupling, retainer and tool. Explain where stored energy remains after a trigger is released.

Answer guidance.

Compressed air remains in the system until the specified isolation/depressurization procedure is completed. Releasing a control is not proof of zero pressure.

Observe: Reading, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Calculate and check the result

Recalculate the worked log and propose two columns that prevent elapsed-time and trigger-time confusion.

Answer guidance.

65 minutes elapsed; 35 minutes recorded operation. Keep start/end, action and duration, with exposure assessment recorded separately.

Observe: Numeracy, Writing through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Create a short illustrated handling/stowing brief

Create a short illustrated handling/stowing brief. A partner introduces a damaged coupling; revise the brief and save the corrected record.

Answer guidance.

The defect removes the affected assembly from use for assessment under the procedure. The brief addresses handling, hose route, retained pressure, tool security and individual communication.

Observe: Digital, Collaboration, Communication, Adaptability, Creativity and innovation through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Safety working around and operating propane tanks

Worked example · The assembly register

Fictional teaching sample: the cylinder and torch are identified, but the hose rating is unreadable and the regulator model cannot be matched to the appliance. A learner proposes connecting them because the threads fit.

The assembly has two unresolved compatibility/condition questions. Matching threads does not establish pressure suitability or the correct sealing design. Keep the assembly out of operation; obtain the appropriate component identification and manufacturer instructions, then have the supervised setup and leak test completed. Record the unknown components rather than replacing unknown values with guesses.

Trace the labelled propane assembly and state the function of each part

Trace the labelled propane assembly and state the function of each part. Identify which parts must be matched to the actual appliance.

Answer guidance.

Cylinder/valve store and control the supply; connector joins the approved assembly; regulator where required controls pressure; hose conveys fuel; appliance uses it. Ratings, connections and configurations require the actual instructions.

Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Write a connection-and-leak-check preparation record for the worked case

Write a connection-and-leak-check preparation record for the worked case. State the rejection conditions and the records required before use.

Answer guidance.

Unreadable hose identification or unverified regulator compatibility prevents acceptance. Use manufacturer-approved testing, never flame; stop/isolate/report a leak under the procedure.

Observe: Writing, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Inspect the actual retained torch instructions on a device and save the publication/page identity

Inspect the actual retained torch instructions on a device and save the publication/page identity. Explain to a partner how use, storage and transport questions differ; improve the record after their feedback.

Answer guidance.

The learner locates real instructions and keeps their model scope. A valid explanation distinguishes fuel-use setup from storage location and vehicle/securement requirements.

Observe: Digital, Collaboration, Adaptability, Creativity and innovation through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Weather effects and precautions

Worked example · A break plan that cannot be used

Fictional schedule: a crew is assigned continuous handwork from 10:00 to 12:00. The written heat plan requires planned cool-rest opportunities, but the relief person has been reassigned. A new worker begins making confused replies.

The plan is not being implemented, and confusion is an emergency warning sign in this context. Stop the affected work, summon emergency/first-aid help and follow the cooling response. Arrange coverage and reassess the work before resuming. The two-hour scheduled block does not establish a safe exposure interval; it reveals a mismatch between the plan and staffing.

Explain and apply the idea

List five factors beyond air temperature that influence heat risk, then match each to information the supervisor must consider.

Answer guidance.

Workload, radiant heat, humidity/air movement, clothing/PPE, acclimatization and individual factors are valid. Explain the relationship rather than treating a list as an exposure assessment.

Observe: Reading, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Mark the time span in the worked case and identify why the plan failed

Mark the time span in the worked case and identify why the plan failed. Write the first emergency communication with location and observed condition.

Answer guidance.

10:00–12:00 is two hours; no safe limit is inferred. Relief removal prevents planned control. Message reports actual location, confusion/condition, people affected and known hazards, and summons help.

Observe: Numeracy, Writing, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Develop a weather check-in card with heat, sun and cold/wet prompts

Develop a weather check-in card with heat, sun and cold/wet prompts. Test it with a partner, revise it and save a version that leaves medical information private.

Answer guidance.

Card helps workers report conditions/symptoms and find assistance without diagnosis or disclosure demands. Each learner records feedback and performs actual save/reopen actions.

Observe: Digital, Collaboration, Adaptability, Creativity and innovation through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Safety and traffic control procedures

Worked example · The plan revision matters

Fictional documents: T-02 revision A places the facility entrance outside the work area. Revision B extends the work beyond that entrance. The crew has B, but a traffic briefing still uses A.

The drawings describe different access conditions. Before work under the changed arrangement, resolve the mismatch through the responsible traffic-control planner/supervisor and brief all affected roles. Record the current approved revision and the entrance provisions. An extra cone added by an unassigned person does not resolve the plan conflict.

Explain and apply the idea

Compare TCP and traffic-assistant training requirements using retained Part 18 sections 18.6.2–18.6.3. Identify what qualification this chapter does not issue.

Answer guidance.

TCP training includes the specified program and knowledge/practical assessment; traffic assistants have workplace training addressing their defined tasks. Neither role is conferred by this reading.

Observe: Reading, Writing through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Calculate and check the result

Annotate the work-zone concept with questions about warning, transition, work area, public route and return to normal traffic. Explain why no spacing can be taken from it.

Answer guidance.

Concept identifies functions, not actual distances or approval. Obtain the road-authority plan/risk assessment and current requirements; address visibility, queues and vulnerable road users.

Observe: Problem solving, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Save a version register for the worked case, then role-play a revision handover. Ask the listener to find the current entrance instruction and improve the brief if they cannot.

Answer guidance.

Register retains A as superseded and B as current only when confirmed by the authorized system. The handover identifies affected people and the changed access provision; digital and individual feedback evidence are retained.

Plant site and gravel pit safety

Worked example · One stopped belt is not the whole system

Fictional process: feeder → crusher → conveyor → screen → stockpile. The conveyor stops and material begins accumulating upstream. A visitor proposes stepping over it while an operator investigates.

A stopped component does not establish isolation or a safe crossing. Accumulation adds a stored-material hazard and connected equipment may restart. Keep the visitor on the authorized route, report the condition and use the site’s controlled shutdown/isolation response. The process map helps identify connected sources; it does not replace the isolation plan.

Explain and apply the idea

Trace the process diagram and identify four different hazard mechanisms, including one that remains when motion stops.

Answer guidance.

Examples: crusher impact/crush, belt nip/entanglement, noise/dust, mobile plant and stored-material collapse. Stopped motion does not remove gravity, pressure or automatic restart.

Observe: Reading, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Write a visitor brief for the worked site using only authorized routes and role boundaries

Write a visitor brief for the worked site using only authorized routes and role boundaries. Include the response to an unfamiliar alarm.

Answer guidance.

Brief identifies induction/contact, PPE/noise arrangement, routes, restricted areas and alarm instructions from the site. Learner seeks the actual emergency instruction rather than inventing an alarm meaning.

Observe: Writing, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Create a digital process-and-access sketch

Create a digital process-and-access sketch. A partner removes a guard symbol and blocks a pedestrian route. Record the defects and revise the access plan through the responsible role.

Answer guidance.

Both changes require controlled response; no improvised crossing or work near unguarded machinery. Retain original/revised sketches and each learner’s explanation.

Observe: Digital, Collaboration, Adaptability, Creativity and innovation through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Light vehicle transporting procedures

Worked example · The trailer rating check

Fictional ratings-only exercise: trailer permitted gross mass 3,500 kg; measured empty trailer 900 kg; proposed machine 2,300 kg; tools/ramps 420 kg. Ignore no other real requirement; this is one arithmetic screening check.

Proposed gross mass = 900 + 2,300 + 420 = 3,620 kg. It exceeds the stated trailer figure by 120 kg, so the proposed loading fails this check. The unused-looking deck space does not change the result. Even a revised load below 3,500 kg would still require axle, tow, hitch, restraint, route and driver checks.

Explain why “under 5,000 kg, therefore only company rules apply” is an inadequate transport decision

Explain why “under 5,000 kg, therefore only company rules apply” is an inadequate transport decision. Make a document checklist for either size category.

Answer guidance.

Relevant law, ratings, vehicle/driver requirements, inspection, securement and route/permits still apply. Weight alone does not identify the complete regulatory regime.

Observe: Reading, Writing, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Rework the example if tools/ramps are reduced to 260 kg

Rework the example if tools/ramps are reduced to 260 kg. State precisely what has and has not passed.

Answer guidance.

900 + 2,300 + 260 = 3,460 kg, 40 kg below the stated gross figure. Only that arithmetic comparison passes; other component, actual-mass, securement and legal requirements remain.

Observe: Numeracy, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Build a versioned load record, ask a partner to add a new attachment and recalculate

Build a versioned load record, ask a partner to add a new attachment and recalculate. Save/reopen the revision and explain who must approve the transport arrangement.

Answer guidance.

Record includes identification, rating basis, actual/estimated masses, additions, configuration and unresolved checks. A changed attachment triggers reassessment, not a copied prior approval.

Observe: Digital, Collaboration, Adaptability, Creativity and innovation through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Complete fatigue awareness training

Worked example · Rest interval versus sleep opportunity

Fictional shift finishes at 20:00; the next begins at 06:00. Total travel uses 2 hours, meals/personal preparation 1.5 hours and a required call 0.5 hour. Calculate the maximum remaining interval before other interruptions.

There are 10 hours between shifts. Subtracting 2 + 1.5 + 0.5 leaves at most 6 hours available. That is an opportunity estimate, not proof of six hours of sleep or a universal fitness threshold. The schedule needs review through the workplace fatigue process, especially before safety-critical work or driving.

Explain sleep debt and circadian timing in your own words

Explain sleep debt and circadian timing in your own words. Separate observed signs from a diagnosis or a judgement about a person.

Answer guidance.

Sleep debt concerns insufficient recovery over time; circadian rhythms affect timing of alertness. Describe behaviour/concerns and use a respectful support response without diagnosing.

Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Recalculate the worked case if a late task adds 90 minutes

Recalculate the worked case if a late task adds 90 minutes. Explain the scheduling implication without declaring an individual fit or unfit from arithmetic alone.

Answer guidance.

Maximum remaining opportunity falls from 6 to 4.5 hours. The change needs prompt planning/fitness-for-task review; actual sleep and individual condition are not known.

Observe: Numeracy, Writing, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Compare the retained CCOHS content with your workplace training checklist

Prepare a clear work-zone briefing

This is a complete fictional classroom case. It does not describe an actual community, Nation, employer or project. Learners may use the supplied case without disclosing personal or cultural knowledge.

A fictional community-access project has a 90 m × 4 m work strip. The plan protects a walking route, uses a designated truck guide and requires a working broom dust-control system. At 08:20, the guide’s radio fails and the broom dust-control indicator shows a fault. A draft note says “continue while the guide shouts.” The crew’s shift gap is 11 hours, with 2 hours travel and 2 hours other responsibilities. No safe clearance or operating temperature is supplied.

Reading · Check the facts

Identify the controlling facts, missing information and one unsupported statement in the brief.

Evidence and feedback: Use the exact case facts; distinguish observation, assumption and missing authority.

Writing · Make the handover useful

Draft a concise handover with location, quantities, status and the next responsible action.

Evidence and feedback: The note preserves verified facts, units and unresolved holds without inventing a release.

Numeracy · Calculate and check

Show the case calculation with units and an independent reasonableness check.

Evidence and feedback: Use the worked numerical key below; show the relationship and its practical limits.

Digital · Direct AI and verify the files

Use an available AI assistant to draft a findings table and editable record from this brief. Request DOCX or a structured document, XLSX with formulas where useful, and a PDF/HTML reading copy. Audit every claim and reopen the actual outputs.

Evidence and feedback: Record the product/capabilities, prompt, source facts, saved files and checks. A chat response alone is not a created or verified file. Check formulas and preserve originals.

Problem solving · Investigate the discrepancy

Explain the discrepancy, what evidence is needed and who should authorize the next step.

Evidence and feedback: Separate the observed issue from a guessed cause; preserve evidence and identify the correct decision role.

Communication · Send and check the message

Brief a partner, ask for a check-back and repair one misunderstanding if it occurs.

Evidence and feedback: Observe an actual exchange and confirmation of the essential facts; do not infer communication from a shared file.

Collaboration · Review together

Agree reviewer/author roles, complete a review and record each person’s contribution.

Evidence and feedback: Both learners take an identifiable turn; feedback is respectful and tied to the case evidence.

Adaptability · Respond to the change

Apply the change card, identify what changes and what stays, and revise the record.

Evidence and feedback: Use the change-response key below; retain the earlier version and do not alter unrelated facts.

Creativity and innovation · Make understanding easier

Create a labelled SVG, annotated plan, short audio explanation or captioned storyboard that makes the issue easier to understand. Test it with a reader/listener and improve one useful detail.

Evidence and feedback: The visual/media has accurate labels, source credits and a stated purpose. Inspect generated diagrams/media; do not invent technical settings. Keep a transcript/captions when producing audio/video.

Change card · Use after the first attempt

The protected walking route must move because an entrance is needed; the responsible lead has not yet approved a new route.

Verified case key

Area = 360 m². Sleep opportunity = 11 − 2 − 2 = 7 hours, not a fitness-for-work determination. Hold affected movement/work, restore communication and dust controls through the authorized process; revise the draft.

Change-response key

Preserve the existing safe controls and obtain an approved revised access plan before dependent movement.
