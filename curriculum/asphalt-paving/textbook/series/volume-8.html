Use this volume

This volume provides complete classroom readings, worked examples, named practice tasks and answer guidance for compaction and final handover. Follow the learning route in each chapter, study the labelled figures and explain your reasoning. The separate answer book contains the worked keys and observation records.

Care for people, place and the next person using the work runs through the cases. Local First Nations, Métis and Inuit learners and partners bring distinct knowledge and priorities. Participation does not require sharing personal stories or cultural teachings, and no diagram claims to represent a Nation’s protocol.

Classroom authoring is complete for the listed competencies. Qualified technical review, actual fleet/site matching, a local teaching pilot and observed practical qualification are separate requirements. The practical records are ready for an instructor to configure and use; they do not certify unobserved work.

Keep the term with its meaning

Aggregate
Mineral particles forming the solid skeleton of the mixture or granular layer.
Binder
The asphalt material that coats and binds the aggregate in the specified mixture.
Compacted thickness
Layer thickness after the required compaction process; distinct from loose depth behind the screed.
Density reference
The specified comparison value used as the denominator of a percent-density calculation.
Gradation
Distribution of aggregate particle sizes, commonly expressed as cumulative percent passing specified sieves.
Head of material
The quantity/distribution of mix immediately in front of the screed.
Job mix formula (JMF)
The accepted mixture proportions and properties under the applicable quality/contract process.
Lockout
Isolation, securing and verification of hazardous energy for work requiring protection.
Lot / sub-lot
Defined portions of work used for sampling, testing and acceptance under the contract.
OMM

RAP

Screed

Study chapter · Outline L1

Compaction equipment

Compaction equipment changes the mixture’s density and surface through a planned application of load and, where fitted, dynamic energy. Choose equipment to suit the mixture, support, production and finished requirements.

Types of Compaction Rollers
Major components of compaction equipment
Basic functions of compaction equipment
Emerging technology

Learning step 1

Types of Compaction Rollers

Manufacturers such as Caterpillar, Ammann, BOMAG and Dynapac offer different roller families and configurations. Compare drum width, operating mass, tire/drum arrangement, working modes, production and access. A small utility roller and a highway tandem roller are not interchangeable simply because both compact asphalt. Confirm the actual model, serial range and OMM. Equipment choice and the rolling plan belong to the responsible crew/quality process.

Learning step 2

Major components of compaction equipment

A steel-drum roller may work statically or with fitted vibration/oscillation. A pneumatic-tired roller applies load through tires; tire condition, pressure, temperature and ballast affect its action under the manufacturer and project requirements. Do not invent tire pressures. A combination roller has both drum and tire contact. Identify engine, travel/steering controls, articulation joint, drums/tires, water/spray system, scrapers, vibration controls, ROPS and seat belt where fitted. Components and permitted modes vary by model.

Learning step 3

Basic functions of compaction equipment

Breakdown rolling applies early compaction behind the paver within suitable mixture conditions. Intermediate rolling develops required density and surface condition as planned. Finish rolling removes marks and completes the surface without damaging it. One machine may perform more than one role where the plan supports it; role names do not prescribe a universal machine count or pass sequence.

Learning step 4

Emerging technology

Oscillation applies a different dynamic action from conventional vertical vibration. Intelligent compaction, mapping and temperature displays can help monitor coverage and conditions. Their displayed values are not automatically the contract’s acceptance density. Confirm what each measurement represents and how it is calibrated/validated. Technology supports operator judgement and quality testing; a colourful coverage map does not prove every point meets specification.

Worked example · Choose by the task

A narrow access area has restricted turning space, while the main road has a high production rate.

Think it through.

Compare suitable machine dimensions, modes, capacity and support for each area, then coordinate the planned roles and testing. Do not select only by the largest operating mass or assume one roller can maintain both areas without a scheduling conflict.

Compaction work safety procedures

A roller repeatedly changes direction near people and other equipment. Safe operation depends on visibility, ground support, communication and the machine’s protective systems throughout the pattern.

Interpret job planning
Machine-specific safety hazards
Traffic control requirements
PPE
Lockout procedures
Secure machine for safety

Learning step 1

Interpret job planning

Review the path, pattern, thickness, temperature conditions, crown, joints, obstacles and coordination of the asphalt train. Identify edges, slopes, structures, traffic and public access. Confirm the current plan and who authorizes a change. Walk the inspection route only under controlled conditions.

Learning step 2

Machine-specific safety hazards

Recognize blind areas, crush/articulation zones, hot surfaces, noise, fumes, hydraulic pressure, rollover hazards and proximity to pavers/other rollers. Inspect and use the fitted ROPS and seat belt according to the OMM and applicable rules; do not modify protective structures. A fixed three-metre spacing is not a universal safe separation. Establish actual clearances from the machine instructions and site assessment, including stopping distance and visibility.

Learning step 3

Traffic control requirements

Use the traffic-control plan, trained personnel, signs and communication arrangement. Keep the public route separate from reversing/turning areas. Obtain acknowledgement before entering another machine’s working zone. If a pedestrian, vehicle or unexpected obstacle enters, stop affected movement safely and restore control before continuing.

Learning step 4

PPE

Wear the selected footwear, head, eye, hearing and high-visibility protection and any task-specific items. Ensure the belt and operator position remain compatible with safe control. PPE does not replace the protective structure, separation or visibility checks. Supply, use and maintenance responsibilities follow current Part 8 and employer arrangements.

Learning step 5

Lockout procedures

Service and hazardous access require the actual machine’s lockout, stored-energy and support procedure. The articulation joint can move and crush; use the prescribed steering-frame lock where required and only in the specified conditions. A parked engine does not remove every hydraulic or gravity hazard. Verify the safe state before work.

Learning step 6

Secure machine for safety

Park on the approved stable ground, apply the specified parking controls and required chocks, and secure keys/access. Position away from unsupported edges and public routes. Record defects and protect people from a machine that could roll or be moved by an unauthorized person. A battery/night switch is only one part of the actual security/isolation arrangement.

Worked example · A shortcut behind the roller

A worker steps behind a roller to shorten the route while the operator is preparing to reverse.

Think it through.

Stop the affected movement and re-establish the controlled pedestrian route and communication. Review the blind-area conflict with the crew. High-visibility clothing does not authorize crossing an active reversing path.

Study chapter · Outline L3

Asphalt Compaction Roller operator roles and responsibilities

The roller operator coordinates density development and surface finish with the paving train. A useful rolling pattern is planned, checked and adjusted through evidence rather than repeated by habit.

Communication techniques
Effective rolling pattern
Determine pattern to achieve ride planning
Responsibilities of Compaction Roller operator

Learning step 1

Communication techniques

Agree machine roles, lane/area, direction-change locations, signal/radio channel and stop response. With multiple rollers, share which area is complete, which still needs work and where testing or handwork is occurring. Use location and pass/coverage terminology consistently. An unacknowledged instruction is not a completed handover.

Learning step 2

Effective rolling pattern

A rolling pattern specifies sequence, overlap, operating mode, speed and repetitions for the actual mixture and conditions. A pass and a coverage must be defined: one machine movement is not necessarily one complete treatment of the full mat width. Establish the pattern with the approved trial/quality process and record changes. Repeatedly rolling one strip while missing another creates uneven treatment even if the total pass count looks high.

Learning step 3

Determine pattern to achieve ride planning

Plan for ride quality before the mat cools. Coordinate steady paving, joints, starts/stops and smooth direction changes. Abrupt turns or stopping in a damaging condition can mark or displace the mat. The finish roller cannot be assumed to repair every earlier profile defect. Use measured feedback and report persistent marks or instability promptly.

Learning step 4

Responsibilities of Compaction Roller operator

The operator monitors surroundings, mixture condition, spray/scraper performance, working mode and the planned pattern. Avoid sharp cuts across the mat and do not allow vibration to continue while stationary where prohibited by the machine/process instructions. Coordinate changes with the lead instead of adding passes independently. Required density, smoothness and surface condition are separate checks; meeting one does not establish the others.

Worked example · Pass count without coverage

Two operators each report six passes, but their paths overlap on one side and leave a strip untreated.

Think it through.

Reconstruct the actual path and coverage map, identify the missed strip and obtain an authorized response while conditions allow. Define pass/coverage consistently for the next run. Twelve reported movements do not establish uniform compaction.

Compaction Roller maintenance

Roller maintenance supports safe control and consistent contact with the mat. Water sprays, scrapers and clean contact surfaces are process equipment as well as housekeeping items.

Service lubrication systems (greasing)
Service air intake system as required (air filters/pre cleaners)
Drain fuel tank sump, water separator fuel filters is equipped
Add fluids as required
Inspect and clean components as required
Housekeeping tasks

Learning step 1

Service lubrication systems (greasing)

Use the actual lubrication chart and fitted system. Confirm product, interval, quantity and safe position, clean fittings and load the grease gun correctly. Articulation-area access requires the specified protection. Do not transfer a grease quantity from a different roller or force a blocked fitting.

Learning step 2

Service air intake system as required (air filters/pre cleaners)

Read intake restriction indicators and service the pre-cleaner/filter through the manufacturer’s method. Inspect seals and prevent contamination of the clean side. Clean only when permitted; otherwise replace the element. An employer’s stricter replacement policy may apply. Do not assume reduced-pressure air is acceptable for every element.

Learning step 3

Drain fuel tank sump, water separator fuel filters is equipped

Where fitted, drain the fuel sump/water separator under the prescribed conditions, collect the liquid and report water/debris. Control ignition and exposure. Keep this routine task separate from high-pressure fuel-system repairs. Record unusual or repeated contamination.

Learning step 4

Add fluids as required

Identify the correct fluid and reservoir, then use the specified level ground, temperature and engine state for checking. Dual-scale dipsticks require the correct reading condition. Clean fill tools and avoid mixing, overfill and dirt/water entry. Report repeated losses and leaks rather than treating top-up as a permanent correction.

Learning step 5

Inspect and clean components as required

Inspect radiator/oil cooler, plugs, dipsticks, batteries, hoses, guards and accessible components. Protect fins and electrical parts during cleaning. Hot coolant, pressurized hydraulics and battery hazards require their actual procedures. Inspect safety systems and report defects for authorized repair. A maintenance checklist does not authorize work beyond the learner’s training.

Learning step 6

Housekeeping tasks

Remove debris and excess grease from safe access areas. Maintain the spray tank, filters, nozzles and approved water-treatment arrangements, and inspect drum/tire cleanliness, scrapers and fitted mats. Blocked sprays can allow pickup and mark the surface. Prepare the water system for freezing conditions using the model’s drain/winterization procedure; “drain all water” requires knowing every affected component. Collect waste, restore guards and record service and defects.

Worked example · A dry stripe on the drum

A drum shows a persistent dry stripe and material begins to pick up in that area.

Think it through.

Report the pattern, stop/hold as required and inspect the spray/scraper system through the safe procedure. A blocked nozzle or another fault is a possibility, not a confirmed diagnosis. Correct and verify the authorized repair before continuing.

Operate Compaction Roller

Compaction operation combines smooth machine control with temperature, density and coverage feedback. The instructor observes the actual machine and conditions; a written pattern alone does not demonstrate operating skill.

Plan work
Test temperature
Test density
Maintain efficient patterns

Learning step 1

Plan work

Inspect the grade for edges, support, obstacles and limits. Confirm roller types/roles, approved settings and pattern, water supply, communications and traffic controls. Complete pre-start checks and daily maintenance. Start and monitor through the matching OMM. Practise forward–stop–reverse–stop on the flat training area, parking controls, travel-mode changes and fitted vibration controls under supervision. Weather, site conditions, grade and quality feedback can require an authorized plan change.

Learning step 2

Test temperature

Measure temperature with the specified instrument and method at safe locations. Infrared instruments read a surface-related temperature and depend on emissivity, distance/spot size and conditions; a probe measures through its contact method. Record location, time, method and units. Mixture-specific compaction conditions govern; do not use a universal temperature window from memory. Cooling rate varies with lift, weather, support and delays.

Learning step 3

Test density

Density testing provides feedback through the approved method and reference. A nuclear gauge such as the Troxler 3430 requires licensed/trained use and applicable CNSC controls; observing it does not authorize operation. Core testing has a different sampling process. Confirm the denominator for percent density and the test location/lot. The test plan determines frequency. Results help the responsible lead evaluate the pattern; a single reading does not prove complete acceptance.

Learning step 4

Maintain efficient patterns

Maintain the approved breakdown, intermediate and finish sequence with smooth direction changes and controlled speed/mode. Demonstrate the assigned confined-edge and longitudinal-joint patterns under the instructor’s plan. Coordinate with the paver and other rollers, track coverage and avoid abrupt turns or damaging stops. Do not interpret repeated forward/reverse movement as permission to stop with vibration engaged. Finish with the model-specific shutdown and a clear record of actual treatment and unresolved areas.

Worked example · Density feedback changes the plan

A trial strip’s recorded density increases over the first approved passes, then stops improving while the mixture cools.

Think it through.

Report the measured results with temperature, location and reference. The quality/crew lead evaluates whether settings, timing, equipment or another factor needs adjustment. More passes are not automatically effective and may damage the mat. Preserve the trial record and authorized revised pattern.

Follow shut down procedures

A roller can move unintentionally after the shift if parking and securement are incomplete. Shutdown also prepares the machine for cleaning, staging, maintenance and transport.

Learning step 1

Shut-down procedures

Choose the designated stable parking position with service access, clear emergency/public routes and suitable ground for checks. Follow the OMM stopping, cooling and shutdown sequence, apply parking controls and required chocks, and inspect safely for leaks or damage. Use the battery/night switch only as directed; it is not the whole lockout procedure. Record defects and the machine’s status.

Learning step 2

Clean equipment

Clean drums/tires, scrapers and spray equipment through the approved safe method. Control movement, heat, pressure and cleaning-product hazards, and contain waste. Never reach toward a rotating drum or articulation zone to remove pickup. Restore guards and account for tools before release.

Learning step 3

Ensure efficient staging

Coordinate staging with the foreperson so the next shift can access the roller without blocking other equipment, deliveries or public routes. Identify the machine, location, readiness and pending service. Efficient staging includes safe access for a machine that cannot restart.

Learning step 4

Prepare for transport

Prepare for transport using the actual OMM configuration and carrier plan. Assign a competent operator/guide and carrier responsibilities, verify mass/dimensions, ramp/support suitability and designated tie-down points. Use the applicable securement requirements and approved loading sequence. The transport driver’s role does not remove the need for clear machine-control responsibilities.

Learning step 5

Secure machine for safety

Secure controls, keys, detachable items and access panels; protect the public and prevent roll-away through the required parking/chocking arrangement. Consider residual heat and stored energy. Communicate out-of-service status visibly through the site system. Recheck the final state before leaving; a completed numerical checklist score cannot override a failed critical securement item.

Worked example · Ready for the next shift

A roller is clean and fuelled but parked where a delivery truck must reverse around it beside a public entrance.

Think it through.

Have the responsible lead arrange a safer approved staging location. Complete the movement and shutdown under the actual procedure, preserve access and communicate the final location/status. “Ready” includes safe placement, not only clean equipment.
