# Follow shut down procedures

Screed shutdown and cleaning are coordinated with the paver. The final state must protect people from heat, movement and unsupported components while keeping the equipment ready for inspection.

## Shut down procedures

Choose the approved parking position with service access and suitable ground. Follow the actual stopping, heater shutdown, component positioning/support and paver parking sequence. Inspect safely for leaks, damage and missing guards. Apply full lockout when the cleaning or service task requires it; normal shutdown alone is insufficient. Secure controls and access and record the final state.

## Clean screed after use

Clean buildup using the manufacturer-approved method, compatible product and SDS controls. Coordinate with the paver crew so shared systems cannot move unexpectedly. Control ignition sources and residual heat; never spray a flammable product onto a hot heater or open flame. A fixed “20-minute clean” is not a universal safe duration. Collect scrapings and wash/cleaning waste, protect drainage and restore guards. Record defects and any work still required before the next start.

## Worked example: Cleaning is delayed

The screed remains too hot for the approved cleaning product and procedure when the scheduled shift ends.

Maintain the safe shutdown state and coordinate the required cooling and cleaning arrangements. Do not spray early to meet the schedule. Record the outstanding task and responsible person so the next shift does not assume cleaning is complete.
