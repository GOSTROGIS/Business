Recognize the symbol.
Read the whole message.

A pictogram alerts you to a type of hazard. The label and safety data sheet (SDS) explain the particular product and its precautions. The same work area can also have machine warnings, traffic signs and emergency signs; each has a different purpose.

In an asphalt crew, check the actual products used for the task: fuels, cleaners, release agents and other supplied materials. Do not assign a WHMIS classification from the word “asphalt”, a container colour or a familiar brand.

Some WHMIS hazard classes and categories have no required pictogram. No pictogram does not mean no hazard. Check the product identifier, complete label and SDS Section 2.

This classroom material develops WHMIS understanding. The employer also provides training for the actual hazardous products, tasks, controls and emergency procedures at the workplace.

Current teaching basis: CCOHS fact sheets revised 28 May 2026, reflecting the December 2022 HPR amendments. The two WorkSafeBC books in the source pack are labelled as 2019 historical explanations.

The pictogram library

Official CCOHS artwork, retained in its standard form. SVG files are faithful vector conversions of the supplied EPS artwork. Frame, symbol and colours have not been redesigned.

Flame over circle

Oxidizing hazards. Can cause or intensify fire. Read the actual product’s hazard and precautionary statements.

Flame

Fire-related hazards. Used for specified flammable, self-reactive, pyrophoric, self-heating and other fire-related classes/categories. The exact hazard is stated on the label and SDS.

Exploding bomb

Explosion or violent reaction. Used in WHMIS for specified self-reactive substances and mixtures and organic peroxides. Read the classification and conditions; do not infer them from the picture alone.

Gas cylinder

Pressure hazards. Used for gases under pressure and chemicals under pressure. The product’s statements identify its particular pressure and related hazards.

Skull and crossbones

Severe acute toxicity. Used for acute toxicity categories 1–3 by the applicable exposure route. Keep the label’s exact route and hazard statement.

Corrosion

Corrosion or serious damage. May identify corrosive-to-metals, skin-corrosion or serious-eye-damage hazards. Read which hazards apply to the product.

Exclamation mark

Harmful or irritating effects. Used for specified acute toxicity, irritation, skin sensitization and other categories. It does not mean “minor” or “safe”.

Health hazard

Serious health effects. Used for specified hazards such as cancer, respiratory sensitization, reproductive toxicity, organ damage and aspiration. The symbol alone does not identify which one applies.

Biohazardous infectious materials

Biohazardous infectious materials. Used for biohazardous infectious materials. This Canadian WHMIS pictogram has a circular border, not the red diamond used for the other illustrated WHMIS pictograms.

Environment

Hazardous to the aquatic environment. This GHS pictogram may appear on products, but environmental hazards are not part of the mandatory Canadian WHMIS hazard classes. Keep environmental precautions from the applicable SDS and site plan.

Three sources of information

  1. Identify
    Match the product name on container, label and SDS.
  2. Read
    Read hazard statements and precautions, not just the pictogram.
  3. Locate
    Find task-specific information in the SDS and workplace procedures.
  4. Check
    Resolve missing or conflicting information with the designated person before the task.

Supplier label

Look for the product identifier, initial supplier identifier, applicable pictograms, signal word, hazard statements and precautionary statements. Required supplemental information may also apply. Supplier label information is provided in English and French. “Danger” and “Warning” are signal words; their use depends on classification.

Workplace label

A workplace label generally contains the product identifier matching the SDS, safe-handling information and a statement that the SDS is available. Requirements and limited exceptions for transferred containers depend on the circumstances and jurisdiction. Use the employer’s procedure; do not treat “only a small amount” as a reason to leave a shared container unidentified.

Find the information in an SDS

An SDS has 16 section headings. Read the current SDS for the exact product. A similar name, an old screenshot or a different supplier’s sheet is not a confirmed match.
