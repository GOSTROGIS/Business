Internal curriculum planning and source-alignment record. This map connects the three-session learner workbook and instructor guide to observable nine-skill evidence. It records the 18 opening activities and their relationship to the continuing Line A classroom sequence. The source citations below document instructional research; the learner and instructor materials provide the complete teaching sequence.

Background source for this internal record: SkillPlan practitioner guide, revised March 2024. Its printed page numbers match its PDF page numbers. References below use those page numbers.

Occupational boundary: Supplied Asphalt Paving/Laydown Technician Program Outline, A1, PDF p.22 / printed p.20. A1 introduces the industry, equipment, road history and uses, resources, statistics, other road types, regulations/funding and environmental comparison. The workbook contains three 60-minute ACCESS IS4S classroom sessions. The outline's 3% allocation covers the whole of Line A, A1–A6, not A1 alone (PDF p.17 / printed p.15). Applying 3% to the supplied profile's 120-hour technical-training total gives 3.6 hours for that whole line; it does not establish this unit's technical-training allocation.

Attribution and source limits

Attribute the practitioner guide to SkillPlan. Its title and SkillPlan attribution appear on p.3. The cover states “Funded by the Government of Canada's Skills for Success Program” (p.1); funding is not authorship. The guide credits Government of Canada (2021) for its nine skill definitions on p.11. Its component panels cite the Research Report to Support the Launch of Skills for Success. This map cites those components as reproduced in the supplied SkillPlan guide, without claiming an independent review of that report.

No explicit open redistribution licence was found in the guide's searchable text. Its advice to seek permission for other workplace documents on p.28 is not a licence for this guide. The workbook uses original tasks and teaching prose, with attribution for the guide's named skill components. Check item-specific rights before reproducing further pages, diagrams or third-party material.

The guide describes its proficiency levels as preliminary (p.19). It separates Levels 1–5 for Reading, Writing and Numeracy from Entry, Intermediate and Advanced for the other six skills. Pages 51 and 68 identify further limitations of the legacy reading/numeracy descriptors. This map assigns no official level, standardized score or trade-competency result. These statements describe the supplied March 2024 guide, not an independently verified later framework update.

Teaching principles used in this unit

SkillPlan principle and pages Application in the actual workbook
Learner-centred, pp.24–25 Invite prior knowledge without requiring personal disclosure. Offer glossary, enlarged pages, assistive tools and appropriate response supports. Preserve each learner's contribution and record the support used. A1-Q08 and A1-Q18 invite learner-selected next practice.
Contextualized, pp.26–28 Use occupation-related purposes, process/equipment cards, a project brief, tables and an information sheet. These are original classroom simulations, not authentic employer documents. A1-Q10 explicitly identifies missing source information rather than turning an old abbreviation into a current rule.
Complexity, pp.32–34 Move from finding details and matching purposes (A1-Q01–Q06), through a worked table example and independent interpretation (A1-Q11), to evaluating limited evidence and revising for changed requirements (A1-Q14–Q17). Add or remove prompts according to observed need. More questions alone do not establish greater complexity.
Direct instruction, pp.35–36 State the outcome, model the process aloud, practise with support and then collect individual evidence. The process map, listening routine, worked table, spreadsheet steps and claim-checking routine provide explicit models. Reading's gradual-release guidance on pp.49–50 supports this approach.
Assessment, pp.37–38 Use observable tasks, individual records, immediate specific feedback and another attempt where needed. Separate mathematical accuracy, source interpretation, digital performance and interaction quality rather than assigning one global impression.
Social-emotional skills, pp.40–42 Explicitly teach the listening/clarification routine in A1-Q07, thoughtful claim-checking in A1-Q14, response to changed requirements in A1-Q16 and feedback use in A1-Q17. These are teachable actions, not fixed personality traits.

The guide recommends authentic workplace documents where available (pp.28, 47). The opening workbook uses classroom simulations. Continue with Industry evidence and decisions: “Report what the data can support”–“Retrieve and preserve a source record” use dated public statistics, “Match a question to a document”–“Check the funding statement” examine workplace requirements, contract references and funding, and “Make the strongest supported comparison”–“Revise a claim when the scope changes” compare sourced material-recovery pathways within an explicit boundary. Record that evidence separately from performance on the fictional opening tasks. The source register retains the evidence limits.

Nine skills mapped to actual tasks

Quoted labels and component numbers are from the indicated guide pages. The evidence descriptions are local instructional criteria. A task may use several skills, while the instructor observes a specific focus skill. Mapping selected components does not claim to assess every component of that skill.

Skill and exact guide page Selected named components Actual question IDs and activity Observable evidence and limits
Reading — p.45 1 “Identify the task that requires you to read”; 2 “Identify the information contained in the document(s)”; 3 “Make connections between different parts of the document(s)”; 4 “Understand and apply the information” A1-Q01–Q06: locate the infrastructure explanation, complete the short history, sequence the process, match equipment purposes, question an unsupported resource claim and compare road types/users. Learner points to relevant workbook wording or a table and uses it accurately. Record the reading support. General knowledge alone does not establish that the learner read and understood the supplied material.
Communication — p.85 1 “Listen with intention”; 2 “Listen to understand”; 3 “Speak with clarity”; 4 “Speak with purpose” A1-Q07: explain the process map, restate a partner's explanation, ask a useful clarification, confirm and swap roles. A1-Q17 revisits receiving and using a reader's feedback. Accurate message, listener restatement and clarification/check-back in an accessible interactive mode. Assess meaning and understanding; accent, speaking speed and eye contact are not criteria.
Collaboration — p.92 1 “Work well with other people”; 5 “Achieve a common goal with others”; 6 “Reflect and improve on how well the team works together” A1-Q07: both partners explain/listen, contribute and agree one improvement; each records their contribution and a useful partner suggestion. Individual contribution, attention to the partner's idea and an agreed improvement. Sitting together or submitting one correct map is insufficient evidence. Broader conflict-management competence is not claimed from this short task.
Numeracy — p.62 2 “Identify the mathematical information”; 3 “Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information”; 4 “Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question”; 5 “Interpret and evaluate the information”; 6 “Share the mathematical information, results and implications” A1-Q09: total the fictional funding. A1-Q11: total employers/workers/contract values, identify a part and whole, calculate a difference, state a supported comparison and check an answer differently. Correct quantities and units, visible reasoning/calculation, correct whole for the fraction, a valid check and an interpretation confined to the fictional table. No claim about actual B.C. industry size, profit or statistical proficiency follows.
Digital — p.71 1 “Use digital devices including computers, tablets, smart phones and other handheld devices”; 2 “Use common digital tools to complete tasks”; 3 “Use digital information” A1-Q12: each learner controls a spreadsheet or accessible table tool, enters/checks data, checks totals, saves and finds/reopens the file. Observe an individual tool turn, checked entry, usable headings/totals, saving and reopening. Record formula use only if performed; the simple-table route records checked totals separately. This local-file task does not evidence online platforms, online safety or online research components. Paper work or a partner's file does not establish Digital evidence.
Writing — p.54 1 “Identify the task that requires you to write”; 2 “Plan the writing task”; 4 “Choose the appropriate language and style for the writing task”; 5 “Choose the appropriate format for the writing task”; 6 “Review and revise your writing” A1-Q13: create a four-to-six-sentence project note for a new team member using the brief, a clearly fictional statistic and one unresolved requirement; receive a partner's interpretation and make a useful edit. An authored text meeting the reader's needs, accurate roles/numbers, clear fictional/source-status language and a visible revision. Assistive authoring is valid when learner control is evident and recorded. A purely oral response without a text product does not evidence this writing outcome.
Problem solving — p.78 1 “Identify the issue to be addressed”; 2 “Gather information to help you address the issue”; 3 “Analyze the issue”; 5 “Address the issue” A1-Q14: identify the overclaim, use C1–C3 to distinguish the distance comparison from a whole-project conclusion, name missing information and rewrite the claim with a useful reviewer question. Claim, evidence and gaps are distinguished; the revised statement and proposed inquiry are justified. Gathering here means selecting information from the provided cards. No external source search, completed technical comparison or comprehensive assessment of all six problem-solving components is claimed.
Adaptability — p.99 4 “Set or adjust your goals and expectations”; 5 “Plan and prioritize”; 6 “Seek self-improvement” A1-Q16: identify the change-card requirements, decide what to retain/revise and adjust the first draft. A1-Q17–Q18: use feedback and identify evidence of the changed approach. A first draft, change note, revised sheet meeting the one-minute/black-and-white/90-word brief, and a stated strategy. Assess the adjusted plan and action, not presumed emotions, personality or willingness to comply.
Creativity & Innovation — p.106 3 “Generate ideas that are novel to yourself or others”; 4 “Develop your ideas”; 5 “Apply your ideas” A1-Q15: sketch two ways to answer the visitor's three questions, choose with a reason and draft. A1-Q17: trial the sheet with a reader and improve it. Two distinct organizational possibilities, a choice tied to the reader, an applied idea and a justified improvement after the reader test. Artistic polish and industry-wide novelty are not required. The innovation concerns explaining information, not inventing equipment procedures.

Three-session progression

Session and duration Questions and focus Teaching and evidence sequence
1. See the whole job — 60 minutes A1-Q01–Q08. Reading, Communication, Collaboration. Activate relevant knowledge and clarify vocabulary. Model finding evidence and reading the five-stage process. Practise matching purposes and relating road types to users. Explicitly teach explain → listen → restate → clarify → confirm. Observe each partner, agree an improvement and collect an individual exit note.
2. Read the industry picture — 60 minutes A1-Q09–Q13. Numeracy, Digital, Writing. Read the fictional owner/contractor/funding brief and identify the historical source-label gaps. Model the East/West table example. Learners independently calculate and interpret the North/Central/South table, then check it. Each learner enters/checks, saves and reopens a digital record. End with a purposeful project note and revision.
3. Check a claim and improve the message — 60 minutes A1-Q14–Q18. Problem solving, Adaptability, Creativity and innovation. Model the claim → evidence → missing information → justified next step routine. Apply it to C1–C3. Develop two information-sheet options and an initial draft. Release the change card after drafting. Revise, test whether a reader can find the three answers, improve and identify evidence for the three focus skills.

These 180 minutes are the sequence in the instructor guide. Device beginners may need another supported turn outside that core timing. Leave Digital not observed until evidence is collected. The guide's advice to provide appropriate starting points and just-in-time support supports this decision (pp.72–73).

Use the existing workbook data and answer key. The East/West worked example and North/Central/South independent table are fictional and separate. Their interpretation is separate from the dated public industry data and source checks in “Report what the data can support”–“Retrieve and preserve a source record”. Do not introduce a second practice dataset into this map or substitute its values into the workbook.

Source-based assessment decisions

  • Reading: The guide supports navigation of authentic document structures and explicit reading strategies (pp.47–50). Its p.51 warns that low digital skill can interfere with an online reading assessment. Here, evaluate source comprehension separately from device use and record read-aloud or other support.
  • Communication: Active listening is explicitly taught, including clarification and paraphrase (p.87). Page 89 cautions against confounding listening with writing. A1-Q07 uses direct interaction rather than making an extended written report the only evidence of listening.
  • Collaboration: The guide says group work alone is insufficient; collaboration needs explicit instruction and structured roles (pp.94–95). Page 96 says to assess interaction quality rather than only the group's product. Observe both partners in A1-Q07. If a disagreement occurs, coach respectful clarification; this unit does not manufacture or grade a separate conflict scenario.
  • Writing: Meaningful tasks, audience/purpose, checklists, peer response and revision are supported on pp.56–57. A1-Q13 evaluates the usefulness and clarity of the project note, with language-mechanics feedback in service of that purpose.
  • Numeracy: Contextualized instruction, learner strengths, accessible approaches and calculators are supported on pp.64–66. Page 67 recommends specific formative feedback and opportunities to retry, and cautions against digital-interface confounds. Preserve the learner's reasoning and check, not only the final number.
  • Digital: The guide recommends needs assessment, simple-to-more-complex tasks and individual support (pp.72–73), plus frequent formative feedback (p.75). The actual task demonstrates local information/tool use. It supplies no evidence of safe online practice merely because a computer is present.
  • Problem solving: Teach a structured process and gather/analyze information before deciding (pp.79–80). A1-Q14 supports a justified limited claim and next inquiry, not a definitive environmental ranking.
  • Adaptability: The guide favours process/effort feedback over personality judgements (pp.100–101) and supported variation in scenarios (p.102). Page 103 notes that assessment models remain under development. Retain before/after drafts and the learner's strategy as evidence.
  • Creativity & Innovation: Relevant idea generation, applied products and systematic development are supported on pp.107–109. A1-Q15 and Q17 make an idea visible and testable. A copy of a supplied layout without alternatives or testing does not show the full intended task.

Feedback descriptions and transfer

Use the instructor guide's four local feedback descriptions exactly:

Description When to use it
Not observed No adequate evidence has yet been collected for this learner and focus skill.
Not yet demonstrated An observed attempt shows that further teaching or practice is needed for the stated criterion.
Demonstrated with support The learner demonstrates the criterion using identified support; record that support.
Demonstrated independently The learner demonstrates the stated task criterion independently under the recorded conditions.

These are classroom feedback descriptions, not SkillPlan or federal proficiency categories and not trade grades. Record learning ID, task/question ID, evidence, support used, feedback description, next teaching step and recheck date. Learners can receive different descriptions for different skills. Recheck after feedback when appropriate.

Record transfer separately. A1-Q18 asks for a proposed future use and evidence from this unit; that reflection alone does not demonstrate actual workplace transfer. A later unfamiliar task or workplace observation can supply separate transfer evidence, with its context and support recorded. Assessment/transfer considerations are discussed on pp.37–38 of the guide.

Curriculum presentation

Retain the established Numeracy Hub direction: ACCESS red headings, gold rules/highlight fills, warm paper, clear labels, numbered worked steps and practical language. Keep task purpose, practice and evidence close together. The ACCESS teaching materials carry this visual and instructional approach through the six-unit Line A sequence.

Learner choice, respectful interaction and access supports are consistent with pp.24–25, 86, 93 and 110. Invite community knowledge when freely offered; do not ask a learner to represent an entire culture or disclose personal experiences. Appropriate local direction and permission remain necessary for any specific community knowledge introduced later.
