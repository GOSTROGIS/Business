## A1 objective and all nine learning tasks

All entries in this section are sourced to outline **PDF p. 22 / printed p. 20**. The objective is broader than equipment identification: it includes road-construction history and the relationship between governing bodies and contractors.

| Task | Accurate paraphrase of the supplied learning task and content | Teaching interpretation / evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| A1.1 | Describe paving and the equipment used, using a basic overview from the pit through to the finished product. | An illustrated process overview and equipment matching are reasonable introductory designs. The source does not supply a finished set of beginner equipment cards. |
| A1.2 | Give a basic historical account of how roads were originally created. | Introduce a brief history question here; the extended history sequence belongs under A2. Do not treat a single origin story as universal. |
| A1.3 | Explain why roads matter as infrastructure. | Learners can identify how a route connects services, goods and community activities. Those examples are original teaching choices. |

| A1.5 | Explain who uses paving and how people rely on it for recreation and trade. | Ask learners to identify varied users and purposes without assuming every learner drives. |
| A1.6 | Interpret relevant statistics about industry size, economics and numbers of employers. | A small labelled fictional table can teach reading quantities, differences or percentages. It must not be presented as real BC industry statistics; real statistics need a dated source and defined geography/measure. |
| A1.7 | Describe other kinds of roads, with ice roads given as an example. | A comparison task should distinguish surface types and contexts without adding invented claims about local communities or travel conditions. |
| A1.8 | Review regulations and funding relevant to paving; the source's content column gives “EPS, MMCD, etc.” as examples. | Treat those as the document's labels. There is no A1-specific practical achievement-criteria block on that page. A locally designed checklist can gather learning evidence, but it should not be labelled the official certification assessment.

## Teaching sequence

See the [three-session A1 curriculum](../README.md) and [instructor coverage map](../unit-01/instructor.md#observable-outcomes-and-a1-coverage). The 180-minute sequence is an original supplementary IS4S design. It is separate from the historical outline's time allocation.

## Boundaries with adjacent competencies

- **A2 — history (PDF p. 23 / printed p. 21):** expands A1's brief historical introduction through ancient roads, original materials/methods, logging and “corduroy” roads, modern placement, and the rationale for road growth. A1 should orient; A2 can investigate chronology, evidence and differing contexts. The historical examples need contemporary contextual research before becoming learner reading passages.
- **A3 — technology (PDF p. 24 / printed p. 22):** covers drum/batch plants, ingredients, resources, production liquids, recycling and the asphalt train. A1 equipment cards may name broad purposes; they should not present plant operation, mix selection, material grades or recycling procedures as already taught.
- **A4 — workers and teams (PDF p. 25 / printed p. 23):** covers jobs, professionalism, safety responsibilities, licensing, advancement, job security, organized labour and working conditions. A1 can identify equipment without claiming that learners have mastered operator roles or that historical licensing/labour-market statements are current requirements.
- **B1 — Safety orientation (PDF pp. 28–29 / printed pp. 26–27):** the source objective is to understand and demonstrate safe working practices. Tasks address PPE, hazards, safety policy, certifications, rigging, site/equipment risks and public safety. Classroom equipment recognition is not a safety induction; current provider/site orientation is needed before equipment access or practical work. Do not reuse dated PPE/legal statements as universal current rules.
- **B2 and equipment-specific safety sections:** address working around haul trucks and each machine. A discussion of communication during an A1 card activity is not instruction or authorization to direct live equipment.

The beginner descriptions below are **proposed paraphrases supported by later outline sections**, not exact source sentences and not operational instructions. Use a “role / where it fits / source” format. Avoid settings, speeds, clearances, temperatures, compaction percentages, maintenance steps, signals or machine-control directions in introductory cards.

| Card | Suitable original beginner wording | What the supplied source actually supports | Citation |

## Hours, pathways and the earlier planning reference

- The supplied **November 2014 profile specifies 120 hours of technical training, described as four weeks based on a 30-hour week**, at PDF/printed pp. 2–3. Four × 30 = 120 hours.
- The earlier pasted planning text referred to an **IUOE four-week / 140-hour** provider program. That remains a separate, previously unverified provider reference. Do not replace the profile's 120 hours with 140, or present the two as the same source/program version. Verify the current provider offering and the ACCESS-specific agreement before reconciling hours.
- The profile separates technical training from equipment-endorsement workplace hours, practical competency sign-off, recommendation for certification and an examination (PDF/printed pp. 2–3). The six-week ACCESS cohort in the schedule is not automatically this entire qualification pathway.
- Profile PDF/printed p. 1 explicitly identifies Grade 10/English 10/Math 10 and one year of related experience as **recommended education/experience, not prerequisites**. Do not relabel them as mandatory entry requirements.
- Keep current requirements marked **confirmation pending** until checked with the current standards and chosen provider. The supplied historical PDFs can guide the initial research structure immediately without making claims about present regulatory compliance.
