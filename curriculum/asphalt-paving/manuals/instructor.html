Manual-reading lesson · Teaching and answer guidance

Read the identity. Find the evidence. Explain the limit.

All manuals

Prepare a complete document-reading session

Allow about 90–120 minutes, or divide the lesson into two sessions. Prepare the six original OEM PDFs, the original Deere HTML manual, their covers/contents for print access, a PDF reader, a writable practice folder and the fictional records. Do not place machines in this activity. Arrange actual device turns if Digital is an intended observation. Confirm that all learners can reach a useful view and that practice IDs carry no private data. Model page navigation using a different heading before assigning MAN-02. The Deere HTML contents and local images/styles work without an internet connection.

The lesson teaches source selection, document navigation and a bounded handover. It does not award an equipment endorsement or demonstrate maintenance performance. Cover matching is necessary evidence for document selection; it does not establish every detail of actual-machine applicability. The manuals include original technical instructions, but the lesson assesses finding and interpreting information, not carrying those procedures out.

Use agreed access tools and record exact solving prompts separately. A learner can use text-to-speech and still independently decide which serial statement applies. If the observer supplies the matching manual, that decision has been supported. A missing device turn is Not observed for Digital, not an inferred failure. Invite another attempt with changed fictional data after feedback.

Model verification note for MAN-04

Manual selection — practice records A to C

“Record A's AR13HA serial 110450 fits the 110301-and-above cover of OEM-03. Record B's AR13HAR serial 100850 fits OEM-04's 100901-and-below cover. Record C's serial 105500 matches neither supplied range. OEM-04's higher revision number and later date do not extend its serial scope. Please confirm C's complete machine record and obtain the applicable publication through the responsible instructor or manufacturer support. These are classroom cover matches; current machine configuration, information and authorization remain separate checks before actual work.”

This is one acceptable concise note. Do not require learners to reproduce its sentences. Preserve the difference between “matches the supplied cover” and “approved for actual work.”

Criterion anchors and feedback

Criterion Observable evidence Useful response when evidence is incomplete
Identify and match Correct model, publication and serial scope, with page evidence Compare the exact boundary words before asking for a fresh decision
Navigate and preserve Actual file opened, passage found, authored note saved and reopened Demonstrate one control with unrelated content, then return tool control
Calculate and interpret Inclusive counts and units/meaning are correct; no machine-count inference Use a three-page range to show why an endpoint counts
Communicate uncertainty Clear match/unresolved status, retained limits and next action Ask which sentence establishes the claimed approval or cause
Coordinate and adapt Both checking roles observed; revision responds to corrected input Record the missing turn or unchanged content, then arrange another attempt
Develop and test an aid Two options, chosen aid, real test observation and limited conclusion Separate a prediction from what the reader actually selected

Calibration: Selecting OEM-04 for record C solely because “Revision 10 is newer” is Not yet demonstrated for matching. After the observer asks “What serial range does that cover name?”, a learner identifies the gap: record Demonstrated with support if the remaining criterion is met, quoting that prompt. A learner who uses enlarged text, compares the boundaries without a solving prompt and records the unresolved result can demonstrate the selection criterion independently. No cover reading or no opportunity is Not observed.
