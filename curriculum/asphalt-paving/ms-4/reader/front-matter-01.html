MS-4 is the Asphalt Institute’s comprehensive manual on the use of asphalt. For 70 years, it has served the asphalt industry as the primary reference manual for contractors, engineers, consultants, specifiers, and user agencies.

With this printing, the Asphalt Institute is pleased to offer the seventh edition of this essential asphalt publication. This new, expanded edition of over 800 pages of text and illustrations showcases the advances in asphalt technology that have evolved since the 1989 edition of MS-4.

Readers of this edition will learn how to use the latest technologies to address a variety of modern pavement challenges, including durability, heavy loads, high traffic volumes, noise, smoothness, and safety to meet the demands of today’s driving public.

This seventh edition of MS-4 continues to offer chapters on types of asphalts, mix designs, mixing facilities, paving, compaction, surface treat- ments, recycling technologies, maintenance methods, rehabilitation, and structural design. But now, this all-in-one desk reference for asphalt high- lights the latest technology.

New topics covered include the folowing:

° Superpave asphalt binder

° Superpave mix design

* Stone matrix asphalt

* Open-graded friction courses

* Quality control and acceptance

° Pavement management

° Rehabilitation of concrete pavements with HMA

New enhanced chapters include these topics:

¢ Petroleum asphalt

° Asphalt modification and additives ° PG grades ° Safety guidelines ° Mineral aggregates ° RAP ° Mixture additives ° Thickness design ° Mechanistic-empirical methods ° Perpetual pavements ¢ Comparison of design methodologies

And you will notice a new look for this new edition of MS-4. The sev- enth edition features an updated design and two-color layout, new graph- ics and photos, state-of-the-practice illustrations and charts, and a new topical/keyword index. Each chapter also includes a detailed reference list for more information when needed.

When the Asphalt Institute (AI decided to give this flagship publication its first major overhaul in almost 20 years, we knew from the start that this would be a team-based project. The expertise required to accurately address such a broad range of topics simply necessitated this approach. We also knew that the sheer scale of the task invited the participation of others.

Our initial steps were to review the 1989 version of The Asphalt Handbook and to ask ourselves what the new handbook needed to include. Dr. Bob Boyer, P.E., former Asphalt Institute senior field engineer and now a consul- tant in Florida, led us through a yearlong process to develop a comprehen- sive and detailed outline for the manual, complete with references for each chapter. We are indebted to Boyer for his dedication to this fundamentally important stage of our project.

As we sought to build our writing team, we explored various alterna- tives with individual authors, partnerships, and companies, all of which added value to our process. We are indebted to them for their insights, advice, and remarkable excitement for what we were undertaking.

Selecting AMEC Earth & Environmental Inc., with Douglas Hanson as our content editor, proved to be the right choice for this project. AMEC’s group of highly qualified experts with unique experience and knowledge combined with the Asphalt Institute’s team to precisely deliver a new man- ual.
