Full-depth (and deep) patches are used for making permanent pavement
repairs. The material in the repair area should be removed to the depth
necessary for reaching firm support as shown in Figure 12.1. This may mean
removing some of the subgrade. It may also require installing additional
drainage. The excavation should extend at least 1 foot (0.3 meter) into the
good pavement surrounding the area to be patched. Patches should be
square-edged and the cuts should be rectangular in shape. A pavement saw
makes a fast and clean cut. If the asphalt layer to be removed is thicker than
the depth of the sawcut, the remainder may be jack-hammered out. The ver-
tical faces of the patch are then given a tack coat as shown in Figure 12.2.
For best results, the patch should be backfilled with a dense-graded HMA
(see Figure 12.3). Then the mix should be spread carefully to prevent seg-
regation (see Figure 12.4). If HMA is not available, an appropriate cold mix,
specialty mix, or proprietary mix should be used. If the patch is more than
6 inches (150 millimeters) deep, the backfill should be placed in layers and
each layer compacted thoroughly. Proper compaction is a major factor in pro-
ducing a permanent patch. A vibratory-plate compactor is excellent for small
patches. A roller may be more practical for large areas. The repair is then com-
pleted by placing the HMA surface layer and compacting it flush with the sur-
