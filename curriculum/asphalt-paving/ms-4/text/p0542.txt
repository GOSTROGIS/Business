The PWL is calculated using the sample mean, the sample variation, and
the number of samples tested. The bottom line is that if contractors and pro-
ducers can get the average values close to the target and produce and place
mixtures with good consistency (ow variation), then the result will be a high
percentage of material within the specification limits (high PWL).

Some agencies use percent defects (PD) instead of PWL, but the statistical
concepts are essentially the same. The PD is generally 100-PWL.

Specification Limits on Quality Characteristics

Specifications establish upper and lower limits for each of the quality
characteristics that will be measured. These are defined as the upper spec-
ification limit (USL) and the lower specification limit (LSL). For example, if
the asphalt content specifications set limits at 5.1 percent to 5.9 percent,
then 5.1 percent is the lower specification limit and 5.9 percent is the
upper specification limit.

The state of Florida studied testing and material variability on their HMA
projects and established upper and lower specification limits for a variety
of test parameters. Table 10.8 presents a simplified example of specifica-
tion limits applied to different quality characteristics based on the general
findings of the Florida study.

The examples of limits presented in Table 10.8 were derived from pub-
lished limits from the Florida Department of Transportation based on
variability of their statistically controlled production after it had been
implemented for several years. Each agency or owner should establish
their own limits based on their own expected variability of materials.
Projects with low production, reduced QA/QC efforts, or with contractors
and agencies inexperienced in PWL projects may necessitate wider limits.
