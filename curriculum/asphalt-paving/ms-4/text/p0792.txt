Pavements for test tracks and racetracks are constructed in all sorts of
climate conditions, and these conditions must be considered during pave-
ment design and construction. These tracks provide very wide, paved sur-
faces, which make them susceptible to thermal cracking. The pavement is
subjected not to heavy loads but to the scrubbing action of the side thrust
of the tire as the vehicles go through the turns of the track. Therefore,
track pavements need to be stable and durable.

Drainage is another important factor. The pavement design should
include a drainage layer within the pavement structure. Special attention
must be given to the areas behind the crash wall and under the stands.
Any water from these areas must be intercepted.

15.10.2 Materials Requirements

Binders

The selected binder should be at least two PG grades higher than that
commonly used for the area where the track will be constructed. The
binder should have a minimum softening point of 180°F (82°C).

The selected binder should be two PG grades higher on the high-
temperature side and can be one grade less to minimize thermal cracking;
the low-temperature grade should be one or two grades lower than that
usually selected, compared with a normal highway binder grade in that
climate. For instance, instead of using a PG 64-22, a PG 76-28 or PG 82-28
should be considered.

Binders for the underlying layer of the pavement structure can be the
same unmodified binder as would be selected by the local DOT for that
region of the state where the track is located. ,

Aggregates

The aggregates used in track surfacing must be a hard, durable material
that provides good surface friction. Granite and slag make good aggre-
gates; however, other sources of hard, non-polishing, durable aggregates
can be used. Very hard, angular aggregates should be avoided because
they tend to shred rubber from tires, resulting in slower races with more
frequent pitstops for tire changes.

The same aggregates used for highway construction can be used for the
underlying layers of the pavement structure.

Mix Design

Mix designers should consider high stability and high density as their main
objectives. Stone matrix asphalt mixes with their high binder content and
thicker mortar should be considered when the local paving industry has
experience using this type of mix regularly. The Indianapolis Speedway used
an SMA mix.
