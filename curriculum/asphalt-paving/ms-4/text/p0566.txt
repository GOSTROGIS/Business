Most modern pavement-profiling devices measure vertical acceleration
in response to the road and provide a pavement profile. Among the instru-

of reference and sensors (laser, acoustic, or infrared) to measure the devia-

Testing Approach

For acceptance testing on new construction, profilographs have been
used in the past. Today, more agencies are using IRI for acceptance pur-
poses. Profilographs produce a trace of the pavement surface, which is
then compared with acceptable limits established by the agency. More
information on the standard test method and equipment used to measure
the pavement profile is available in ASTM E 1274, “Standard Test Method
for Measuring Pavement Roughness Using a Profilograph.”

Surface Friction

Adequate skid resistance, or surface friction, is an important functional
characteristic that influences directional control and stopping ability on
a wet or dry pavement. Most agencies become aware of surface friction
problems during inclement weather, when the potential for hydroplaning
(in which the vehicle tire has lost contact with the pavement surface and
is actually riding on a thin film of water) is high. However, safety can also
be compromised on dry pavement surfaces where surface friction is low.

Surface friction is a measure of the resistance provided by the pavement
surface to the sliding of tires when they are prevented from rotating. In
addition to pavement surface characteristics, surface friction can also be
influenced by factors such as tire wear, vehicle speed, tire pressure, and
the presence of water.

Surface friction information is typically collected as part of a safety
program to reduce crash potential. However, agencies are beginning to
incorporate surface friction measures into their pavement management
systems to help identify areas that might benefit from treatments that
restore pavement surface friction characteristics.
