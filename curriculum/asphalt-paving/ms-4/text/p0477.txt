9.5 Troubleshooting

This section is not meant to be an exhaustive summary of all issues
relative to compaction. Rather, it will cover the following problem areas:
pickup, low density, erratic density, and roller marks. A key to successful
compaction is the use of an experienced quality-control technician with
a density gauge and infrared thermometer. With these two tools and the
authority to work with the roller operators to adjust the rolling pattern as
needed, a quality-control technician is a major asset to a paving operation.

9.5.1 Pickup

Mix pickup leaves scars on the mat and is not aesthetically pleasing.
Pickup is defined as mix sticking to the drums or tires of the roller.
* Pick up on steel drums is typically due to a lack of water-spray sys-
tems and scrapers/pads on the equipment.
e Pick up on pneumatic-tire rollers is typically caused by tires that are
too cold, are not properly inflated, or lack pads/scrapers.

9.5.2 Low Density

In brief, if the target density is not being achieved, the operation needs to
be adjusted. Merely speeding up the rollers is counterproductive and will actu-
ally lower density. In fact, slowing the paving and rolling operation should be
among the first things to consider, along with the following:

° Establish a new test strip to evaluate the paving-and-rolling process.

e Check the temperature of the mix upon delivery and ensure that it is
adequate to achieve density and uniformity between loads.

* Check the calibration of the density gauge against a core to ensure
that the gauge is properly calibrated.

° Make sure the paver has not increased speed because the rollers will
speed up to catch up.

° Ensure that the rollers have been and continue to be compacting
using the established rolling pattern at the proper speed, frequency,
and amplitude.

° Check the environmental conditions to determine whether something
significant has changed (air temperature, cloud cover, wind speed,
shade) because this can affect the time available for compaction.

° Consider shortening the rolling zone.

Increase the number of coverages and recheck density.
