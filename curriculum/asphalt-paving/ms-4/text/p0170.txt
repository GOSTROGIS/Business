In specifications for asphalt mixtures, the gradation requirements for
the aggregate structure need to be established for the appropriate maxi-
mum (and the nominal maximum) size of aggregate and the type of aggre-
gate gradation. Maximum and nominal maximum sizes set the upper end
of the grading band, and the grading type influences the band or range of
maximum and minimum values for each identified sieve size. Examples of
common aggregate sizes and their corresponding gradation specifications
are contained in Table 3.6.

Composite-Aggregate Size Fractions
The composite-aggregate gradation is usually considered to have three
major fractions: coarse aggregate, fine aggregate, and mineral filler. For
many years, the dividing size between coarse and fine aggregate was the
No. 8 (2.36 millimeters) sieve, but many specifications today stipulate the
No. 4 (4.75 millimeters) sieve as the dividing size. The separation between
fine aggregate and mineral filler has been and remains at the No. 200
(0.075 millimeter) sieve. Thus, the fractions are broken down this way:
° Coarse aggregate is the portion of material retained on the No. 4
(4.75 millimeters) sieve.
e Fine aggregate is the portion of material passing the No. 4 (4.75 mil-
limeters) sieve and retained on the No. 200 (0.075 millimeter) sieve.
° Mineral filler is the portion passing the No. 200 (0.075 millimeter) sieve.
