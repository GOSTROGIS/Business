To help ensure the reliability of a pavement distress survey and to minimize
variations in survey results between raters, survey crews should follow the
established survey procedures as closely as possible. Detailed distress surveys
require raters to correctly differentiate between distress types and to use pre-
scribed definitions outlined in a distress identification manual to distinguish
between different severity levels. Distress identification manuals also provide
guidance on how to measure different types of distress and often provide tips
for recording a distress that is difficult to discern in the field.

Before beginning a pavement distress survey, each rater should be
trained in accordance with the distress identification procedures to mini-
mize discrepancies in the field. In addition to being able to identify each
distress type included in the survey procedures, the raters should be famil-
iar enough with the procedures to know whether all severities associated
with a particular distress should be collected or whether only the highest
severity is recorded. Other details, such as how distress in a patched area
should be handled, ought to be discussed before the start of the survey.
These considerations are important for maintaining consistency in the
survey data since raters may differ in what they consider a “good” road.
