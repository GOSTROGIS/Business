Asphalt-rubber is defined by the (ASTM) D8 as: “a blend of asphalt
cement, reclaimed tire rubber, and certain additives in which the rubber
component is at least 15 percent by weight of the total blend and has
reacted in the hot asphalt cement sufficiently to cause swelling of the
rubber particles.” Information about the production and qualities of
asphalt-rubber can be found in Chapter 2, “Petroleum Asphalt,” and from
the Rubber Pavements Association (www.rubberpavements.org).

Asphalt-rubber is viscous and includes a dispersion of particulates that
require application at higher rates and temperatures than other asphalt
binders. Specially modified distributors that have larger and more pow-
erful circulating pumps, larger heating systems, and larger spray nozzle
openings are used.

Construction Process

The chip-seal construction procedures discussed in Chapter 12, “Asphalt
in Pavement Maintenance” also apply to the construction of a SAMI. The hot
rubber modified asphalt is sprayed with a properly calibrated distributor. It
is spray-applied on an existing pavement at the rate of 0.6-0.8 gallon/yard?
(2.7-3.6 litre/meter’). The binders should be placed when the air tempera-
ture is above 70°F (21°C) and rising and the pavement surface is dry.

The liquid membrane is then covered with %-inch (4.75 millimeter) or
%4-inch (12.5 millimeters) one-size aggregate at the rate of 30-35 pounds/
yard’. As with any hot-applied chip seal, it is imperative to use clean, dry
cover stone to promote adhesion between the binder and aggregates. The
cover stone can be precoated with 0.5-1.5 percent by mass residual asphalt
to improve adhesion by removing the surface dust and keeping moisture
from being absorbed into the chips. Alternately, some agencies require that
the chips be delivered hot to the project. Table 13.3 provides the aggregate
gradation for the construction of a SAMI.
