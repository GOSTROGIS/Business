In order to determine the amount of binder in the RAP and to obtain
uncoated aggregates for further testing, it is necessary to extract the binder
from the aggregates. This can be accomplished by either burning off the
asphalt in a binder ignition oven or by extracting the asphalt using a
solvent in a centrifuge, vacuum, or reflux extractor. With all the methods,
the asphalt content of the RAP can be determined, but if testing is to be
performed on the extracted binder, one of the solvent extraction methods
must be used.

With the ignition method, caution must be exercised when evaluating
the results for asphalt content and aggregate gradation because some
aggregates or constituents within the aggregates degrade in the intense
heat and airflow in ignition ovens. The degradation may be in the form
of material loss and/or particle breakup into smaller particles. Correc-
tion factors for asphalt content and, if necessary, for gradation are usually
determined at the testing-lab on control mixes containing virgin aggre-
gates and binder. Without having samples of the original virgin aggregate,
determining an ignition of a correction factor for the RAP is not feasible.
Hence, a correction factor for the aggregate may have to be estimated.
Experience with aggregates from the surrounding areas and with HMA
mixes produced with those aggregates can provide guidance for choosing
correction-factor estimates or for determining whether the ignition oven is
even an appropriate method to use for the area.
