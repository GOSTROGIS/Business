Miscellaneous Asphalt Uses

Some types of asphalt construction require special consideration of
either the construction methods or the composition of the asphalt mix-
ture. Asphalt as an engineering material is versatile and, when designed
properly, is ideal for many forms of paving and other applications.

15.7.1 Bridge-Deck Paving

General

Asphalt surfaces are desirable for bridge decks because they are not
affected by salts used for de-icing, and if properly constructed they may
waterproof the bridge floor, thus preventing water and salts from damag-
ing it. Asphalt surfaces can also be highly skid-resistant, providing a safe
bridge deck.

Asphalt Mixes

Dense-graded asphalt mixtures are recommended for leveling and inter-
mediate courses. In areas where chemicals are expected to be used to
de-ice the pavement, aggregates that do not react with de-icing chemicals
should be selected. A lightweight aggregate may be desirable to reduce
the weight of the asphalt pavement on the bridge.

Both dense-graded and stone matrix asphalt HMA mixtures have been
used for bridge decks. In many cases on special projects, very stiff polymer-
modified asphalt binders (up to PG 82-22) have been used to prevent
rutting or other forms of permanent deformation in that HMA layer. Also
specially designed asphalt waterproofing membranes have been placed on
the deck before the HMA layer is placed.

15.7.2 Construction Methods

The surface to be paved, whether it is a PCC or a corrugated steel bridge
deck, must be cleaned of all loose and foreign matter. Power brooms and
compressed air tools may be necessary to accomplish this task. The sur-
face of the PCC deck is then given a tack coat of SS-Ih or CSS-1h asphalt
emulsion diluted with an equal amount of water and applied at a rate of
0.05-0.20 gallons per square yard (0.2-0.9 liter per square meter).

After mill scale is removed, steel decks usually require painting with a
heavy asphalt paint. Vertical surfaces are also painted to a depth greater
than the thickness of the subsequent paving. The tack coat is scrubbed in,
thus providing a continuous asphalt film with no holes in the coating.
