10.4 Sampling and Testing
Plan for Quality Control

Some form of quality-control (or process control) is provided during
production of all HMA. As an example, consider a plant operator who
is using a temperature gauge to measure the temperature of the heated
aggregate before batching. The batch-plant operator will discard the initial
aggregate that is not up to temperature, based on the results of these tem-
perature readings. When the aggregate reaches the target temperature, the
operator will proceed with preparing the first batch of HMA. This example
demonstrates how the operator uses data to control and adjust the batch-
ing process. The temperature reading is a process control test.

There are two key aspects of true quality-control testing. The first is mea-
suring specific characteristics and inspecting appropriate activities that
affect the quality of the finished product. The second aspect is providing
the results of those measurements and inspections in a meaningful way and
within a time frame that allows adjustments to the production process. The
subsequent adjustments or corrective action can thus prevent appreciable
non-complying materials from being incorporated into the finished work.

The quantity of quality-control data and the level of control vary greatly
from one project to another. Some project specifications require the con-
tractor to exercise extensive control and mandate specific tests and test
frequencies. Other specifications allow the contractor or the supplier to
determine the level of testing based on their own judgment of what data
is needed to adequately control the production operation.

10.4.1 Selection of Sampling Methods and
Sample Frequency

Sampling methods and sample frequencies used for quality-control
are sometimes established by the project specifications and sometimes
selected by the contractor/supplier. Many pavement owners or agencies
dictate sampling methods and give minimum sample frequencies. The
contractor, of course, is free to perform any additional testing he deems
appropriate.

Methods and locations of sampling can have significant influence on the
outcome of the parameters being measured for quality-control. Sampling,
therefore, is an important aspect of the overall quality-control program.
