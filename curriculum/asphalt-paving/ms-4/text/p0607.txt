12.2 Patching

Most pavements require patching at some time. If holes do not occur
from traffic loading and natural causes, then service cuts and trenches
produce them. The kind of defects requiring patching vary from cracked
areas and shallow abrasions to deep holes.

Patching requires skill and close supervision. Prompt repair of small
breaks helps to control expenditures because once an area is broken and
water enters the subgrade, a large failure will result.

42.2.1 Materials

Hot Mix Asphalt

HMA mixes make long-lasting patches that rapidly develop stability. They
should be used whenever it is practical and economical. See Chapter 4, “Hot Mix
Asphalt (HMA) Design,” and Chapter 7, “Manufacture of Hot Mix Asphalt.”

Cold Mixes

Other maintenance materials are cold-plant asphalt mixtures employing
emulsified asphalt for immediate use or for short-term stockpiling. Slow-
curing cutback asphalt or emulsion containing solvent may be used for
stockpiled patching mixes. When using cutback, be sure to check local envi-
ronmental regulations. See Chapter 15, “Special Uses of Asphalt and HMA.”

Proprietary Mixes

Producers have developed proprietary mixes, with special blends of
aggregate and specialized binders, for specific purposes. As a rule, these
mixes work well when used for their intended purpose. Information on
these mixes can be found in trade magazine advertisements.

Specialty Mixes (Innovative State Highway Mixes)

Many state Department of Transportation (DOT) maintenance divisions
have innovative mixes they have developed for pothole patching, spalled
concrete joints, skin patching, etc. The DOTs are a good source of informa-
tion about these specialty mixes.

In general, it is prudent to check with the local DOT to determine what
materials are recommended, especially regarding proprietary mixes.
