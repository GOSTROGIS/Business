Rutting

A rut is a surface depression in the wheelpaths that may also have trans-
verse displacement along the sides of the rut. Rutting is caused by consoli-
dation or lateral movement of any of the pavement layers or the subgrade
under traffic. It may be caused by insufficient design thickness, lack of
compaction, weaknesses in the pavement layers due to moisture infiltra-
tion, or weak asphalt mixtures. Rutting is illustrated in Figure 11.17.

Settlement or Grade Depressions

Depressions and settlements are localized pavement surface areas with
elevations lower than the surrounding pavement area. Minor depressions are
often not noticed until after a rain when water ponds in the depression, caus-
ing a “birdbath” that can be hazardous to motorists. In dry weather, depres-
sions can be observed where staining is present. Depressions may be caused
by settlement or failure in the lower pavement layers or by improper con-
struction techniques. An example of a depression is shown in Figure 11.18.

Upheaval or Swell
An upheaval or swell is a localized upward displacement in a pavement
due to swelling of the subgrade or some portion of the pavement structure.
