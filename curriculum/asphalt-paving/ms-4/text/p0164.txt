The procedure for determining the effective specific gravity of the
combined aggregate in a mix actually takes place during the laboratory
work for the HMA mix design. Data obtained from determining the maxi-
mum specific gravity of asphalt mixtures (ASTM D 2041) is used in calcu-
lating effective specific gravity (see Chapter 4). This important property
affects the optimum binder content in a mix and the determination of the
percentage of air voids in the compacted mix.

Freeze-Thaw/Weathering Resistance

Freeze-thaw resistance is an aggregate’s ability to resist degradation
caused by cycles of freezing and thawing when the aggregate is in a satu-
rated condition. Internal expansive forces develop in the pores of the
aggregate each time the absorbed water transforms into ice. Weathering
resistance can include the freeze-thaw resistance factor, but it also refers to
the aggregates’ ability to withstand the volume changes and degradation
that occur with cyclic wetting and drying. An aggregate’s ability to resist
the effects of wetting and drying cycles and freezing and thawing cycles
can also be called the aggregate’s durability or soundness property.

In moist and freezing climates, aggregates must exhibit a certain level
of durability or soundness to resist deterioration in stockpiles in the
interim stage (after aggregate processing and before HMA production).
However, once the aggregates are introduced into HMA production, the
need for weathering durability is debatable. Moisture within the aggre-
gates is driven out in the dryer, and the aggregates receive a coating of
impermeable asphalt binder.

The test most commonly used to determine an aggregate’s durability
is the sulfate soundness test—AASHTO T 104 (ASTM C 88). In this test,
an aggregate sample is sieved into specified size fractions. The separated
fractions are then immersed for 16 to 18 hours in a saturated solution
of either sodium or magnesium sulfate for absorption of the solution
into the aggregate pores. The samples are then removed from the solu-
tion, allowed to drain for a short period, and then dried in an oven until
they reach constant mass. The samples are then cooled to room tempera-
ture, and the cycle of immersion and drying is repeated. Typically, five
cycles are specified. At the end of the required number of cycles, each
size fraction sample is washed to remove the salts, and then dried and
sieved over the specified screen. The mass loss of each fraction sample is
determined and expressed as a percentage loss. A weighted loss for the
composite aggregate is calculated based on the original aggregate grada-
tion. The applicability of this test to durability is given in ASTM C 88: “The
internal expansive force, derived from the rehydration of the salt upon
re-immersion, simulates the expansion of water on freezing.”

Typical standards and agency specifications set maximum allowable loss
values in the 10-20 percent range for test durations of five cycles.
