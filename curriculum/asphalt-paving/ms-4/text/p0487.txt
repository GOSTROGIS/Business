Lot sizes are normally established by project specifications. It is important
to have lot sizes defined in advance of construction. The size and method
of measurement of a lot will be used to determine locations and quantities
of quality-control and quality assurance tests. The lot will also be used to
define what portion of the work is represented by a given set of test results.
The limits of the lot are critical if corrective action or removal is required.

10.2.2 Random Sampling

Use of random sampling techniques is an important part of obtaining un-
biased representative samples. Random sampling methods can be simple to
use if good forms and/or other tools are used. With widespread availabil-
ity of personal computers, simple spreadsheet documents can be used to
easily generate sample locations and make implementation less daunting.

Random sampling can be applied to any of the sampling interval methods
(time, quantity, and length). Random sampling is achieved by taking the
number of samples to be taken that day and distributing the times, quantities,
or locations selected for each sample in a random way. For example, assume
that the production of 5,000 tons (4,536 tonnes) of material is planned and
a sample frequency for every 1,000 tons (907 tonnes) has been selected.
Nonrandom techniques may lead planners to take the first sample at
about 1,000 tons (907 tonnes) and the second sample at about 2,000 tons
(1,814 tonnes) and so on until all five of the samples for that day are
collected. Using random sampling, a random number (numbers between
0 and 1 obtained from a chart) would be assigned to each of the five
samples. Each of the random numbers is multiplied by the total ton-
nage (5,000 or 4,536) to determine at what point in production to take
the sample. For example, if the random number for one of the samples
is 0.43218, then that sample would be taken when the day’s accumula-
tive tonnage reaches 2,160 tons or 1,959 tons (5,000 X 0.43218 = 2,160)
(4,536 = 0.43218 X 1,960).

An accurate estimate of the day’s production is needed to determine
random sample locations. In some cases, lots with different sizes may have
a different number of tests. For example, if the project estimate for produc-
tion is 6,520 feet (1,978 meters), and the quality-control plan establishes
the sample frequency for coring at every 1,000 feet (305 meters), then that
lot will contain seven core locations.
