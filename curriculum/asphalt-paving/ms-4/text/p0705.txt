The physical properties of the asphalt pavement are affected by the
ambient temperature. If the pavement is cool, the asphalt layer is stiff and
brittle and can be pulverized into small pieces very easily (see Figure 13.27).
Increasing temperatures cause the pavement to become more plastic, and
slabbing (asphalt lifting or breaking off in large pieces) can occur. The most
efficient temperature range for sizing the reclaimed material is 50°-90°F
(10°-30°C).

If additional pulverization or remixing is required for uniformity, the
reclaimed material should be lightly compacted and reshaped. This
process accurately controls the mixing depth. The rear wheels of the
reclaimer compact or reduce the thickness of the fluffed-up material dur-
ing pulverization. With light compaction and reshaping, the reclaimed
material will have uniform thickness, which will allow better control of
the treatment depth.

When an asphalt emulsion is added to the reclaimed material, a nurse
truck is connected to the reclaimer (see Figure 13.28). The reclaimer is
equipped with a meter capable of controlling the rate of flow and the total
amount of asphalt emulsion being added to the reclaimed material. The
meter should be linked to the forward speed of the reclaimer so that the
amount of asphalt emulsion added will change according to the opera-
tional speed of the reclaimer. If asphalt emulsion is used, some agencies
add lime or cement to improve the early strength of the mix and to speed
up the curing process.
