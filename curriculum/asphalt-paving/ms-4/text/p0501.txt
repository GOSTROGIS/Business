10.3.4 Maximum Theoretical Specific
Gravity

Testing for maximum theoretical specific gravity of HMA mixtures
(sometimes called the Rice test) is accomplished by using a vacuum to
saturate the test specimen in water. The vacuum causes the water to boil
and drives water into any available pore spaces that exist between coated
particles. The saturated specimen is then weighed in air and in water and
specific gravity values are calculated. The result is the specific gravity of
the mix at zero air voids.

Recent changes to the test procedure have added more restrictions on
the vacuum levels applied to the specimen and have introduced vibrating
frames to produce consistent levels of agitation for each specimen. Use of
these modern procedures will reduce the variability between laboratories
on a given project.

‘Test procedures for maximum theoretical specific gravity are AASHTO
T 209 or ASTM D 2041.

The test values of maximum theoretical specific gravity are used in con--
junction with other tests. It is the basis of the determination of air voids when
compared with laboratory specific gravities and can be a basis for compaction
values when compared with cores sampled from completed pavements.
