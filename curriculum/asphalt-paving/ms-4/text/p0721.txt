14.3 Fractured Slab Techniques

Two procedures have been developed to fracture PCC slabs, thereby
reducing the stresses and deformations that occur in an asphalt overlay—
crack/break-and-seat and rubblization. The crack/break-and-seat procedure
reduces the effective size of the PCC slabs and seats them firmly onto the
underlying layers with heavy rollers. The rubblization process eliminates
slab action by reducing the PCCP to aggregate-size pieces, resulting in a
layer that functions in a similar way to an aggregate base course.

14.3.1 Crack/Break and Seat

Crack/break and seat minimizes joint and crack movement by shortening
the effective slab length and firmly seating the broken PCC pieces onto the
underlying layer (see Figure 14.7). The PCC is broken using a drop hammer,
and then seated with a large, ballasted rubber-tire roller. The intent is to create
slabs that are small enough to reduce the movement at the cracks in the PCC
layer that were caused by low temperatures and daily temperature variation,
while still being large enough to develop flexure under loading.

Cracking and seating is used on JCPC pavement, while breaking and seat-
ing is used when the PCC pavement is JPCP. The broken slabs are seated
to re-establish firm support between the underlying layers and the slabs,
reducing the amount of differential vertical movement under traffic.
