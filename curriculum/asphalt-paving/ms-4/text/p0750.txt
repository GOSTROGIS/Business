During mixing, attention should be paid to the blade’s position. The
blade should be positioned so that a complete rolling action of the mate-
rial is obtained as the windrow is manipulated. Also during mixing, care
must be taken not to incorporate extra material into the windrow and, at
the same time, not to lose any material out of the windrow. After mixing
and aeration have been completed, the windrow is placed to one side of
the area to be surfaced in preparation for spreading.

Rotary Mixing

A rotary-type mixer consists of a mobile mixing chamber mounted on a
self-propelled machine (see Figure 15.3). The chamber is open at the bot-
tom and has widths ranging from 6 to 10 feet (1.8 to 3.0 meters). Inside the
chamber are one or more transverse rotating drums that contain attached
carbide-cutting teeth. This type of equipment is particularly useful for
mixed-in-place cold recycling when old hot or cold mix surfaces are to
be incorporated into the mix. The blades or cutting teeth serve a twofold
purpose—cutting the in-place material to a specified depth and mixing it
with asphalt. As the machine moves forward, it strikes off the freshly mixed
material to a predetermined level. The asphalt can be introduced in one
of two ways. In some mixers, the asphalt comes through a spray bar that
