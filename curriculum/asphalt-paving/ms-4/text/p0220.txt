3. Superpave HMA Mix
Design System

4.3.1 Introduction

In 1987, a five-year FHWA study to improve the procedures for the
performance of HMA pavements was conducted. The result was the
Superpave (SUPerior PERforming Asphalt PAVEments) system. This sys-
tem was developed to give pavement engineers and contractors the tools
they needed to improve the performance of HMA pavements subjected
to extremes of traffic loading and temperature. The Superpave system as
implemented consisted of two interrelated elements: an asphalt binder
specification (discussed in Chapter 2, “Petroleum Asphalts”) and a mix
design and analysis system. Since the implementation of Superpave in the
mid-1990s, research has continued on the development of performance
models. The status of that research is summarized in Section 4.8 in this
chapter. The following summarizes the Superpave mix design system:

The Superpave mix design procedure consists of:

1. Selecting asphalt and aggregate materials that meet the design
criteria
2. Developing several aggregate trial blends that will meet the
Superpave gradation requirements

3. Blending the asphalt binder with the aggregate trial blends and
short-term oven aging of the mix

4. Compacting the specimens

5. Analyzing the volumetric properties of the mixture (using the
procedures described in Section 4.2)

6. Selecting the best aggregate blend as the design aggregate struc-

ture based on the desired performance criteria for the mixture

A detailed discussion is provided in the Asphalt Institute’s SP-2, Superpave
Mix Design. The detailed procedures for conducting the calculations associ-
ated with Superpave mix design are included in SP-2 and the Mix Design
Program, SW-2. This program is written in strict conformance with the SP-2
manual. It is an Excel-based program that allows users to modify the pro-
gram components to their needs.

4.3.2 Materials Selection

Asphalt Binder

The asphalt binder to be used in the mix is chosen based on environ-
mental and traffic loadings using the procedures described in Chapter 2,
“Petroleum Asphalts.”
