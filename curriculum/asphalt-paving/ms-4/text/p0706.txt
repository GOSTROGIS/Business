If foamed asphalt is used, the reclaimer must be attached to a nurse truck
and be equipped with a computerized onboard foam-generating system. It
must also have a storage tank for the water used in the foaming process,
and a system for injecting the foamed asphalt into the reclaimed material.
The system should be linked to the forward speed of the reclaimer and the
volume of the reclaimed material being processed.

13.7.4 Shaping and Compaction
Procedures

A motor grader is used to move and place the reclaimed material to
the desired longitudinal grade and cross-slope (see Figure 13.29). The
amount of motor grader work required to place the reclaimed material
depends on the original shape of the roadway, the specified final shape of
the roadway, and the type of wearing surface that will be used. The final
grade should be similar to that required of an untreated base course. The
smoothness of the final HMA surface is directly related to the smoothness
of the pulverized and reclaimed layer. To ensure a smooth final surface,
tight blading or trimming the reclaimed surface may be necessary.

The reclaimed material is generally not at the optimum moisture content
for compaction. Either aeration will be required to dry the reclaimed mate-
rial, or moisture will have to be added to aid compaction. A water truck can
be used as a supply truck to the reclaimer’s onboard liquid additive, or it can
be used to add water to the reclaimed material through a spray bar.
