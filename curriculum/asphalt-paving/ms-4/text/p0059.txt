Atmospheric distillation is used to separate various “overhead” crude
fractions and a non-boiling component known as an atmospheric residue.
Crude flows continuously through a furnace that heats the oil and strips
out any light materials present. The heated crude flows through an
atmospheric tower where light components flash to the upper reaches of
the tower and subsequent lower boiling components reach lower sections
of the tower. Different boiling components are collected as they condense
