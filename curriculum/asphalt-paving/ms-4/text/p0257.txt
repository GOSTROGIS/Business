4.8.2 Open-Graded Friction Course

Description and Usage

Open-Graded Friction Courses (OGFC) provide an open mix with permeable
voids that provide drainage on the surface of an HMA pavement under wet
conditions. The water drains vertically through the OGFC to an impermeable,
underlying layer and then laterally to the day-lighted edge of the OGFC. In
addition to minimizing hydroplaning and providing high friction resistance on
wet pavements, an OGFC layer will also provide the following advantages:

° Reduce splash and spray (see Figure 4.28)

° Reduce the potential for hydroplaning

° Enhance visibility of pavement markings

° Reduce nighttime surface glare in wet weather

° Reduce tire-pavement noise
While open-graded mixes provide many advantages, they have the following
limitations:

¢ Snow- and ice-control procedures may need adjustment when they

are used,

° They may ravel and shove when used at intersections, locations with

heavy turning movements, and airfield ramp terminals.

° They may require special patching and rehabilitation techniques to

maintain adequate drainage.

Open-graded mixes have been used throughout the United States since
1944. In many states they have been used extensively for many years. In
the early 1970s, many states experienced problems with OGFC layers.
These problems were raveling, delamination, loss of permeability after
a few years of service, and stripping of the underlying layers. These
problems have been addressed by one or more of the following: use of
