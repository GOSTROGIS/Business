The dust fraction of crushed aggregates, including naturally occurring
rocks (such as limestone) and synthetic aggregates (such as slag), can be
used as mineral filler. Many times, the fine aggregate may be processed
further in grinders or mills to create mineral filler.

Hydrated lime is a dry powder that comes from the specialized pro-
cessing of limestone. Limestone is crushed and calcinated (controlled time
and temperature heating) at temperatures above 1650°F (900°C) to create
quicklime. The quicklime is in turn treated with sufficient water to satisfy
its chemical affinity for water, creating hydrated lime. Hydrated lime is
also used as an antistrip additive. See “Hydrated Lime” in this chapter.

Hydraulic cements are fine powders that chemically react with water
creating a binder that sets and hardens into a stone-like material. The most
commonly available hydraulic cement is Portland cement.

Fly ash is the fine-grain, powdery particulate material that is produced
from the burning of pulverized coal in a coal-fired boiler or furnace and
collected from the flue gas. Fly ash has been used as a substitute mineral
filler in HMA mixtures for many years. Most fly ash sources are capa-
ble of meeting the gradation requirements and other pertinent physical
(nonplastic) and chemical (organic content) requirements of mineral filler
specifications. It is possible that some sources of fly ash that have a high
lime (CaO) content may also be useful as antistripping agents in HMA
mixes. See “Fly Ash” in this chapter.

Loess comes from sedimentary, unconsolidated deposits of windblown,
silty material. This fine material (cock flour) was created by the grinding
of rocks during glacial activity. Loess is typically thought of as the silt that
makes up the fertile topsoil in some parts of the world.

Kiln dusts are fine by-products of PCC and lime high-temperature
rotary-kiln production operations that are captured in the air pollution
control dust collection system (e.g., cyclones, electrostatic precipitators,
and baghouses). Both fresh Cement Kiln Dust (CKD) and Lime Kiln Dust
(LKD) can be used as mineral fillers in HMA mixtures. The lime compo-
nents of the CKD and LKD can assist in promoting stripping resistance.
