Sampling ;

Because of the resources required to conduct a pavement condition survey
of an entire pavement network, some agencies utilize sampling techniques
to determine the condition of each pavement section. Agencies that use this
approach typically divide each pavement section into inspection units of a
predetermined size, such as 2,500 square feet for asphalt-surfaced roads.
A representative number of inspection units (or samples) are selected using
statistical analyses. In general, a higher degree of sampling is required when
a higher level of confidence is required or when there is considerable vari-
ability in the overall pavement condition.

Ride Quality

The longitudinal profile of a road provides an indication of the road’s
tide quality. Early measures of ride quality were based on the subjective
ratings of passenger comfort within a vehicle traveling at posted speeds.
Since that time, equipment has been developed to measure ride quality,
which is typically reported in terms of an International Roughness Index
(IRD). Ride quality, which is also referred to as roughness or smoothness,
is a popular measure of a road’s functional condition because it represents
a condition measure that the public recognizes. The measurement of ride
quality is common on highways and other high-speed facilities.
