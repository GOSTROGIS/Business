Approaches Used

Three approaches are generally used to prioritize pavement needs: ranking,
benefit/cost analysis, and optimization. Each technique is discussed here in
more detail in increasing order of sophistication.

Ranking

The simplest method of prioritizing project needs is to rank them according
to agency-specified criteria. In most agencies that use a ranking technique,
pavement needs are ranked from those in worst condition to those in the best
condition. Along with the ranked listing are treatment costs so that projects can
be selected from the ranked list until funding is depleted. In some instances,
traffic volumes are used to supplement the ranked list so that higher volume
facilities are selected for the program before facilities with the same pavement
condition but with lower traffic volumes. Since most agencies using a ranking
technique are doing so to fix the worst roads first, this approach often results
in the least cost-effective strategy for managing a pavement network. This is
especially true because alternative strategies (such as preventive maintenance
strategies) are not typically incorporated into the ranked list.

Benefit/Cost Analysis

projects and treatments is a multi-year benefit/cost analysis. Under this
