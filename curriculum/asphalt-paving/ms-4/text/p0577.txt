Block Cracking

Block cracks are interconnected cracks that form a series of large, rectangular
pieces on the pavement surface that are typically greater than one foot
(0.3 meter) in each direction. Block cracking is usually found when the HMA
surface has hardened and the daily temperature cycles cause shrinkage in
the HMA layer; however, it may also be caused by volume changes in a
fine-aggregate HMA mix containing low-penetration asphalt and adsorptive
aggregates. Low traffic levels can aggravate this latter situation because the
restorative working properties of asphalt require frequent traffic applications.

Block cracking typically covers a large area of pavement, including areas
without traffic. The size of the blocks and the presence of block cracking
in nontrafficked areas help to differentiate block cracking from alligator
cracking in the field. An example is provided in Figure 11.10.

Edge Cracking

Longitudinal cracks within one to two feet of the outer edge of a
pavement are referred to as edge cracks. Over time, as vehicles pass over
the weakened area, further break-up of longitudinal cracks may take
place, resulting in crescent-shaped cracks that branch to the edge of the
pavement. Edge cracking is illustrated in Figure 11.11.
