Dryers have a burner and fan fitted at one end to create a hot gas stream
that dries and heats the aggregate. Burner and dust control equipment
allow dryers with fossil-fuel burners to function very effectively from both
a particulate- and gaseous-emission standpoint.

Dryers are inclined and rotate. They function by picking up and drop-
ping aggregate material into the hot gas stream. Inside the dryer are
steel flights, or lifters. These flights pick up the aggregate material and
drop it through the hot gas stream as the dryer rotates (see Figure 7.9).
To prevent incomplete combustion and hydrocarbon contamination, the
aggregate material never passes through the burner flame. Special burner
flighting is installed in the dryer for the length of the flame. These special
flights pick up the aggregate, trap it against the outside wall of the dryer,
and carry the material over top of the flame. Gravity causes the material
to advance slightly with each rotation because the dryer is installed on
a slight incline. Retention time typically is three to five minutes but can
be longer, depending on the dryer’s size, length, and flight configura-
