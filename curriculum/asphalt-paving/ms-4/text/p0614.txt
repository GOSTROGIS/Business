If the procedure involves pavement removal, mill to a depth necessary
to remove the distressed material. Thoroughly clean the area with high-
pressure air, brooms, and/or water. After the area is clean and dry, apply a
tack coat to the edges and the bottom of the patch area.

After the tack coat has set, rapidly place the HMA over the patch area. For
surface patches, a fine-aggregate or sand mix is preferable, especially if the
patch is placed without milling the existing pavement. Without milling, the
surface patch must be feathered to zero thickness at the edge of the patch.
Compact the patch with equipment appropriate for the size of the patch.
When compacted, the patch should be level with the surrounding surface.
A fog seal should be applied to the feather-edges to reduce the possibility
of raveling.

12.2.4 Spray-Injection Patching

Spray-injection patching has existed since the early 1980s. The proce-
dure gained prominence after the Strategic Highway Research Project of
the late 1980s and early 1990s determined that it is a viable method of
repairing small pavement defects with semipermanent repairs, particularly
during inclement weather (wet and/or cold weather).

This procedure requires a specially equipped truck or trailer mounted
unit (see Figure 12.10).
