The asphalt emulsion should break and cure before placement of asphalt
concrete. Shortly after placement of the tack coat, the emulsion will break.
The color will change from brown to black, and the water will begin to
evaporate. Once the water has evaporated, the mechanical properties of
the residual asphalt will return and result in a strong adhesive bond. The
rate of this breaking-and-setting process will depend on the environmen-
tal conditions. Generally, the emulsions used for tack coats will break and
be fully cured shortly after placement. During this process, equipment
(rollers, delivery trucks, etc.) should be kept off the surface. Tack coats
leaving surplus asphalt on the surface can form a slip plane. Excessive
asphalt may also work its way up into the overlying course, which may
eventually flush asphalt to the surface. A light tack coat does no harm and
it bonds the surfaces, a necessity for good construction. Work should be
planned so that no more than the necessary tack coat for the paving shift
is placed on the surface. All traffic not essential to the work should be kept
off the tack coat.

Vertical faces of existing pavements such as curbs and gutters should also
be sprayed or painted with a uniform coating of approved tack material.
This work should be done such that exposed curb or gutter surfaces are
not stained. The use of low-penetration tack material such as an SS-1h or
CSS-1h will also reduce the amount of tracking. Tracking is the unwanted
coating or staining of adjacent pavement surfaces, both on and off the
project, by trucks or equipment tires.
