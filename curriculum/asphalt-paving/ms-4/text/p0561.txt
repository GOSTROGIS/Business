11.1.5 Role of Pavement Management in
Pavement Preservation

One cost-effective strategy that an agency may consider for preserving
its pavement network is a pavement preservation program that includes
the use of low-cost preventive maintenance treatments to preserve the
functional characteristics of a pavement in relatively good condition.

Definitions
The Federal Highway Administration’s (FHWA) Pavement Preservation
Expert Task Group defines a pavement preservation program as follows:

A program employing a network level, long-term strategy that enhances
pavement performance by using an integrated, cost-effective set of
practices that extend pavement life, improve safety and meet motorist
expectations.

These programs incorporate the use of preventive maintenance treat-
ments, which have been defined by the AASHTO’s Standing Committee on
Highways as follows:

A planned strategy of cost-effective treatments to an existing roadway
system and its appurtenances that preserves the system, retards future
deterioration, and maintains or improves the functional condition of
the system (without significantly increasing the structural capacity).

Preventive maintenance treatments are typically applied to roads in
relatively good condition before extensive amounts of deterioration are
present. Examples of preventive maintenance treatments include crack
sealing, chip seals, microsurfacing, and thin hot mix overlays.
