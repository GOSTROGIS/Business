These three characterization methods provide the user with a great deal
of control over the analysis.

For granular layers, the DAMA user is offered two methods of characterizing
resilient modulus. The user can enter the resilient modulus directly for each
month of the year. Alternatively, the user can prescribe a stress-dependent
relationship that better models frost effects or moisture variations.

The subgrade layer allows for direct entry of the resilient modulus for
each month of the year. This monthly variation allows the user to pre-
scribe up to 12 data points per year, adequate for description of a freeze-
thaw cycle.

The material property handling within the DAMA model provides the
user with great influence to shape the model to best reflect actual condi-
tions in the field.

For the pavement design curves published in MS-1, Thickness Design—
Asphalt Pavements for Highways and Streets, MS-11, and MS-23, certain
simplifying assumptions have been made regarding the material properties
in the pavement structure. MS-11 and MS-23 design curves for air-carrier
airports and heavy wheel loads are limited to full-depth asphalt pavements,
where asphalt-bound layers are used for all layers above the subgrade. For
MS-1, several commonly used pavement sections were modeled: full-depth
asphalt, HMA-over-granular base, and HMA-over-emulsified base. For pave-
ment sections that depart from these standard sections, the MS-1 design
curves are not applicable.

6.1.3 Highways, Roadways, and Streets

The Asphalt Institute method of thickness design for pavements sup-
porting highway vehicles is covered in detail in MS-1, and is one of the
methods included in SW-1.

Engineers accomplish thickness design by graphical solution of the appro-
priate pavement design curve. Selection of the appropriate design curve is
based on climate and anticipated material type (full-depth asphalt, asphalt-
over-granular base, etc). These curves represent hundreds of presolved design
problems over a wide range of input values, using the DAMA analysis tool.
The result is an efficient design tool that is applicable to everything from
interstate highways to residential streets and all roadway classifications in
between. If the pavement supports highway-type vehicles, from automobiles
to long-haul tractor semi-trailer vehicles (Figure. 6.2), then the MS-1 design
curves apply. This method has also been incorporated into SW-1.

to select among three mean annual air temperature (MAAT) levels: 45°F,
60°F, and 75°F (7°C, 15.5°C, and 24°C). These MAAT levels bracket those
found throughout much of North America and are representative of the
climates in New York, South Carolina, and Arizona, respectively. Engineers
