Slippage Cracking

Slippage cracks are easily identified by their distinctive crescent- or half-
moon-shape cracks, as illustrated in Figure 11.15. They are usually caused by
vehicular turning or stopping movements in pavements with a low-strength
surface mix or a poor bond between pavement layers. The cracks are caused
by the horizontal forces of stopping or turning vehicles, so the ends of the
curved crack point in the opposite direction of the vehicle thrust.

Distortion

Distortions in the pavement layer are a result of instability in the HMA
layer or weaknesses in the base or subgrade layers. The distortion may
appear in a number of different forms, including rutting, shoving, corruga-
tions, depressions, or upheavals. Cracking may or may not accompany the
distortion. Each of the typical types and causes of distortions are discussed
further in this section.
