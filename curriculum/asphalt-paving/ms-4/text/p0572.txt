reconstruction. An RSL of 0 indicates that a pavement section has reached the
threshold condition and no longer functions as it was originally intended.
Overall network conditions may be reported in terms of an average RSL
or by the percent of the network in different RSL categories (such as
0-2 years, 3-5 years, 5~10 years, 10-15 years, and greater than 15 years).
As with a pavement condition index, feasible maintenance and rehabilitation
treatments can be determined for each RSL category.

When reporting pavement conditions, it is important that the information
be conveyed in a manner that can be quickly understood. For this reason,
many agencies choose to report network conditions in terms of condition
categories such as good, fair, and poor. To report pavement conditions
in this manner, an agency must define each condition category in terms of
its pavement condition indexes. For example, in Figure 11.7 a PCI between
70 and 100 might represent a road in good condition, a PCI between 40 and
70 might represent a road in fair condition, and a PCI less than 40 might
represent a road in poor condition. Any number of condition categories can
be used, depending on the needs of the agency.

Forecasting Future Conditions

Once a pavement condition index has been developed, statistical tools
can be used to develop pavement performance models that reflect the rate
at which the pavements deteriorate over time. The inclusion of models that
forecast future pavement condition is one of the most important factors
differentiating a pavement management system from a pavement database.
Prediction models allow an agency to forecast future pavement condi-
tions under any number of budget scenarios or under different treatment
strategies so that the long-term impacts of these options can be more fully
understood and compared.
