Perpetual Pavement design also requires careful attention to material
selection. Hard angular aggregates must be carefully selected, processed,
and blended together to form a strong “stone-skeleton” to support intended
traffic loads. Properly selected Performance Graded asphalt binders will
improve rut-resistance at high temperatures and diminish the effect of
transverse low temperature cracking. The combined result of these mix
type and material selection strategies is a pavement structure that requires
only periodic surface renewal in response to distresses confined to the
wearing course.

It should be noted that the Perpetual Pavement design concept is separate
and distinct from the Asphalt Institute thickness design method. More detail
on this method is provided in Section 6.2.3.

6.1.2 Input Variables

Data-input requirements for the Asphalt Institute design method depends
on the procedure selected by the user. The design curves were produced
and intended for everyday use and require minimal information as input
into the procedure. DAMA, on the other hand, is intended for advanced
structural analysis of pavement structures with the requirement of more-
detailed user inputs.

With either procedure, there are four types of inputs required to
use either AI method: climate, traffic, subgrade strength, and material
properties. The following is a brief description of these input variables.

Climate

Air temperature is the climate-related user input in the AI method. Air
temperature affects the design of the pavement structure in two impor-
tant ways. First, the stiffness of HMA and other asphalt-stabilized layers is
directly dependent on the pavement temperature, which can be calculated
from air temperature. For example, high pavement temperatures result in
a reduction of the stiffness of HMA layers. As layers with lower stiffness
do not distribute loads as effectively as stiff layers, pavements at higher
temperature are more susceptible to plastic deformation caused by con-
centration of Joad than pavements that are at low temperature.

For granular base and subgrade soils, temperature determines whether
freezing and thawing conditions will occur. Frozen materials become stiff,
while thawing weakens unbound materials, particularly subgrade soils.

In DAMA, mean monthly air temperature is used to directly character-
ize the climatic variation for a specific pavement structure using location-
specific temperature data. For design-curve analyses such as MS-1, the
user is asked to select a mean annual air temperature appropriate for a
geographical location.

Traffic
The AI method is based on an analysis of individual wheel loads and the
response of the pavement system to the wheel loads.
