When a free-floating screed is used, five factors must be considered:
° The angle of attack

° The head of material

¢ The forward motion (paving speed)

° The screed weight

° HMA stability (temperature and gradation)

Angle of Attack

The angle of attack is the angle of the screed in relation to the paving
grade. The screed pivots about the screed pivot point. The angle can be
adjusted by the depth-screw assembly or by raising or lowering the screed
tow point. The depth-screw is adjusted manually by the screed operator.
Tow-point changes are generated by automatic grade and slope control.
The angle of attack creates an extrusion angle under the screed; once the
extrusion angle can support its weight, the screed is free to float at that
average depth. Pavers have a natural leveling effect, placing asphalt thin-
ner over the highs and filling in the lows of the base. Increasing the angle
of attack allows more material to flow under the screed, causing the screed
to rise until it reaches the elevation that finds equilibrium of the forces that
act upon the screed. The paver will continue to place HMA at the newly
established depth until one or more of the forces acting on the screed
is changed. Reducing the angle of attack results in less material passing
under the screed, and the screed will drop until the new depth is reached
and a new equilibrium of forces is established.

Head of Material

The head of material is the depth of material being evenly spread in
front of the screed by the auger. Controlling the head of material is the
most important factor in laying a smooth HMA pavement. The head of
material is affected by the volume of the HMA in the auger chamber. Once
the head of material has been established, it must be maintained within a
tolerance of +1 inch at all times. Starving the screed of material will result
in the screed settling and will cause a dip in the pavement. Immediately
placing a large amount of HMA in front of the screed will guarantee a
bump as the screed attempts to crawl over the excess material deposited
in its path. Ideally, the head of material should be maintained at a height
that exposes one half of the auger and not vary by more than +1 inch
(25 millimeters).

Paving Speed

Nonstop paving at a uniform forward speed results in the highest-
quality and smoothest pavements and should be the goal of every paving
crew. Changing the paving speed will change the angle of attack on the
screed, causing a corresponding change in the mat thickness. Manual adjust-
ments to offset this depth change must be made with the depth screws.
