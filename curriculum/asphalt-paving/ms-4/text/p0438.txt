Related Paving Operations

8.6.1 Hand-Spreading Operations

When the major portion of a project will be placed manually without the
aid of a paving machine, mixes should be designed for good workability.
A good mix design technician has several options for making mixes that
are easier to place manually.

Placing and spreading by hand should be done very carefully, with the
material distributed uniformly to avoid segregation of the coarse aggre-
gate. When the HMA is dumped in piles, it should be placed in a man-
ner where the shovelers have to move the least amount of material the
least distance. Sufficient space should be available so that workers are not
required to stand in the HMA.

HMA should not be broadcast or spread from shovels because segregation
will result. Rather, it should be deposited from shovels into small piles and
spread with lutes. Any part of the HMA that has formed into lumps and does
not break down easily when struck with a shovel edge should be discarded.
After the material has been placed, and before rolling starts, the surface should
be checked with straightedges, and all irregularities should be corrected. On
cool days, careful attention must be paid to the HMA temperature. Rolling
should begin as soon as possible to ensure adequate compaction.

8.6.2 Supplemental Operations and
Appurtenances

Items such as roadway widening, shoulders, superelevated curves,
curbs, ditches and revetments are considered supplemental paving opera-
tions. In some cases, such as roadway widening and shoulder construc-
tion, specially designed pieces of equipment and attachments allow the
work to be done easily and efficiently. Certain roadway appurtenances,
such as asphalt curbs, dikes, ditches, spillways, and slopes, are increas-
ingly becoming part of paving contracts. For these jobs, special paving
equipment or special HMA mixtures (or both) may be required.
