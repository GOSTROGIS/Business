Nonsurfaced Granular Roadway

If an HMA pavement is placed on an existing granular roadway, the sur-
face should be shaped similar to untreated granular bases. The granular
roadway differs primarily in that it has been used as a roadway by traf-
fic and has typically been in use for a considerable time. It is advisable,
when the granular roadway is rough and uneven, to bring the surface to
the proper line and grade by scarifying the top few inches, blending in
more aggregate material as necessary, compacting, and then priming with
asphalt. If the granular roadway has had a previous asphalt treatment
and is rough and uneven, but structurally sound, it is advisable to place a
leveling course of HMA ahead of the first layer of the surface course. Oth-
erwise, the surface may have to be recycled by scarifying the surface and
mixing it with a portion of the existing base. The engineer should inspect
the surface well in advance of placing the surface courses and determine
what grade corrections need to be made. As with untreated granular bases,
the surface should be cleaned and swept before initiating paving opera-
tions, including any leveling courses that may be needed.

8.2.2 Preparation of Existing Pavement
for Overlay

The amount of preparation needed for an existing HMA pavement
before overlay will depend on the condition and the type of distress in the
existing pavement. Portions of the pavement that have “failed” (fatigue/
alligator cracking) should be removed and replaced with full-depth HMA.
Potholes need to be properly repaired, cracks need to be sealed or filled,
and rutting areas must be removed by cold milling. If these distresses
cover more than 40 percent of the pavement area, the use of cold in situ
recycling or full-depth reclamation should be considered. The procedures
for proper pre-overlay repairs are described in Chapter 12, “Maintenance
of Asphalt Pavements,” and the procedures for cold milling, recycling, and
full-depth reclamation are described in Chapter 13, “Rehabilitation Meth-
ods for Asphalt Pavements.” The repair procedures for Portland cement
concrete (PCC) pavements are described in Chapter 14, “Rehabilitation of
Concrete Pavements with HMA.”

8.2.3 Horizontal and Vertical Grade Control

grades. Before placement of the first lift of the HMA pavement on the base
course, elevations should be checked to ensure that the correct grades have
been achieved. Grades for aggregate bases should generally be within 0.05-
