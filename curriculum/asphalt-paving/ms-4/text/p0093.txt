A key feature of the testing machine is the gripping system used to
attach specimens to the alignment rods that apply the tensile load (see
Figure 2.32). The grips have a ball-joint connection that ensures no bend-
ing within the binder test specimen.

The environmental control system includes an environmental chamber
(or liquid bath) and a mechanical refrigeration unit capable of producing
and precisely maintaining temperatures as low as -36°C. In the case of an
environmental chamber, air is used as the cooling medium. If a liquid bath
is used, a solution of potassium acetate and water is used as the cooling
medium.

At the end of the conditioning period (G05 minutes), each of the six
specimens is tested individually. After mounting the specimen on the
end grips, a tensile load is applied by pulling one end at a strain rate of
3 percent per minute (1.0 millimeter per minute), while the other end
remains fixed, until the specimen fails. A test typically requires less than
a minute from load application until failure. A test result is considered
legitimate when fracture occurs within the midsection of the specimen
with the constant cross section. A specimen that breaks near the end insert
should be reported as an “end break.”
