Remixing can be either a single-stage or a multistage process (see Figure
13.16). The treatment depth for the single-stage process is typically 1-2
inches (25-50 millimeters). The treatment depth of the multistage method is
1%4-3 inches (37.5—75 millimeters).

The heating of the existing pavement is done in much the same way as
the surface recycling procedure. Scarifiers and/or hot milling drums (Figure
13.18) loosen the softened material, which is then augered to the center of
the unit. These units have a moldboard for collecting the RAP. Depth control
is accomplished by automatic sensors operating off a short reference ski or
matching shoe.

In the single-stage method, the loose material is directed into a mixing
chamber consisting of a pugmill or a mixing drum.

The multistage process (Figure 13.16) consists of one or more pre-heater
unit(s), two heater-milling units, a pugmill mixer, and a standard paving
machine and compaction equipment. The pre-heater provides initial heat-
ing of the surface and removes the surface moisture. Then, a heater-mill-
ing unit heats and mills the existing pavement. The loosened material is
windrowed in the center of the lane (see Figure 13.18). The typical depth
for this first pass is 1-1% inches (25-37.5 millimeters).

The second unit follows directly behind the first unit and continues
the heating and removal process. The distance between these two units
must be kept to a minimum to ensure the least amount of heat loss. The
second unit picks up the milled material from the first unit as it mills
1-inch below the windrow. This unit lifts and removes the loose material.
If required, new HMA is dropped into a hopper on the second unit. From
the hopper, it is dispensed evenly over the windrow from the first unit.
This material is carried over the heating chamber by a slat conveyor that
has a variable-speed control that matches the speed of the second unit. It
is dropped into the windrow created by the final milling head. This milling
head mills the second 1-inch layer of the softened material into a windrow.
