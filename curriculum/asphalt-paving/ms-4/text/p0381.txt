If the aggregate flow is increased, a new target asphalt flow rate is calculated
and the control system will adjust the asphalt flow. If the aggregate flow
decreases, the opposite happens. This entire process is continuous.

Changeable delay times are built into the flow-control circuits. When the
plant is set up, tests are done to determine the length of the delay time
between the belt scale reading and the aggregate material reaching the
asphalt injection point for that plant. Therefore, changes in the aggregate
scale do not cause an instantaneous change in the asphalt flow. The plant
control system has delay times set up not only for the asphalt flow but also
for each cold-feed bin and for every other ingredient. These flow delays are
very important for a continuous process. When changes to production are
made, mix integrity must be maintained, so the delay times are carefully
calibrated to ensure that production-rate changes and mix changes can
be executed without shutting down the process. An alarm notifies the
operator when a mix change is complete and material can be directed to
another storage silo.

Ingredients other than asphalt binder, including other liquid additives
and bulk additives, are controlled in the same fashion as the liquid asphalt
binder. They all take their key from the aggregate belt scale signal. When
mixes containing RAP are being produced, the control system also reads
the belt scale on the RAP conveyor and adjusts the virgin-asphalt binder
to compensate for the asphalt that is included in the RAP.

The belt scale reading is very critical in producing quality HMA in the
drum-mixer process. Additional attention needs to be paid to the area
of the main belt where the belt scale is located. Before startup, the area
should be inspected and cleaned to make sure the framework is free of
dust and dirt buildup that may affect the readings. Wind screens mounted
around the belt scale will prevent wind loading that can artificially add or
subtract weight from the readings. A belt tensioner or deadman (weighted
idler roller) to take up slack as the belt stretches helps to keep constant
tension on the belt as it passes across the belt scale and helps improves
the accuracy of the readings.
