13.1 HMA Overlays over

Asphalt Pavements

The most popular method of pavement rehabilitation for both hot mix
asphalt (HMA) pavements and Portland cement concrete (PCC) pavements
is the asphalt overlay. This chapter discusses the rehabilitation of HMA
pavements, and Chapter 14, “Rehabilitation of Concrete Pavements with
HMA” covers the use of HMA overlays for PCC pavements. Other options
for resurfacing, such as surface treatments, are discussed in Chapter 12,
“Asphalt in Pavement Maintenance”. Properly designed and constructed,
an asphalt overlay is a cost-effective method of correcting surface deficien-
cies, restoring user satisfaction, and increasing the load-carrying capacity
of the roadway.

The first step in developing an asphalt pavement rehabilitation project
is to conduct a detailed project-level pavement evaluation using the proce-
dures detailed in Chapter 11, “Pavement Management.” This information is
needed for identifying available rehabilitation alternatives and for design-
ing the project. In addition, the designer needs data to assess the structural
adequacy of the pavement. This data is collected by sampling and testing
the materials in the pavement, by conducting in situ testing, or by conduct-
ing deflection testing. In some situations, the project’s physical constraints
may require pavement reconstruction rather than overlay.

The three purposes for overlaying an asphalt pavement are the following:

° Heavy structural overlay. A heavy structural overlay is more than
5 inches (125 millimeters) thick. These overlays considerably improve
the load-bearing capacity of the pavement and usually have the same
design period as a newly constructed pavement.

¢ Structural overlay. A structural overlay is 3-5 inch (75-125 millime-
ters) thick. These overlays add strength to an old pavement and last
longer than functional or maintenance overlays.

° Functional or maintenance overlay. A functional overlay is 0.75-3
inches (20-75 millimeters) thick. Functional overlays are intended
to address ride quality, surface friction or other minor problems for
pavements that are structurally sound. Thin HMA overlays are also
a popular choice for preventative maintenance. A functional overlay
may be placed after the surface has been cold milled to replace the
material that was removed. This technique is commonly called “mill
and fill,” or referred to as an inlay.
