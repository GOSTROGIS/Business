Organic (Wax-Like) Additives

Special organic wax-like additives are mixed with the binder to reduce
viscosity at temperatures above about 212°F (100°C) and then increase
the viscosity at temperatures below about 212°F (100°C). The shift from
viscosity reduction to viscosity increase in the binder occurs near the con-
gealing point of the wax.

Mineral Additives

A number of minerals that have water bound internally have been used
as additives in order to introduce water into an HMA mixture. The water
is liberated gradually over time, resulting in an almost continuous foam-
ing action. This foaming action of the liquid binder acts as an extender
and lubricant that enables the mix to be workable at lower temperatures.
Production temperatures can be reduced to the 266°-293°F (130°-145°C)
range. The materials are added in a manner that produces up to 0.6 percent
water by weight of total mix.

Low-Energy Asphalt

Another WMA process, developed in France, uses wet fine aggregate.
This process is called Low-Energy Asphalt (LEA). A key to the energy say-
ings in this process is that it takes five times more energy to turn water into
steam than it takes to heat the aggregate from 32°F to 212°F (0°-100°C).
Fine aggregates tend to have the highest moisture contents. In the LEA
process, the coarse-aggregate fraction is dried and heated to a tempera-
ture of approximately 300°F (149°C). A specially formulated surfactant is
then added to the binder. The coarse aggregates are then coated with the
binder, which results in a thick film coating because the fine aggregate
has not yet been added. The wet fine aggregate is then mixed with the
asphalt-coated coarse aggregate, resulting in a foaming action that aids in
the coating of the fine aggregate.

Foam Processes

Foamed asphalt is created by injecting cold water, typically 1-5 percent,
into the hot asphalt binder using special equipment. The water rapidly
evaporates, producing a large volume of foam, which slowly collapses.
Foamed asphalt differs from foaming mineral additives in that the water
and asphalt binder are directly mixed together in the presence of com-
pressed air. The expanded foamed binder is then injected directly into the
mixing chamber containing the aggregate. In a variation of the foamed
process, the aggregates are heated and coated with a very soft binder. A
very hard asphalt binder is then foamed into the mixing chamber. The
combined soft and hard binder produces the intended binder grade.
