Traffic analysis in this method involves estimating the number of ESALs
to be supported over the design life of the pavement. Every highway-
type vehicle, from small automobiles on two axles to large multiple-axle
tractor-trailer combination trucks weighing more than 100,000 pounds
(45,000 kilograms), can be measured in terms of ESALs. The process of
equating anticipated vehicle traffic to ESALs is covered in detail in MS-1.
SW-1 goes even further by automating ESAL calculations. Both methods
allow the user to factor in traffic growth over the pavement design life.

Subgrade strength is characterized as resilient modulus, M.. Field-test
results such as CBR and R-value can be correlated to M, using standard
correlations. It is strongly recommended that subgrade conditions over
the length of a project are carefully evaluated and that an adequate num-
ber of tests are used to establish a representative subgrade M_. Additional
guidance on soil testing can be found in Chapter 5 of this manual and in
MS-10.
