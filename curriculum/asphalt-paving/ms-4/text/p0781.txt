Noise from traffic is due to a large number of moving noise sources
spaced along an entire roadway. This type of noise is classified as a line
source because noise is transmitted along the entire roadway. As the dis-
tance from the line of source increases, the noise level decreases at a lower
rate than it would from a single-point noise source. This is because the noise
from the more distant sources in the line begins to contribute more noise
relative to that of the closest individual point source. The net result is that
for line sources, a doubling of the distance results in a 3 dB(A) reduction in
the noise level instead of 6 dB(A). Thus, if a point that is 5 meters from the
center of the noise source (the center of the lane) has a noise level of 85
dB(A), then a point 10 meters from the edge of the roadway would have a
noise level of 82 dB(A). This is illustrated in Figure 15.14.

15.6.3 Noise Characteristics of HMA
Pavements

The proper selection of the pavement surface can result in a significant
reduction in the noise level alongside the roadway. The selection of pave-
ment type and the maximum gradation of the aggregate will affect the
noise level. Selection criteria are discussed in the following paragraphs.
As a pavement ages, its noise level increases because of changes in the
surface texture. The HMA surface may start to lose particles, crack at the
surface, and in the case of an OGFC, result in clogged pores from accumu-
lated dust or deicing materials.
