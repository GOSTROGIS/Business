After the road surface has been preheated, a heating/scarification unit
passes over the surface (see Figure 13.13 for a close-up of the scarifying
tines). If necessary, a recycling agent is added to the scarified layer. The
control system for adding the recycling agent is linked to the forward speed
of the scarifying unit. As previously discussed, the recycling agent rate will
vary according to the properties of the asphalt binder in the HMA being
scarified. Generally, it is applied at a rate of up to 0.5 gallon/yard? (2 litre/
meter’). The scarified material is struck off with a heated, vibrating screed
using manual grade control.

Compaction is generally accomplished by using a pneumatic roller as a
breakdown roller, followed by a static steel-wheeled or double-drum steel
vibratory roller for finish rolling. Static rolling is preferred where very thin
lifts are being compacted to avoid damaging the mixture.

The compacted recycled surface can be left as the wearing course on
a low-volume roadway, or it can function as a pretreatment for an HMA
overlay or surface treatment. Lower costs and higher production generally
result if the placement of the wearing course and the scarifying operation
are separate and independent operations that do not delay each other.
