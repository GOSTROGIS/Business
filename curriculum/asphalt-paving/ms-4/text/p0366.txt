7.3.1 Aggregate Gradation Control with a
Batch Plant

Aggregate gradation is controlled in two places in a modern batch
plant—in the cold feed and in the screening unit/hot bins (see Figure
7.19). Changes in the past few years have complicated the gradation
control process in a batch plant.

Batch plants were originally designed to accept a crushed aggregate
product and a sand. The screens at the top of the batch tower were used
to separate the aggregate into fractions so that different mixes could be
constructed, some with larger rocks and a larger percentage of stone for
base or binder lifts and some with more sand and smaller stones for wear-
ing surfacees. When batch plants were first designed, purchasing presized
aggregates for hot mix production was not an option. The batch tower's
first job was to size the aggregates.
