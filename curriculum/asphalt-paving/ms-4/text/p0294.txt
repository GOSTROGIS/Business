After the test is completed, the specimen is left in a covered mold for at
least a half hour before the next part of the test procedure is begun.

The expansion pressure test is used to determine the expansion pres-
sure a soil develops under water. This test is performed in such a way that
a large change in sample density does not occur during testing. Pavement
thicknesses required to withstand expansion pressures can be determined
and compared with those determined by the R-value test.

The specimen is allowed to stand for at least a half hour after the comple-
tion of the exudation test. A perforated brass plate with stem is placed on the
face of the compacted specimen in the mold. The mold is then positioned
in the calibrated expansion device, and 200 milliliters of water is poured on
the specimen in the mold. Expansion pressure is allowed to develop in 16
to 24 hours, after which the deflection of the calibrated spring steel bar is
read and the expansion pressure determined.

The R-value test provides a rapid means for determining a measure of
the stability or resistance to plastic deformation of compacted materials by
the Hveem Stabilometer (see Figure 5.4). It is the same device used for the
Hveem method of asphalt mix design.

The specimen that was tested for expansion pressure is forced from the
mold into an adjusted stabilometer. The stabilometer is then positioned in
the testing machine. The head is lowered until it contacts the specimen but
applies no load. A lateral pressure of 5 pounds per square inch (35 kilo-
Pascals) is then applied with the stabilometer pump. Next, the specimen
is loaded at the rate of 0.05 inch (1.3 millimeters) per minute and lateral
pressure readings are obtained at 80 and 160 pounds per square inch (550
and 1100 kiloPascals) vertical pressure.

Vertical loading must be stopped at 2000 pounds (8.896 kiloNewtons)
and reduced immediately to 1000 pounds (4.448 kiloNewtons). The lateral
pressure is reduced to 5 pounds per square inch (35 kiloPascals). The turns
displacement dial indicator is set at zero and the pump handle is turned at
two revolutions per second until the stabilometer gauge reads 100 pounds
per square inch (689 kiloPascals). The number of turns is recorded, and
