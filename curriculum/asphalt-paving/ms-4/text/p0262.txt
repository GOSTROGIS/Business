After an initial asphalt content has been estimated, the Florida DOT mixes
three 1200-gram samples—one at the estimated asphalt binder content, one
at 0.5 percent above the estimated binder content, and one at 0.5 percent
below the estimated binder content. These samples are mixed at 320°F
(160°C). After the sample has been thoroughly mixed, it is placed in a pie
plate. The pie plate is placed in an oven at 320°F (160°C) for one hour. It is
taken out and cooled. The bottom of the pie plate is inspected to determine
whether there is excessive draindown. As with the FHWA procedure, a slight
puddle of asphalt cement at the points of contact between the aggregate
and the glass plate is suitable and desirable after the 60-minute period.

4.8.3 Asphalt-Treated Permeable Base

General Description

An asphalt-treated permeable base (ATPB) is built as a base for an HMA
pavement. The purpose of this layer is to intercept the water that enters
through the pavement surface before it can reach the unbound layers, result-
ing in lowering the strength of those layers. The water enters the pavement
through cracks in the surface and/or a poorly compacted and therefore
permeable HMA layer. The layer should be designed to provide sufficient
air-voids to make the layer permeable (approximately 10 percent). The per-
formance and maintenance of these systems is discussed in more detail in
Chapter 5 of this handbook.

Mix Design Characteristics and Procedures

The mix design of an ATPB is very similar to that used for an OGFC
layer. The goal is to provide a free-draining layer with sufficient asphalt
binder to cement the particles together and to prevent stripping of the
asphalt binder from the aggregate.

Table 4.11 presents gradations being used by different agencies for an
ATPB layer.

The asphalt binder can be either the standard grade of binder used by the
agency (such as a 64-22) or a modified binder. The primary concern in the
choice of asphalt binder is insuring the coating of the aggregate and minimiz-
ing the amount of draindown during construction. Coating is evaluated by
visually observing the mix in the laboratory during mixing. Different binder
contents should be evaluated. The draindown test discussed in the sections
on SMA and OFGC can be used to evaluate the draindown characteristics of
the mix. If the draindown exceeds 0.3 percent by weight of aggregate, steps
should be taken to reduce the draindown, such as using fibers or a polymer-
modified asphalt binder.

The air-voids in the mix can be evaluated in the same manner as with
the OGFC mixes. The mix should be compacted using 50 gyrations with
a SGC and the bulk specific gravity of the ATPB determined using the
CoreLok® procedure.
