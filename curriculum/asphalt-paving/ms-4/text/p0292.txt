Test Procedure

The R-value test (ASTM D 2844) is made on a relatively small sample,
and it is necessary to remove all large material. If 75 percent or more of the
material passes the 34-inch (19.0 millimeters) sieve, the material retained
on that sieve is removed. If less than 75 percent of the material passes the
34-inch (19.0 millimeters) sieve, then all material retained on the 1-inch (25.0
millimeters) sieve is removed. The soil is then separated over the No. 4 (4.75
millimeters) sieve but recombined in the proper proportions with the coarse
sizes to obtain 1200-gram (2.65 pounds) samples plus an extra 200 grams
(0.44 pounds) for moisture content determinations.

In each R-value test, four test briquettes at different moisture contents are
prepared. Each briquette has one-half to two-thirds the amount of water nec-
essary to saturate the sample or exude moisture between 100 and 800 pounds
per square inch (689 and 5516 kiloPascals). After mixing, loose material is
allowed to cure overnight in a covered container. Then more water is added
and mixed until the estimated saturation moisture is attained. The first bri-
quette is used as a pilot specimen to obtain specimen heights of 2.5£0.1
inches (63.52.5 millimeters) and to have exudation pressures both above
and below 300 pounds per square inch (2068 kiloPascals).

Specimens are compacted by a kneading compactor, which is the same
as that used for the Hveem method of asphalt mix design. The compaction
procedure provides a kneading action intended to give soil particles the
same orientation and contact pressure obtained during field compaction.

The prepared soil sample is placed in the compactor feeding trough with
the material distributed evenly along the full length. A 4-inch (101.6 milli-
meters) diameter by 5-inch (127 millimeters) tall mold is fitted into a special
holder for placing in the compactor. Material is deposited in the mold by a
special procedure while the kneading compactor is operating and the foot
pressure is set at 250 pounds per square inch (1724 kiloPascals).

When all material has been placed in the mold and 10 extra blows have
been added to level and seat the material, the sample is compacted by
using 100 blows with a foot pressure of 350 pounds per square inch (2413
kiloPascals).
