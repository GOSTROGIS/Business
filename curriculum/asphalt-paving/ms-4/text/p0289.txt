Surcharge weights within 5 pounds (2.27 kilograms) of the weight of
the base material and pavement are added. In no case, however, should
the surcharge be less than 10 pounds (4.54 kilograms). The sample is
immersed in water and initial measurements taken for swell. After
96 hours, or a shorter period for soils that readily take up moisture,
final swell measurements are made to determine swell as a percentage
of initial specimen height. The sample is removed from the water and
the surcharge weights and perforated plate are removed. The sample is
allowed to drain for 15 minutes and then weighed and prepared for the
penetration test.

The penetration test is conducted with surcharge weights in place, equal
to those used during the soaking period. The mold assembly is placed in
a loading frame or hydraulic press. The piston is first seated with an initial
10-pound (44 Newton) load, and all dials are set at zero. A load is then
applied at the uniform rate of 0.05 inch (1.27 millimeters) of penetration
per minute. The total load readings are taken at the following penetrations
shown in Table 5.3:

Presentation of Data

After the test has been completed, the penetration load in pounds per
square inch (megapascals) is calculated and the load-penetration curve plot-
ted. In order to obtain true penetration loads from the test data, the zero
point of the curve is adjusted to correct for surface irregularities and the
initial concave upward shape of the curve, if present. If the curve is uniform
as in example No. 1 of Figure 5.2, the CBR value is calculated from the
recorded loads. For surface irregularities, as in example No. 3 of Figure 5.2,
extend the straight-line portion of the curve to the base to obtain a corrected
origin, or zero. If the curve has a reverse bend, or concave upward shape, as
in example No. 2, draw a line tangent to the steepest point of the curve (A),
and extend the line to the base to obtain a corrected origin or zero point (B).
Then read the corrected load values for 0.1 inch (2.5 millimeters) penetration
(C) and 0.2 inch (5 millimeters) of penetration (D).
