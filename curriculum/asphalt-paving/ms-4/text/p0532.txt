of inertial profilometers, and AASHTO has developed provisional stan-
dards for calibrating and standardizing the equipment. The procedures are
intended to enhance the accuracy and repeatability of tested values.

Inertial profilometers can be sensitive to the location and the number
of the measuring sensors on the test vehicle. This is because the roadway
profile may be different in the wheelpath, for example, than it is in the
center of the lane. One way to increase repeatability is to add sensors and
use an average profile. Repeatability between multiple profilometers gen-
erally requires the profilometers to have multiple sensors. With enough
sensors, and careful driving path selection, roadway rutting can be mea-
sured by the profilometer.

Inertial profilometers are used extensively by agencies for acceptance
testing of pavement smoothness and for pavement management data col-
lection. Historically, they have not been used for quality-control purposes,
but manufacturers have now developed lightweight devices mounted on
small four-wheeled vehicles that can be used almost immediately after
construction at slower speeds and for shorter distances.
