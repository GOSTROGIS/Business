Before removal and recycling, pavements can be sampled by coring
or by cutouts. After milling or crushing, RAP can be sampled in any of
the same ways that virgin aggregates are sampled—from stockpiles, haul
trucks, conveyor belts, and material handling transfer/discharge points.
Regardless of the sampling scenario, it is vital that the sampling methods
provide samples that are representative of the produced material.

For samples taken from the pavement, cores are typically taken to the
full depth of the pavement and then trimmed off to the proposed depth
of removal. Standards pertaining to sampling from roadways, such as
AASHTO T 168 (ASTM D 979), can be used as a guide to obtain HMA pave-
ment cores. With pavement sampling, it is important to remember that the
milling and processing of the RAP may change the gradation of the RAP
aggregate somewhat from the gradation in the pavement cores. Some state
transportation departments have developed degradation factors for grada-
tion based on experience with the states’ local materials. Stockpiles of the
processed RAP should be checked to verify the actual RAP gradation.

RAP can be sampled from haul trucks transporting milled material from
the pavement to the plant location. When sampling from a truck, a trench
with a level bottom should be dug across the mass of the RAP. Samples
should then be obtained by shovel from three locations equally spaced
across the trench.

Sampling RAP from stockpiles is similar to sampling stockpiled virgin
aggregate. It is usually best to use front-end loaders or other equipment
for sampling from stockpiles. However, if samples are obtained by shov-
eling, care should be taken to not include any of the crusted outer layer
Gif any) of the stockpile in the samples. Shoveled samples should be taken
from at least 10 random places around the stockpile.

It is necessary to obtain representative samples of the RAP not only
for laboratory mix designs but also for determining its key properties by
testing. In a new HMA mix, the asphalt binder in the RAP contributes to
and becomes a part of the total binder. It is important to know how much
asphalt binder is present in the RAP. Depending on the amount of RAP
desired for a new mix, it may also be necessary to determine the physi-
