Toughness and Tenacity

A third test conducted by some agencies in the United States is the Tough-
ness and Tenacity test. This procedure was first introduced by Benson in the
1950s. In the test, a metal hemispherical head is embedded in hot asphalt
to a depth of approximately 11 millimeters. After cooling to 77°F (25°C),
the head is attached to a tensile test machine and pulled from the asphalt
binder at a rate of 51 centimeters per minute (see Figure 2.51). The load is
measured throughout the test and a load-deformation curve is plotted (see
Figure 2.52). Toughness and Tenacity values are determined based on the
area under different portions of the load-deformation curve.

DSR Phase Angle

In the standard DSR test used for Performance Graded asphalt binders,
the complex shear modulus (G*) and phase angle (8) are determined. The
phase angle is a measure of the elastic and viscous components of the
asphalt binder. Some user agencies specify a maximum phase angle for
modified asphalt binders. Unfortunately, the phase angle is influenced by
the stiffness of the asphalt binder. Thus, it is not a true reflection of the
elasticity—in the form of an elastomer—of the asphalt binder.

DSR Creep-Recovery

A relatively new test being evaluated by researchers for characterizing
the properties of modified asphalt binders is the DSR repeated creep-
recovery test. This test, originally proposed by researchers in 2000, has
been modified to include multiple stress levels. It is now referred to as the
multiple-stress creep-recovery (MSCR) test.
