12.1 Introduction

This chapter provides practical information about methods and termi-
nology that applies to the use of asphalt in pavement maintenance.

Large differences in soil types, climate, traffic, and other factors make
for a variety of problems, even within small areas. Some regions have
heavy rainfall, while others are hot and dry; some pavements must accom-
modate vehicles carrying heavy loads, while others are subjected to only
lightweight traffic.

Despite these differences, there are maintenance methods that can be
used equally well in all regions. This chapter provides an overview of the
methods used to maintain hot mix asphalt (HMA) pavements.

There are about two million miles of paved roads across the United States.
All require maintenance. The original investment in these structures was
in the billions of dollars. Their maintenance now costs millions of dollars
annually. Obviously, timely and proper maintenance is imperative. Pave-
ment maintenance is the routine work performed to keep a pavement,
under normal traffic conditions and forces of nature, as nearly as possible
in its as-constructed condition. Preventive maintenance not only preserves
the pavement surface but also prevents early deterioration. It should not be
regarded as a temporary expedient but as an investment in the pavement
structure and as insurance against costly pavement renovation.

The three categories of pavement maintenance operations are the
following: ;

° Preventive maintenance is the routine work done to prevent deterio-
ration of a pavement. It includes crack sealing, surface treatments, thin
overlays, drainage maintenance, etc. The idea behind preventive main-
tenance is to expend funds and materials while the pavement is in rela-
tively good condition in order to retard deterioration and thus prolong
the life of the pavement. This can be done at a very reasonable cost.
A good analogy for preventive maintenance is the painting of a house.
When the paint fades, it is repainted; the responsible homeowner does
not wait until the paint is cracked and peeling. As with painting a house,
the effectiveness of the preventive maintenance is directly related to the
condition of the pavement. Conducting preventive maintenance activities
on a sound pavement in good condition is very effective. Conducting an
inappropriate repair (in either method or timing) can accelerate the rate
of pavement deterioration. In summary, preventive maintenance is com-
pleting the right maintenance activity at the right time.
