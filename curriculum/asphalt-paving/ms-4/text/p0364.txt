Portable silos commonly have load cells built into them, allowing trucks
to be loaded and ticketed on a negative-weigh basis. Figure 7.17 shows such
a silo.

Silos at stationary plants are usually mounted over truck scales, allowing
the tare weight, gross weight, and net weight to be captured with each load.
Multiple silos and multiple scales are not uncommon. Figure 7.18 shows
such a system.

The use of a batcher or a gob hopper is a good practice and is generally
required by specification for silos of any type. Batchers collect mix in small
quantities at the top of the silo and drop the mix in mass into the silo. This
action minimizes the segregation that can occur when silos are fed continu-
ously. Batchers and gob hoppers need to be adjusted so that the maximum
size batch is dispensed into the silo each time. These devices typically control
segregation on most traditional mix designs, but some mixes are especially
prone to segregation, especially if they are “gap-graded” or have especially
large rock in them. Segregation can also occur in the batcher/gob hopper
itself if the device is not fed directly in the center, fed obliquely at an angle,
or positioned significantly below the feeding device.
