13.7.2 Stabilization with Asphalt
Materials

Asphalt works best where minimal plastic clay fines are present. If sampling
and testing an existing pavement reveals that some plastic clay is present,
it is may be possible to treat the material with lime to reduce the plasticity
in preparation for asphalt stabilization. Stabilization with asphalt materials
should not be used when the PI of the soils being stabilized exceeds 10 or
the minus 200 (0.075 millimeter) material exceeds 25 percent.

Emulsified Asphalt

Various asphalt emulsion binders have been used for FDR. Mixing grade
asphalt emulsions (slow set cationic or anionic) are usually used, with
the specific type dependent on the characteristics of the materials on site.
To increase the initial strength and resistance to water damage, small
amounts (1-1.5 percent) of either lime or cement can be added to a mix
using an asphalt emulsion. They can be added as dry material or as slurry
(lime, cement, and water). The mix design procedures discussed for use
with cold-recycling should be followed when asphalt emulsions are used
for FDR.

Foamed Asphalt

Foamed asphalt, also used for FDR, is created when small amounts of
water and compressed air come into contact with a hot asphalt binder in
a specially designed foaming nozzle. As the water vaporizes, it expands
to create tiny bubbles within the asphalt binder, decreasing the asphalt
binder’s viscosity and providing an appearance similar to black shaving
cream. Only 1-3 percent water by weight of the asphalt is required. to
foam an unmodified PG asphalt binder. The tiny foam bubbles carry a thin
film of asphalt that interacts with fine aggregates to create a mortar that
coats large particles. The water flashes to steam immediately during the
reaction and is not part of the asphalt binder.

Foam-stabilized material can be placed, shaped, compacted, and opened
to traffic shortly after mixing. It remains workable for a period of time and
can be stockpiled and placed at a later date by including additional mois-
ture for compaction. Generally, the material being treated with foamed
asphalt should have between 5 and 20 percent minus 200 materials. Fines
that exist in most crushed-limestone base materials or that are created
in the reclaiming process from soil-cement base are ideal for use with
foamed asphalt.
