All hauling units should be continually monitored for signs of mechanical
problems or leaking fluids (except air conditioning condensate). If prob-
lems are found, the hauling unit should be immediately removed from the
lineup until permanent repairs can be made.

8.3.2 Delivery of HMA to the Paver

When the loaded hauling unit has arrived at the paving site, the HMA
must be moved into the paver without disturbing the HMA gradation or
without losing temperature. The driver, after arriving in the staging area,
should “break the load” by raising the truck bed slightly until the HMA
moves against the tailgate. This procedure allows the HMA to exit the
truck bed in a mass rather than in a dribble, avoiding segregation as the
HMA flows into the hopper (see Figure 8.6).

The paver operator or dump operator, should position the loaded truck
12-18 inches (300-450 millimeters) short of the paver, with the dump bed
partially raised. This positioning keeps the truck from knocking the paver
backward and creating an indentation in the mat. As the paver moves
forward and makes contact with the hauling unit, the driver should be
instructed to release the tailgate and raise the dump bed as quickly as
possible. When the tailgate opens, the material will move in mass into the
hopper, thereby reducing the chances of segregating the material. When a
wheel-locking device is not being used, the experienced truck driver will
anticipate this sudden rush of material and briefly apply additional brake
pressure to prevent the hauling unit from being forced out of the hopper
and spilling material on the pavement directly ahead of the paver. While
the dump bed is emptying, the driver should maintain just enough pres-
sure on the brakes to keep the truck from rolling away from the paver.
The flow of HMA is coordinated between the paver and truck driver. The
bed should be raised to the fully extended position as quickly as possible
without overflowing the paver hopper. Once the bed is empty, the paver
signals for the truck bed to be lowered before pulling out of the paver hop-
per. The hauling unit should be driven to a predetermined cleanout area,
where remaining HMA can be removed from the truck bed and around the
tailgate area and apron. Do not dump HMA in front of the paver or allow
beds and aprons to be cleaned in front of the paver. See Figure 8.7 for the
steps associated with properly loading the paver.
