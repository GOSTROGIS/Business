10 Mix Selection

An HMA pavement surface must serve the demands of multiple
traffic loadings and environments. The HMA mixture chosen for the
project must meet the project requirements. The pavement designer
has more choices now than in the past for the type of mix selected
and for the materials (aggregates and asphalt binders) to be used on
the project. This has led to the development of specialized mixes to
address very specific problems. New design procedures such as the
Superpave design procedure and others discussed earlier in this chap-
ter have been implemented. In many cases, higher-quality materials
are being specified than are needed for the particular project. Because
of these concerns, the National Asphalt Pavement Association—in a
coordinated effort with the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA)
and an Expert Task Group consisting of representatives from the pav-
ing industry and Departments of Transportation—developed National
Asphalt Pavement Association Information Series 128, HMA Pavement
Mix Type Selection Guide. This document provides detailed information
on mix selection. As described earlier in this chapter, there are three
primary types of mixes used for the construction of HMA pavements:
fine and coarse graded dense mixes, stone matrix asphalt (SMA) mixes,
and Open-Graded Friction Courses (OGFC) mixes.

4.10.1 Pavement Layers and Traffic Levels
(definitions)

The following provides a short description of each of these layers in a
pavement structure. Generally, materials of lesser quality are needed for
each layer as they progress down in the pavement structure.

* Surface layer—contains the highest-quality materials. It pro-
vides friction, smoothness, noise control, rutting resistance and
durability.

° Intermediate layer or binder layer—consists of one or more lifts of
HMA below the surface layer. It provides structural strength to the
pavement.

° Base layer—consists of one or more lifts of HMA placed at the
bottom of the pavement structure.
