7.2.10 Fiber Additive Equipment

Fiber additives are specified for some hot mix formulas to help ensure
that a uniform film thickness is maintained between the aggregate par-
ticles and to reduce the possibility of draindown of the binder in the mix.
They are produced from both mineral fiber and cellulose fiber materials.

Fiber additives are popular with mixes that contain a high percentage
of large-size or same-size aggregate and a low percentage of fine aggre-
gates to fill the voids between these large aggregate particles—like Open-
Graded Friction Course (OGFC) and stone matrix asphalt (SMA) mixes.

Fibers are typically added pneumatically with a machine that shreds
and blows the fibers into the mixing area of the plant. This machine uses
a negative-weighing principle where the bundle of fibers deposited in the
machine is continuously weighed, and the declining weight is calculated
and displayed on the control system as it is separated and blown into the
plant.

In a drum-mixer application, this machine runs continuously and there
is a continuous weight display of the fiber additives. The flow rate of the
machine is typically tied into the plant master controls and the aggregate
belt scales.

In a batch-plant application, the machine starts and stops based on the
target weight of fibers for each batch. With a batch plant, fibers can also
be added by hand in bag form by dropping a pre-packaged bag of fibers
down a chute directly into the pugmill with each batch.

7.2.11 Finished Product Storage Silos and
Load-Out Equipment .

Finished product storage silos allow the plant to run continuously whether
or not trucks are present. Silos are required for drum-mix plants, because the
mix flows continuously from the mixing area when the plant is running and
must be elevated for dispensing into trucks. Silos on batch plants allow the
plant to run continuously, thereby increasing daily output.

Silos can be either the surge or the storage type. Surge silos are typically
not insulated and are often open-topped bins. This type of storage unit is
lighter and can be made portable so that it works well with portable plants.
Storage silos are fully enclosed and heavily insulated. They generally have
heated cones, and some have sidewall heat. Top and bottom seals are com-
mon on storage silos. Storage silos allow longer storage times for mixes,
without significant heat loss or oxidation to the mix. Storage silos are mas-
sive units requiring large foundations, making them semi-permanent installa-
tions. Regulatory agencies sometimes rate storage silos for storage periods or
limit maximum storage times. Allowable storage times are sometimes based
on the absorption characteristics of the aggregate, rather than on the features
of the plant equipment.
