5.1.4 Introduction

The performance of an HMA pavement is directly related to understanding
the soils upon which it is built. The strength of both the untreated base course
and the subgrade soil are directly related to the moisture content; therefore,
the drainage of the pavement is extremely important.

Likewise, drainage is also an important aspect in asphalt pavement
design and construction. The latter part of this chapter reviews some key
issues of pavement-related drainage.

If not thoroughly knowledgeable about soil mechanics and the effect
of different soils and their drainage, the reader is encouraged to consult a
qualified geotechnical engineer for further guidance.

5.1.2 Objectives for Classifying Subgrade

The objective behind using any soil classification system for road and
airport construction is the ability to predict performance of a given soil
subgrade on the basis of a few simple tests. These test results, along with
the soil classification procedure and criteria, allow an engineer to identify
the soil type and characterize it according to other soils with similar char-
acteristics and properties.

Grouping soils into a classification system is not the final objective, but
it should be regarded as a tool to understand soil action and behavior as
they relate to subgrade performance.

5.1.3 Classification Systems

The two classification systems commonly used to describe soils used
for pavement construction are the American Association of State High-
way and Transportation Officials (AASHTO) Classification System for Soils
and the Unified Soil Classification System (USCS). Both systems are
described in detail in many soil mechanics textbooks. The reader is
referred to An Introduction to Geotechnical Engineering, by R. D. Holtz
and W. D. Kovacs.

The principal soil classification system used in the United States by
highway engineers is the AASHTO system. This system was originally
developed by the U.S. Bureau of Public Roads in about 1928. The present
AASHTO system, which dates from 1945, was the result of an extensive
revision by the Highway Research Board.
