Crack Sealing/Filling

Crack sealing/filling has two purposes:

° To prevent the intrusion of water into the underlying layers of the
pavement structure, thus further weakening the pavement

° To prevent incompressible materials from entering the crack, thus
causing further deterioration as the pavement expands and contracts
with temperature changes

Crack sealing is usually done with liquid asphalt, cutbacks, or asphalt
emulsions, and is considered a temporary procedure. These crack seal-
ers usually maintain a tightly sealed crack only as long as the pavement
remains stationary, both longitudinally and vertically. Once expansion and
contraction or excessive vertical movement occurs, the seal’s effective-
ness can be lost and the cracks must be resealed. In climates with severe
temperature changes, resealing may be necessary every year. In moderate
or mild climates, resealing will be necessary less often. Although the cost
per unit of this type of crack sealing is relatively low, the frequency with
which it must be done might dictate a more lasting solution.

Crack filling is accomplished using a specially prepared hot poured
sealant. Crack filling is considered a permanent seal. Three to eight years
is the expected life, depending on the material used, the pavement condi-
tion, and the techniques used when applying the crack seal.

Along with proper drainage, crack sealing/filling is probably the single
most important maintenance activity. Many pavement distresses can be traced
to the intrusion of water into the pavement structure. Keeping water out of the
pavement is essential to delay or prevent more serious distresses.
