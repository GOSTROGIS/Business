will depend on the width of the cracks. If they are narrow, the crack-
sealant materials will not enter the crack. If the cracks are less than % inch
(8 millimeters) wide, crack sealing is probably not appropriate. Wider
cracks should be blown out with compressed air and cleaned of debris.
The crack-sealing material should be placed in a clean and dry crack. Its
level should be slightly lower than that of the surrounding surface and
should not spill over the sides of the crack. Some agencies require that
crack sealing operations be suspended at least six to twelve months prior
to the placement of an overlay. The reason is that when the overlay is
placed on a newly crack-sealed surface the pavement expands, closing the
crack and pushing the filler material up and out of the crack, which will
cause a bump in the overlaid surface. If the surface has extensive crack-
ing, milling the surface before overlay can be very effective. The cracks
are usually wider at the surface and milling off 1-2 inches of the surface
can result in having much narrower cracks below the new overlay. These
cracks are sealed with the tack coat during the construction process. A
detailed discussion of crack sealing/filling is in Chapter 12, “Asphalt in
Pavement Maintenance.”
