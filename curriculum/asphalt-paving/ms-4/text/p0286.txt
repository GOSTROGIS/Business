5.2.2 Laboratory Test Methods

The CBR, with its numerous variations, is probably the most widely used
strength index for designing asphalt pavement structures. This method was first
developed by the California Division of Highways in the 1920s. It was modified
by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers for use on airfields during World War Il
and is still widely used in airfield pavement design and for highway and road-
way design outside the United States. While primarily a laboratory test, field
CBR test methods have been developed for use in evaluating soils in situ.

The Resistance Value (R-Value) test method for evaluating treated and
untreated materials for bases, subbases, and subgrades was developed by
F. N. Hveem and R. M. Carmany of the California Division of Highways.

The Resilient Modulus (M) method was developed by the Asphalt Insti-
tute based on a test originated at the University of California by H. B. Seed,
C. K. Chan, and C. E. Lee. This laboratory test is used to determine the stiff-
ness of a soil specimen for conditions that represent stress in pavements
subjected to moving wheel loads.
