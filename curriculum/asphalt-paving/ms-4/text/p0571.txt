Composite indexes are very useful for communicating the overall
condition of a section and for comparing conditions of multiple sections.
In addition, a composite index provides an indication of the overall level
of repair needed, as shown in Figure 11.7.

In addition to using a composite index, some agencies use individual
indexes or raw distress information to identify feasible maintenance and
rehabilitation treatments. For example, fatigue cracking, patching, and
rut distress may be combined to determine a structural condition index
while longitudinal and transverse cracking may be combined with block
cracking to form a functional condition index. Pavement sections with
a low structural condition index are not good candidates for preventive
maintenance treatments although a section with low functional condition
indexes and high structural indexes might be.

Reporting Current Conditions

The most common use of pavement condition information is to report
overall network conditions. This information is reported in a number of
different formats depending on the needs of the intended recipients. For
some individuals, a report that presents the overall network conditions by
functional class, surface type, or some other delineator may be appropriate.
Several agencies regularly publish reports on the state of the road network
after each data collection cycle. Other agencies publish limited pavement
condition information on their web sites.

One of the most effective ways of communicating pavement condition
information involves displaying pavement condition information on color-
coded maps. This approach has a strong visual impact, especially when
roads in poor condition are shown in red. This method of displaying pave-
ment condition information is especially useful for presentations to elected
officials and upper management.
