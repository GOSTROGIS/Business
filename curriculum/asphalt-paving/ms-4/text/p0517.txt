10.6 Placement Quality-Control
Data Analysis

The primary quality factors for placement of HMA are compaction,
thickness, and smoothness. Placement quality-control, therefore, focuses
on these same factors. These three factors are discussed below. Tempera-
ture is also discussed because it plays such a vital role in controlling and
achieving compaction.

Similar to quality-contro] data for hot mix production at the plant, it can
be very useful to summarize the placement quality-control data to get a
better overall picture of what is happening during placement and compac-
tion. The two key factors measured—smoothness and compaction—are
critical to the performance of an asphalt pavement, so understanding and
controlling these aspects of the construction operation are very important.
Tables 10.7A and 10.7B (metric) show examples of a quality-control data
summary for HMA placement. For complete quality-control documenta-
tion, summaries should be compiled and given to the quality-control man-
ager on a regular basis. For interpretation and analysis of the data, it is
useful to get an overall understanding of what is happening on the project
and then to evaluate the details of particular issues more specifically.

10.6.1 Temperature

Temperature is perhaps the most critical variable in HMA compaction.
The temperature of the mix is, therefore, very important to those respon-
sible for compaction. One difficulty that the compaction crews have is that
the HMA goes through so many other operations before it is ready to be
compacted. The temperature is affected by each operation, and the com-
paction equipment operators and foremen have little influence on them.
Good quality-control, which includes temperature readings at all phases
of production and delivery, will help provide HMA at the appropriate
compaction temperatures to the rollers. Some quality-control specifica-
tions require temperature readings to be recorded at many points along
the way and reported each day. Specifications for a project using a batch
plant, for example, may require that temperature readings be recorded
for the asphalt cement prior to mixing, the aggregate after heating, the
mixture immediately after mixing, and at delivery to the paving machine.
With this kind of data stored for each day’s production, it would be easy
to determine the root cause of temperature problems if they occurred on
a particular day. More importantly, it would be easy to establish a correc-
tive action plan to get the temperatures where they need to be for proper
compaction.
