To overcome this problem, two approaches have been used to determine
the bulk specific gravity of an OGFC. The first is to use dimensional] analysis.
In this technique, the weight of the sample is determined and the volume of
the sample is obtained by carefully measuring the sample (height and diam-
eter) and using that data to compute the volume. The bulk specific gravity is
then determined by dividing the weight of the sample by the volume of the
sample. Another approach is to use the CoreLok® vacuum-sealing device as
discussed in Section 4.2.3.

Determination of Optimum Asphalt Content

Using the selected design gradation, the designer should prepare OGFC
mixes at three binder contents. These samples should be subjected to the
draindown test (ASTM D 6390). A maximum draindown of 0.3 percent by
weight of mix is recommended for an OGFC mix.

Using the optimum asphalt binder content and gradation, the designer
should conduct the Cantabro abrasion test on unaged and aged compacted
samples. The test evaluates an OGFC material’s ability to resist abrasion loss.
A Los Angeles Abrasion machine is used to conduct the test. Each specimen
is placed in the machine without the charge of steel balls. The machine is
run for 300 revolutions at a speed of 30 to 33 rpm at room temperature. The
percentage of abrasion loss is calculated using the following equation:

Resistance to abrasion will generally improve by increasing binder con-
tent or by using a stiffer PG binder grade of asphalt cement. In addition to
the testing of the unaged mix, the design mix should be subjected to aging
and then tested with the Cantabro test. OGFC mixes have a very high air-
void content, which means that the asphalt binder in OGFC is prone to
hardening at a faster rate than a dense-graded HMA. This may result in
a reduction of the cohesive and adhesive strength of the binder, which
could lead to raveling of the mix. Aging is accomplished by placing five
compacted specimens in a forced-draft oven at 85°C (185°F) for 120 hours.
The specimens are cooled to 25°C (77°F) and stored for four hours, after
which the Cantabro test is conducted. The average abrasion losses on the
five specimens should be less than 30 percent, with no specimens having
an abrasion loss of more than 50 percent.
