11.1.4 Pavement Management Analysis
Levels

Pavement management systems are used in transportation agencies to
support decisions at three different levels: the project level, the network level,
and the strategic level. Each of these levels is described further in this section
of the chapter.

Project-Leveil Analysis

As the name implies, a project-level analysis involves decisions regard-
ing the maintenance and rehabilitation of specific pavement sections. At
this level, detailed pavement evaluations are conducted to determine the
extent of any deterioration present, to identify the probable cause of the
deterioration, and to design the appropriate treatment to correct the deficien-
cies. Pavement management systems can support decisions at this level by
providing information on the deterioration rate of a pavement section, main-
tenance and construction histories, or other factors that may influence the
selection of the appropriate repair strategy.

Network-Level Analysis

Pavement management systems are most commonly used at the network
level, where the maintenance and rehabilitation needs of the entire road net-
work are considered with respect to the amount of funding available. At the
network level, the agency’s maintenance and rehabilitation needs are priori-
tized so the most cost-effective use of available funding can be determined.
Pavement needs are typically considered over a multiyear period and differ-
ent maintenance and rehabilitation strategies are considered in the pavement
management system. The initial recommendations from the pavement man-
agement system provide general guidelines for the type of work needed and
possible locations where the work should be performed. The development of
final work plans is usually an iterative process that involves moving neighbor-
ing projects from one year to another, or combining similar projects, to gain
economy of scale. The work list is further developed at the project level in
order to develop final project work plans and treatment designs.

Strategic-Level Analysis

In recent years, pavement management professionals have recognized the
contributions of pavement management information to support an agency’s
strategic decisions. At this level, individuals such as governmental officials,
upper agency management, and the engineers in charge of pavement main-
tenance, and bridge management all make decisions that play a part in deter-
mining pavement performance targets, establishing needed funding levels to
achieve the performance targets, distributing funds among regions or districts,
and establishing a pavement preservation policy. When strategic-level deci-
sions must be made, there is a direct need to be able to utilize the information
from the pavement management system in order to demonstrate the conse-
quences on the long-term pavement condition of various treatment strategies,
funding levels, or performance targets.
