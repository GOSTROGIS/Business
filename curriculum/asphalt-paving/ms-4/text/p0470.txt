Rollers must be kept in good condition, capable of reversing direction
without jerking or backlash. The line of rolling should not be suddenly
changed nor should the direction of rolling be suddenly reversed, thereby
displacing the mix. Any pronounced change in direction should be made
on stable material. Care should be taken when turning wide rollers to pre-
vent lateral shoving of the mix, especially near unsupported edges.

If rolling causes material displacement, the affected areas should be
loosened at once with lutes or rakes and restored to their original grade
with loose material before being rerolled. Rollers should not be permitted
to stand on the finished surface before it has thoroughly cooled or set.

9.4.5 Sequence of Roller Operations

The number of rollers required on a project is related to the type of
mix, the production rates, and the desired density levels. It is impossible
to establish one roller pattern that will achieve density on all mixes. How-
ever, the generally accepted sequence has three phases: breakdown or
initial rolling, intermediate rolling, and finish rolling. Breakdown rolling
provides most of the increase in density during the compaction operation.
Intermediate rolling further densifies and seals the surface. Finish rolling
removes roller marks and other blemishes left from previous rolling and
minimally increases density.

Breakdown Rolling

Breakdown rolling is usually accomplished with steel-wheel rollers.
Vibratory or static-weight tandem rollers are recommended. Pneumatic-tire
rollers, however, have also been used successfully for breakdown rolling,
but normally should be limited to base courses.

The weight of the roller used for breakdown rolling depends to a large
degree on the temperature, thickness, and stability of the mix being placed.
Generally, a roller weighing 8-12 tonnes (7300-10,900 kilograms) is used
for this operation.

In general, it’s best to start the rolling operation on the low side of the
mat, which is usually the outside of the lane being paved, and progress
toward the high side. The reason is that these HMA mixtures, when hot,
are not as stable as current mix types and tend to migrate toward the
low side of the mat under the action of the roller. If rolling is started on
