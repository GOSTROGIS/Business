The drying and heating of the existing pavement surface is accom-
plished by one or more preheating units (see Figure 13.12). These units use
indirect radiant or infrared heaters generally fueled with propane. The
newest units use high velocity, super heated air that passes through small
holes in a plenum passing near the pavement surface. This super-heated
air is then captured and circulated through the heating unit to re-heat the
air. Multiple units operate close to one another to maximize the penetra-
tion of the heat into the pavement. The preheating units should increase
the temperature of the pavement without hot spots or scorching the exist-
ing pavement. The temperature of the scarified layer should be at least
230°F (110°C) and less than 300°F (150°C). The effectiveness of the pre-
heating process depends on the temperature of the heat source, the ambi-
ent conditions (air and pavement temperatures), moisture content, and the
length of time the pavement surface is exposed to the heat. If the ambient
conditions are such that the pavement is not being properly heated, the
time the surface is exposed to the heat source must be increased. This can
be done by slowing the rate at which the source moves across the surface
or by adding heating units.
