Scrap Tires (Ground Rubber)

Ground rubber may be sized from particles as large as 34 inch (19 millimeters)
to as fine as the No. 100 sieve (150 microns) depending on the type of size-
reduction equipment and the intended application. Production of fine-grain
tire rubber is achieved by buffing at ambient temperature, while larger pieces
of tire rubber are produced in a cryonic process (at a very low temperature
where rubber is brittle). The production of ground rubber is achieved by
granulators, hammer mills, or fine-grinding machines. Granulators typically
produce particles that are regularly shaped and cubical with a comparatively
low-surface area. The steel-belt fragments are removed by a magnetic sepa-
rator. Fiberglass belts or fibers are separated from the finer rubber particles,
usually by an air separator.

Ground rubber has been used as a fine-aggregate substitute in HMA
pavements. In this process, ground-rubber particles are added to the HMA
as a fine aggregate in a gap-graded friction-course type of mixture. This
process, commonly referred to as the dry process, typically uses ground-
rubber particles ranging from approximately “4 inch (6.4 millimeters)
down to the No. 20 sieve (0.85 millimeter). HMA mixes in which ground-
rubber particles are added as a portion of the fine aggregate are referred
to as rubberized asphalt. Another process is where very fine-grain tire
rubber is homogenized in the asphalt at the refinery or terminal. This is
called terminal blend asphalt rubber.

Roofing-Shingle Scrap (Mineral Matter Only)

There are two types of roofing-shingle scrap. They are referred to as tear-
off roofing shingles and roofing-shingle tabs—also called prompt roofing-
shingle scrap. Tear-off roofing shingles are generated during the demolition
or replacement of existing roofs. Roofing-shingle tabs are generated when
new asphalt shingles are trimmed during production to the required physical
dimensions. The quality of tear-off roofing can be quite variable.

The largest component of roofing shingles (60-70 percent by mass) is
the mineral material. There are several types in each shingle. These can
include ceramic granules (comprising crushed-rock particles—typically
trap rock, coated with colored, ceramic oxides), lap granules (coal slag
ground to roughly the same size as the ceramic granules), backsurfacer
sand (washed, natural sand used in small quantities to keep packaged
shingles from sticking together), and asphalt stabilizer (powered limestone
that is mixed into the asphalt).

Roofing shingles incorporated into HMA mixes not only modify the asphalt
binder but also, depending on the size of the shredded material, function like
an aggregate or mineral filler. Organic-felt and glass-felt particles in particular
tend to function like a mineral filler substitute.
