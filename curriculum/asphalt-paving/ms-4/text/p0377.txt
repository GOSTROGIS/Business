by proportioning these individual bins to the target mix formula. This,
of course, depends on the availability of consistently sized individual
aggregates from the aggregate supplier. If the individual aggregates are
not consistent in gradation, then a consistent hot mix cannot be produced
with a drum-mixer. Drum-mixers and the drum-mixing process depend on
the availability of consistent aggregates to make high-quality mix.

The output from the cold-feed bins is really controlled in two ways. The
primary adjustment is the variable-speed motor driving the belt feeder. In
addition to this, the bins are fitted with adjustable gates. This procedure
allows the producer to control the maximum and minimum flows from
the bin. Some mixes require a large quantity of one material—in this case,
the gate is opened wide. Some mixes require a small quantity of another
material, and in this case the bin gate might be lowered. Variable-speed
motors can be inconsistent in their flow at very low rates. They can also
reach their maximum speed at the 95 percent, 96 percent, or 97 percent
range. The most linear range is typically between 10 percent and 90 per-
cent speed. Some are linear between 5 percent and 95 percent speed. It
is best practice, therefore, to use the adjustable gate to ensure that the
motor-drive combination is functioning in the 5-95 percent or 10-90 per-
cent range at all times. Between these speed ranges, if output is charted to
speed, the line usually has a consistent and predictable slope.
