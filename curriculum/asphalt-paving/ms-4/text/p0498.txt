10.3 Testing Methods

The ultimate goal of HMA testing is to accurately measure the appropriate
quality of the materials used. Laboratories must strive to reduce the varia-
tions caused by testing so that test results can be confidently used to evalu-
ate the materials and production methods. However, all parties involved in
quality-control and the acceptance testing process must understand the vari-
ability of test results that comes from the testing methods themselves. All
parties must recognize that the variability related to the test method can be
reduced but never eliminated. This is especially important for test parameters
that will be used for pay factor determination, rejection or acceptance of the
materials produced.

The test methods used will be established in the quality-assurance plan
and the project specifications. Agencies or owners may change or modify
the standard test procedures. It is important that the laboratories get a
clear understanding of the methods to be used before starting the project.
Agencies modify standard methods to reduce variation from one lab to
another. ASTM D 979, Standard Practice for Sampling Bituminous Paving
Mixtures, for example, covers sampling of HMA at points of manufacture,
storage, delivery, or in place. A variety of sampling methods are included
within this one standard, so an agency’s project specifications might stipu-
late which method is required. Many agencies hold pre-paving meetings
for projects. These meetings are a good forum for clarifying the methods
and the roles of the parties involved in quality-control and. acceptance
testing.

The following sections describe testing usually provided for quality-
control and acceptance of HMA.

10.3.1 Aggregate Gradation

A tremendous amount of work goes into processing the aggregate mate-
rials to achieve a specific gradation in the finished product. The final gra-
dation can be impacted by processes in the mining, crushing, washing,
stockpiling, and handling, or in the blending of the aggregates. Quality-
control must be exercised throughout all processes in order to achieve the
final gradation.

Different sampling methods may be used in the various stages of process-
ing aggregates, but the test procedures are generally the same. An excep-
tion to this is the use of unwashed gradations for quality-control testing.
