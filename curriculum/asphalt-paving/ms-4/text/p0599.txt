Optimization

The most sophisticatedapproach for determining the optimal program uses
mathematical programming techniques as part of a two-step optimization
process. First, the objectives for the analysis are defined in mathematical
terms, such as maximizing network conditions. Any constraints to the
analysis are also defined, such as limits on funding available or minimum
allowable conditions. Using mathematical programming techniques such
as linear programming or dynamic programming, the optimal solution
for the problem set is determined. At this first stage, recommendations
are stated in terms of the number of miles to move from one condition
category to another.

The second stage of the analysis matches pavement sections to the
recommended strategy. For instance, if the optimal strategy indicates that
20 miles of roads in poor condition should be moved into good condition
(through a treatment such as reconstruction), then the agency must identify
the 20 miles of poor pavement that will be included in the program.

Because of its complexity, a true optimization analysis is used by only
a few large agencies.

11.4.5 Maintaining the Pavement
Preservation Program

To ensure the success of a pavement preservation program, the
preservation philosophy should be integrated into the agency’s decisions at
all levels. Several keys to the success of an ongoing pavement preservation
program are listed here.

Support of Upper Management

The support of upper management is critical to the success of a
pavement preservation program, especially in the provision of the funds
needed for pavement preservation activities. Upper management support
is also important when outside pressure for fixing the worst roads first is
applied. Using a pavement management system, upper management can
demonstrate the negative long-term impacts of projects initiated through
political or other outside pressure and often minimize the inclusion in the
program.
