In many areas, the use of cutback asphalt is not permitted because of
the release of volatiles into the atmosphere. Local air quality requirements
should be checked to determine if cutbacks can be used. As a substitute,
asphalt suppliers have developed special penetrating emulsion materials
for use as prime coats, which are spray applied in the same manner as
cutback asphalts.

Tack Coats

Tack coats are a very light application of liquid asphalt applied with a
distributor to an existing nonporous surface. The purpose of a tack coat
is to promote bonding between the underlying surface and the new HMA
layer. Normally, an asphalt emulsion or hot asphalt cement is used for a
tack coat, but some agencies allow the use of other asphalt products. The
tack coat should be placed on a clean, dry pavement, free of any materials
that might prevent bonding. The application rate will vary depending on
the type and condition of the surface being overlaid. The goal is to place a
thin, uniform coating of asphalt covering 90-100 percent of the pavement
surface. The application rate for a tack coat is normally 0.05-0.15 gallons
per square yard (0.25-0.70 liter per square meter) of diluted slow-setting
cationic or anionic emulsion (SS-1, SS-1h, CSS-1, or CSS-ih). A diluted
emulsion is an emulsion that has been diluted using one part water to one
part asphalt emulsion. If a given emulsion contains 65 percent residual
asphalt in its original state, then after a 50-50 dilution, the product applied
to the roadway would contain a 32.5 percent residual asphalt. Spread rate
and uniformity are hard to control when using an asphalt distributor set
at a very low application rate. By diluting the tack coat, the emulsion can
be applied at a much higher spread rate while providing a uniformly thin
coating of residual asphalt. A low application rate is usually used between
new or un-trafficked pavement layers. An intermediate rate is required
when resurfacing an existing, relatively smooth, asphalt pavement. The
highest rate is used on old oxidized, cracked, pocked, or milled HMA or
PCC pavement surfaces.
