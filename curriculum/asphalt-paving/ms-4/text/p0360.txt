¢ The baghouse cleaning and fines return must stop when the dryer
stops. This keeps large quantities of dust from being transferred to
the mixing area of the drum or deposited in the fines bin on the batch
plant when the aggregate feed stops.

* Large and rapid increases in production rate should be avoided. It
takes time for the dust to be collected on the bags, cleaned off the
bags, and then returned to the system. Large jumps in production
rate can be immediately accomplished by resetting the cold feed bins,
but it takes time for the dust corresponding to the production-rate
increase to be collected on the bags and returned to the mix. The mix
produced during that time will be short of -200 mesh (75 micron)
material and high in voids. Large, rapid decreases in production have
the opposite effect. Most modern control systems ramp production up
and down in small intervals because of this.

¢ Fines-return equipment inspection should be done regularly. When
the fines-return equipment wears, it may only be able to return a
certain quantity of fines. Test results are characterized by a shortage
of fines and an increase in voids above a certain rate on the plant
system.

When partial dust return is required, several equipment options exist
for both batch plants and drum-mix plants. Most plant owners purchase a
storage silo for transferring the collected fines for reuse or for storage for
later use. If the silo fills to capacity, accumulated fines are usually trucked
off and wasted or used for other purposes, such as in the manufacture of
slurry seals. Figures 7.14 and 7.15 show this type of equipment.

Baghouse dust stored in silos can either be reintroduced into the process
volumetrically with a calibrated feeder valve (with output charted to the
speed of the feeder valve, as similarly done in the calibration process of
cold feed bins), or it can be weighed back into the process.

With a batch plant, dust can be returned with a calibrated feeder to the
hot aggregate elevator, be weighed back through the aggregate weigh
hopper as an additional hot bin draw, or be weighed in a separate scale
and then deposited into the mixer. All these options are common.

In a drum-mix plant, material can similarly be returned through a
calibrated valve, or a negative-weighing pod can be used to continu-
ously weigh dust back. Figure 7.16 shows this type of negative weigh pod
system.

Local specifications often determine which method of dust reentry is
desirable or required. The important point is that many options exist for
returning captured dust to the mix production process. Mix requirements
and local specifications dictate the required equipment options.
