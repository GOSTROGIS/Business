The mix can be designed using the Superpave design procedures discussed
in Chapter 4, “Hot Mix Asphalt (HMA) Design.” But, rather than having a
design air-voids content of 4 percent, these mixes should be designed having
a design air-voids content of 2—4 percent. This will allow more asphalt in the
mix, thereby improving durability.

The asphalt binder grade chosen for a recreational facility should be
based on the climate conditions. A standard, unmodified asphalt binder
can be used. It is suggested that the cold temperature properties of the
selected binder be one grade lower than that used for highways (if avail-
able locally). For example, if a PG 64-22 binder is used for highways in a
region, a PG 64-28 or PG 58-28 would be appropriate for the recreational
facility.

Construction Considerations

Before construction, all vegetation and all unsuitable soils should be
removed and the subgrade should be scarified and compacted to at least
95 percent of modified proctor density (ASTM D 1557/AASHTO T 180). After
compaction, the subgrade should be proof-rolled with a heavy pneumatic
roller or dump truck to ensure that there are no soft spots. Any soft spots
identified should be removed and replaced with a stable material. After
compaction, the subgrade should be treated with an herbicide. This must be
done carefully to insure that surrounding vegetation is not adversely affected.
If an aggregate base course is used, it should also be compacted to at least
95 percent of modified proctor density.

For bicycle/walking paths, the asphalt layer can be placed in one lift.
To enhance surface smoothness on tennis courts and running tracks, an
asphalt layer placed in two equal lifts is recommended. Most tennis court
specifications require that tolerance not vary more than % inch in 10 feet
(3 millimeters in 3 meters) when measured in any direction.
