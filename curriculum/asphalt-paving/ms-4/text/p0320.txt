Economics demands that pavement designers use lower-cost unbound
granular base materials when possible to reduce the initial cost of the pave-
ment structure. But while these materials are less expensive to produce and
place, they are not as effective in distributing traffic-induced stresses to the
subgrade. As a result, thick layers are required to support even low-volume
truck traffic. Typically, conventional HMA pavements consist of thick aggre-
gate base layers (6-30 inches) (150-750 millimeters) coupled with relatively
thin HMA surface courses @-5 inches) (75-125 millimeters). In a conven-
tional design, the HMA wearing surface acts to provide a smooth riding, all-
weather, waterproof surface, while the majority of the structural capacity is
derived from the granular base layer. For many agencies in the United States,
conventional HMA pavement sections provide an affordable solution for low-
to moderate-volume roads.

Full-Depth Asphalt

A full-depth asphalt pavement is one in which asphalt mixtures are
employed for all courses above the subgrade or improved subgrade.
HMA or asphalt-treated base layers are placed directly on the subgrade.
Asphalt-stabilized base layers in a full-depth pavement need not be
constructed as thick as the comparable aggregate base layer in a con-
ventional HMA pavement. The HMA wearing course creates a strong,
durable, smooth, and cost-effective pavement structure.

Full-depth asphalt pavements are able to resist traffic loads in an efficient
“low-profile” pavement section. Asphalt-aggregate mixtures resist these
traffic-induced stresses far better than unbonded aggregate layers, which
have no tensile strength. Therefore, an HMA layer spreads the wheel load
over a broad area. As a result, less total pavement structure is required.
Additional benefits of using full-depth asphalt pavements include reduced
permeability and subsurface drainage costs, increased speed of construc-
tion, and an extended paving season when placing thick lifts.

Full-depth asphalt pavements have been in place in the United States
since the 1960s and 1970s, especially in high-volume traffic applications.
Our collective experience over 40 years of building successful full-depth
asphalt pavements has served as an important step toward developing the
Perpetual Pavement philosophy.

Perpetual Pavement

Perpetual Pavements are designed and built to last longer than 50 years
without requiring major structural rehabilitation or reconstruction. Like full-
depth asphalt pavements, Perpetual Pavements are designed using HMA or
asphalt-stabilized layers in all layers above the subgrade. What sets Perpetual
Pavements apart is the additional care taken in mix type and material selection
at all layers within the pavement structure.

The Perpetual Pavement concept improves upon the full-depth asphalt
design method by incorporating a fatigue-resistant base layer and rut-
resistant intermediate and surface layers into the pavement structure.
These mix type selection techniques will minimize fatigue and rutting in
the pavement structure.
