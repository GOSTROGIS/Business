Missing tips, excessive clearance between the tips and the liner, and
improper pattern to the tips all negatively affect the mixing efficiency of
the pugmill.

Overfilling or underfilling the pugmill also causes inadequate mixing.
When the pugmill is overfilled, the asphalt binder and the aggregates that
were drawn last do not have a chance to be pulled down into the rest
of the materials. Both asphalt content and gradation will be negatively
affected and inconsistent throughout the batch. Significantly underfilling a
mixer may also result in less than desirable results and cause segregation
in large-rock mixes. A reasonable batch size should be respected.

Batch mixing time should be long enough to produce a homogenous
mixture of evenly distributed and uniformly coated particles. Mixing time
can be established for a particular mix by the procedure noted in AASHTO
T 195 (ASTM D 2489). This method bases the degree of mixing on the
percentage of coarse particles that are completely coated with asphalt. The
method is correlated with mixing time.

The test involves separating coarse aggregate particles from the mix on
a selected sieve size and examining 200 to 300 particles under a strong
light. Any speck of uncoated aggregate particle visible to the naked eye is
classified as uncoated. Usually particles coated at 90 and 95 percent are
minimums for base and surface mixes, respectively. The least time needed
to achieve these minimum coating levels is the most desirable mixing
time. Most mixes will undergo additional mixing and coating as they are
handled through slat conveyors, storage silos, and paving equipment. The
common total batch mixing time is 25-35 seconds.

7.3.4 Recycled Mix Production with a
Batch Plant

With a batch-type plant, two basic systems for recycled mix production
are popular: the weigh-hopper method and the bucket-elevator method.
With both systems, virgin aggregate is super-heated in the dryer and then
the RAP is introduced later in the plant process. The super-heated virgin
aggregates heat and dry the wet ambient RAP. The steam and dust released
from the RAP has to be ducted to the exhaust system and the dust col-
lection equipment. Because the RAP is not introduced into the aggregate
dryer (as it is in a drum-mixer plant), additional ducting has to be added
to the plant to accomplish this goal.

With the weigh-hopper method, RAP is either introduced into the
aggregate-weigh hopper in the batch tower as an additional ingredient, or
an additional weigh hopper is added specifically for the RAP. The control
system weighs the RAP. One must adjust the binder content in the final
mix recipe for the usable binder in the RAP (see Chapter 4, “Hot Mix
Asphalt GHMA) Design”).
