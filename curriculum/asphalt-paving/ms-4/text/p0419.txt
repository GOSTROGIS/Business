Screed Weight

The screed weight is usually translated into pounds per square inch
(kilograms per square meter), or the vertical force applied by the screed
into the HMA mixture being laid. This unit weight is constant except when
extending or retracting the variable-width screed. Care must be taken to
keep the head of material from fluctuating when extending the screed.
When the screed is operated manually, a slight change in the angle of
attack may need to be made by using the screed-depth controls to maintain
the desired HMA pavement thickness. If automatic grade and slope con-
trols are being used, the angle of attack will automatically change. Using
the manual override on the auger controls can help minimize the screed-
depth changes. When extending the screed width, the augers should be
controlled manually to supply a constant volume of material in front of
the screed extension. When the extension is retracted, the auger should be
manually shut off to reduce the amount of material in front of the exten-
sion so that it can be retracted without causing the screed to rise.

HMA Stiffness (Loose)

A change in the temperature of the HMA or the gradation of the aggre-
gates can result in a change in the stability or stiffness of the loose HMA
during paving. This change in stiffness will cause the screed to either rise
or fall as it reacts to a change in vertical force and seeks to rebalance all
of the forces acting upon it.

Screed Extensions

Screed extensions are rigidly mounted, fixed-width, or hydraulically
powered variable-width attachments that extend beyond the width of
the main screed to facilitate paving widths that are wider than the basic
machine width. Both types of extensions provide full screed plates with
heat and compaction similar to the cross section of the main screed. This
allows the HMA placed by the extension to have the same characteristics
in terms of texture, smoothness, and precompaction as the material placed
by the main screed. Both types of extensions can be angled differently
than the main screed, allowing adjacent lanes and shoulders to be paved
at a different cross-slope than the main screed.

Variable-Width Screeds

With a variable-width screed, the paving width can be changed with the
push of a button while still maintaining a uniform surface across the entire
HMA mat being placed (see Figure 8.17). Extensions on the variable-width
screeds can be either front- or rear-mounted and will require different
adjustments to achieve a quality HMA pavement. The extensions can usu-
ally be sloped positively or negatively at the outer edges of the main screed
so the adjacent lane(s) can be paved at the same time as the mainline.
