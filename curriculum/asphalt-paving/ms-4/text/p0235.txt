3. Manufacturing of test specimens with a particular blend or
gradation of aggregates. These test specimens are prepared for
a range of different asphalt contents so that the test data curves
show a well-defined “optimum” value. Tests are conducted on
specimens that differ by 0.5 percent increments of asphalt content,
with at least two asphalt contents above optimum and at least
two below optimum. For example, for a mix where the estimated
asphalt content is 4.5 percent, three samples each would be made
at 3.5, 4.0, 4.5, 5.0, and 5.5 percent asphalt by weight for a total of
15 samples.

a. These samples are compacted using the Marshall compaction
hammer (Figure 4.18). Depending on the requirements of the
agency, generally either 50 blows or 75 blows are used.

b. The bulk specific gravity of each compacted sample is deter-
mined for use in the voids analysis.

c. The samples are tested in the Marshall testing machine (Figure
4.19) for stability and flow. The Marshall stability and flow values
are determined. The requirements for Marshall stability and flow
will vary with the agency and the engineer.

4. A minimum of 2 additional uncompacted samples would be used
for the determination of the maximum specific gravity as discussed
in Section 4.2. The data from the maximum specific gravity test
and the bulk specific gravity test are used, along with the specific
gravities of the aggregates, to determine the volumetric properties
of the mix. The data is plotted as shown in Figure 4.20.

The information shown in the plotted data is used to determine the
optimum asphalt content for the mixture. The most common method is
to determine the asphalt content that corresponds to 4 percent air-voids.
That asphalt content is then used to determine the values for Marshall
stability, flow, VMA, and VFA. For the example shown, the asphalt content
would be 4.7 percent. Each of the remaining criteria are compared against
the specification requirements. If they fall within the specification require-
ments, the asphalt content at 4 percent air-voids is the asphalt content
chosen for production.

4.4.3 Mix Design Criteria

Whether the asphalt paving mix will be satisfactory from the design
asphalt content selected is determined by applying certain limiting criteria
to test data for the mix at its design asphalt content. An engineer design-
ing an HMA mixture for an agency should check that agency’s specific
requirements. The design criteria in Table 4.6 are recommended by the
Asphalt Institute. If all the criteria are met, then this is the design asphalt
content. If any of the design criteria are not met, then some adjustment is
necessary or the mix may need to be redesigned.
