5.5 Drainage of Asphalt

Pavement Structures

5.3.1 Introduction

Adequate drainage of the pavement structure is considered the most
important element in ensuring long-term pavement performance. The
proper design of the drainage system for a particular pavement is of para-
mount importance. This section of the manual is a brief overview of the
issues to be considered in designing pavement drainage. For additional
details, consult MS-15, Drainage of Asphalt Pavements or the resources
listed at the end of the chapter.

Reason for Drainage

Highway engineers recognize the critical need for good drainage in
designing and constructing pavements. Probably no other feature is as
important in determining the ability of a pavement to withstand the effects
of weather and traffic, and in providing trouble-free service over long
periods of time. Reducing the moisture content of the granular base and
subgrade through the use of effective drainage directly affects fatigue
cracking of the HMA surfaces, and thus the life of the pavement.

Water in a subgrade is usually slow to evaporate or drain. Even in dry
weather, the subgrade soils may remain damp or wet indefinitely. Often, voids
in porous materials become subterranean reservoirs unless they are drained.

With a few exceptions, most subgrade soils contain silt and clay, which
weaken when wet. This weakening may be hastened by vibrations from traf-
fic. If the subgrade is not properly shaped and sloped for drainage, water
may collect at the interface of the granular base or subbase and the subgrade
soils. As this water saturates the subgrade, the subgrade soils tend to lose sta-
bility and thus the strength required to support the loads on the pavement.
Under traffic, wet soil may be worked into a fine-grained slurry that pushes
up into voids in the granular base or subbase course, thus destroying the
grain-to-grain interlock and weakening the granular materials.

Design
In developing the various features of a drainage system, consideration
should be given to the following principal purposes:
1. To collect and drain water from the pavement surface
2. To collect subsurface water in the granular layers and keep the
subgrade moisture content as low as possible
3. To prevent or retard embankment erosion
4. To intercept water from surrounding areas and carry it from
the area
5. To lower the groundwater table
