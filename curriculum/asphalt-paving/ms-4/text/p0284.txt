Under the AASHTO system, soils having approximately the same general
load-carrying capacity and service characteristics are grouped together to
form one of seven basic groups (A-1, A-2, A-3, A-4, A-5, A-6, and A-7). In
general, the best highway subgrade soils are classified as A-1. Poorer soils
are rated in numerical order, with the A-7 group being the poorest. The
exception is the A-3 group. Soils classified A-3 are better subgrades than
soils in the A-2 group. From this, it can generally be assumed for a given
set of conditions that the thickness of the pavement section must progres-
sively increase as the soil classification group increases from A-1 to A-7,
except as noted.

The USCS is based on the Airfield Classification System developed by
Professor Arthur Casagrande of Harvard University during World War I.
In 1952, Casagrande, together with the Bureau of Reclamation and the
U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, modified the system for a broad range of
applications, including dams and foundations.

The USCS groups soils based on grain size and plasticity. The system is
rooted in the concept that coarse-grained soils can be classified accord-
ing to grain-size distribution, while it is more appropriate to classify fine-
grained soils based on plasticity characteristics. These characteristics are
evaluated by plotting the plasticity index (PD versus the liquid limit (LL)
on a standard plasticity chart. The position of the plotted points yields
information for predicting soil behavior as an engineering construction
material. The following properties form the basis of soil identification:

® Plasticity and compressibility characteristics

A soil is given a descriptive name and letter symbol to indicate its
principal characteristics.
