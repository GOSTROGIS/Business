15.11 Porous Pavements

15.11.11 Introduction

A porous asphalt pavement can be used to reduce the runoff from
parking lots and paved roads. A porous asphalt pavement consists of a
permeable asphalt surface placed over a granular working platform on top
of a reservoir of large single size stone. The asphalt surface is an open-
graded friction course designed using the principles in Chapter 4, “Hot Mix
Asphalt (HMA) Design.” The lower reservoir of large single-size stone has
sufficient voids to create adequate storage capacity. A properly designed
porous asphalt pavement can provide a solution to storm-water runoff
problems as well as groundwater table recharge. The National Asphalt
Pavement Association’s Design, Construction, and Maintenance Guide
for Porous Asphalt Pavements, Information Series 131, contains detailed
information on the design and construction of porous asphalt pavements.
The following summarizes that information.

15.11.2 General Design Principles

A porous asphalt pavement consists of a porous asphalt course (usually
an OGFC), a top filter course (of %-inch [12.5 millimeters] aggregate), a
reservoir course of aggregate (114-3 inches; 37.5-75 millimeters thick), a
filter fabric and the existing soil or subgrade material. Figure 15.18 shows
a typical porous pavement. The site conditions, such as soil characteristics,
local topography, climate, and traffic are the key items in determining the
applicability of a porous pavement for a specific site. In addition, local
storm-water regulations must be followed. The porous pavement should be
designed for a 25-year/24-hour storm event. Other environmental factors
must be considered. Frost penetration must be evaluated in determining
the reservoir course thickness. Porous pavements are not recommended
for barren areas subject to moderate to high winds because of the pres-
ence of blowing dust in those areas. Dust, sand, and salt used for snow
and ice removal should be kept off a porous pavement because they can
clog the voids in the porous pavement surface.
