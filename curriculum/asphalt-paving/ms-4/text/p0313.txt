geotextile, clean mortar sand surrounding a perforated pipe encased
in a suitable geotextile sock (to keep the sand out of the pipe) has
provided excellent long-term performance as a drain. The sketch on

* Geotextiles have successfully served as replacements to aggregate
filters that prevent the migration of fines into untreated aggregate lay-
ers. In this type of installation, the geotextile typically lies between
the layer with fines (subbase or subgrade) and a clean aggregate base
layer. It should be noted that a geotextile will not work as a separa-
tion layer in cases of ‘reversing’ flow. This can occur with thin granular
layers or with heavy wheel loads. The use of a separating layer also
prevents the buildup of hydrostatic or seepage pressures.

Installation of geotextiles as filter layers is critical to performance. Sufficient

care should be taken to ensure adequate overlap in both the vertical and

horizontal directions and that the geotextile is installed according to manufac-
turer’s recommendations. If the geotextile has gaps or tears, or if it has been
installed with insufficient overlap, it will not perform as intended. Installation
is relatively easy and cost-effective as compared to placing a two layer aggre-
gate filter (a coarser aggregate next to the pipe and a finer filter aggregate
next to the subgrade soils) in separate layers in a trench without contamina-
tion. Care must be taken in backfilling the trenches to ensure that the pipe is

not crushed by construction equipment.
