The asphalt binder for SMA mixtures has typically been a stiffer binder
than is usually specified for dense-graded mixtures. They are usually polymer-
modified mixes. The use of stiffer binders will tend to reduce the draindown of
the asphalt binder during construction. A modified asphalt may also improve
the high and low temperature properties of the SMA mix.

A stabilizer is typically used in the SMA mixture to prevent draindown of
the asphalt binder during construction. Two types of fibers have been used:
cellulose fibers and mineral fibers. Both types of fibers have been used
successfully (Figures 4.25 and 4.26). The mineral fiber is usually heavier
than the cellulose fibers and is therefore added at a higher percentage (by
weight) so that a sufficient volume of fibers is added.

Selection of Desired Gradation

Once satisfactory materials have been identified, the optimum aggregate
gradation and binder content must be determined. This is accomplished by
first selecting an appropriate aggregate blend. This blended gradation should
provide an aggregate skeleton with stone-on-stone contact and furnish a mix-
ture that meets or exceeds the minimum voids in mineral aggregate. The con-
dition of stone-on-stone contact is defined as the point at which the voids
in the coarse aggregate fraction of the blend of the compacted SMA mixture
(VCA, ,.) is less than the VCA of the coarse aggregate fraction of the aggregate
blend alone (VCA,,.) in the dry-rodded test (AASHTO T 19 or ASTM C 29).
