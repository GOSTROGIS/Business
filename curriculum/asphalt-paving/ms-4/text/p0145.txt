3.1.1 Introduction

The amount of aggregate in asphalt paving mixtures is generally 90-95
percent by weight or 75-85 percent by volume. Aggregate is primarily respon-
sible for the load-supporting capacity of pavement; accordingly, performance
of a hot mix asphalt (HMA) mixture is heavily influenced by the aggregate.

The general term aggregate refers to any hard, inert mineral material used
for mixing in graduated particles or fragments. ASTM D 8 defines aggregate
as “a granular material of mineral composition such as sand, gravel, shell, slag,
or crushed stone, used with a cementing medium to form mortars or concrete
or alone as in base courses, railroad ballasts, etc.”

Aggregates can be either natural or manufactured. Natural aggregates
are generally fragments extracted and processed from rock formations
or rock deposits. Manufactured aggregates are derived from raw materi-
als that have been physically or chemically changed or from industrial
by-products that have been processed.

3.1.2 Origin of Rocks

The purpose of this section is not to describe in detail the origin of bed-
rock, from which most aggregate material is derived. A brief discussion. is
in order, however, because knowledge of the origin of rock is important for
understanding the production and performance of mineral aggregates.

Rocks are divided into three general classifications: sedimentary,
igneous, and metamorphic. These classifications describe how rocks are

Sedimentary Rocks

Sedimentary rocks are formed by the accumulation of sediment deposited
by water or wind. With sufficient overburden, the sedimentation re-solidifies
more or less to solid rock. The sediment may consist of rock or mineral frag-
ments or particles of various sizes (conglomerate, sandstone, shale), the remains
or products of animals or plants (certain limestone and coal), the product of
chemical action or evaporation (salt, gypsum), or mixtures of these materi-
als. Some sedimentary deposits consist of particles blown from volcanoes and
deposited on land or in water. A characteristic feature of sedimentary depos-
its is a layered structure. This stratification is a direct result of the method of
formation—the material having been deposited over some lake or sea bottom.
