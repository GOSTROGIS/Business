Implementation of testing programs varies from one agency to another.
Some agencies require contractors to provide specific quality-control test-
ing, while others rely only on their own test results. Some agencies are
now using contractor quality-control test results in the acceptance deci-
sion. When alternative contracting mechanisms are used by public agen-
cies, such as “Design Build” and “Construction Manager at Risk,” new
paradigms may be needed to understand each entity’s responsibility in the
quality-assurance program.

Quality-assurance programs lead to comparisons of test results from
multiple laboratories. This has created an increased emphasis on accu-
racy and repeatability of test results. Many agencies now require labo-
ratories and technicians to be pre-qualified before providing testing in
their quality-assurance programs. Many state highway agencies have their
own certification programs for laboratories and technicians, but national
certifications are also available from organizations such as the American
Concrete Institute (ACD, the National Institute for Certification of Engineer-
ing Technicians (NICET) and the Asphalt Institute Mix Design Technologies
Certification Program for technicians, and the AASHTO Materials Reference
Laboratory (AMRL), or the National Voluntary Laboratory Accreditation
Program (NVLAP) for laboratories. ASTM D 3666, “Minimum Requirements
for Agencies Testing and Inspecting Bituminous Paving Materials,” is a
standard specification to address the testing laboratory and technician
capabilities in performing tests on bituminous materials. This standard can
be a good reference when establishing a quality-assurance program.

10.1.2 Job-Mix Formula

For quality-control and acceptance of hot mix asphalt, specification lim-
its are often based on a job-mix formula (JMF) established as part of the
mixture design. The mix design will produce a JMF that establishes the tar-
get values for production of the HMA for the project. The JMF is the com-
bination of aggregate and asphalt binder materials proposed for use on a
project that, when tested using the required mix design procedures, yields
results that meet all the established design criteria. At a minimum, the JMF
should include the following target parameters from the mix design:

° Asphalt content

° Aggregate gradation

° Laboratory air voids

° Stockpile blend percentages

° Hot-bin blend percentages (for batch plants)

° Other parameters specified for quality-control such as mixture volu-

metrics or aggregate angularity

* Specific materials used (that is, sources for asphalt cement, admixtures,

ageregate descriptions, etc.)
