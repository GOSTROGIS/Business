A continuous flow of HMA material from the plant to the paver ensures
a consistently high-quality HMA pavement. The tonnage to be produced,
the size and types of trucks, the time to make one trip from plant to
paver, how much HMA to be placed and compacted—all are details that
must be considered. These parameters dictate the necessary number of
trucks. The time between each delivery must be maintained at a consis-
tent level, and the trucks must not be allowed to bunch up at the paver
or at the plant. Trucks may be delayed in traffic (construction traffic con-
trol can be a major cause of backups that delay the material delivery
trucks). If a break in the delivery of material occurs, the paver should be
stopped using appropriate techniques such as the rapid stop/start method
(the paver is stopped and started without an extended acceleration/
de-acceleration period). Paving should not resume until a sufficient num-
ber of trucks are on the job site to ensure nearly continuous paving. Start-
ing and stopping the paver for each truck as it arrives on the project will
reduce the smoothness and quality of the finished pavement.

8.3.1 Types of Haul Trucks

Three types of haul vehicles are used to transport HMA from the plant
to the paver: end-dump, bottom- or belly-dump, and live-bottom. Proper
trucking procedures are necessary for maintaining a continuous flow of
material to the paver hopper.

End-Dump Trucks

The end-dump truck (see Figure 8.2) delivers mix directly to the paver
hopper and may have three to six axles. It can have a capacity of 12 to
20 tons (11 to 18 tonnes). More axles mean more capacity. The advan-
tage of the end-dump truck is its short wheelbase, which makes it easy
to maneuver. The end-dump truck can also be a semitrailer truck (see
Figure 8.3), which is essentially a large dump truck, with a capacity of 20
to 25 tons (18 to 23 tonnes). The disadvantage with this type of truck is
that improved operator skill is needed and its large dump bed can be the
source of problems with overhead obstructions (wires, bridges, and trees).
Because of this truck’s size, special care must be exercised when loading
the truck because of the increased potential for aggregate segregation.
