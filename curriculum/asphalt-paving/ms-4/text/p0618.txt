After cleaning, the cracks should be checked for depth. A backer rod
should be placed in large deep cracks to conserve sealant. The backer rod
should be a compressible, non-shrinking, nonabsorbent material with a
melting point higher than the temperature of the sealant. The backer rod
should be about 25 percent wider than the crack so that it does not slip
into or float out of the crack after installing the sealant.

Once the cracks are prepared, they are sealed with liquid asphalt. The
material may be applied using hand-carried pour pots, wheel-mounted
pour pots, or the wand of a small distributor. Pour only the amount of
material that will seal the crack. Do not try to completely fill the crack
because this is a waste of sealant. The idea is to just coat the surfaces of
the crack, with a small amount of sealant settling in the bottom of the
crack (see Figure 12.12).

Any spillage or excess material on the pavement surface should be
removed with a squeegee. Any surface so cleared of material should also
be dusted with blotter sand to prevent tracking of the sealant by traffic.

This procedure applies to small, medium, and large cracks. Hairline
cracks will not accept sealant, and because hairline cracks are so nar-
row, significant amounts of moisture will not penetrate anyway. If hairline
cracks are numerous, a mass crack-filling procedure should be used.
