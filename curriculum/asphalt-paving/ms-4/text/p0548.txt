Performance-based specifications are quality-assurance specifications
that describe the desired levels of fundamental engineering properties
(resilient modulus, creep properties, and fatigue) that are predictors of
performance and appear in primary prediction relationships (models that
can be used to predict stress, distress, or performance from combinations
of predictors that represent traffic, environment, supporting materials, and
structural conditions).

Performance Specifications are specifications that require the con-
tractor to take responsibility for the actual performance of the product
produced. These specifications define the time frame and establish clear
performance standards. Performance specifications are also called War-
ranty Specifications and are addressed in Section 10.10.

10.9.2 Performance-Related Specifications

Performance-related specifications use quality characteristics already
being measured and specified by many agencies. These characteristics are
then correlated to the life-cycle cost of the pavement.

Three key points distinguish performance-related specifications from
others. One is that the quality characteristics that are tested and used
for acceptance are related to fundamental engineering properties of the
pavement. Another is that the fundamental engineering properties use
mathematical models to predict the extent and the timing of distress in the
pavement. The third point is that the specifications use the distress predic-
tion in mathematically predicted life-cycle costs for establishing contract
requirements, including penalties and rewards.
