A uniformly graded aggregate structure consists of particles that are
primarily of one size or their range of sizes is very narrow. The amount
of particle-to-particle contact is low, and the interlock of the aggregates is
low. The amount of voids between the particles is high; thus, permeability of
the aggregate structure is high. Uniformly graded aggregates are not usually
desired in asphalt mixtures.

Some asphalt mixes have an aggregate structure that is called open-graded.
This is actually a uniformly graded aggregate structure with a very small
amount of fine-aggregate particles. Open-graded mixes are most often used
for their characteristics of high permeability and high surface friction. Surface
courses made with these mixes are also called “porous friction courses.”

A well-graded aggregate structure has an extensive distribution of particle
sizes ranging from fine to coarse. The smaller aggregate particles fill in gaps
and voids between larger particles. The grading is considered dense in that
the concentration of aggregate particles is high and the amount of voids
between aggregate particles is small. Permeability of the aggregate structure
is low, and interlock between aggregate particles is high. Paving mixtures
with such aggregate structures may be easily compacted but tend to tightly
pack and have air-void contents that are too low.

Gap-graded aggregates refer to the gradation characteristic in which there
is a “gap” in the particle-size distribution. There is a range of particle size
fractions (typically the mid-size range) that is either devoid of particles or
the amount of particles in that range is low. Gap-graded aggregates are
not as dense in particle concentration as well-graded aggregates. In turn,
the amount of voids between aggregate particles and the permeability are
higher than that of well-graded aggregates. Many times, asphalt mixtures
must necessarily have the greater voids content of a gap-graded aggregates
so that the mix can contain an adequate amount and coating thickness of
asphalt binder. Gap-graded aggregate structures typically have good inter-
lock between particles.

Two other dense-gradation types that are often mentioned are “coarse-
graded” or “fine-graded.” Fine-graded aggregate structures usually contain a
greater amount of fine aggregate, generally resulting in HMA mixtures with
higher asphalt content, a lesser compactive effort needed during placement,
a more closed-surface texture, and an increased potential for surface defor-
mation. Coarse-graded aggregate structures, on the other hand, contain a
lesser amount of fine aggregate. HMA mixtures with a coarse grading tend
to have a lower asphalt content, require more compactive effort, have a more
open-surface texture, and have a lower potential for surface deformation.
