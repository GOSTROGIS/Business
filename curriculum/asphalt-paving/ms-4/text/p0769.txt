The HMA overlayment design involves placing the HMA layers in a similar
manner except that no intervening ballast is used between the HMA layer
and the ties (Figure 15.9). The ties are placed directly on the smooth, plain
HMA surface. Cribbing aggregate is placed between and at the end of the
ties to restrain track movement. This design has been used extensively with
high-speed rail facilities. In the U.S., the HMA overlayment method has been
limited to special applications, such as rail transfer yards.

15.3.2 Desirable Benefits and Applications
of HMA Underlayment

An HMA underlayment has the following benefits:

e A strengthened track-support layer below the ballast to uniformly
distribute reduced pressures to the roadbed (subgrade)

e A waterproofing layer and confinement to the underlying roadbed to
provide consistent load-carrying capability for track structures even
on roadbeds of marginal quality

° An impermeable layer to divert water to side ditches, essentially
eliminating roadbed (subgrade) moisture fluctuations, to consistently
maintain adequate underlying support

° A consistently high level of confinement for the ballast to provide
high shear strength and uniform pressure distribution

° A resilient layer between the ballast and roadbed to reduce the like-
lihood of subgrade pumping without substantially increasing track
stiffness

e An all-weather, uniformly stable surface to place the ballast and track
superstructure
