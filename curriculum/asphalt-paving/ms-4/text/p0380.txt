The asphalt pumping and metering system on a drum-mix plant is a con-
tinuously operating unit. It pumps liquid asphalt continuously and measures
the result of that flow with a meter. It can increase or decrease the flow
dynamically with the plant’s automation.

There are two key equipment elements to this process—the asphalt flow
control mechanism and the asphalt meter or measuring device.

Several types of flow control mechanisms have been used over the years,
but all manufacturers now favor a variable-speed motor on the asphalt pump
for this purpose. If more asphalt is required, the pump is accelerated. If less
asphalt is needed, the pump is slowed. Most producers have converted their
plants to this technique because it eliminates the maintenance on the wear
parts of the previous systems and is generally considered trouble-free.

Figure 7.30 illustrates how this process works. The belt scale reports the
wet aggregate flow to the control system. The control system will adjust the
wet scale value for the moisture in the material, based on the aggregate-
moisture data entered by the operator. Entering the proper moisture quantity
is important and is typically done by entering the moistures of the individual
materials into a “moisture screen.” The control system then calculates the
combined moisture based on the mix recipe and the individual material
moistures, and removes this combined moisture percentage from the wet
weight to arrive at a dry weight. The plant control assumes the dryer will dry
the material adequately and therefore uses this calculated dry weight signal
for proportioning the liquid-asphalt binder. Based on the required asphalt
percentage in the mix, which comes from the mix formula, the control sys-
tem calculates the required asphalt flow. It then sends a signal to the flow-
control mechanism, which more often than not is a variable-speed motor,
