With the bucket-elevator method, RAP is introduced with the virgin
aggregate into the bucket elevator. The steam release starts in the bucket
elevator, which results in a continuous steam release that is less immediate
and less rapid than in the weigh-hopper method. This makes the steam
ducting easier to design, install, and manage—explaining why this type of
system is popular. With this method, however, the RAP is fractionated and
separated into the different hot bins of the batch tower. This means that
the asphalt content and the gradation of each hot bin must be analyzed
so the proper weights for each hot bin and the new liquid binder can be
properly determined. It is common to find belt scales fitted to both the
virgin-aggregate conveyor feeding the dryer and the RAP conveyor feeding
the bucket elevator. With these scales, the ratio of RAP to virgin aggregate
can be maintained.

More information about producing recycled mixes in a batch-type plant
is available from the National Asphalt Pavement Association in Information
Series 123, “Processing and Handling Reclaimed Asphalt Pavements (RAP).”
