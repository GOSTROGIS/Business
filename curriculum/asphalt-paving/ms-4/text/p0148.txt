Gravels are widely distributed, but deposits are rarely found without
some proportion of sand and possibly silt. Sandy soil mixtures frequently
contain some clay and silt. Beach sands, some of which are now far inland,
are a uniform-size material, but river sands often contain large amounts of
gravel, silt, and clay.

Transporting agents also influence the particle shape. Rounded boulders
and pebbles included in deposits in glaciated areas are one example. Another
is the smooth, rounded gravel and sand particles in flowing streams.

Pit or bank-run material is usually screened to proper size, separating
sand from gravel and even further separating gravel into different sizes.
The larger particles of natural gravel are often crushed to make them
more suitable for asphalt paving mixtures. Crushing typically improves
the particle shape by making the rounded particles more angular; it also
improves the distribution and range of particle sizes. Many times, pit or
bank-run material must also be washed to remove dirt (excessive amounts
of silt and clay) before using it for asphalt paving.
