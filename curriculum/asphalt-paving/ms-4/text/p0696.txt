the roller does not cause excessive displacement. The setting of an asphalt
emulsion can take from 30 minutes to 2 hours, depending on the ambient
conditions. The mix will turn from brown to black when the emulsion breaks.
Delaying compaction may result in the formation of a crust on the surface of
the mix, making compaction more difficult. Some agencies add lime, Portland
cement, or Class C fly ash, to accelerate curing so the roadway can be opened
to traffic sooner (caution—type F fly ash should not be used because it does
not have pozzolanic characteristics).

Initial compaction should be done with the roller traveling parallel to the
pavement’s center line. The rolling should begin at each edge and work toward
the center. Banked curves should be compacted with the roller starting at the
edge of the low side of the bank and then moving toward the superelevated
edge. To prevent tearing, gouging, or distortion of the pavement, a minimum
of one roller pass should be completed before any turning.

Breakdown rolling should continue until the roller “walks out” of the CIPR
mix with either a large pneumatic roller or vibratory steel-wheel roller operat-
ing in static mode. Some agencies do not recommend a steel-wheel roller for
breakdown rolling because of a concern that sealing moisture in the mix will
delay curing. Breakdown rolling is followed by intermediate rolling with a
12-ton (10,800 kilogram) or more double-drum vibratory roller in static mode
to remove the roller marks (see Figure 13.24).
