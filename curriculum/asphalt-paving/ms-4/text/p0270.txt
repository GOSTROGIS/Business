In the constant stress method, load cycles are applied until either the
specimen fails to take more load or it breaks. The number of cycles required
for a specimen is logged. Similarly, the test is conducted on several sam-
ples with different magnitudes of load and logged in a log-log plot. In the
constant stress test, more samples with higher stiffness perform better
than the samples with lower stiffness. The test results from this test are
applicable to thick HMA pavements.

In the constant strain beam fatigue test, constant strain is maintained
in a sample, but the magnitude of the load is allowed to decrease with
increasing load cycles. In this method, the samples do not break during
the test. Failure is defined when the applied load is reached at a certain
percentage of the original applied load. Usually, failure is defined as 50
percent loss in initial beam stiffness. The test results are applicable to thin
pavements—less than 2 inches (50 millimeters) thick. A typical plot of fatigue
data in the constant strain beam fatigue test is presented in Figure 4.34.

4.9.5 Empirical Tests

Empirical tests have been used to relate the performance of an HMA pave-
ment to a standardized test. The results from the test do not provide funda-
mental engineering properties that can be used in calculations of stresses
and strains in an HMA pavement. Rather, they have shown through field per-
formance to be indicative of performance. The three most commonly used
empirical tests for HMA pavements are the Marshall test, the Hveem test, and
the indirect tensile test; these were described earlier in this chapter.
