Resilient Modulus (M_)

General Theory

The resilient modulus (M) is used to characterize subgrade soils and
unbound base/subbase materials. The resilient modulus test provides a
material stiffness that more closely simulates the behavior of the material
under a moving wheel. It can be used in the mechanistic analysis of mul-
tilayered systems for predicting roughness, cracking, rutting, etc. This test
covers the determination of resilient modulus of untreated fine-grained
soils for conditions that represent stress in pavements subjected to mov-
ing wheel loads. The results of the test describe the resilient character of
a soil specimen when a repeated axial deviator stress of fixed magnitude,
frequency, and load duration is applied while the specimen, in a triaxial
chamber, is surrounded by pressure that acts as a static, all-around stress.

Test Procedure

Resilient modulus testing of unbound materials is performed in accor-
dance with one of two current procedures: (AASHTO T 307), Determining
the Resilient Modulus of Soils and Aggregate Materials or (AASHTO T 292),
Resilient Modulus of Subgrade Soils and Untreated Base/Subbase Materials.
A triaxial cell suitable for use in resilience testing of soils is shown in
Figure 5.5. The equipment is similar to most standard cells, except that
it is somewhat larger to facilitate the internally mounted load and defor-
mation measuring equipment, and has additional outlets for the electrical
leads from the measuring devices. (Triaxial cells with suitable externally
mounted load and deformation measuring equipment also may be used.)
For the type of equipment shown, air is used as the cell fluid.

The deformation measuring equipment consists of Linear Variable
Differential Transformers (LVDTs) attached to the soil specimen by a pair
of clamps. Two LVDTs are used for the measurement of axial deformation.
The clamps and LVDTs are shown in position on a soil specimen in
Figure 5.5. Preferably, load is measured by placing a load cell between
the sample cap and the loading piston. In addition to measuring devices,
suitable signal excitation, conditioning, and recording equipment must
also be maintained.

The external loading source may be any device capable of providing
varying repeated loads in fixed cycles of load and release. These devices
range from simple cam and switch control of static weights or air pistons
to closed loop electro-hydraulic systems. A load duration of 0.1 second and
load-release duration of 0.4 to 3 seconds has been found to be satisfactory
for most applications.

The confining pressure and deviator stress used should approximate the
expected in-place state of stresses. A confining pressure of 2 pounds per
square inch (14 kiloNewtons) and deviator stress of 6 pounds per square
inch (41 kiloNewtons) are reasonable values for most testing.
