ensuring that the geotextile is placed without wrinkles. A geotextile should
not be placed on a milled surface, but if it is, special care should be taken
to ensure that the geotextile is bonded to the surface. Care should be
taken in treating existing cracks if the geotextile is placed on a surface that
does not have a leveling layer. Cracks wider than 4 inch should be blown
out and filled with a sealant. Any distressed areas with base or subgrade
failure should be replaced. Inadequate surface preparation can result in
poor performance of the completed HMA overlay.

After surface preparation is complete, an asphalt membrane is applied
using a calibrated distributor truck. See Chapter 12, “Asphalt in Pavement
Maintenance” for a discussion of the proper operation of the distributor.
Selection of the proper type and application rate, along with a uniform
distribution of the membrane, is essential to the performance of the inter-
layer and thus the overlay. The manufacturer’s recommendations should be
carefully followed regarding the type and amount of asphalt that should
be applied with a specific geotextile.

The quantity of asphalt binder applied varies with the type and supplier
of the geotextile. The amount of tack coat applied also depends on the
condition of the underlying surface. An aged, weathered surface requires
a higher application rate than a new HMA leveling course. It is essential
to apply the membrane uniformly across the surface. A common range
of application rates suggested by manufacturers is 0.20-0.30 gallon/yard*
(1.4 litres/meter”). The membrane should extend beyond the edges of the
paving geotextile by 2-3 inches (50-75 millimeters). The temperature of
the asphalt binder should be high enough to allow uniform application.
Special care must be taken during application of the membrane in the
overlap zone when two passes of a distributor are used to obtain cover-
age over the entire width of the geotextile being placed. The membrane
should be placed over a sufficient length such that the distributor can place
a uniform quantity. Care should be taken to avoid placing the membrane
too far ahead of the paving process. Small, variable-width areas should
be applied with the hand spray boom on the back of the distributor to
ensure proper coverage. Before placing the membrane, the surface should
be swept to remove debris. If weather or equipment breakdown cause
paving operations to be suspended, the surface should not be opened to
traffic because of safety concerns. Uncovered geotextile can have very low
skid resistance.

The geotextile should be placed as near to the distributor truck as pos-
sible (See Figure 13.4). Machine placement by an experienced crew is the
preferred method. The geotextile should not be placed during a strong wind
of more than 20 miles per hour (32 kilometers per hour) because the wind
will cause kiting of the geotextile, which inhibits laydown and causes wrin-
kles in the geotextile. The geotextile laydown machine shown in Figure
13.4 holds a roll of geotextile on a spool and maintains a tension of about
14 pound per square inch (9.6 Newton per square centimeter). As the
geotextile is pulled off the roll, the broom presses it into the tack coat.
