Bottom- or belly-dump trucks generally require that a dump operator
be stationed on the grade to open the gates and control the spread rate of
the HMA material onto the grade in front of the paver. The dump opera-
tor is responsible for maintaining the proper amount of material to keep
the paver hopper uniformly charged. During paving, if the quantity on the
grade is found to not match the paving width or depth, material should be
added or removed—without stopping the paver.

Horizontal-Discharge Truck

Horizontal-discharge hauling units have a conveyor system that moves
the material from the hauling unit into the paver hopper without the need
to raise the truck bed (see Figure 8.5). This system has a safety advantage
because the bed does not need to be raised, and segregation is minimized
as the material is moved in mass on the conveyor system as it is dis-
charged into the paver hopper or material transfer device (described later).
Appropriate maintenance procedures must be followed for these systems
to operate properly. Proper cleaning of the conveyor ensures that cold
chunks are not left in the truck bed. Daily checks between loads ensure
that the conveyor system is operational and that no loose or damaged
components will break during the workday.
