Automatic Slope Control

An HMA paver equipped with an automatic slope control can hold the
screed at a different cross-slope from the slope of the surface that the
paver is riding on. The slope control uses a pendulum or bubble level
device to establish a perfectly vertical reference, from which a perpendicu-
lar plane will establish a true horizontal line. Using this true horizontal
line, a cross-slope can be created and maintained across the screed width
to build pavements with a cross-slope, such as superelevated pavements
on curved sections of roadway. Because the slope can be adjusted while
the paver is moving, the cross-slope can be “dialed” in or out and held con-
stant as the paver enters and exits a transition from flat to superelevated
pavements. When automatic slope control is used in conjunction with an
automatic grade control setup, the side of the screed closest to the refer-
ence line will be the pivot side and the cross-slope will be set positively or
negatively from this side as required by the project specifications.

References Available for Grade and Slope Control

Longitudinal reference lines for the automatic grade and slope-control sen-
sors to follow allow a pavement to be built to a specific vertical and hori-
zontal alignment. Fixed reference lines that are surveyed and set in advance
of the paving operation are the most accurate and used mainly on new con-
struction. Mobile references are devices attached to and travel along with the
paver as they reference off of the existing surface that the paver is traveling
on. Consequently, their use is limited to projects that have prepared surfaces
that are acceptable to use as a reference. Whichever reference system is used,
it is important to remember that the paver’s automatic controls will follow
the reference line accurately and closely duplicate the lines and grades in the
HMA pavement. Consequently, the finished HMA pavement will be no better
than the reference in terms of line, grade, or smoothness.
