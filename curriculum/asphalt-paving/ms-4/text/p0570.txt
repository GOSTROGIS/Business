11.2.4 Use of Pavement Condition
information

The results of a functional pavement evaluation provide the information
needed for the pavement management system to identify and prioritize
maintenance and rehabilitation needs. Typically, pavement distress and
roughness information are used to calculate pavement condition indexes
that can be used to compare the condition of one pavement section with
another and to identify the level of repair that might be needed to address
the deficiencies identified. Over time, pavement condition information
helps establish rates of pavement deterioration that can be used to fore-
cast future pavement conditions under different funding scenarios.

Pavement Condition Indexes

To facilitate the use of pavement distress information in a pavement man-
agement system, pavement condition indexes are calculated based on the
type, severity, and quantity of distress present. Pavement condition indexes
typically range in value from 0 to 10 or 0 to 100, with the highest values
corresponding to pavements in excellent condition. Points are subtracted
from a perfect score based on the distresses observed in the field to cal-
culate the index. The complexity of the pavement condition index calcula-
tion varies depending on the type of survey being conducted.

Pavement condition indexes can be calculated for each type of distress
present or a composite index can be used that aggregates all of the dis-
tress into one index representing the overall condition of a pavement
section. Some agencies use a combination of these two types of indexes;
the individual indexes are used to identify appropriate maintenance and
rehabilitation treatments and the composite index is used to report the
overall network conditions.

In local agencies, a composite index is most commonly used. For exam-
ple, the pavement rating system for low-volume asphalt pavements that
is included in several Asphalt Institute publications requires the rater to
assign a value between 0 and 5 for all functional distress present (such as
longitudinal and transverse cracking) and 0 to 10 for distress representing
structural forms of deterioration (such as alligator cracking and rutting) or
other important deterioration mechanisms (such as deficient drainage or
poor ride quality). The defects associated with each distress are summed
and subtracted from 100 to determine the condition rating for a pavement
section.

A more detailed approach to calculating a composite index was
developed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers for use in its Micro PAVER
pavement management system. This approach, which is documented in
ASTM D 6433, “Standard Practice for Roads and Parking Lots Pavement
Condition Index Surveys,” uses a similar approach, but separate deduction
values have been developed for each distress type, severity, and quantity
calculation.
