They will generally require documentation verifying compliance with
certain project requirements related to the quality-control program. The
following items are usually required as part of written quality-control
plans for HMA production based on the NCHRP Synthesis Report:

* Responsibility matrix showing who is responsible for reviewing
quality-control results and directing any corrective action
Sampling and testing plan identifying specific tests and frequencies to
be used for the project

° Documentation demonstrating that the plant meets the required qual-

ifications, certifications, and inspection frequencies

* Documentation demonstrating that the laboratory that will provide the

quality-control testing meets the required qualifications and accredita-
tions

¢ Documentation demonstrating that the technicians who will provide

the sampling and testing meet the required qualifications and
certifications

* Example report forms, including control charts that summarize certain

key data on plots that can rapidly show trends that need attention

¢ Written procedures describing criteria that will be used to identify

“out of control” production, what action steps will be taken when
materials fall outside of those criteria, and what the timing/deadlines
of these actions will be

The timing of sampling and testing as well as the turnaround of the
test results is critical for the quality-control plan to effectively con-
trol the quality of materials produced. If a belt sample is taken at the
beginning of production and tested for gradation, the test could be
completed in a matter of hours and reported back to the plant man-
ager. If those test results are not provided in a timely way to the plant
manager—or other person in charge of the plant—then the data may
contribute nothing in controlling the plant process. Timeliness in
communication of test data is essential, especially if the quality-control labo-
ratory is not directly employed by the HMA producer. Good quality-control
plans must address these lines of communication as well as the timing and
responsibilities of the various players. When several different companies
are all involved in the quality-control program, formal and informal lines
of communication must be established in order to offset potential barriers
created by the various contractual responsibilities of each party.
