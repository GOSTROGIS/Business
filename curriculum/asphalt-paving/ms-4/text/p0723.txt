14.3.2 Construction

The objective of the crack-and-seat process is to break the pavement into slabs
ranging in size from 18 to 48 inches (0.5 to 1.5 meters). Breaking the pave-
ment into the smaller sizes has shown to provide better performance. The
PCC slab must be cracked full depth. A test strip should be established to
ensure that the equipment and processes used are sufficient to thoroughly
crack the PCC pavement. Cores should be drilled directly over cracks to verify
that the slab is being cracked throughout the entire depth of the layer.

Cracking and Seating JCPC Pavement

Various types of equipment have been used to crack JCPC, including pile
drivers, guillotine hammers, whip hammers, and impact hammers. Production
rates will vary with the equipment type and the thickness of the pavement.
The most common method used is the guillotine hammer (see Figure 14.8),
which has high productivity and covers the full width of a lane.

After the JCPC has been cracked, the pieces must be firmly seated onto
the underlying layer to prevent rocking under traffic. Seating is accom-
plished with a large pneumatic roller that forces the broken slabs into
full contact with the foundation. Two passes of a 50-ton (450 kilonewton)
roller are specified for the seating operation. Excessive rolling should be
avoided because it can result in a loss of interlock between the cracked
pieces or in shear failure of the existing subbase or subgrade.

Breaking and Seating JRCP Pavement

As previously defined, breaking and seating is conducted on existing JRCP
pavement, and the process must rupture the reinforcing steel to be effective.
This process requires that the slabs be broken into pieces smaller than those
required for crack and seating. For breaking and seating, it is recommended
that the slabs be broken into pieces measuring 12-18 inches (300-460
millimeters). The steel should be cut by transverse sawing of the pavement at
routine intervals to ensure debonding in the transverse direction. The seating
process is accomplished with a heavy pneumatic roller in a manner similar
to that used in the crack-and-seat process, except that additional passes are
needed to ensure rupturing of the steel. Five to seven passes of a 35-50 ton
(315-450 kilonewton) rubber tire roller are recommended. Any soft spots or
local failures identified in the seating process require careful removal and
replacement. It is important to address drainage or unsuitable foundation
conditions during this process. The broken PCC should be replaced with
a full-depth HMA patch. The areas to be replaced should be sized to accom-
modate the equipment necessary to properly place and compact the new
material.
