Thicker Lifts

Although thicker lifts allow more time to achieve density, the greater
mass of material often requires more compactive effort. Vibratory rollers
compact more efficiently than static rollers and are almost always used
during compaction of thick lifts. A typical rolling train consists of vibratory
rollers operating as breakdown rollers, followed by vibratory or pneu-
matic intermediate rollers, and finishing with a static steel-wheel roller or
vibratory roller operating in the static mode.

Leveling Courses—Uneven Bases

Pneumatic-tire rollers are very useful when compacting leveling courses
over uneven surfaces or when placing variable-depth layers. The action of
the tires allows for compaction into the uneven areas that a wide steel-
drum might bridge over.

Non-Mainline Applications

Most rolling patterns are described to be set up for constant-width pav-
ing or mainline paving. Often paving is done in irregular areas, curved
sections, cul-de-sac, or parking areas. Static rollers are common in these
areas, aS are pneumatic and vibratory rollers. Again, a combination of roll-
ers generally works best. Split-drum steel rollers can make tighter turns
without tearing or cutting the mat.

Statistics-Based Specifications

These types of specifications are based on how uniform and how close to
the target all the density tests are. Monitoring density is vital, and adjusting
the roller pattern as conditions change will ensure the best possible specifica-
tion with a statistics-based specification such as Percent Within Limits (PWL).
