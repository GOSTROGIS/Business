14.2.1 Design Considerations

Minimum Thickness

Saw-and-seal is commonly considered when the overlay thickness that can
be placed is limited. Examples include urban streets with curb and gutter
sections, intersections and driveways, and highways with vertical clearance
restrictions. As previously suggested, an overlay is best applied in two lifts to
provide the greatest opportunity to improve ride quality and surface drain-
age, but there have been numerous examples where a single lift of HMA
has been placed, has been sawed and sealed, and has provided excellent
performance. The minimum recommended thickness for a single lift of
HMA is 2 inches (50 millimeters). For two lifts, the minimum thickness is
3% inches (90 millimeters).

Joint Configuration

Many state DOTs have specifications for this process. The engineer should
check with the local DOT to ensure that his or her specification follows their
requirements. The typical requirement for the joint is that the HMA layer be
cut to one-third to one-half of the thickness of the HMA layer. This can range
from % inch wide Gf a reservoir for the sealant is used) to % inch wide if
no reservoir is used. Figure 14.3 presents the procedure most DOTs utilize,
which is the % inch cut with a reservoir for the joint sealant material.

Urban and Other Areas

When using saw-and-seal in urban areas where curb and gutter sections
are present, the saw cuts should line up with the control joints in the gut-
ter sections, even if a slightly skewed saw cut results. The saw cuts should
not be spaced more than 40 feet (12 meters) apart, and for thin JPCP, it
may be beneficial to have the cuts spaced closer, such as 30 feet (9 meters)
apart. For parking lots, airports, and other large areas of pavement,
saw-and-seal has been shown to be effective when the saw cuts are made
so that the pavement is cut into 50-foot (15-meter) squares.

14.2.2 Construction

Preparation Before Overlay

Before placing an HMA overlay, the existing concrete pavement should
be repaired as needed. Concrete panels with working cracks, corner
breaks, moderate-to-severe spalling, and joints exhibiting poor load trans-
fer should be repaired before placing the overlay. Referencing the existing
joints in the concrete pavement is critical. Fixed reference markers should
