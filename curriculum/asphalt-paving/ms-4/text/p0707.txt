The level of compaction achieved in the reclaimed material is directly
related to the performance of the final pavement. Figure 13.30 shows
a reclaimed surface before compaction. Compaction of reclaimed mixes
using an asphalt emulsion as a stabilizing agent must be accomplished
soon after the emulsion begins to break. This is when the emulsion begins
to change from brown to black, which also indicates that the moisture is
evaporating from the reclaimed material. The compaction process becomes
more difficult as the emulsion breaks.

Compaction of reclaimed mixes using foamed asphalt should occur
shortly after placement of the mix. The mixes will remain workable as
long as the moisture content stays at or above the moisture content at
the time of foamed-asphalt injection and mixing. The compaction process
should be completed before the foamed asphalt mix begins to dry out.

Compaction should be done with heavy pneumatic or steel-wheel
rollers. The depth of the reclaimed material that must be compacted
and the desired degree of compaction will influence the weight and the
amplitude/frequency of vibration for vibratory compactors and the static
weight and tire pressure of pneumatic rollers. The roller pattern should
be established to determine the best mixture of roller types and number
of passes.
