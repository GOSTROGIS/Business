Reference Density Specifications

Typical density specifications represent a comparison between the
in-place density of the pavement that is achieved after final compaction,
and a “reference” density. One of three reference densities is typically used
in density specifications: maximum theoretical density, laboratory bulk
density, or control-strip density. Use of the maximum theoretical density to
determine HMA pavement density compliance is preferred by the Asphalt
Institute.

The relationship between the reference-density measurements and the
air voids of the in-place pavement is shown against each type of refer-
ence density. Eight percent is selected here because it is believed that if
this level of compaction is achieved at the time of construction, 4 percent
air voids will be achieved in a few years after further densification of the
pavement under traffic (see Figure 10.14). Some agencies may prefer a
target less than 8 percent air voids in the compacted pavement. A target
higher than 8 percent is not recommended.
