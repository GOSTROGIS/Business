15.4.3 Pavement Thickness

A thorough soils investigation should be conducted to determine the
strength of the existing soils and drainage characteristics. The investigation
and design recommendations should make sure that the project is not being
built on unstable soils (such as expansive clays or collapsible soils) that will
cause significant problems. These must be dealt with during design and con-
struction. The drainage of the site must be thoroughly evaluated because
saturated soils can have a dramatic effect on the load-carrying capacity of
the pavement. When areas of poor soils are encountered, their removal and
replacement with a higher-quality material should be considered.

For bicycle/walking paths, the type of traffic that will operate on the sur-
face should be considered. Periodic vehicular traffic, such as emergency,
patrol, snow removal, maintenance, or other vehicles, must be considered in
the design. For most paths, full-depth asphalt provides an excellent surface
because of its speed of construction. The thickness of the HMA surface will
vary from 3 to 5 inches (75 to 125 millimeters) depending on the traffic and
the soil conditions of the site. It may be desirable to place a base course of
crushed stone or reclaimed asphalt pavement to help distribute the load or as
a construction aid to ease the grading and layout of the path.

Tennis courts should generally have 3 to 4 inches (75 to 100 milli-
meters) of HMA placed on a 4- to 8-inch (100 to 200 millimeters) layer
of compacted base course (consisting of crushed aggregate or reclaimed
asphalt pavement). It is also recommended that the subgrade be leveled
and compacted to provide a finish elevation of 4 to 6 inches (100 to 150
millimeters) above the surrounding ground surface. This will provide for
water runoff and vegetation control around the perimeter.

Asphalt Mix Design

The type of mix to be used for bicycle/walking paths and other recre-
ational facilities should be different than that used for parking lots or high-
way facilities. The mix-design criteria used for asphalt pavements designed
for vehicular traffic will not, in most cases, provide a mix of the needed
durability and workability required for recreational facilities. A mix devel-
oped for recreational use will generally contain more asphalt binder, have
less air voids, and be less prone to oxidation, raveling, and cracking than a
typical roadway mix. The objective of the asphalt mix is to provide a cost-
effective mix that will have the following characteristics:

° Sufficient asphalt to provide for a durable surface

° A smooth uniform surface texture

° Sufficient workability to allow ease of handling during construction

and proper compaction

Hot mix asphalt for recreational facilities should have a fine-graded aggre-
gate blend with a nominal maximum size of % inch (12.5 millimeters) or
less. For some applications such as tennis courts, it is recommended that a
nominal maximum size of % inch (9.5 millimeters) or less be considered.
