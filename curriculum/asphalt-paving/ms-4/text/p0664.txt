The advantage of this approach is that the first lift or leveling course provides
an unblemished surface and reduces the potential for the wrinkling of the
geotextile during placement. Geotextiles should not be used where there is
substantial horizontal or vertical movement at the joint or the crack in the
existing pavement. The use of geotextiles in the pavement structure may
preclude the use of any type of recycling technique in the future. Geotextiles
placed on longitudinal joint cracking, where the horizontal movement of the
existing pavement is small, have proven to be effective.

The specifications for geotextiles used when rehabilitating an asphalt
pavement usually include the following details:

Weight (0z/yd’/gm/m7). This is the quantity of geotextile needed to
absorb a sufficient amount of tack coat to form a membrane.

Grab tensile strength [pounds (Newtons)]. This property is important
for preventing the geotextile from separating during the placement
process and to ensure that the geotextile performs as a reinforcing
layer.

Elongation at break (percent). This is determined during the grab
tensile test and is required to insure that the geotextile will not pull
apart during placement.

Asphalt retention [gallon/yard*(litre/meter’)]. This is an indication
of how much asphalt is necessary to saturate the geotextile and make
a bond. Different geotextiles will absorb different amounts of asphalt,
depending on the weight and thickness of the geotextile.
Miscellaneous. The specifications will require that the geotextile be
kept in a plastic sleeve before placement to prevent ultraviolet-ray
damage to the geotextile; and that the melting point of the geotextile
is at least 300°F (150°C) to prevent the geotextile from melting during
construction.

check with the DOT or the geotextile manufacturer for their recommenda-
tions on the specified product being considered.
