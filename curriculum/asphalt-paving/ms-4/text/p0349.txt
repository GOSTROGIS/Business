HMA producers are encouraged to consult with the liquid asphalt supplier
for proper storage requirements.

Pipes that transfer the liquid asphalt to the mixing area of the plant are
insulated and surrounded with an outer pipe or “jacket” containing hot oil
from the plant’s heating system to keep the binder at the proper tempera-
ture throughout the entire process.

Special processing equipment is available to blend mixture additives
into the hot asphalt lines or plant mixing area. Antistrip additives, fibers,
RAP, or other mixture modifiers are often added after the aggregate has
entered the drum. Special processing equipment is also used for mixing
crumb-rubber additives and latex into the liquid asphalt for special mixes.
This equipment varies by application requirement and is usually computer
controlled.

Plant personnel should monitor storage temperatures regularly to make
sure the binder is kept at the proper temperature. Thermometers in the
tanks and lines are connected to digital displays and graph recorders in the
control room for this purpose. Binder temperature affects not only pump-
ing and metering efficiency but also coating characteristics, so ensuring
that binder temperature is maintained throughout the process is critical for
proper mixing operations. Heater temperatures should be set at required
storage temperatures, or not more than 20°-30°F (11°-13°C) above those
temperatures. Excessively high temperatures cause the asphalt to build up
and harden around the heating elements. These excessively high tempera-
tures can also prematurely age the binder. The recording devices provide
visual proof that specification temperature limits have not been exceeded.
The objective of storing asphalt binders at an HMA plant is to maintain
an adequate supply of material to keep the plant running between binder
deliveries. The amount of storage needed at an HMA plant is a function of
anticipated production rate, type of binder(s), and the anticipated delivery
schedule.

7.22 Aggregate Stockpiling

Aggregates are delivered to plant sites by truck, rail, barge, or belt con-
veyor directly from the aggregate source. Aggregates are usually stockpiled
onsite and then fed into cold feed bins with a front-end loader. A plant
can also be fed directly from the stockpile by feeders under the stockpiles
or by trucks dumping directly into the cold feed bins. The most common
approach is to feed the bins from a stockpile with a front-end loader.

Operational concerns for HMA production at the plant site focus on the
following key areas.

Cross Contamination
Materials should be kept separated from one another by space or by
dividers before being loaded into the cold feed bins for mixing.
