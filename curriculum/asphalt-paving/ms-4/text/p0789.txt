15.9 Environmental

Applications of Asphalt

15.9.1 General

For decades, many civil engineering solutions to environmentally sensi-
tive projects have incorporated asphalt. It has been used because it is flex-
ible, strong, chemically stable and nonreactive, impermeable, recyclable,
economical, and readily available. All sorts of asphalt materials are used in
environmental applications: sprayed-on membranes of emulsified asphalt,
asphalt cement, and modified asphalt, dense-graded HMA, open-graded
HMA, hydraulic mixes, sand mixes, and mastic asphalt liners.

Contrary to the fears of some environmental groups, scientists have
established that asphalt, in the form of an emulsion or incorporated into a
mixture of aggregates, does not leach into water, contaminate soil, or kill
fish. The results of leachability tests of new HMA show very low levels of
leachable compounds, well below any current Environmental Protection
Agency guidelines. Scientists have proved that asphalt is an environmen-
tally safe material.

15.9.2 Examples of Environmental Uses of
Asphalt

1. Fish hatcheries. Oregon and Washington have more than 35
asphalt-lined fish hatchery ponds. These ponds are used to raise
fingerling fish until they are large enough to be released. A typical
hatchery pond is 5-7 feet (1.5-2.1 meters) deep and covers about
a half-acre (0.2 hectares). The pond liner is constructed of 2 or
3 inches (50-75 millimeters) of HMA over 8 inches (200 milli-
meters) of aggregate base course. Most liners are sealed with an
asphalt emulsion so that bacteria cannot inhabit the tiny surface
irregularities in the HMA liner.

lined water reservoirs have been constructed over the past 100
years. The design of a reservoir liner must address permeability,
durability, flexibility, resistance to weathering, the ability to absorb
stresses during filling and drawdown cycles, long-term loading
stresses caused by settlement in the supporting subgrade, and the
weight of maintenance vehicles when the reservoir is periodically
cleaned. The composite liners required for large drinking water
