15.1.3 Suitability for Pavement
Construction

Cold-laid asphalt mixes may be used for surface, base, or subbase courses if
the pavement structure is properly designed. Cold-laid surface courses are suit-
able for light and medium traffic. When used in the base or subbase, they may
be suitable for all types of traffic. Also when used on large jobs, cold-laid paving
mixtures reduce construction costs, but not necessarily the strength or quality
of the pavement structure. High quality cold mixes, if properly designed and
constructed, can provide a structural equivalency equal to that of hot mixes.

Asphalt pavements made by cold-laid methods can be designed and built
to meet current traffic requirements. A thicker pavement can be achieved by
adding additional aggregate material if required, during in-place mixing or
by adding additional pavement courses.

15.1.4 Mix Composition

A wide variety of aggregates and soil-aggregate combinations can be
used in cold-mixes. In many cases, material from road cuts, pits, or nearby
quarry sites may be economically crushed or used. Often the entire output
of a rock crusher less than a certain maximum size is used.

Suitable local materials also include granular soils having up to 20 percent
passing the No. 200 (75 microns) sieve, as well as clean sands and gravel.

Granular materials that are mostly fine aggregate and have a relatively
high percentage of mineral dust may cause mixing, aeration, and compac-
tion problems.

Well-graded aggregates are always desirable for any course of the asphalt
pavement structure. However, poorly graded and gap-graded aggregates
can be adequate for base courses when they are properly prepared by
road mixing methods and procedures.
