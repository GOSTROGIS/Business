8.4 Paving Equipment

HMA pavers in use today differ in size and detail with many intricate
parts and adjustments, but all have similar operating principles. The paver
receives the HMA directly from the hauling unit, MTD, or windrow pickup
machine and moves the HMA through the paver to place and spread the
HMA on the roadway before beginning the compaction process (see Figure
8.10). Figure 8.11 traces the flow of asphalt mix from the receiving hopper
at the front of the paver to the finished pavement behind the screed at the
rear of the machine. The paver spreads the mixture in a uniform layer of
desired thickness and shape, or finishes the layer to the desired elevation
and cross section, making it ready for compaction. Modern pavers are
supported on crawler treads or pneumatic-tire wheels. These machines
can place a layer of less than 1 inch (25 millimeters) to approximately
10 inches (250 millimeters) in thickness over a width of 6 to 32 feet
(2 to 10 meters) Working speeds generally range from 10 to 70 feet G to
20 meters) per minute.
