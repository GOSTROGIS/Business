By controlling the individual lift cylinder for each pair of hammers,
the MHB can rubblize in widths of 2-13 feet (0.6-4 meters) at a produc-
tion rate of 500-1500 square yards (418-1254 square meters) per hour.
This allows the multiple-head breaker to rubblize lanes that are 12.5 feet
(3.8 meters) wide, while traveling at speeds of 0.07-0.20 miles per hour
(0.11-0.32 kilometers per hour). Production rates are comparable to the
RPB and depend on the strength and thickness of the slab and the underly-
ing subgrade.

Rubblization using an MHB requires a single pass over an area to be
rubblized. The tractor or power unit travels on the unbroken slab as it
moves forward and tows the breaking unit behind. Because it is a single-
pass technique, the MHB does not have to travel on the rubblized surface.
Fracturing of the slab is controlled by a set combination of drop height,
frequency of the impacts, and the forward speed of the machine, as deter-
mined by a test strip/pit.

Part of the process of using an MHB is to roll the broken pavement
using a smooth steel-wheel roller onto which a Z-grid pattern has been
attached. This breaks any elongated surface particles into a more desir-
able, cubical shape. Its operation is described later in this section.

Cut Off and Remove Any Exposed Steel Reinforcement

Reinforcing steel or wire mesh that emerges above the surface during
rubblization must be removed by cutting with a torch that is flush with the sur-
rounding surface. Otherwise, reinforcing steel, wire mesh, and load-transfer
devices should be left in place.
