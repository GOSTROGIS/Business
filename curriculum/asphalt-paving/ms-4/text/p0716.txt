14.2 Saw-and-Seal

Saw-and-seal has been used for many years to slow the rate of deterioration
of reflection cracks in relatively thin overlays of jointed PCCP. The technique
consists of carefully referencing all the joints in the existing pavement before
overlay and then sawing and sealing contraction joints in the HMA overlay
directly over the underlying joints. When properly done, a weakened plane is
formed directly above the joint defining the ultimate cracking pattern in the
overlay. The saw-and-seal procedure will do the following:

° Provide maintainable joints

e Reduce or eliminate the spalling associated with reflection cracking

° Provide for good ride quality

° Provide a neat, well-maintained appearance

Saw-and-seal has most commonly been used on jointed-reinforced con-
crete (JRPC) pavements on highway and street projects in New England
and Louisiana, as well as on airfield pavements. JRPC pavements gener-
ally have longer joint spacing than jointed-plain concrete (JCPC) pave-
ments, resulting in longer slabs and thus greater opening and closing
movement at the joints. It should only be considered for jointed concrete
pavements that are in good structural condition, have good joint load
transfer, and have intact panels (no working cracks) and minimal spalling.
Thought should be given to how the joints can be practically referenced
before selecting this approach, because missing the joint by more than
one inch usually results in a jagged, irregular reflection crack alongside a
neat sawed and sealed crack.

This technique should not be considered for the following:

¢ Overlays on JCPC that have a high frequency of mid-panel cracking

(more than 20 percent) or badly deteriorated cracking. Panels with
working cracks should either be patched or replaced with a new con-
crete panel.

¢ Overlays on JCPC with badly deteriorated joints and/or significant

patching at or near the joints

* Overlays where there is insufficient load transfer across the joints as

evidenced by %-inch (0.0625 millimeters) faulting and/or pumping.
When tested using nondestructive testing equipment, joints should
have a minimum 60 percent load-transfer efficiency.

° Overlays of pavements with wide joints—greater than 34 inches

(19 millimeters)
