Another variant of the hot-side method is holding the roller drum
approximately 6 inches (150 millimeters) away from the longitudinal joint
on the first pass toward the paver. This leaves a narrow strip of uncom-
pacted mix. As the roller reverses direction, the roller drum is moved over
to overlap 6 inches (150 millimeters) on the cold mat, trapping the narrow
strip under the drum and forcing it into place.

For lifts greater than 3 inches (76 millimeters), the rolling process should start
12-15 inches (300-375 millimeters) from the lower unsupported edge, with
subsequent passes covering the interior portion of the mat. Succeeding passes
of the roller should then gradually progress toward the edges of the spread.
The uncompacted edge provides initial confinement during the first pass,
thus minimizing lateral movement of the mix. After the central portion of the
spread has been compacted, the mix will have cooled slightly and can sup-
port the roller. This will allow the edge to be compacted with minimal lateral
movement.

Cold-side rolling. A method that is sometimes used is to place the roller
on the previously placed lane so that not more than 6 inches (150 millime-
ters) rides on the edge of the newly spread material. Here it is operated
to pinch and press the uncompacted material into place. The roller should
continue to roll along this line, its position being shifted gradually across
the joint until a thoroughly compacted, neat joint is obtained. Vibration
should not be used when the roller is operating on a cold mat. This is
normally the least efficient rolling method because the hot mat is cooling
while the roller is mostly operating on the cold mat.

Rolling Edges

When rolling a longitudinal joint or HMA edge against an existing con-
crete gutter or curb lip, the roller should not span the joint in a manner
that puts part of the roller weight on the gutter. By bridging the roller on
the PCC curb, insufficient compaction along the HMA edge may result.
One common method to obtain good HMA compaction along an existing
curb is to require the edge of the finished pavement to have an elevation
slightly higher than the adjacent gutter. This ensures that the full compac-
tive force of the roller will be on the HMA.
