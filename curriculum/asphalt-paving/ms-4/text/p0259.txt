NCAT Procedure

The National Center for Asphalt Technology (NCAT) developed a pro-
cedure that is described in NCAT Report 00-01, Design, Construction, and
Performance of New Generation Open Graded Friction Courses. The selection
of the design gradation should consist of blending at least three trial blends
ranging from the coarse to the fine side of the gradation band, with one fall-
ing in the middle of the band. For each trial gradation, the dry-rodded voids in
the coarse aggregate fraction (VCA, .) should be determined using AASHTO
T 19 or ASTM C 29.

For each trial gradation, two specimens should be compacted at between
6.0 and 6.5 percent asphalt binder using 50 gyrations of a Superpave Gyratory
Compactor. If a stabilizer is to be used in the final mix, it should be included
in the trial mixes. If the VCA, ,_ is equal to or less than the VCA,,_, then stone-
on-stone contact exists. If all the trial blends meet this requirement, choose
a trial gradation that provides the highest air-voids (V,) content.

To determine the VCA,, , the designer must determine the bulk specific
gravity of the mix (G, ,). The measurement of the bulk specific gravity (G,,,)
of a compacted OGFC sample is a problem. This test is conducted by the
water displacement method (AASHTO T 166 or ASTM D 2726) using satu-
rated surface dry (SSD) specimens. The SSD method has been proven to
work for dense-graded HMA mixtures. A problem with OGFC mixes is that
they have large internal air-voids (15 to 25 percent) that are interconnected.
