12.5. Thin Overlays
=o (Maintenance Blanket)

A thin asphalt surfacing—a thickness of 1-1% inches (25-37.5
millimeters)—can be used to extend the life of an asphalt pavement. This
use of a thin asphalt surfacing may also improve the ride quality and cor-
rect surface defects such as low skid resistance. Many times, a thin overlay
is used in combination with milling operations (discussed in Chapter 13,
“Rehabilitation Methods for Asphalt Pavements”). This type of surfacing is
very applicable for use on residential streets. Milling may be required to
maintain a minimum curb reveal.

A thin overlay should be used when preventive maintenance is the goal.
Existing structural damage (fatigue cracking) should be repaired before
the overlay, and the cause of any rutting (greater than 4 inch) should be
determined and corrected. Pavement layers that have rutting problems
should be removed and replaced with materials that have sufficient stabil-
ity to withstand the traffic loads.

The pavement materials and thickness should be chosen on the basis of
the traffic and the structural needs of the pavement. The thickness should
be a minimum of three times the nominal maximum aggregate size (as
defined in Chapter 4, “Hot Mix Asphalt Mix Design”). Thus, for a pavement
with a nominal maximum aggregate size of 4% inch (12.5 millimeters), the
thickness should be at least 1% inches (37.5 millimeters).
