Maximum Particle Size and Grading

Two key properties of an aggregate that go hand in hand are maximum
particle size and grading. ASTM D 8 defines the maximum size of an
aggregate as “the smallest sieve opening through which the entire amount
of aggregate is required to pass.” AASHTO MP 323 refers to the maximum
size of an aggregate for Superpave mixes only as “one size larger than the
nominal maximum aggregate size.”

AASHTO MP 323 defines it as “one sieve size larger than the first sieve to
retain more than 10 percent of the material.” Care must be exercised when
using and referring to the terms. For good paving results, the lift thickness
of HMA should be at least 2 to 2% times the maximum aggregate size in
the mix or 3 to 4 times the nominal maximum aggregate size in the mix.

The grading or gradation of an aggregate is the distribution of the vari-
ous particle sizes. Gradation is typically determined by both a washed
sieve analysis and a dry sieve analysis. ASTM C 136 (AASHTO T 27) details
the procedure for dry sieving.

In this procedure, the mass of a sample of dry aggregate is determined
and then the sample is passed through a series of sieves with progressively
smaller openings (see Figure 3.4). Once separated, the mass of material
retained on each sieve is measured and compared with the total sample
weight. The aggregate gradation is ultimately expressed as the cumulative
percentage passing each sieve size. Figure 3.5 gives an example of sieve
analysis data for an aggregate that contains both fine and coarse particles. A
graphical plot of the particle distribution is also shown.

Dry sieving alone may be satisfactory for general checks during aggregate
production but does not appropriately determine the amount of fine particles
smaller than the No. 200 (75 microns) sieve. Therefore, before performing
the dry sieve analysis, a washed sieve analysis should be made as detailed
in AASHTO T 11 (ASTM C 117). They describe the procedure in this way:
“A sample of the aggregate is washed in a prescribed manner, using either
plain water or water containing a wetting agent, as specified. The decanted
wash water, containing suspended and dissolved material, is passed through
a 75 microns (No. 200) sieve. The loss in mass resulting from the wash treat-
ment is calculated as mass percent of the original sample. . . .”
