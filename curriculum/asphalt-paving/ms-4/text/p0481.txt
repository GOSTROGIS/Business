10.1 General

Long-term performance of hot mix asphalt GAMA) pavements is achieved
by using quality materials and placement techniques during construction.
The life of a pavement and the cost of maintenance can be very sensitive
to seemingly minor variations in materials properties, such as aggregate
gradation, asphalt content, mixture volumetrics, and compaction.

Highway agencies and other owners of asphalt pavements use physi-
cal test results to evaluate the quality of materials and the methods used
in their projects. Each agency’s overall quality-assurance program deter-
mines testing requirements. These programs generally include three types
of tests: quality-control, acceptance, and independent assurance.

10.1.1 Quality-Assurance Programs

The definitions used in this chapter have been adapted from the TRB
Transportation Circular, Glossary of Highway Quality Assurance Terms.
The following terms are presented relative to quality-assurance programs.
This chapter focuses mainly on these terms as they relate to sampling and
testing, and is limited to aggregates and HMA mixtures.

Quality Assurance. All planned and systematic actions necessary to
provide confidence that a product or facility will perform satisfactorily in
service. :

Quality Control (also called Process Control). Actions and consider-
ations necessary to assess and adjust production and construction pro-
cesses in order to control the level of quality being produced in the product.
Quality-control practices are usually provided by contractors and/or mate-
rials suppliers to monitor the product manufacturing process.

Acceptance. Sampling and testing, or inspection, to determine the
degree of compliance with contract requirements. Acceptance practices
are usually provided by the owner or his designated representative to
determine whether specific terms of the plans and specifications have
been met.

Independent Assurance. Management tool that requires a third party,
not directly responsible for process control or acceptance, to provide an
independent assessment of the product and/or the reliability of test results
obtained from the process-control and acceptance testing. This third-party
testing and inspection is usually provided by the owner but accomplished
by a laboratory that is not involved in the quality-control or acceptance.
