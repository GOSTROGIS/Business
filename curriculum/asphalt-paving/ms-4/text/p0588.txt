Bleeding or Flushing

Bleeding, or flushing, leaves a thin film of asphalt on the pavement surface
resulting from excess bitumen. Bleeding is easy to identify in the field by its
slick, glossy appearance and the tendency of the asphalt to often be sticky to
the touch (especially in hot weather), as illustrated in Figure 11.23. Bleeding
can be caused by an improperly constructed seal coat, too much asphalt in
a mix, too heavy a prime or tack coat, or excessive sealant in the cracks or
joints under an overlay. Traffic can also cause bleeding if the asphalt layers
are overcompacted and the binder is forced to the surface.

Polished Aggregate

Aggregates that have been worn to a smooth finish are called polished
aggregates. Some aggregates, such as limestone, may polish quickly under
traffic. Once the aggregates polish, they are a potential skid hazard, especially
when wet. An example of a polished surface is provided in Figure 11.24.

Surface Treatment Distress

Because of their unique properties, some types of distress may occur only
in an asphalt surface treatment and not in other types of asphalt pavements, .
such as loss of cover aggregate and streaking. Other distresses discussed
earlier in this chapter, such as depressions, raveling, corrugations, and
potholes occur in both hot-mix asphalt and bituminous surface treatments.
