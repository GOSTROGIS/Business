The new Mechanistic Empirical Pavement Design Guide (MEPDG) for
highways proposes using a modulus value of 150 Kips per square inch
(689 Newtons per square meter) for characterizing rubblized concrete pave-
ment. The Asphalt Institute agrees with the value of 150 Kips per square for
typical highway pavements. An airfield research project conducted by the
Asphalt Institute suggested that the typical back-calculated modulus may be
influenced by concrete slab thickness. The following ranges were found:

° Rubblized slabs 6-8 inches (152-203 millimeters) thick: moduli from
100-135 Kips per square inch (689-930 Newtons per square meter)

° Rubblized slabs 8-14 inches (203-356 millimeters) thick: moduli
135-235 Kips per square inch (930-1620 Newtons per square meter)

° Rubblized slabs thicker than 14 inches (356 millimeters): moduli from
235 Kips per square inch-400 Kips per square inch (1620-2758 Newtons
per square meter)

The minimum overlay thickness is 5 inches (125 millimeters); the overlay

thickness range for interstate highways has been 8-14 inches (203-356
millimeters).

14.4.3 Construction

The procedural steps in the rubblization technique are essentially the
same for airfields and highways. These steps are the following:

° Prepare the surface and remove any existing overlays.

¢ Install a subdrainage system (if necessary).

° Rubblize the concrete pavement.

° Cut off and remove any exposed steel reinforcement.

* Remove exposed joint-sealing material.

° Roll the rubblized concrete pavement.

° Remove and patch any unstable areas.

° Place asphalt HMA leveling course/HMA overlays.

Prepare the Surface

For the rubblization equipment to work properly, any material from a previ-
ous overlay must be removed before rubblizing the underlying concrete.

Adjacent pavements that are not to be rubblized must be isolated from the
rubblized pavements. This is best accomplished by a full-depth sawcut or by
cutting a relief trench with a wheel saw. Either method allows the rubblizing
equipment to work up to the project limits without damaging the structural
integrity of the pavement that is to remain in place.

Underground structures, utilities, and in-pavement fixtures must be
identified as needing protection during the rubblization process. Isola-
tion or removal and replacement may be required during the rubblization
process. Provisions must also be made to raise all in-pavement features to
match the grade of the new HMA pavement.
