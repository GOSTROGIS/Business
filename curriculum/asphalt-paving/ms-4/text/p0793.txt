45.10.3 Construction Practices

Race and test track construction is very complex construction work. This
is especially true for the paving contractor. The contractor not only deals
with closer tolerances, a smoother surface, and higher densities, but also
must accomplish those tasks while working on steep slopes. Construction
work certainly tests the ingenuity of the contractor’s personnel.

Anyone contemplating being involved in race or test track design or
construction should consult with a person who has previously built a
track.

For slopes 18 degrees or less, paving procedures are similar to normal
highway paving. On slopes between 18 and 24 degrees, the rollers need
lateral support, which can usually be accomplished from the lower side
(Figure 15.17). Care must be taken to ensure that the lower portion of
the roller does not dig into the mix. Material transfer vehicles are needed
to deliver the mix to the paving machine. Adjustments must be made to
the paving machine, to ensure proper delivery of the mix to the screed.
Once the slope is steeper than 20 degrees, all the equipment from the
top or bottom of the track must be anchored. Crawler tractors, backhoes,
or cranes make good deadmen that can move along the top of the track
at the paving speed. Heavily weighted crawler-mounted machines con-
nected by I-beams of different lengths can be used to anchor the paver
from the bottom of the slope. Large crab-steering, shoulder-widening
machines can be used in the same configuration to hold up compaction
rollers.

Test tracks pose some added problems. To maintain neutral steer, they
require parabolic cross sections. Normal screeds on paving machines can-
not be configured to the required shape. This limitation necessitates the
use of a screed with several sections. These special screeds usually have a
break at 2-foot (0.6-meter) intervals. Compaction of the pavement on these
parabolic sections also poses a problem. Either a roller with split drums or
a series of smaller, narrow rollers are required.

Consideration should be given to diamond grinding the underlying layer
before placing the surface course, eliminating the need to solve roughness
problems with just the final surface.

Joints are very important and must be properly constructed to ensure
good smoothness and proper density. The number of transverse construc-
tion joints should be minimized, and where production allows, a single
paving lane should extend one full lap around the track. All joints should
be tack-coated.
