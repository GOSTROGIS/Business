10.6.3 Mixture Compactability

Some mixtures are easier to compact than others. It can be useful when
controlling the paving operation to understand the effect of the mixture and
the variables that affect compactability.

Experience says that coarse-graded mixtures with low fines tend to require
significant effort to compact. Success with such mixtures can be achieved
with excellent compaction methods and good quality-control measures.

Two other mixture parameters that should be of particular interest to the
paving crew or paving subcontractor are air voids and lift thickness. The
following sections describe these two mixture properties and how they can
affect compaction. These two items should be carefully planned and moni-
tored with respect to their impact on the compactability of HMA.

Laboratory Air Voids

If a mixture is produced with laboratory compacted air voids that are higher
than the target, additional compactive effort in the field will be required to
achieve density. Suppose, for example, that a project has an established roll-
ing pattern that achieves about 94 percent compaction when the mixture
is produced at 4 percent laboratory voids. If the laboratory air voids of the
mixture suddenly increased by 1.5 percent (to 5.5 percent), then the same
rolling pattern in the field would yield something on the order of 1.5 percent
less (about 92 to 93 percent).

When changes in laboratory air voids create a need for additional com-
pactive effort, the establishment of a new or revised rolling pattern may be
needed. Even if no air void limits are specified for production, the laboratory
compacted voids must be controlled to produce a compactable mix.

Compaction problems can occur as well if the air voids are low, espe-
cially in projects with statistical acceptance requirements. In those cases,
fluctuations in air voids will cause fluctuations in compaction and can
affect pay factors that include standard deviations. Good consistency in
laboratory air voids of the produced mix is essential for maintaining good
control of compaction.

Lift Thickness

Lift thicknesses must be well planned, and compactability should be the
driving factor in that decision. If the desired lift thickness is less than the rec-
ommended values, then a mixture with a smaller nominal maximum aggre-
gate size should be used so that proper compaction can be achieved. See
Chapter 9 for recommended lift thicknesses based on nominal maximum
ageregate size.
