9.5.3 Erratic Density

Erratic density is more difficult to troubleshoot because, by its nature, it
is not uniform. In addition to ensuring all the items listed in the previous
section have been checked, one should look for changing conditions and
erratic roller operation. Changes in density, temperature, mix characteris-
tics, paver speed, etc. should be monitored.

9.5.4 Roller Marks

Roller marks are any marks or deficiencies caused by the rollers.

* Pneumatic-tire rollers can “pick up” if the tires are not hot, properly
inflated, and clean.

Rollers sitting behind a stopped paver will leave marks in the mat that
will be almost impossible to remove. If a roller has to stop, it must
stop in an area where the mix has cooled.

¢ If a roller is turned too sharply while it is moving, the edge of the
drum can cut or tear the mat.

° If marks are still present after the finish roller has completed, the
finish roller is either too light or the time between intermediate and
finish rolling was too great (mix cooled too much).

° Poorly maintained equipment, such as worn or pitted drums, can
imprint patterns in the mat.

The key to uniform density throughout the compaction operation is
keeping track of the little details and monitoring conditions. Compacting
hot mix asphalt is not difficult, what is challenging is doing it consistently
all the time.
