Developing a Program

A pavement preservation program can be initiated with a decision to
allocate funds to preservation treatments at different levels depending
on the pavement conditions. The appropriate amount of funds allocated
to preventive maintenance, rehabilitation, and reconstruction activities
depends on the current and projected condition of the agency’s pavement
network. However, using the pavement management system, an agency
can evaluate different funding allocations to determine which strategy
comes closest to meeting the agency’s targeted conditions. Agencies that
have been most successful with their pavement preservation program have
initiated other activities to support the preservation program, including
training at all levels within the organization, the establishment of dedicated
funds for preventive maintenance treatments, and the establishment of
guidelines that help ensure that treatments are consistently used effectively
throughout the organization.

Estimating Needs

The pavement management system can be used to identify funding needs
based on current and projected pavement conditions. To estimate needs, an
agency must first establish either a minimum condition level (above which
the network should be maintained) or a targeted condition level for each
road classification (such as arterials, collectors, and local roads). Using
the pavement condition information in the pavement management system,
the number of miles of roads that do not meet these condition levels can
be estimated along with the cost of repair at these levels. Together, this
information is used to estimate the total pavement needs of the agency. By
tracking the change in pavement needs over time, an agency can quickly
determine if it is “catching up” or “falling behind.”

Setting Goals

Information from the pavement management system can also be used
to set performance targets for the pavement network. This is typically
an iterative process in which anticipated funding levels are input into
the pavement management system and expected condition levels at a
particular point in time are calculated. By varying the funding levels input
into the analysis, a realistic estimate of future conditions can be determined
and a targeted goal can be established. In most cases, different pavement
condition levels are established for different road classifications in a
typical network, reflecting the differences in traffic levels, traffic speeds,
and customer expectations. For example, a lower targeted condition might
be established for local streets than for arterial roads that serve a larger
number of vehicles traveling at higher traffic speeds.

The establishment of a goal is an important part of a pavement
preservation program because it helps the agency measure the program’s
effectiveness. The best goals are based on information from the pavement
management system, can easily be remembered, define the expected
conditions, and set the timeframe for achieving the targets.
