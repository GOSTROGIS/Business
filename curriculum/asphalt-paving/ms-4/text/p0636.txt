Once the length of the shot has been determined, the distributor
driver merely has to maintain a preset speed. Although the distributor
contains several metering devices to indicate asphalt application rate,
it is necessary to make specific calculations after each shot. This can
be done quite simply using the formula that follows. To check the rate
of application, it is necessary to know the number of gallons (liters)
of asphalt sprayed as well as the length and width of the spread.

Apply the aggregate.

All aggregate needed for the planned spread should be on hand
before starting. When the distributor moves forward to spray asphalt,
the aggregate spreader should follow immediately behind it. The
asphalt must be covered within one minute. Otherwise, the increase
in viscosity that takes place within that time may prevent good wet-
ting and binding of the aggregate. It is also important that aggregate
be spread uniformly at the specified rate. In a single application,
aggregate normally does not stick to the asphalt if applied more
than one particle thick. It is useless and wasteful to apply the aggre-
gate at a rate greater than for a single layer.

Excellent control is possible with mechanical spreaders. A uniform
application rate can be ensured with a properly adjusted spreader if
a tachometer is used to maintain uniform speed. Another aid in con-
trolling distribution rates is to mark the length that each truckload of
ageregate should cover.

Roll aggregate into the asphalt binder.

Rolling seats the aggregate into the asphalt membrane and thus pro-
motes the bond necessary to resist traffic stresses. Pneumatic-tire rollers
should be used on all surface treatment jobs. Pneumatic-tire rollers give
uniform pressure over the entire area. Better results will be obtained if
two self-propelled rollers are used with each aggregate spreader.
