With a drum-mix plant, setting the different feeder speeds actually
establishes the gradation blend in the final mix.

Modern batch plants often have more feed aggregates than hot bins,
with the blending of two or more materials that have similar gradations
but different characteristics (like natural fine aggregates and manufactured
fine aggregates). Therefore, most modern batch plants use belt feeders
with variable-speed motors and calibration charts and also set feeder
speeds for their feed bins to keep final gradation accurate, much as drum-
mix plants do. When final gradation is controlled at the cold-feed bins, the
screening unit in the plant is usually bypassed to increase production and
reduce screen wear.

It is important that the calibrated output of these belt feeders be checked
periodically for maximum mix accuracy. Changes to the speed/output
relationship can occur because of buildup inside the feed bins or fatigue to
the motor/drive unit. Some regulatory agencies require calibration checks
at certain intervals or before the start of large projects. Calibration accu-
racy should be checked on a regular basis, regardless of requirements.
