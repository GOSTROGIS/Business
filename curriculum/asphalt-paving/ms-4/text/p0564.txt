A comprehensive pavement distress survey provides a good indication of
the overall condition of the pavement and suggests the primary mechanisms
causing the deterioration.

Manual Surveys

Pavement condition surveys may be conducted using either manual or
automated approaches. In a manual survey, individuals collect the pavement
distress information by walking the pavement surface or by viewing the pave-
ment surface from a slow-moving vehicle traveling along the shoulder. Man-
ual surveys are more commonly used in areas without heavy traffic volumes,
which might hinder the data collection process or impede the safety of the
survey crews. An example of a manual pavement condition survey proce-
dure for low-volume asphait-surfaced roads is provided as Appendix E in the
Asphalt Institute’s MS-17, Asphalt Overlays for Highway and Street Rehabili-
tation. A copy is also available in the Asphalt Institute’s Information Series,
IS-169, A Pavement Rating System for Low-Volume Asphalt Roads.

Automated Surveys

Where traffic conditions must be considered, some agencies conduct
automated surveys using data collection vehicles that capture digital or video
images of the pavement surface at prevailing speed limits; these images are
later processed in the office. In addition to addressing some of the safety con-
siderations associated with the conduct of pavement condition surveys, many
of the vehicles used to conduct automated surveys are also equipped with
sensors to record rutting, roughness, and faulting measures. These vehicles
may also include a camera for taking panoramic shots along the roadway,
which can be useful for monitoring the condition of signs or other road-
way hardware. Some devices are equipped with special lighting to eliminate
problems caused by shadows or to facilitate surveys conducted at night. An
example of an automated data collection van is provided in Figure 11.4.

The images captured during the survey process are typically post-processed
in an office. Sensor data are usually processed in real time and reported in
accordance with guidelines established by the agency. Distress data may be
processed. automatically using crack recognition software capable of recog-
nizing and quantifying variations in grayscale that relate to the cracks on the
pavement surface. Alternatively, the images may be viewed at a workstation
where distress details are manually reviewed at a computer and distress quan-
tities are recorded. This latter approach is referred to as a semi-automated
method of distress processing.

The use of this technology has some disadvantages. The primary disad-
vantage is that it is difficult to discern characteristics associated with verti-
cal displacement when using this technology. For example, it is difficult
to determine whether there is a vertical displacement along a crack using
this technology. Some pavement condition survey procedures use verti-
cal displacement along a crack as a means of determining crack severity.

that prevent the equipment from operating at constant speeds. Because
