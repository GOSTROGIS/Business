Soft and weak particles are often identified using AASHTO T 112
(ASTM C 142). Such particles often break down in stockpiles when they are
exposed to moisture, thus creating smaller particles and unwanted fines. In
this procedure, the particles finer than the No. 200 (0.075 millimeter) sieve
are removed by washing the material following the procedures detailed in
AASHTO T 11 (ASTM C 117). The material is then separated into fractions of
designated sizes and soaked in water for 24 hours. The soaked particles are
each individually squeezed between fingers to break down into smaller pieces
any material that might be clay lumps or friable particles. The material is then
washed over a designated sieve, and the dry mass of the retained material
is determined. The amount of clay lumps and friable particles is calculated
by determining the amount of material passing the designated wash screen
and expressing it as a percentage of the original sample mass before the
24-hour soak.

For HMA, organic materials are usually identified on a visual basis and
specifications typically reflect this.
