Introduction

Thickness design for flexible pavements is an effort to economize the
amount and the type of material used to support the projected traffic,
on the given subgrade, under the specific climate conditions at the site.
Whether thickness design is applied to major interstate highways, city
streets, airports, or county roads, its objective is to strike a balance between
two competing goals—structural capacity and life-cycle cost.

The state-of-the-art method in flexible pavement design is the Mechanistic-
Empirical (M-E) method. The M-E method typically models the pavement as a
multilayered elastic system and uses the principles of mechanics to calculate
deflections, stresses, and strains at any point in the structure in response to
an external wheel load. Calculated strains are then compared with observed
pavement performance to determine the required pavement thickness. In the
case of flexible pavements, two performance criteria are widely considered
critical: limiting horizontal tensile strain at the bottom of the hot mix asphalt
(HMA) layer to avoid fatigue, and limiting vertical compressive strain at the
top of the subgrade to avoid rutting.

The Asphalt Institute method encompasses two types of design proce-
dures, both of which have been incorporated into SW-1, Asphalt Pavement
Thickness Design Software.

° DAMA—Advanced Structural Analysis. DAMA is a_ powerful
computer-based M-E program that allows engineers to analyze any
pavement system having up to five layers in which the materials can
be described in terms of elastic modulus and Poisson’s ratio. Engi-
neers using DAMA consider monthly temperature variation, including
freeze/thaw, and its effect on pavement materials to estimate remain-
ing life to failure. DAMA is embedded into SW-1, Asphalt Pavement
Thickness Design Software.

° Design Curves. The Asphalt Institute publishes pavement design curves
in the following manuals for specific transportation design applications:

° 1S-154 General Aviation Airports

° MS-1 Highways, Roads, and Streets

° MS-11 Air-Carrier Airports

¢ MS-23 Heavy Wheel Loads (Ports/Industrial Applications)

The curves presented in these publications were produced by presolving
a large number of problems over a wide range of input values using DAMA.
The resulting design curves provide users with a powerful solution based
on M-E, in a handy manual that can be used in the office or the field.
