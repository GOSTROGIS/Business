Some clays and clean sands require lower compaction pressures. It may be
necessary, if the surface is uneven, to smooth and level the tamped surface.

The exudation pressure test is used to determine the compressive stress
necessary to exude water from the compacted specimen. Results from
tests on several specimens are used to determine the R-value at a standard
pressure of 300 pounds per square inch (2070 kiloPascals).

In this test, a bronze disk is placed on top of the tamped specimen in
the mold, and a piece of filter paper is placed on the disk. The assembly is
then inverted so that the filter paper is on the bottom. Before the assembly
is positioned in the testing machine, it is placed on the contact plate of the
moisture exudation indicator (see Figure 5.4).

A ram is used to force the sample down in the mold to the contact plate
of the exudation indicator. The testing machine then applies a load to the
ram at the rate of 2000 pounds (8.896 kiloNewtons) per minute until water
exuding from the sample on the indicator plate lights five of the six outer
lights of the indicator. Total load on the sample at that moment is con-
verted to pounds per square inch (kiloPascals) and recorded as exudation
pressure. Specimens having exudation pressures of less than 100 pounds
per square inch (689 kiloPascals) or more than 800 pounds per square
inch (5520 kiloPascals) are usually discarded.
