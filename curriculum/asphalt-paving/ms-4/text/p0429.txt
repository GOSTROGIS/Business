Establishing the Proper Paving Speed

Planning is necessary for achieving a high quality asphalt pavement.
Planning begins with establishing the amount of material to be produced,
the number of trucks needed, the hauling distance, and the amount of
HMA that can be placed and compacted before the temperature falls
below compaction levels. The most common paver operator error is exces-
sive paver speed. The paver operator must monitor the arrival rate of the
material to the job site, the flow of material through the paver, and the
pace of the compaction train. Paver speed should be closely monitored to
match the consumption rate of material through the paver with the HMA
production and delivery.

Calculating Paver Yield

During HMA placement, the paving supervisor and/or the inspector
should periodically check the yield of the paving operation. The purpose
is to cross-check the thickness measurements to ensure that the HMA is
being placed at the proper average thickness. Some agencies use paver
yield (or spread rate) as an acceptance criteria in lieu of thickness. This
check can be made on each truckload of HMA, but adjustments to the
thickness should not be made that often. A longer distance (1,000 feet; 300
meters) may be more appropriate.

The first step in calculating paver yield is to determine the post-
compaction unit weight (pounds per square yard; kilograms per square
meter) of the HMA. This can be determined from the bulk-specific grav-
ity from previous cores or from nuclear density measurements. The post-
compaction unit weight of the HMA and the mat’s thickness, width, and
length can be used to calculate the yield. The following is an example
problem and its solution:

Problem

A series of trucks has delivered 425.5 tons (386.0 tonnes) of HMA to
the project. This HMA covered 3,500 feet (1,066.9 meters) of pavement.
The paver is placing the HMA 12 feet (3.66 meters) wide and 1% inches
(38.1 millimeters) thick (compacted). The HMA has a unit weight of 151.5
pounds per cubic foot (2,426.8 kilograms per cubic meter).

Solution (English units)

Calculate the required HMA weight:

(length x width x depth x in-place density)
(2,000 pounds/ton)

(3,500 feet) x (12 feet) x (0.125 foot) x (151.5 pounds per cubic foot)
(2000 pounds/ton)

= 397.7 tons of HMA

Therefore, 7.0 percent more HMA than needed is being placed
(425.5/397.7) or an average of 1.60 inches (versus the 1.5 inches
required).
