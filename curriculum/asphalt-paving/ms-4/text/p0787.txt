15.8 Leaching Pads

Because asphalt is an inert material, it can be used to construct all sorts
of structures for handling very corrosive materials.

15.8.1 Uses of Leaching Pads

Leaching pads are used to extract metals from low-grade ores, such as

copper, gold, etc. Acid solutions are sprayed over stockpiles of the ore, and

‘the solutions leach out the metals. The metals are then recovered from the
leachates.

15.8.2 Design of Leach Pads

Leach pads are designed using hot mix asphalt with a high binder con-
tent of a soft grade to assure a flexible pavement capable of withstanding
some subgrade settlement.

To ensure that the pad pavement is impermeable, leach pads are sealed
with a high softening point (polymer-modified) asphalt.

Also included in the design are dikes, channels, and retention ponds for
gathering and holding the leachate.

15.8.3 Materials

Mix Binder—The binder selection should be one or two grades softer
than what would usually be used for asphalt pavements in the climatic
area where the pads are to be constructed. Example: If a PG 70-10 is the
normal paving grade used, then select either a PG 64-10 or a PG 58-10.

Mix aggregate—Use a %-inch (19 millimeters) nominal-size aggregate,
with a dust-to-binder ratio of at least 1, and design for 0 air voids.

Membrane seal—Use a 170/200 pen or high softening point material
that is applied at the rate of 0.35 gallon per square yard (1.10 liters per
square meter).

15.8.4 Construction of Leaching Pads

The following steps should be followed in constructing a leaching pad.
1. Prepare the subgrade and compact to a uniform density.
2. Place 4 inches (100 millimeters) of the hot mix asphalt and compact
to 96 percent of maximum specific gravity.
