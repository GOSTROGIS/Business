Mineral Aggregate

During the development of Superpave, pavement experts agreed that
certain aggregate characteristics were critical to a well-performing HMA.
These characteristics were broken into two categories: consensus proper-
ties and source properties. A detailed discussion of these properties and
test procedures is included in Chapter 3, “Mineral Aggregates and Mixture
Additives.

Consensus Aggregate Properties

The consensus aggregate properties used in the Superpave mix design
procedure are the following: coarse aggregate angularity, fine aggregate
angularity, flat and elongated particles, and clay content. The criteria for
these properties are based on the traffic level and position within the
pavement structure. The materials near the pavement surface are subjected
to higher stress levels and require materials of higher quality. The criteria
should generally be applied to a proposed aggregate blend rather than
individual components. The desired consensus properties are detailed in
Table 4.2.

Source Aggregate Properties

The source properties used in the Superpave mix design procedure
are the following: toughness or Los Angeles Abrasion, sodium or mag-
nesium sulfate soundness and deleterious materials. These properties are
source specific, and the requirements vary throughout the United States
because of the variety of aggregate types available in the various geo-
graphical areas. The requirement for a maximum loss value from the Los
Angeles Abrasion test will typically range from 35 percent to 45 percent.
