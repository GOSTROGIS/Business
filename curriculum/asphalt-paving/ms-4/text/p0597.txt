11.4.3 Types of Maintenance and
Rehabilitation Treatments

Three general treatment categories that are considered for a pavement
depending on the type and amount of deterioration present: preventive
maintenance, rehabilitation, and reconstruction. A general description of
each level of repair that might be considered is presented here. Further
information on pavement maintenance and rehabilitation treatments can
be found in Chapter 12, “Maintenance of Asphalt Pavements” and Chapter
13, “Rehabilitation of Asphalt Pavements” respectively.

Preventive Maintenance

Preventive maintenance treatments are generally applied to pavements
in good condition and without much structural deterioration. They include
a variety of low-cost treatments aimed at addressing specific types of
functional deterioration or slowing the rate of pavement deterioration.
Examples of preventive maintenance treatments include crack sealing,
surface seals, thin hot mix overlays, and microsurfacing.

Rehabilitation

As pavement deterioration progresses, more substantial treatments may
become necessary. Treatments included in this category extend the life of
existing pavement structures by either restoring existing structural capacity
or by increasing pavement thickness to accommodate existing or projected
traffic loading conditions. Pavement rehabilitation may be considered
minor rehabilitation or major rehabilitation. Minor rehabilitation treatments
include non-structural enhancements, such as thin functional overlays.
Major rehabilitation, on the other hand, adds structural enhancements to
a pavement section. An example of a major overlay treatment is a thick
overlay.

Reconstruction

Reconstruction is the replacement of the entire existing pavement
structure, typically including the removal and replacement of all pavement
layers including the base. New or recycled materials may be used in a
reconstructed pavement section. Reconstruction is the most substantial
level of repair considered in a pavement management system. It is
recommended on pavement sections that are considered to be in failed
condition.

11.4.4 Prioritizing Project Needs

Most agencies are faced with the challenge of having insufficient funds
to address all their pavement needs. Pavement management systems assist
agencies in this situation by determining the most cost-effective use of
available funds. A number of different techniques can be used to determine
the optimal use of funds, as discussed in the following sections.
