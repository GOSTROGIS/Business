Asphalt is first classified, and attempts are made to trace the
connection between asphalt and petroleum.

P. C. LeSage conducts the first exposition of the modern theory
of the origin of asphalt, in France.

First asphalt composition of prepared roofing appears in Sweden.
Rock asphalt is used in France for floor, bridge, and sidewalk
surfacing.

In London, John L. McAdam begins building and improving
roads with an interlocked and compacted stone surface. This
“macadam” innovation has a profound impact on the roadways
of the future.

Asphalt paving blocks are first used.

In Lyons, France, asphalt-mastic pavements are first used.

The first account of asphalt deposits in the United States is revealed
in Connecticut. Also, the first exhaustive treatise on the chemistry of
asphalt, by J. B. Borissingault in Paris, is published.

Rock asphalt is imported and used in sidewalk construction in
Philadelphia.

First composition asphalt roofing is used in the United States.
Near a remote hamlet called Black Creek in southern Ontario,
Canada, Charles Tripp and James Miller Williams registered the
world’s first commercial oil well.

First modern asphalt road, composed of 2 inches (50 millimeters)
of compressed asphalt mastic, is laid in Paris, France.

First compacted asphalt pavement is laid in London, England.
First U.S. asphalt roadways are laid in Newark, New Jersey.

First sheet asphalt pavement is laid on Pennsylvania Avenue in
Washington, D.C. using imported asphalt from Trinidad Lake.
Barber Asphalt Paving Company is heavily involved in early pav-
ing equipment, development, and construction of nearly half of
all the early sheet asphalt pavements.

Warren Brothers builds the first successful, reproducible asphalt
concrete pavements. They also pioneer the development of early
hot mix asphalt facilities.

Approximately 20,000 tons (18,000 tonnes) of asphalt cement is
produced in the United States from the refining of petroleum.
