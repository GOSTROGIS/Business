Remove and Patch Any Unstable Areas

During rubblization and rolling, any area found to be unstable must be
removed and replaced. It is recommended that the section be replaced
with a high-quality aggregate base or with HMA.

Place Asphalt HMA Leveling Course/HMA Overlays

Rubblized surfaces cannot be trimmed or fine graded like conventional
granular surfaces. Larger pieces will be disturbed by fine-grading equip-
ment, resulting in the loss of particle interlock and an overall reduction in
the strength of the rubblized layer. Leveling courses may be necessary to
accommodate grade and profile corrections.

Placement of the HMA overlay on a rubblized surface is accomplished
according to the procedures discussed in Chapter 8, “Hot Mix Asphalt
Paving.” Tack coats are not used because of the pickup of the rubblized
surface by the construction equipment. Belly-dump trailers and windrow
elevators for transferring the HMA into the hopper of the paving machine
should not be used to place the first lift of HMA over rubblized PCC. It is
suggested that the first lift be placed with a tracked paving machine. It is
also recommended that traffic be kept off the surface until the entire HMA
overlay has been placed.

14.4.4 Specification Criteria and Inspection

The rubblization process will result in smaller particles near the surface of
the pavement; these particles will range from sand size to 3 inches (75 milli-
meters) to 12-15 inches (300-380 millimeters) at the bottom of the rubblized
layer on very thick PCC pavements. The actual size will vary depending on
the slab thickness, the type of reinforcement, the subgrade strength, and the
equipment used to rubblize. The specifications usually require that the con-
tactor dig a test pit on the first day of production to verify the efficiency of
the rubblization process. The specification requirements vary. Airfield proj-
ects funded by the Federal Aviation Administration require at least 75 per-
cent of the particles smaller than 3 inches (75 millimeters) at the surface and
12 inches (300 millimeters) in the bottom half. Other highway projects require
all the particles to be less than 10 inches (250 millimeters) on the surface.
