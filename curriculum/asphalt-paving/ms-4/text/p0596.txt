Benefits and Limitations of Tools

There are advantages and disadvantages to each approach used to
determine appropriate treatments in a pavement management system.
All approaches are similar in that they define the conditions that must exist
before a treatment is considered feasible in the analysis. Some agencies
prefer the decision-tree approach because it is generally easier to explain
than “if-then” statements. However, they can become quite detailed and
difficult to draw when many factors are taken into account.

There are several limitations to the use of these tools that should be
considered by any agency establishing a pavement management system.
First, in some cases, treatment rules are defined in a manner so that only
one given treatment is appropriate for a given set of conditions. While
this might sometimes be true, in many cases, multiple treatments may
in fact be appropriate, and the pavement management system should
determine the cost-effectiveness of each strategy. Therefore, the treat-
ment rules should be set up to allow multiple treatments to be selected
when appropriate.

Second, the agency has to be sure that no conditions are excluded in
the analysis. This is a common mistake made when defining treatment
rules because of gaps in the rules. For instance, one rule might define a
treatment for pavement conditions between 50 and 70 and another might
define treatments for pavement conditions between 75 and 90. A gap occurs
between the pavement conditions 70 to 75 where no treatment is defined.

Finally, because treatment practices can change within an agency,
it is important that the treatment rules and cost be reviewed at least
annually to ensure that they reflect current conditions. Otherwise, the
pavement management system could be making treatment recommen-
dations that do not reflect agency practices or estimating the cost of the
treatment erroneously.

Determining Treatment Effectiveness and Timing

The effectiveness of any maintenance or rehabilitation treatment is
dependent on its design, construction, and timing. This is especially true
with preventive maintenance treatments because there is a narrow window
during which they can be applied effectively. A pavement management
system can help an agency determine the most cost-effective time to place
various treatments using the benefit/cost ratio described earlier.

Developing Guidelines for Treatment Selection

To assist agencies in consistently using treatments effectively, a number
of agencies have developed guidelines for treatment selection that assist
field personnel in choosing projects and that guide construction inspection
procedures. These guidelines define the conditions that might exist for a
treatment to be considered a feasible alternative, including distress type,
severity, and extent. Photos of appropriate pavement surfaces for each
treatment might also be included in the guidelines to help field personnel
better understand when a pavement section is considered appropriate.
