11.2 Pavement Evaluation

11.2.1 Introduction

A pavement management system relies on objective, repeatable pavement
condition information to determine current and future maintenance and
rehabilitation needs. Therefore, conducting a meaningful pavement evalu-
ation is one of the first steps in the identification of feasible maintenance
and rehabilitation strategies. Two types of pavement evaluations are nor-
mally conducted as part of an agency’s pavement management practices:
a functional evaluation and a structural evaluation. A functional evaluation
considers the surface characteristics of a road, including certain types of
cracking, surface smoothness, noise, and surface friction characteristics.
A structural evaluation is used to determine the ability of the pavement
structure to carry traffic loadings. A structural evaluation typically requires
detailed information about pavement layer thicknesses, paving layer mate-
rial properties, subgrade support conditions, traffic, and the response of
the pavement to loading. Nondestructive testing (NDT) using falling weight
deflectometer (FWD) devices is one of the most common approaches to
determining the response of the pavement under traffic loadings. More
detailed information on methods used to evaluate pavement functional
and structural characteristics follows.

11.2.2 Functional Characteristics and
Evaluation

Pavement surface characteristics are typically incorporated into pave-
ment condition surveys to support the types of analyses conducted with
a pavement management system. Three of the most common methods of
evaluating the functional condition of a pavement are discussed here.

Pavement Distress

By collecting pavement distress information, an agency is able to
determine the relative amount of pavement deterioration present and
the appropriate level of repair that is needed to correct the deficiencies.
Many different types of pavement distress are included in a pavement
condition survey, as discussed further in Section 11.3. Some types of pave-
ment distress (such as transverse and block cracking) reflect deteriora-
tion mechanisms that are more functional in nature, such as deterioration
caused by climate or material properties. Other types of pavement distress
(including alligator cracking or rutting) indicate load-related deterioration.
