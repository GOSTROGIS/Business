The handbook is divided into the following five sections:

° Chapters 2, 3, and 4 cover the materials used to build HMA pavements
and how to design those materials.

* Chapters 5 and 6 cover the design of an HMA pavement, starting with
the subgrade or foundation soils and then the actual thickness design
of the HMA layer.

° Chapters 7, 8, 9, and 10 cover the construction of the asphalt layer,
starting with production and moving through to quality control of the
HMA.

* Chapters 11, 12, 13, and 14 present information on the management
of the HMA pavement, including maintenance and rehabilitation.

* Chapter 15 covers special uses of asphalt and HMA.

Chapter 2—Petroleum Asphalt

This chapter discusses the manufacture of the asphalt binder to obtain
desired properties. It also discusses the historical tests that have been
used, and are still used in many parts of the world, to describe the proper-
ties of asphalt cement. It describes in detail the Performance Graded (PG)
system for specifying asphalt binders and the testing associated with that
system. It also describes the manufacture and testing of emulsified and
cutback asphalts. A brief discussion of air blown asphalts is provided.
Finally, the steps that must be taken to ensure that those working around
asphalt cements do it in a safe manner are also provided. ,

Chapter 3—Mineral Aggregates and Mixture Additives

This chapter discusses aggregate processing and aggregate properties.
Included in this discussion are sources of aggregates and the properties
of those aggregates as they affect the quality and performance of an HMA
mixture. The tests used to evaluate the quality and properties of vari-
ous aggregate sources are also discussed. The tools needed to analyze a
composite aggregate blend are presented, and example problems are pro-
vided. The properties and handling of recycled asphalt pavement (RAP)
materials are discussed, along with the proper procedures for sampling
and testing RAP. Recent developments in the construction of HMA pave-
ments have introduced the use of mixture additives. The use of these
materials is discussed.

Chapter 4—Hot Mix Asphalt Mix Design

This chapter presents the basic concepts of HMA mix design and the
steps to calculate the volumetric properties of an HMA mixture. It covers
the Superpave mix design procedure in detail and introduces the back-
ground of the Marshall and Hveem mix design procedures. This chapter
also covers the use of reclaimed asphalt pavement (RAP) in an HMA mix
