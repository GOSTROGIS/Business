13.2.5 Drainage

If drainage problems are a contributing factor to the need for the overlay,
they must be addressed. A frequent cause of failure is inadequate pavement
and subgrade drainage. An assessment of the drainage-related pavement
damage is critical in the development of the overlay design. Correcting a
drainage problem can be as simple as cleaning or deepening the side ditches
to permit rapid runoff of surface water, or the correction may be as complex
as installing a longitudinal drainage system. Chapter 5, “Soils and Drainage
for Asphalt Pavements,” contains a discussion of the design and construction
of a longitudinal drainage system.

13.2.6 Interlayer Techniques

Function of an interlayer
An interlayer is a layer of material placed between an existing pavement
(either HMA or PCC) and the overlay. The purpose of the interlayer is to
minimize the reflection cracking in an overlay. Reflection cracking is the
result of horizontal and vertical movements in the cracks in the underly-
ing asphalt pavement. Reflection cracking results in increased roughness
and infiltration of surface water into the underlying layers. This increase
in moisture results in a weakening of the support for the pavement. The
use of an interlayer will not always prevent reflection cracking, but it has
been found that the proper use of an interlayer will:
° Retard the occurrence of the reflection cracking (delay the onset of
the cracking)
° Reduce the severity of the crack when it does occur
¢ Provide a waterproofing membrane for the underlying layers
° Reinforce the asphalt overlay to reduce horizontal stresses in the
asphalt layer

Geotextiles

Woven or non-woven synthetic geotextiles made of polypropylene, poly-
ester, fiberglass, nylon, and combinations of these are used as interlayers for
HMA pavements. There are many of these products on the market, and the
engineer is encouraged to contract the local DOT and various suppliers for
information on a specific product. They are placed directly on the existing
pavement surface after a heavy tack coat is applied. The geotextile is rolled
or brushed into the tack coat and an overlay is placed on top of the geotex-
tile. Many agencies place the geotextile within the overlay. This requires two
lifts of HMA and a minimum overlay thickness of 3 inches (75 millimeters).
