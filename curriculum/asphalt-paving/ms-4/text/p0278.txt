These mixes will have higher asphalt contents and lower permeability (due
to greater aggregate surface area and smaller size voids), which will result
in improved durability (ess block and shrinkage cracking). For applica-
tions where the surface will receive little or no vehicle traffic, such as bike
trails and walking paths, consideration should be given to designing the
mix with 22 percent air-voids rather than 4 percent air-voids (standard
requirement) to increase the durability of the mix further and reduce non-
load associated cracking.

The lower levels (binder and base courses) should use the larger aggre-
gate sizes. These mixes provide increased resistance to pavement deforma-
tion. This allows the placement of thicker lifts and requires less asphalt
cement due to less surface area.

The lift thickness for all mixes (surface, binder and base) is dictated by the
nominal maximum aggregate size. If the nominal maximum aggregate size
is too large with relation to the lift thickness it will be very difficult to obtain
proper compaction of the mat. It is recommended that the lift thickness-be
three times the nominal maximum aggregate size for fine graded mixes
and four times the nominal maximum aggregate size for the coarse graded
mixes. These same criteria will apply for SMA mixes. For OGFC mixes the
thickness should be two times the nominal maximum aggregate size.
