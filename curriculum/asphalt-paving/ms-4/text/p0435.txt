slope board used to double-check the specified slope. As soon as true
grade and slope have been established, the controls should maintain the
desired profile.

8.5.3 Longitudinal Joints

A poorly constructed longitudinal joint can greatly affect the life of an
HMA pavement. Joint deterioration starts when air, water, and contami-
nants find their way into the joint, through either segregation, poor den-
sity, or the two mats not bonding properly. As the joint deteriorates, it will
require joint sealing, patching or premature resurfacing. The following
suggestions will greatly improve the chances of constructing a longitudi-
nal joint that is free of segregation, compacted to specified density, and
well bonded to the previously laid HMA pavement:

° Steer a straight line when laying the first pass

* Create as close to a near-vertical edge on the cold-side of the joint as

possible

° Minimize segregation on both outside edges of the screed.

° Apply a tack coat or other approved sealant to the cold side of the

joint

° The hot side of the joint (second pass) should have sufficient depth to
allow proper rolldown and a smooth match with the cold side
