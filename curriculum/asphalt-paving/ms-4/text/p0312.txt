An additional adverse effect of the spring thaw may occur in granular
bases over fine-grained subgrades. The fines can be carried into the granu-
lar base, transforming it from a satisfactory base material into one of low
load-carrying capacity. Under the right conditions a sand filter blanket,
approximately 6 inches (150 millimeters) thick, or a filter fabric, can pre-
vent the migration of fines.

The effects of spring thaw can be minimized by using adequate depths
of granular soils or full-depth asphalt pavement and by maintaining proper
drainage.

5.3.5 Geosynthetic Fabrics

The design of subsurface drainage systems should give consideration
to geotextiles as alternative designs. Geosynthetic fabrics (geotextiles) are
manufactured from a number of different materials, including polypropyl-
ene, polyester, nylon, polyethylene, and polyvinyl chloride. The function of
geotextiles, when used as filters, is to allow groundwater removal without
the buildup of excessive seepage forces or water pressures. The geotextile
must also prevent piping or subsurface erosion of the soil which leads to
settlement. In these applications, water flows through the geotextile into
a water-conducting medium, usually a trench filled with a selected drain-
age aggregate and a slotted or perforated pipe, which quickly removes
the water.

The use of geotextiles for subsurface drainage may be categorized in
accordance with the following three general applications.

° Geotextiles are used as an envelope surrounding the single-size, clean
coarse aggregate (i.e. less than 20 percent retained on a No. 4 sieve
(4.75 millimeters)) in a trench. The geotextile is intended to prevent
subgrade soil from moving into and eventually clogging the stone while
still allowing water to pass through the geotextile. A more efficient sys-
tem is to install drainpipes in the aggregate-filled trench rather than
relying solely on the porous aggregate to carry the runoff. Typically, a
4- to 8-inch (100- to 200-millimeter) perforated flexible plastic pipe is
installed within 5 feet (1.5 meters) of the surface. The sketch on the
right in Figure 5.11 shows this configuration.
