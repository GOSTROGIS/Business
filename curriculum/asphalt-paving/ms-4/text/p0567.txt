Vehicles in slip mode operate on the premise that the highest friction fac-
tor, or critical slip, is found before the test wheel is locked. This phenomenon
resulted in the development of the automatic control systems used on most
aircraft today. Examples of slip devices include the Griptester and SAAB Fric-
tion Tester. Slip testing devices have seen widespread use on highway pave-
ments in the United States.

The third approach to measuring surface friction is the yaw mode, which
measures a sideways friction factor caused by a tire turned at an angle from
the direction of motion. The Mu-Meter is an example of a yaw mode testing
device. Another device for measuring sideways friction, the Sideways-Force
Coefficient Routine Investigation Machine (SCRIM), has a test wheel mounted
at 20 degrees to the direction of motion and operates at speeds of approxi-
mately 40 miles per hour.

Surface friction characteristics can also be estimated using macrotexture
data provided as part of a pavement roughness evaluation. The results show
good correlations to friction measures from more traditional approaches and
increase the potential use of friction data as part of a network-level pavement
management analysis.
