Rolling Patterns

Most paving contractors will create defined “rolling patterns” for a par-
ticular placement on the first day of paving. Nuclear density test data can
play a key role in developing these rolling patterns. A rolling pattern is
defined as the number and sequence of successive passes of compac-
tion equipment over an asphalt pavement. The rolling pattern is generally
adjusted until the desired compaction is achieved. It is a trial and error
process. Details of compaction principles and rolling procedures can be
found in Chapter 9, “Hot Mix Asphalt Compaction.”

In order to provide meaningful information to the quality-control man-
ager, the quality-control density testing must be done at many points in
the rolling operation. Rolling patterns are established on the first day of
paving and ideally done during construction of a test strip. The test strip
will provide calibration of the nuclear gauge with cores cut from the pave-
ment, and the rolling pattern may need to be adjusted. The following
example describes how the quality-control testing is used in the develop-
ment of a compaction control-rolling pattern.

Rolling Pattern Example

The equipment that will be used for the rolling pattern must be the

rolling, and a static steel-wheel compactor for the finish rolling.
