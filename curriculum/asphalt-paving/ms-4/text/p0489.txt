Stockpile samples are often used for development of mixture designs, so it is
critical that the sampling techniques produce truly representative samples.

Because stockpiled aggregates have a tendency to become segregated,
collecting representative samples from stockpiles can be challenging.
Visual observation of the pile, some knowledge about its production,
a good common-sense approach, and proper sampling techniques are
required to overcome the challenges.

Conveyor Belt Sampling

Conveyor belt sampling techniques are generally used to obtain aggre-
gate samples (Figure 10.3). They can be used to sample individual aggregate
materials in production, as they are being transported to a stockpile, or
combined aggregates that have been blended and are being transported to
the dryer of a hot plant. They generally provide good samples if the belt
is properly cut and cleaned, but the procedures are inconvenient because
the belt conveyor must be stopped to retrieve the sample.

retrieve samples without stopping conveyor belts or other plant produc-
