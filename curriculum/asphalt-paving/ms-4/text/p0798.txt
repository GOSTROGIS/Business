15.12 Colored Asphalt

Although not widely done, asphalt pavements can be colored something
other than the usual shades of black and grey. The first coloring method
is to use a natural-color aggregate and the wearing (surface) course. As
the asphalt binder wears way from the surface with traffic, the aggregate
is exposed, revealing its natural color. A second way to color asphalt is
to use an additive in the asphalt binder such as an iron oxide (rust) to
give it a reddish tint. Green, yellow, and orange are also possible with
different ores. Other colors require different metal compounds. A spe-
cial synthetic binder (resin) that contains no asphaltenes has been used
because it colors more readily. By tinting the asphalt binder, the hot mix
asphalt pavement is colored full depth, so there are no surface wear-off
concerns. A third way to color asphalt is to coat the surface with a material
that penetrates the voids and bonds well to an asphalt pavement, such as
an epoxy-fortified acrylic emulsion. Many colors are available with these
types of surface coatings. Care should be taken that these surface coatings
do not negatively affect surface friction if the pavement is used for vehicu-
lar traffic. One possible disadvantage of these systems is that they may
wear off with time and need to be renewed. The second and third options
require the use of specialty products, and more information about them
can be obtained by contacting individual manufacturers.
