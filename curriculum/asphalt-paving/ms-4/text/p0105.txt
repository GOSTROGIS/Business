When the phase separation of an emulsified asphalt begins, the emulsion
is said to “break.” Once the emulsion breaks, the asphalt droplets coalesce
and produce a continuous film of asphalt on the aggregate or pavement. The
speed of this coalescence of asphalt is related to the setting characteristics of
the grade. As expected, coalescence occurs more quickly with the rapid-setting
grades. The manner and rate at which emulsified asphalts break depends
largely on the quantity and properties of the emulsifying agent used in manu-
facture of the emulsion, and on the relative proportion of water and asphalt.

Cutback Asphalts

Petroleum solvents used for dissolving asphalt cement are variously
called distillate, diluent, or cutter stock. If the solvent used in making
the cutback asphalt is highly volatile, it will quickly escape by evapora-
tion. Solvents of lower volatility evaporate more slowly. Cutback asphalts
are divided into the following three types based on the relative speed of
evaporation:

° Rapid-curing (RC)—asphalt cement combined with a light diluent of
high volatility, generally with a boiling point similar to gasoline or
naphtha. Grades include RC-70, RC-250, RC-800, and RC-3000.

° Medium-curing (MC)—asphalt cement combined with a medium diluent
of intermediate volatility, generally with a boiling point similar to kero-
sene. Grades include MC-30, MC-70, MC-250, MC-800, and MC-3000.

* Slow-curing (SC)—asphalt cement combined with oils of low volatility.
Grades include SC-70, SC-250, SC-800, and SC-3000.

Slow-curing cutback asphalts may also be referred to as road oils. This
term originated in earlier days when asphalt residual oil was used to give
roads a low-cost, all-weather surface.

The degree of liquidity developed in each cutback asphalt depends
principally on the proportion of solvent to asphalt cement. To a minor
degree, the liquidity of the cutback may be affected by the hardness of the
base asphalt from which the cutback is made. The more viscous grades of
cutback asphalt are indicated by higher numbers in the grade designation
and may require a small amount of heating to make them fluid enough for
construction operations.

Uses of Emulsified and Cutback Asphalts

Emulsified asphalts have advantages over asphalt cements in that they
can be used with cold as well as heated aggregate, and with aggregate that
is dry or damp. Their ability to be used with damp aggregate gives emulsi-
fied asphalts an advantage over cutback asphalts as well.

Emulsified asphalts are used both for road construction and for many
specialty applications. The rapid-setting grades are designed to react
quickly with aggregate and revert from the emulsion state to asphalt. They
are used primarily for spray applications, such as aggregate (chip) seals,
sand seals, and similar surface treatments. The RS-2 and CRS-2 grades have
a higher viscosity to minimize the potential for runoff.
