designed to drain completely within 24 hours. This feature allows the
underlying soils to dry out between storms and thus preserves capacity
for the next storm. Recommendations are that the thickness of the porous
pavement structure exceed the depth of the frost penetration in the area
where the porous pavement is being placed and that the pavement should
be at least 2 feet (0.6 meter) above bedrock and 3 feet (1 meter) above the
water table.

At the bottom of the porous pavement, a filter fabric should be placed
to prevent fines from migrating into the layers above it. The fabric will also
provide some stability for construction of the reservoir layer.

15.11.3 Construction

Constructing the porous pavement near the end of any other construc-
tion work prevents other construction debris from clogging the porous
surface. The site area should be protected from excessive heavy equip-
ment operating on the surface, which compacts the soil and reduces per-
meability. The following is a summary of the recommended construction
sequence:

1. Excavate the subgrade using either track equipment or vehicles
with over-sized tires to reduce the amount of compaction of the
subgrade.

2. Place a filter fabric on the uncompacted subgrade.

3. Place 1% to 3 inches (37.5 to 75 millimeters) of rock on the filter
fabric in lifts to the design thickness, keeping construction equip-
ment off the fabric. It should be lightly compacted with plate
compactors or light rollers.

4, Place a thin 1- to 2-inch (25 to 50 millimeters) layer of %-inch
(12.5 millimeters) crushed aggregate over the rock layer.

5. Place a 2- to 4inch (50 to 100 millimeters) layer of an OGFC. The
OGFC should be designed as discussed in Chapter 4, “Hot Mix
Asphalt (HMA) Design.” A polymer-modified asphalt should be
considered.

15.11.4 Maintenance

The porous pavement should be inspected periodically to check for
surface ponding, which indicates possible clogging. The porous surface
can be vacuumed, flushed or jet washed to help maintain the pavement’s
porosity.
