Filter Material and Drain Rock

The purpose of pavement subdrainage is to enhance the long term (10
to 15 years) performance of the pavement. Thus, it is necessary to design
the drainage system for long-term performance. Such a system requires
the use of filters around drainage pipes to ensure performance. The filter
allows water to flow to the drain and, at the same time, to prevent the loss
of the fine particles from the soil. The design of a granular filter for any
particular soil was defined by Terzaghi and Peck as follows:

D15, filter < 5 * D85, soil (for soil retention)
D15, filter > 4 * D15, soil (for adequate permeability)

where D,, refers to the diameter of the soil or filter particle at the stated
percent passing.

The material that meets these criteria for most subgrade soils is washed
mortar sand, which is a clean, essentially one-size sand. Unfortunately, the
permeability of mortar sand, while greater than that of most subgrade soils
by several orders of magnitude, is still low for high-flow situations. Con-
sequently, for adequate drainage, a perforated pipe is typically required.
Such pipes are usually wrapped in a geotextile sock to prevent the sand
particles from washing into the pipe.

Drain rock, on the other hand, essentially consists of one-size stone
material (i.e., greater than % inch [4.75 millimeters]), which results in very
high permeability. It is apparent, however, that drain rock will never meet
the retention criteria for most subgrade soils. As a result, the fine particles
from the adjacent subgrade soils will wash into the drain stone and clog
the material. This loss of soil will result in settlement above and/or beside
the subdrain.

The permeability of a granular filter material is reduced very rapidly as
the percentage of fine material is increased. To prevent these issues, a filter
fabric is sometimes placed between the drain stone and the subgrade soils.
The choice of geotextile and the proper installation are critical to the suc-
cess of this type of construction. An inappropriate geotextile will still allow
fines from the subgrade to pass and result in clogging and settlement.

Subsurface Drainage Schemes

The following approaches to subsurface drainage are suggested.

As noted previously, the installation of continuous subdrains under each
curb in pavements designed with an urban cross section will extend the
life of the pavement. If the expense of a continuous system is prohibitive,
a system of stub drains could be used. For large paved surfaces, this same
stub drain system installed in a cruciform pattern at the catch basins is
also recommended.
