A fine-textured, well-graded HMA surface mixture is required for asphalt
curbs. Asphalt curb HMA differs from regular paving HMA in that the quantity
and the grade of asphalt cement are important factors. Curb and gutter HMA
mixtures perform better when a stiffer grade of asphalt cement is used, and
at a higher content (0.5-1.0 percent higher) compared to normal paving mix-
tures. This additional asphalt increases durability and facilitates compaction.

The usual layout practice is to place string or chalk lines on the existing
pavement to guide the curb machine. A thin coating of tack is applied to
the pavement surface to ensure that the curb is bonded to the pavement.

The temperature of the HMA at the time of placement is very impor-
tant. If the HMA temperature is too low, adequate compaction is impos-
sible to obtain. If the HMA temperature is too high, the curb may slough
off. Normally, a temperature range of 250°-285°F (120°-140°C) is satisfac-
tory. Machine-laid work usually does not require additional compaction. But
