Longitudinal (Linear) and Transverse Cracking

This category of distress includes linear cracks in the pavement surface
that are either parallel or perpendicular to the centerline of the road,
with the exception of linear cracks that are in the wheelpaths (which
are considered fatigue cracks). Longitudinal cracks are parallel to the
pavement centerline and may be caused by the following:

° A poorly constructed paving joint crack, as shown in Figure 11.12

° Shrinkage of the asphalt layer

° Daily temperature cycling

¢ Cracks in an underlying layer that reflect up through the pavement

(with the exception of joint reflection cracking, which is distinguished
as a separate type of crack)

° Longitudinal segregation caused by the improper operation of the paver

Transverse cracks, which are perpendicular to the centerline, can also be
caused by shrinkage in the asphalt layers or by cracking in an underlying layer.
An example of transverse cracking in shown in Figure 11.13. Longitudinal
and transverse cracks are not considered load-related cracks.

Reflection Cracking (PCC Joint Reflection Crack)

Reflection cracking includes cracks that propagate through the HMA layer
of an overlaid Portland cement concrete (PCC) pavement at its joints, or
cracks that propagate through an overlay over a deteriorated asphalt surface.
