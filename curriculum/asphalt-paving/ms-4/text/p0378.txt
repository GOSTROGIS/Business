When drum-mixers first arrived on the hot mix production scene,
microprocessor controls for plant operation did not exist. Feeders, or cold
feeds, therefore, were typically calibrated with charts similar to that shown
in Figure 7.27. The operator, using these types of charts, had to calculate
the required speed for each feeder, mix recipe, and desired production
rate. Since the 1980s, computerized control (see Figure 7.28) has become
the standard for both batch and drum production.

The operator is also given a mix recipe screen, where the desired recipe
is entered. A copy of such a screen is shown in Figure 7.29. In running mix,
the operator selects the Mix Design Formula (MDF) and the production
rate. The processor calculates the required feeder speeds for each feeder
based on both the MDF and the desired rate. When the plant is started,
the feeders start individually in sequence, which minimizes wasted mix
on startup and shutdown. The processor adjusts the feeders to the proper
speed setting for the production rate and mix design.

Feeders are calibrated in dry weights because the same amount of aggre-
gate flows from the feeder at a given speed, regardless of the surface mois-
ture. For proper ratio blending, then, it is important to subtract the surface
moisture when the calibration process is executed. Figure 7.28 illustrates
the means of entering the moisture content during the calibration process.
