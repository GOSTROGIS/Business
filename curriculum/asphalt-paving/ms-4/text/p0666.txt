It applies a uniform tension and pressure to the geotextile, resulting in a
smooth surface without wrinkles. Wrinkles that develop during placement
should be slit and folded over flat. If necessary, the wrinkles should be
tacked down with either an asphalt binder or an asphalt emulsion. This
process should also be used on curves. An overlap of 1-3 inches should be
used between separate passes of the geotextile.

On overlays where a leveling course has been placed, it may be necessary
to apply a light tack coat to the geotextile surface before placing the second
layer. The HMA should be placed soon after the geotextile is placed. Two or
three passes of a pneumatic roller is sometimes necessary to bond the geo-
textile to the old pavement (being careful not to roll up the fabric on the tires
of the roller). Construction traffic may pull the asphalt through the geotextile,
causing the geotextile to become saturated in the wheel paths. As a result, the
truck tires may become covered with asphalt and pull up the geotextile. The
contractor can remedy this by broadcasting a small amount of screenings or
HMA in the wheel paths to reduce the adhesion to the tires and/or reduce
the number of trucks operating on the geotextile. Also, if the paving machine
begins to slip, the contactor can spread a small amount of HMA beneath the
wheels or tracks on the paver to improve traction.

During inspection of the placement of the paving geotextile, the follow-
ing items should be monitored:

° The asphalt membrane should be applied with a calibrated distribu-

tor, and the yield on the tack coat should be checked periodically.

° The geotextile should be placed in such a manner that it is smooth

with no wrinkles.
