Hot Mix AspHat (HMA)
DESIGN

Hot mix asphalt (HMA) consists of a combination of aggregates
uniformly mixed and coated with asphalt cement. To dry the
aggregates and obtain sufficient fluidity of asphalt cement for
proper mixing and workability, both the aggregate and the asphalt
must be heated before mixing—hence the term hot mix. Regardless
of the design procedures used, the design of an HMA mix consists
of the following three steps:

¢ Selection of the type and gradation of the mineral aggregate

° Selection of the type and grade of asphalt binder

° Selection of the amount of asphalt binder to satisfy the project-

specific requirements

Since the majority of HMA is classified as dense-graded, the
majority of this chapter is dedicated to designing dense-graded
mixes. These designs include the Superpave system, which is
becoming the dominant practice in the United States and the
Marshall method and the Hveem method. Designing specialized
mixtures such as OGFCs and SMAs is also covered. Other topics
covered in this chapter include incorporating RAP, using the Bailey
method, mixture performance testing, and selecting the proper mix
for a specific application.
