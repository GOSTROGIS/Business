The final mix should be evaluated for moisture susceptibility using

Determination of optimum mixing temperature

A 1000-gram sample of aggregate is mixed with the proposed asphalt
binder at the asphalt content determined above. It is mixed at a mix tem-
perature corresponding to 800 centistokes using a temperature viscosity
curve as discussed in Section 4.3. When the aggregate is completely coated,
the mix should be transferred to a Pyrex glass plate (see Figure 4.34) and
spread with a minimum of manipulation. The plate should be placed in
an oven at the mixing temperature. Observe the bottom of the plate after
60 minutes. A slight puddle of asphalt cement at the points of contact
between the aggregate and the glass plate is suitable and desirable after
the 60-minute period.

Resistance to the effects of water

An immersion-compression test (AASHTO T 165 and T 167) (ASTM D 1075
and D 1074) is conducted on the designed mixture. The samples are prepared
at the optimum mix temperature as determined above. Use a molding pressure
of 2000 pounds per square inch. After a four-day immersion at 120°F (49°C),
the Index of Retained Strength should not be less than 50 percent. Additives to
promote adhesion will provide adequate retained strength when necessary.

Florida DOT Procedure

The initial asphalt content is determined based on the experience of the
engineer or technician accomplishing the design. Factors to be considered
are the gradation of the aggregate and the absorptive characteristics of the
ageregate. A finer gradation will require more asphalt, and an absorptive
aggregate will require additional asphalt binder.
