Finish Rolling

Finish rolling is done primarily for the aesthetic improvement of the
surface. It should be accomplished with steel-wheel tandems operating in
static mode (without vibration) while the material is still warm enough for
removal of roller marks.

Compaction is the last step that will determine the performance of the
HMA. There is a seemingly endless variety of roller patterns to accomplish
this task. It is a critical function, and finding the right pattern for produc-
ing consistent density results while maintaining smoothness and texture is
a rewarding and long-lasting effort.

9.4.6 Rolling Joints and Edges

Transverse Joints

Just as a variety of different roller patterns are available, a number of dif-
ferent techniques are also available for compacting a transverse joint. Unless
there are restrictions on either side of the lane being placed (live traffic, bar-
riers, curbs, etc.), most operators like to cross roll the joint with the roller on
the previously laid material, except for a 6-inch (150 millimeters) projection
of the wheels when using a tandem (see Figure 9.12) and of one rear wheel
when using a three-wheel roller. This operation should be repeated with
successive passes covering 6-8 inches (150-200 millimeters) of fresh mate-
rial until the entire width of a drive wheel is on the new mix.
