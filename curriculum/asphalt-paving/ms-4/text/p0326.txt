Thickness design in this method utilizes design curves that were developed
from numerous runs of the DAMA analysis tool; however, for ease of use,
significant simplification has reduced the number of input values required
in this method.

All pavement designs for GA airports are assumed to be accomplished for a
MAAT = 60°F (15.5°C), so no climate input is required. Analysis is for a single-
wheel load and the required input is maximum aircraft weight. The number of
departures and other load-related information is standardized for this design pro-
cedure. For material properties, only three combinations of pavement materials
are considered in this design, further simplifying the design procedure:

¢ Full-depth asphalt concrete

° 2-inch (50 millimeters) HMA-over-emulsified-asphalt base

e HMA over a 6-inches (150 millimeters) layer of unbound granular base

Design thicknesses contained in the GA design charts are based on
main-gear wheel loads equal to 47.5 percent of the gross weight. Above
a gross weight of 30,000 pounds (13,500 kilograms), equivalent single-
wheel loads were determined for dual-tire gears. A minimum total thick-
ness of 4 inches (100 millimeters) of asphalt structure is recommended
for these pavements, regardless of subgrade strength.

Altogether, the design curves developed for GA airports offer an easy-
to-use design system based on mechanistic-empirical design principles
tailored for use at general-aviation airports.

Air-carrier Airports

Air-carrier airport pavements accommodate aircraft of 60,000 pounds
(27,000 kilograms) gross weight or more (Figure 6.4). The comprehensive
design manual MS-11, details the air-carrier airport design method, and it
is incorporated into SW-1.

The air-carrier design method uses pavement design curves developed from
numerous runs of a computer-based M-E analytical model. To account for the
differences between aircraft gear and wheel geometry, design aircraft passes
are converted to strain repetitions of an equivalent standard aircraft.

The design curves allow variation in MAAT from 40°F (4.4°C) to 75°F (24°C).

The air-carrier design method considers only full-depth asphalt
pavements, and no consideration is given to pavement sections utilizing
granular base materials. This conforms to Federal Aviation Administration
(FAA) design requirements for stabilized base materials for design aircraft
over 100,000 pounds (45,000 kilograms) gross weight.

The basic steps of the air-carrier pavement design procedure are as
follows:

1. Climate—establishing the appropriate MAAT for the project

2. Traffic—identifying design aircraft and number of departures during
the design period

3. Subgrade—describing subgrade strength in terms of resilient
modulus

4. Solution—working through the curve (or software) to establish the
design thickness for a full-depth asphalt section.
