Condition Survey

A pavement management system uses the results of pavement condition
surveys to help identify current and future maintenance and rehabilitation
needs. All recommendations from the pavement management system are
based on the results of the pavement condition survey so the importance
of this component cannot be underestimated. The results of the survey are
used to report current pavement conditions (usually in terms of a condition
index), to forecast future conditions, and to determine appropriate mainte-
nance and rehabilitation strategies.

The next section of this chapter discusses the different types of pave-
ment condition surveys that are used to support a pavement management
analysis. These techniques range from windshield surveys in which a sub-
jective rating is assigned by a rater to represent the current pavement con-
dition to more objective survey approaches in which distress type, severity,
and quantity are recorded. Many agencies have begun using automated
vehicles to collect pavement condition information. These vehicles oper-
ate at near-traffic speeds, collecting digital images of the pavement surface
and using lasers to record pavement surface irregularities like roughness,
faulting, and rutting.

Regardless of which approach is used, it is important that the survey pro-
cedures are followed consistently from one survey to another survey (and
from one rater to another rater) and that they represent actual conditions.
In general, objective condition surveys are more repeatable than subjective
rating approaches, but they are often more labor intensive. Since it is impor-
tant that pavement condition surveys are updated at least every three years,
each agency must carefully evaluate the costs associated with each pavement
condition survey procedure to ensure that the approach selected matches the
resources available to support this activity.

Database

The inventory and condition information is stored in a pavement manage-
ment database so it is available for use in generating reports and evaluating
different maintenance and rehabilitation strategies. Databases can take a vari-
ety of forms, but relational databases are most commonly used in pavement
management today.
