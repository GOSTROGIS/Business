Although compaction increases the stability of most soils, some soils
decrease in stability when scarified, worked, and rolled. Some soils also
shrink excessively during dry periods and expand excessively when
allowed to absorb moisture. They may also lose strength when reworked—
for example, silts that can become “quick” when remolded. When these
conditions exist, special treatment is required under the supervision of a
qualified geotechnical engineer.

Surface irregularities in an HMA pavement structure can result from
traffic densification of the underlying courses, usually in the wheel paths.
Such irregularities indicate that one or more courses were not adequately
compacted during construction or that soft, unstable areas exist in the
structure. To detect these deficiencies, proof rolling of the subgrade is
sometimes performed during construction. Proof rolling is the application
of heavy, pneumatic-tire rollers or large loaded dump trucks over the sub-
grade, usually following initial compaction by conventional means. Proof
rolling accomplishes two purposes: it locates unstable areas and achieves
additional compaction.

Prepared Subgrade

A prepared subgrade is one that has been worked and compacted. This
may be the foundation soil or a layer of stabilized soil, select soil, or oth-
erwise improved subgrade material. The riding quality of the pavement
surface depends largely on proper construction and preparation of the
foundation material. The roadway should be shaped and rolled so that the
paving equipment has no difficulty in placing material at a uniform thick-
ness and smooth grade. Weather conditions should be suitable, and the
roadway surface should be firm, dust-free, dry, or just slightly damp when
paving operations are started.

Untreated Base

Untreated granular bases should be properly shaped and compacted
immediately before paving. This procedure will restore any proposed line or
grade that may have been damaged during construction. When asphalt pave-
ment courses are to be placed on an untreated aggregate base, loose granular
particles and dust should be lightly swept from the surface. Care should be
taken, however, not to dislodge or otherwise disturb the bond of the aggre-
gate in the surface of the base. When the loose particles have been properly
removed, only the tops of the aggregate in the surface will be exposed. After
the surface has been cleaned, it is ready to be primed with asphalt. For a
discussion about prime coats, see Section 8.2.4, “Prime Coats.”
