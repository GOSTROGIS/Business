Fly Ash

The performance of fly ash as an antistrip has been mixed. It is believed
that the calcium content, and in particular the free or available lime (CaO)
content, is instrumental in the performance of a fly ash as an aid to prevent-
ing asphalt stripping. Fly ash can be introduced into the HMA production
process in the same ways as lime.

Polymeric Treatment

Polymeric aggregate surface treatment (SBR latex) provides a protective
barrier on the aggregate that repels water and waterproofs the aggregate
while providing an improved bonding with the asphalt. When properly
applied, the polymeric-aggregate treatment will turn a hydrophilic aggregate
into a hydrophobic aggregate, increasing the water resistance of the HMA.

One of the additional benefits of using polymeric-aggregate treatment is
that the amount of asphalt binder required in the mixture may be lowered,
resulting in cost savings for the HMA contractor. As the polymer coats the
porous aggregate, less asphalt binder is absorbed into the aggregate.

The SBR latex concentrate must be diluted to 15 percent solids before
use, which is usually accomplished automatically when using an approved
application unit. The latex is then applied to the aggregate on the aggre-
gate feed belt just before entry into the drum dryer. Typical latex appli-
cation rates vary from 0.05 percent to 0.10 percent with an estimated
reduction in asphalt binder content of 0.85 percent to 0.40 percent for
marginal aggregates.
