11.4.2 Selecting the Appropriate
Treatment

One of the primary uses of a pavement management system is the
identification of appropriate maintenance and rehabilitation treatments to
address the types and the extent of pavement deterioration. A pavement
management system can recommend either the appropriate level of repair
or a treatment. Each approach is described in this section.

Determining the Appropriate Level of Repair

In some agencies the pavement management system is used to determine
the level of repair needed rather than the specific treatment that might be
recommended. For instance, a pavement section might be recommended
as a candidate for preventive maintenance, minor rehabilitation, major
rehabilitation, or reconstruction, depending on the results of the pavement
condition survey and the agency’s treatment policies. Average cost
information is associated with each level of repair so the estimated cost
of each project can be determined. Average rates of deterioration for each
treatment are used to predict future conditions. While this approach is
fairly easy to set up and use in the pavement management system, the
use of average cost and performance data may not provide the level of
accuracy desired by some agencies. Agencies that prefer to conduct more
detailed project-level pavement evaluations before selecting the appropriate
treatment might prefer to use this approach in their network-level pavement
management analysis.

Selecting Appropriate Treatments

An alternate approach is to define specific treatments in the pavement
management system so the analysis can determine the optimal treatment
and timing for each project. This approach requires more detail in setting up
the pavement management system because treatment rules, performance
models, and impact rules must be established for each potential treatment.
Treatment rules define the feasibility of a treatment, performance models
approximate the rate of deterioration after a treatment has been applied,
and impact rules define the changes that take place after a treatment has
been applied (for example, any change in surface type or a change in
pavement condition).

Two approaches used to select appropriate maintenance and
rehabilitation trees—decision trees and decision matrices—are discussed
here in more detail.
