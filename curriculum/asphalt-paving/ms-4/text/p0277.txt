4.10.2 Dense Graded Mixes

A dense graded mix is a mixture where the aggregate particles have an
even distribution from coarse to fine. The goal is that the mix chosen will
be stable and durable. To achieve these, it is generally necessary to intro-
duce enough asphalt into the mix to insure durability but not so much as
to reduce the stability of the mix below that needed for the traffic levels
on the pavement. To insure that the chosen mix performs properly, it also
must be built as it is designed and proper quality control procedures must
be implemented. A special concern is the need for obtaining proper com-
paction on the roadway.

Dense graded mixes are defined by their nominal maximum aggre-
gate size (defined earlier in this chapter and in Chapter 3, “Mineral
Aggregates and Mixture Additives”) and can be further defined as either
fine-graded or coarse-graded. A fine-graded mix will have a higher pro-
portion of fine sand-sized particles than a coarse-graded mix. Table
4.13 presents the criteria for fine/coarse graded HMA mixes. Coarse
mixes are defined as having a combined aggregate gradation that
passes below the primary control sieve control point, and fine mixes
are defined as having a gradation that passes above or through the pri-
mary control sieve control point.

In general, the fine gradation mixes with the smaller nominal maximum
aggregate sizes will be used for the surface mixes (top 1% to 2 inches).
