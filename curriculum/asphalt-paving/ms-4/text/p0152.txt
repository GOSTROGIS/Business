mechanisms occurring in the rock. Compression-based equipment, such
as jaw, cone, gyratory, and roll crushers, essentially squeeze or compress
rock between their crushing surfaces and fracture it. In impact crush-
ers, rock particles are impacted against other rock particles and against
surfaces inside the crusher at high speeds. The rock particles shatter in
impact crushers.

The amount of reduction in particle size, the shape of the aggregate par-
ticles, and the amount of fines or minus No. 4 (4.75 millimeters) material
vary with the different sizes and kinds of crushers, as well as with the rock
type and rate at which the rock is fed into the crusher. In order to yield the
most appropriate end products, multiple crushing stages with optimized
crushing equipment for each particular stage coupled with appropriate
screening sequences are common.

Screening

The screening operation is the part of the processing procedure in
which aggregates are separated according to size. Material may be passed
through single-, double-, or even triple-screening applications in order to
make the various needed sizes of aggregate. Before or during screening
applications, material can be routed to crushing operations. Additionally,
material can be conveyed to washing operations before, during, or after
screening applications.

Several types of equipment are used for screening, but the most com-
mon is the vibrating inclined screen. This equipment allows the slope,
vibration speed, stroke, and direction of rotation of the screen surface
to be adjusted as needed. Typically, units with multiple-deck screens are
used in production facilities. Screen surfaces are available in various types
and materials and with a variety of sizes and shapes of screen openings.

Washing

Aggregates may need to be washed during production to remove dust
generated during crushing. Depending on the nature of sand and gravel
deposits, washing may also be needed to remove silts, clays, and other
deleterious substances such as coal and soft or lightweight fragments.

The various types of washing equipment include high-pressure spray
nozzles, screw washers, log washers, and rotary scrubbers. Sprayers
mounted above the screens might adequately rinse some unwanted mate-
rials, such as dust, from aggregates. Other materials, such as more highly
plastic clays and cemented aggregates, may need the rigorous agitation of
a log washer.

Handling and Stockpiling

The handling and stockpiling of aggregates is an integral component of
aggregate processing for producing a quality product. The methods used
for the pickup or loading of material by shovels and loader buckets should
avoid segregation. Feed bins, chutes, gates, and conveyors should be sized
and operated in such a way that a proper and consistent rate of feed is
maintained to crushers, screens, and washers.
