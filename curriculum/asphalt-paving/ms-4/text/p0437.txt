8.5.4 Notched-Wedge Joints

The notched-wedge joint was developed to help prevent premature
failures of longitudinal joints. The notched wedge (see Figure 8.27) can
improve the joint structure over the vertical edge joint by increasing the
bond surface area between the two mats. The initial pass is laid with a
1-foot (300-millimeter) taper with a built-in 4- to 1-inch (19-25 millimeter)
notch or vertical face at the end of the taper. The notch prevents the frac-
turing of aggregate when the joint is rolled by preventing the wedge from
being tapered down to zero.

Another plus for the notched wedge is traffic safety after the first pass
is laid. The notched wedge provides a ramp for traffic to get safely from
one side of the joint to the other. Because of this safety feature, paving
can continue the entire day on one side of the pavement and move back
the next day to pull the lanes even, instead of moving back at mid-day.
The screed extension that lays the notched wedge is available from several
manufacturers whose operational procedures must be followed to pro-
duce a sound, durable longitudinal joint.
