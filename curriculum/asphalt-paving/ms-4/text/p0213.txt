The volume of asphalt binder absorbed by an aggregate is always less
than the volume of water absorbed. Consequently, the value for the effective
specific gravity of an aggregate should be between its bulk and apparent
specific gravities. When the effective specific gravity falls outside these lim-
its, its value must be assumed to be incorrect. The maximum specific gravity
of the total mix, the aggregate gradation and total asphalt content should
then be checked for possible errors. The G,, is needed to calculate asphalt
absorption and to back-calculate the G, at other asphalt percentages.

In the example, the three specific gravities are as follows:

Bulk Specific Gravity (G,) 2.651
Effective Specific Gravity (G_) 2.726
Apparent Specific Gravity (G_) 2.806

Bulk Specific Gravity of the HMA Mixture

The bulk specific gravity of a compacted sample is the mass of aggre-
gate and binder divided by the bulk volume (i.e., volume of aggregate,
binder, and air). Two methods are used to determine the bulk specific
gravity of a mix. The first is the AASHTO T 166 (ASTM D 2726) procedure
and the second is the CoreLok® procedure.
