makes very high production rates possible. The self-propelled spreader is
connected to the aggregate truck by means of a self-locking hook, which
locks on to the truck as the truck backs into the rear of the machine.
The spreader then pulls the truck along the spread area. A receiving
hopper is located at the rear of the machine. The material is moved
from the receiving hopper to the spreading hopper by means of two
individual—usually automatically controlled—conveyor belts.

By adjusting the speed of each conveyor belt, the flow of aggregate into the
hopper can be set to match the flow from the hopper. Material-level sensors in
the spread hopper, combined with belt speed control, assure the operator of a
consistently full-spread hopper without a second person on the machine.

With individually controlled gates, the operator can utilize as little as
6 inches (150 millimeters) of the width of the spreader to as much as the
complete width of the spreader without leaving the operator’s seat or
having a second person on the machine. This facilitates constructing radii
and intersections and variable roadbed widths. The material, when drop-
ping into the spreading hopper, usually passes through a scalping screen
at the top of the hopper. This allows the scalping of any oversize mate-
rial that may get into the receiving hopper from the truck. Material drops
down into the spreading hopper and flows over a spread roll. The aggre-
gate particles are then deposited onto the asphalt-coated surface.

The calibration and adjustment of all types of aggregate spreaders
should be made according to manufacturers’ instructions and operation
manuals. Some additional suggestions to ensure good results:

e A tachometer used as an aid in maintaining uniform spreader-speed

has proven to be highly successful.

* Distribution rates can be closely monitored by marking the length
that each truckload of aggregate should cover.

* A quick check on the amount of aggregate being applied (rate of appli-
cation) can be made by laying a section of cloth or building paper
that measures 1 square yard (1 square meter) on the pavement—or by
supporting a shallow box measuring 1 square yard (1 square meter)
above the asphalt with nails or screws—and by passing over it with the
spreader. The cloth, paper, or box is then carefully lifted, and the aggre-
gate is weighed. This gives the application rate of the aggregate being
spread.

Rollers

The seating of aggregate particles is an important part of the surface treat-
ment operation. There are several types of compactors, but pneumatic-tire
rollers are normally used.

A self-propelled pneumatic-tire roller (Figure 12.21) with tire pressures in
the range of 60-90 pounds per square inch (415-620 kiloPascals) is recom-
mended for use on asphalt-aggregate surface treatments. The tires force the
aggregate firmly into the asphalt binder and to the flat side without crushing
the particles. Steel-wheel rollers bridge the smaller-size particles and small
depressions in the surface. Steel-wheel rollers also might crush the softer parti-
cles so that degradation takes place even before traffic uses the new surface.
