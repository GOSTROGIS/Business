in the selection of sampling methods for HMA production.

10.4.2 Selection of Testing Methods

In the past, control of HMA production was almost exclusively driven
by asphalt content and gradation. As production capabilities become
more refined, and as the technical understanding of the factors affecting
longevity of asphalt pavements progresses, there is an increased emphasis
on controlling air voids and other volumetric properties of the mix. Many
agencies have now added mixture volumetric tests to their requirements for
both quality-control and acceptance for production of HMA.

Based on NCHRP Synthesis report 346 “State Construction Quality Assur-
ance Programs,” most state highway agencies in the U.S. select the sampling
and testing methods that are used for quality-control on their projects. These
testing requirements are typically identified in the project specifications.
Many agencies include quality-control line items in bids to reimburse con-
tractors for costs associated with providing the tests. The synthesis report
identified the following parameters that are normally required for quality-
control testing programs.

° Asphalt content

° Aggregate gradation

° Compaction

* Volumetric properties (air voids, VMA, VFA)

° Ride quality

° Aggregate characteristics (CAA, FAA, F&E, SE, etc.)

° Pavement thickness

Many of the test values will be compared with the JME When that is the
case, the same tests should be used for quality-control as were used in devel-
opment of the job-mix formula. It would not be advisable, for example, to
use Marshall density values to evaluate air voids on mixes that were designed
using the SGC. Portable SGC equipment is available and being utilized for
quality-control and acceptance on many projects. For more information on
the Superpave system or the SGC, refer to the Asphalt Institute’s manual
SP-2 “Superpave Mix Design” and Chapter 4, Hot Mix Asphalt (HMA) Design.
