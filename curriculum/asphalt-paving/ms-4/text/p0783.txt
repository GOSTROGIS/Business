surface depends on four factors: the interconnected air voids or perme-
ability of the surface, the thickness of the OGFC layer, the gradation (the
maximum size of the aggregate) of the OGFC, and the quantity of the
binder used in the OGFC mixture.

The optimum surface for noise reduction in an OFGC is a mix with more
than 15 percent air voids. The increase in air voids results in improved
slash-spray and hydroplaning reduction characteristics of the pavement
surface. The mix gradation will affect the noise level of the OGFC. As
the gradation gets coarser, the noise level increases at low frequencies.
Research has shown that the use of a coarser aggregate for an OGFC will
increase the megatexture of the pavement and thus increase the noise
level at the lower frequencies. The asphalt binder generally will have little
effect on the noise level of an OGFC pavement. But studies conducted
by the Arizona Department of Transportation have shown that the use
of a crumb-rubber modified-asphalt binder can reduce the noise level an
additional 1-2 dB(A).
