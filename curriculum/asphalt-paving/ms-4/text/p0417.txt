Screed Heating Systems

At the start of a paving shift, the screed is much colder than the HMA being
laid. To keep the asphalt material from sticking to the colder metal of the
screed, it must be preheated. Two types of heating systems are used to pre-
heat the screed before each day’s paving: diesel and electrical. Diesel burners
are simple and easy to use, but they require proper operational procedures so
that excess fumes are not produced when air and fuel in the burner chamber
are out of balance. Electrical heating systems have heating elements immedi-
ately above the screed plate that are surrounded by special heat transfer oil
that helps heat the screed uniformly without hotspots. This system minimizes
fumes and provides a more even heat across the entire screed plate. Electrical
heating systems operate in a couple of different modes. In one, the screed
is heated to operational temperature and the heating system shuts off. The
other heats the screed to the desired temperature and cycles on and off to
maintain this temperature throughout the paving operation.

Free-Floating Screed

Free-floating screeds (see Figure 8.16) have been a standard feature of
HMA pavers since they were first introduced in the 1930s. The basic prin-
ciple of the free-floating screed is that, when it is pulled into the material
deposited in front of it by the augers, it will automatically ride up or down
seeking the level where the path of its flat screed plate is parallel to the
direction of pull. At this point, the forces (listed below), which act upon
the screed, will be in balance and the screed will continue to lay a uniform
mat thickness until one or more of the forces change.
