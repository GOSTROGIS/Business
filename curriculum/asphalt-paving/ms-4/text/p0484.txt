The JMF that results from the mix design becomes the “recipe” for
producing that mixture in the hot plant. Specifications often dictate how
closely the HMA supplier must adhere to this recipe.

The specifications may require, for example, that the binder content of
the material produced shall not vary by more than 0.4 percent from the
binder content in the JMF (established in the approved mix design). The
concept is that the mix design provides evidence that a mixture made
with given aggregates, at a certain binder content and gradation, and com-
pacted with a specific amount of energy will comply with specific quality
indicator tests. These indicators can include parameters such as aggregate
absorption, tensile strength ratio (TSR), volumetric properties, and strength
parameters such as Marshall stability. The premise is that if the mixture is
controlled to be reasonably compliant with the fundamental mix-design
properties (that is, gradation, binder content, and air voids), then the mix-
ture produced will essentially comply with the quality properties (TSR,
Stability, and VMA) established in the mix design. This assumption allows
fewer tests for quality-control and acceptance than were required in the
mix design. The assumption also requires a revised mix design if any of
the quality-control or acceptance parameters change significantly.

More information on mix designs and their procedures can be found in
Chapter 4, Hot Mix Asphalt (HMA) Design or in Asphalt Institute’s publica-
tions MS-2, Mix Design Methods for HMA and Other Hot-Mix Types or SP-2,
Superpave Mix Design.
