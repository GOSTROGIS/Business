10.5 Plant Production

Quality-Control Data Analysis

Quality control of plant production involves producing, analyzing, and
reporting a tremendous amount of data. The organization of this data
is generally established in the quality-control plan. There are three key
areas for which the data must be properly analyzed, documented, and
understood. The first of these is in verifying the job-mix formula. Because
the formula is developed in the laboratory and made from samples taken
weeks before production begins, it is always prudent to verify that the
job-mix formula will produce a quality mix and that the parameters estab-
lished in the mix design can be achieved. The second key area is organiz-
ing and documenting the daily quality-control data. This is necessary so
that the data is meaningful to the users and readily available if problems
occur or if adjustments need to be made. The third key area is in adjust-
ing the production process. A good understanding of the test results and
reasons for the data are the foundation of the good judgment necessary to
make appropriate adjustments. More detailed descriptions of these three
key areas of quality-control data analysis are presented below.

10.5.1 Job-Mix Formula Verification

Field verification of the HMA mix design is the first step in implement-
ing the overall quality-control plan. It involves testing and analyzing the
field-produced mixture to verify that the criteria established by the specifi-
cations are being met. Verification is necessary at the beginning of produc-
tion of each mixture or job mix formula to determine what differences exist
between design and production and if adjustments need to be made.

HMA mixture designs are developed in the laboratory, usually with
aggregates sampled from the project and before production begins. Test-
ing and analysis of the anticipated performance of that mix is generally
accomplished as part of the design.

It has been documented in many cases that even though a mix is designed
for a certain percent of air voids in the laboratory, a plant-mixed sample
containing the proper asphalt content, aggregate gradation, and compaction
by the same laboratory method may show different volumetric properties.
In their study of field testing and field laboratory test results titled FHWA
Demonstration Project 74, “Field Management of Asphalt Mixes,” FHWA
found substantial differences between the laboratory mix design and the
volumetric properties of the field-produced mixture. Based on the study,
these changes tend to result, on average, in slightly lower air void and VMA
values than the targets established by the laboratory-prepared JMFs.
