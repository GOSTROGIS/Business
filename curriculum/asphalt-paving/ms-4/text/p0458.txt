9.2.5 Cool Weather Paving

Placing and compacting HMA is difficult in cool weather and poses
more challenges than just the reduced time available for compaction. It
is particularly important to ensure that the equipment is properly main-
tained and that the sequence of delivery, placement, and rolling is well
coordinated. Special attention should be given to pneumatic-tire rollers to
keep the tires warm to avoid pickup, which will be discussed later.

Also in cool weather, the temperature differential between the air and
the mix is substantial, causing the surface to cool much more rapidly than
the internal portion of the mix. This differential cooling may cause a thin
crust to form at the surface. This crust might develop microcracks (called
checking) that later may cause the pavement surface to ravel prematurely.
Cold surfaces may also cause immediate solidification of the mixture that
is in contact with the surface, resulting in “honeycombing” in thick mats
and possibly poor adhesion or slippage in thin mats.
