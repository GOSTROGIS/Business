7.2.6 Dust Collection Equipment

The gas velocity in an aggregate dryer is quite significant and will
remove fine aggregate fractions from the dried aggregate mixture. Dust
collectors are installed on hot mix plants primarily to collect the dust for
environmental reasons, but captured dust is often reused, depending on
the type of equipment installed on the plant.

There are two stages of dust collection on plant systems—primary
collectors and secondary collectors. Primary collectors gather the large
dust/fine aggregate particles. Secondary collectors gather the smaller
particles.

Most primary collectors are either the knockout-box type or the cyclone
type. Figures 7.10 and 7.11 show these types of collectors installed on
plant systems. The fines collected in primary collectors are returned 100
percent to the plant process, without any contemplation of partial return.
Most people consider this dust to be sand-size particles, but in reality they
also have a fairly high percentage of material passing the 200-mesh or
75-micron sieve. The same style of primary collectors are found on both
batch and drum-mix facilities.

Secondary dust collectors fall into two categories—wet washers (or wet
scrubbers) and baghouses (or fabric filter collectors). This equipment is
positioned immediately upstream of the exhaust fan and is connected to
the fan with ductwork. Figures 7.11 and 7.12 show these types of systems
installed on hot mix plants.

Wet washers clean the dust from the gas stream by injecting additional
water into the system. This makes the dust particles heavier, allowing
them to fall out of the gas stream. Collected dust is very wet—basically a
wet sludge—and so cannot be returned for use in the mix process. This
effluent is pumped to a settling pond where the dust settles to the bottom.
The particle-free water is then recycled from the surface of the pond and
reused for dust collection.

A baghouse cleans with a dry process, allowing the gas to pass through
a fabric filter supported by a wire cage. Dust is collected on the outside
of the fabric filter while the dryer is running, and cleaning air periodically
passes through the bag from the opposite direction, causing the collected
dust to fall into the hopper of the baghouse. This collected dust can either
be returned to the mix-production process or removed from the system.
