Permeable Surface/Lining

Essentially, the procedures for placing permeable surface lining mixes
are the same as those for impermeable asphalt mixes. However, they are
generally placed in one layer 3-6 inches (7.5-15 centimeters) thick, rather
than in two or more courses.

There is no real effort to obtain density in open-graded drainage layer
courses; the intent is only to firmly seat the aggregate particles. Exces-
sive rolling can be harmful because of aggregate degradation or by the
breaking of the asphalt bond between particles, particularly if the mix
temperature is below 150°F (66°C). For this reason, generally, only a static
steel-wheel roller is used.

Porous Drainage Layer

The same construction for permeable linings is used for porous drain-
age layers, with the expectation that the mixing temperature at the hot mix
facility should not exceed 250°F (121°C). Higher temperatures may cause
the asphalt to drain from the aggregate particles and settle in the bottom of
the truck bed as it is hauled to the paving site. This problem is not usually
encountered with mixes such as mix composition D.

Asphait seals

An asphalt seal may be applied to an asphalt surface for a number of
reasons. It may be designed to make the surface more watertight, or to
protect it from abrasion by waves, water currents, or even by ice. It may
also be used to protect the surface from mud curl or the curling of drying
algae along the waterline, or to reduce the rate of oxidation of the exposed
asphalt surface. These seals are generally of two types.

° Sprayed asphalt sealers/asphalt cement. (It should be noted that
emulsified asphalt or cutback asphalt cannot be used as a seal for
reservoirs that are used for drinking water, to avoid the residual
emulsifying agent or cutter stock from contaminating the water.) The
asphalt should be sprayed over the surface of an asphalt lining at the
rate of 0.25 gallons per square yard (1 liter per square meter). This
will provide a film coating as much as 0.04 inch (1 millimeter) thick.
A continuous film coating will fill and seal any exposed pores and
increase the water-tightness of the asphalt lining. It will also tend to
fill and seal small cracks in the surface that may have been caused by
improper rolling procedures in compacting the lining.

The surface should be clean, dry, and free from loose material.
A sloped surface usually necessitates hand spraying. This should be
done in a back and forth sweeping motion to build up the film and to
keep the asphalt from flowing down the slope.

° Asphalt mastic seals. Asphalt mastics applied to the surfaces of
asphalt linings provide water proofing and extra surface protection
from mechanical abuse. Asphalt mastics permit a heavier coating than
sprayed asphalt applications, and well-designed mixtures can make
the surface resistant to abrasion by waves, or scouring by waterborne
sands.
