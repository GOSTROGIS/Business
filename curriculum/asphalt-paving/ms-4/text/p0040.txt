1910: First drum mixers and dryer mixers are used at mixing facilities.

1919: The Asphalt Association (later renamed the Asphalt Institute) is
founded in New York City to serve the users of petroleum asphalt
through programs of engineering, research, and education.

1920: The Bates Experimental Road is built in Illinois.

1921: The Federal Aid Road Act of 1916 is amended, enabling the build-
ing and improving of roadways to enable progress in the United
States.

1930: The Asphalt Institute is instrumental in the crusade to develop
early pavement material and construction procedures.

1941: The U.S. Defense Act of 1941 authorizes the building of roads
essential to the war effort.

1946: The post-World War II United States begins a new era of road
building. Over the next 10 years, 50,000 miles (80,000 kilometers)
of new roads are constructed. During the same period, enough
automobiles are manufactured to stretch 200,000 miles (22,000
kilometers) bumper to bumper.

1952: The WASHO Road Test is constructed in Idaho to evaluate the
design of flexible pavements.

1956: President Eisenhower signs the historic Federal Aid Highway Act,
authorizing the building of 41,000 miles (66,000 kilometers) of
the U.S. Interstate and Defense Highways System.

1958-1960: The AASHTO Road Test is built in Ottawa, Illinois.

1960: The full-depth pavement concept is conceived.

1962: First International Conference on the Structural Design of Asphalt
Pavements takes place at the University of Michigan in Ann
Arbor.

1979: Record-high usage of asphalt in the United States, exceeding
38 million tons (34 million metric tons), is recorded.

1984: Blue Book, Accelerating the Search for Innovation, Special Report
202, is published.

1986: Brown Book, SHRP Research Plans, is published.

1987: SHRP begins.

1992: TRB SHRP is completed; FHWA begins implementation.

1992: First pilot Superpave projects—two in Wisconsin, one in Indiana,
and one in Maryland—are constructed.

1993: AASHTO creates provisional standards.

1994: Expert task groups form.

1995: Regional Superpave centers are established.

1996: First major Superpave projects are constructed.

1996: Lead States Team is formed.

1999: TRB Superpave Committee is formed.

2002: Lead States Team program ends.

2005: TRB Superpave Committee ends.
