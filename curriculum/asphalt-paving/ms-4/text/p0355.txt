Flights can be configured for different material and local conditions.
Small aggregate particles heat quickly and easily, although they are typi-
cally wetter and require a higher burner position and exhaust fan damper
position to remove the additional water from the rock. Coarse mixes
with large quantities of stone have more mass for each particle and often
require a more aggressive flight pattern for maximum heat transfer into
the stone. The equipment manufacturer can provide advice for adjusting
flight configurations.

Flights also wear as they dig through the aggregate material on the bot-
tom of the dryer. As their leading edges wear down, drying efficiency is
reduced. If moisture is retained in the mix, quality and placement will be
compromised and the long-term serviceable life of the pavement may be
affected.

Moisture retention in the mix can be monitored simply by weighing
produced mix, drying it, and then weighing it again. Any weight loss can
be attributed to moisture in the aggregate material. Reducing the feed rate
will usually solve the problem.

Elevated exit gas temperature is another indication that the flights are
either worn or that the configuration is wrong for local conditions. Flights
can easily be replaced if they are worn. The pattern of the flight can also
be changed for more drying efficiency.

Another indication that the dryer is functioning inefficiently for project
conditions is a significant drop in material temperature after production. If
the aggregate in a batch plant—or the mix in a drum-mixer plant—loses
temperature significantly between the time it leaves the dryer and is loaded
into the truck during normal production Gmore than 25°-30°F/14°-17°C),
the aggregate is either not completely dry or not heated all the way through.
Mixes with a high percentage of large aggregate often require elevated
dryer exit temperatures to compensate for the longer time it takes for the
colder center of the stone to heat-stabilize, but a temperature drop might
also indicate that the stone is not completely dry. When significant tem-
perature drops are encountered, moisture retention tests are needed. If no
moisture is present, the uncoated stone can be overheated to compensate
for the eventual temperature drop.
