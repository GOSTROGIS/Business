3.3.1 Introduction

Reclaimed Asphalt Pavement (RAP) is removed and/or reprocessed
pavement that contains asphalt and aggregates. RAP has become a very
valuable commodity in the paving industry. Properly processed RAP yields
high-quality, graded aggregates and small-aggregate agglomerations coated
by asphalt cement (sometimes called black sand). Black sand contains a
high percentage of (old) binder. Black sand alone is not considered RAP.
Its addition to the new mix in high percentages will result in a dry and
noncompactable mix. When RAP is included as a component in new HMA
mixtures, the needed amount of new or virgin materials is reduced. Not
only does this save money and natural resources, but it also eliminates
a waste product that otherwise would require disposal. RAP has been
used extensively in new HMA pavements for several decades and has
consistently exhibited equal performance to mixtures made with all new
material.

3.3.2 Reclaiming Existing Pavements

Processing RAP

The processing of RAP entails the removal of the existing asphalt pave-
ment material and the crushing and screening of the material to reduce it
to an appropriate size. Removal is generally accomplished by milling or
full-depth conventional ripping.

Milling is achieved using a rotary-drum, cold-milling machine. The mill-
ing or cutting drum on a milling machine contains a patterned layout of
carbide teeth. As the milling machine passes over the pavement, the teeth
on the rotating drum grind into the pavement to a controlled depth. Typi-
cal mill depths have been up to 2 inches (50 millimeters) for a single pass,
but equipment is now available to mill 12 inches (00 millimeters) or
more. The milling or grinding action of the rotating teeth crushes the RAP
as the material is removed such that almost all the particles are smaller
than 2 inches (50 millimeters). Many times, the milled RAP is suitable for
use in new HMA without further crushing or screening. Typical milling
equipment (see Figure 3.12) is configured to pick up the milled material
and place it on an integral conveyor belt where the material can be loaded
directly into trucks for hauling to a production facility or stockpile site.
