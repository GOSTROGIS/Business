The effective asphalt binder content of the mix also affects workability. As
the asphalt content increases, the film thickness of the asphalt binder on the
aggregate particles increases. At compaction temperatures, this increased
film thickness increases the lubricating effect of the asphalt binder and up
to a certain point makes compaction easier.

Mix Types

Three main types of mixes are used in HMA construction: dense-graded,
open-graded, and SMA. These different mix types are discussed in Chap-
ter 4, “Hot Mix Asphalt Design.” Each has specific characteristics that will
affect its ability to be compacted. In general, the more compactive effort
required in the laboratory for the mix to achieve target air-voids require-
ments, the tougher it will be to compact in the field. Open-graded mixtures
generally do not require a great amount of compactive effort in the field.

Mix Temperature

The temperature at which an HMA mixture is produced affects both the
ease of compaction and the time available for compaction. The time available
for compaction increases with mix temperature, but there are limits as to how
high the production temperature should be to avoid damaging the asphalt
binder. Upper limits for mix temperature are discussed in Chapter 2, “Petro-
leum Asphalts,” and more specifically in Asphalt Institute’s (EC-101), Best
Management Practices to Minimize Emissions During HMA Construction,
which covers best practices for storage and handling of asphalt binders. The
upper temperature limits increase with higher PG grades and are generally
higher for modified binders. The best temperature to begin rolling is the max-
imum temperature at which the mix will support the roller without moving
horizontally.

At the time of placement, the mix temperature is essentially uniform
throughout the thickness of the lift. However, the top and bottom surfaces
cool more rapidly than the interior portion of the mat. Extreme tempera-
ture differences can lead to “heat checking” or other surface deficiencies,
or to honeycombing near the bottom of a thick lift placed during cold
weather. Careful surface inspection during compaction and observation of
the condition of core specimens removed from the pavement is necessary
to judge whether these problems exist.

Environmental Effects

The rate at which the mix cools affects the length of time during which
density can and must be achieved. The following conditions all affect the rate
of cooling of the HMA: ambient (air) temperature, the temperature of the sur-
face upon which the mix is being placed, the wind speed, and the time of day.
Cool air temperatures, strong winds, and cool surfaces all shorten the time for
compaction. This may make compaction more difficult. Paving during the day
will usually allow for a longer paving time because of the sun’s effect on the
air temperature and on the underlying surface temperature. Conversely, night
paving tends to allow less time for compaction.
