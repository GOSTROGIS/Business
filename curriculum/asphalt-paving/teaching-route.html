Connect the books in the outline's sequence

The six authored Line A units provide the main classroom route. The opening case introduces the work. Manual and specification lessons provide document-reading practice at the relevant point in that route. Choose the amount of practice from observed need; do not add every published session time together and call it an approved technical timetable.

The equipment pictures introduce vocabulary later needed in I2 (paver), J1 (screed) and L1 (compaction equipment). These are narrow preparation links. They do not turn those later competencies into complete technical units or practical assessments. J2 concerns screed safety; it is not the component-identification competency.

A repeatable picture-reading lesson

  1. Name the trade word. Say or display the exact term. Offer a short explanation beside it, rather than replacing it with a different technical meaning.
  2. Locate it. Follow the numbered callout, legend, row heading or clause. Identify the document, model, edition and page as applicable.
  3. Explain its purpose. Model one example: “The callout points to the hopper. In this machine description, it receives mixture.” Keep identification separate from instructions for operating it.
  4. Try another. The learner finds another labelled part or table cell and explains the evidence. Offer a magnified crop, printed page, read-aloud or pointing support.
  5. Check with another person. The listener restates the term and meaning. Resolve uncertainty by returning to the picture and text.
  6. Change one condition. Supply a different model, corrected record or different table column. Ask what still applies and what must be checked again. Keep the first and revised answers.

For a cropped image, open the linked full page when a legend or surrounding condition is needed. A figure caption identifies the source; it does not authorize equipment use. Full manufacturer notices remain with the original manual. Original teaching diagrams and cropped publisher figures are identified separately in their notes.

Make local learning relevant

Invite learners to select a public or fictional road-use context: access to services, a community facility, recreation or trade. Provide enough case information for someone without local work experience to participate. Personal experience, language and community knowledge are shared by choice. Nation-specific knowledge, imagery and protocols require direction from the people who hold that knowledge; a textbook illustration does not supply it.

At the end of a lesson, keep the activity ID, version, learner contribution, support used, criterion observed and next practice. Use the existing feedback descriptions: Not observed, Not yet demonstrated, Demonstrated with support, or Demonstrated independently. Classroom evidence and workplace authorization remain separate records.

Machine identification and WHMIS classroom route

Before developing B–L technical instruction, use the complete competency/source map, tool register and delivery gap record. The visual tools and rigging guide connects actual manufacturer figures, WorkSafeBC Part 15, measuring instruments and site requirements to nine-skills evidence. Its short document exercises support preparation; the full occupational units and practical assessments still require development and qualified review.

Use the 20-figure CAT parts atlas with its ten tasks and separate answer guide. The 22-publication source library supplies full original page sequences and smaller PDF parts. Use the WHMIS reference, six tasks and answers for product identity, symbol reading and SDS navigation.

The identification tasks do not supply a complete later technical unit or practical assessment. For actual equipment, obtain the model/serial-specific operation manual and complete parts catalogue; coverage and outstanding identity fields are recorded explicitly.
