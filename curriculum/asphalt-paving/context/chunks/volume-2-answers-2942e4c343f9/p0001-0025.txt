Use this volume
This volume provides complete classroom readings, worked examples, named practice tasks and answer
guidance for people, tools and safe work. Follow the learning route in each chapter, study the labelled
figures and explain your reasoning. The separate answer book contains the worked keys and observation
records.

Care for people, place and the next person using the work runs through the cases. Local First Nations, Métis
and Inuit learners and partners bring distinct knowledge and priorities. Participation does not require
sharing personal stories or cultural teachings, and no diagram claims to represent a Nation’s protocol.


    Classroom authoring is complete for the listed competencies. Qualified technical review, actual
    fleet/site matching, a local teaching pilot and observed practical qualification are separate
    requirements. The practical records are ready for an instructor to configure and use; they do not certify
    unobserved work.




Read the picture, then test the idea
    1. Identify the object, process or quantity shown.
    2. Read labels, units and the figure’s stated limits.
    3. Connect the image to the chapter explanation.
    4. Try the exercise and check against evidence.
    5. Explain the result to another person and revise if needed.



Use AI as a checked working tool
Claude and ChatGPT can help explain provided documents and create supported files. Claude Code,
Cowork and Codex can help with permitted multi-file tasks and repeatable workflows when their tools are
available. Give a clear purpose, named sources, output formats, limits and checks. Ask for a findings table
with page/cell evidence; verify it against the source. Reopen DOCX, XLSX, HTML or PDF outputs. For study
audio/video, verify the script, captions, diagrams and actual playback. A generated result is not a technical
approval.

Working safely around haul trucks

Worked example · A missed acknowledgement
Fictional sequence: 08:10 the ground worker calls the truck identifier; 08:11 an unrelated driver replies;
08:12 the assigned driver begins backing. The assigned driver has not acknowledged the direction. A
pedestrian also approaches the facility entrance.

The sequence contains no confirmed instruction between the assigned people. Stop the movement through
the agreed procedure, protect the pedestrian route and re-establish the identified driver/signaller
exchange. The three timestamps show order, not proof of a safe movement. The handover should record
the wrong-driver reply and the access conflict; simply repeating the original call leaves the cause
unresolved.


  Explain and apply the idea

  Annotate the truck/paver interaction diagram with blind-area, pinch-point, swept-path and overhead-
  hazard questions. Describe the location a ground worker must avoid and what must be confirmed
  before choosing a signalling position.

  Answer guidance.

  Identify the truck/paver gap, reversing/swept path and overhead envelope; choose a position only
  through the actual plan with visibility and escape. No drawing dimension establishes clearance.

  Observe: Reading, Problem solving, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution,
  calculation, explanation or saved work.




  Calculate and check the result

  Rewrite the three radio messages in the worked case so the intended driver and acknowledgement
  are unambiguous. Rehearse a lost-contact event with spoken classroom roles only.

  Answer guidance.

  Messages identify the vehicle/person, intended action/location and confirmation. Loss of contact
  invokes the agreed stop response; resume only after shared understanding and access control are
  restored.

  Observe: Writing, Communication, Collaboration through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Safety working around and operating power
brooms

Worked example · The clean strip and the dusty doorway
Fictional observation: a broom produces a visibly clean 80 m strip, but dust travels toward an occupied
doorway and its fitted water system has stopped spraying. The operator proposes finishing the last 20 m
first.

The achieved 80 m does not validate the exposure control. The planned run is 100 m; 20% remains, but
that arithmetic is irrelevant to accepting an uncontrolled hazard. Stop the affected work under the
procedure, protect the doorway and have the dust-control fault assessed. Resume when the task controls
are established, not when the inconvenience feels small.


  Explain and apply the idea

  Identify brush, supports, operator position and any dust-control components visible in the RB50
  manual image. Separate visible features from optional equipment that must be checked.

  Answer guidance.

  Use the original labels. Do not infer an option from another model or a brochure. Explain brush
  rotation, movement envelope and the purpose of dust suppression.

  Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  Calculate and check the result

  Check the worked-case percentage and write a defect/exposure report that distinguishes work
  completed from safe conditions.

  Answer guidance.

  80 + 20 = 100 m; 20/100 × 100 = 20% remains. Record water-system failure, dust direction, affected
  people and interim response; no completion percentage overrides controls.

  Observe: Numeracy, Writing, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Safety working around and operating air
hammers

Worked example · Count exposure honestly
Fictional log: 09:00–09:20 setup; 09:20–09:35 tool in use; 09:35–09:45 pause; 09:45–10:05 tool in use.
The worker reports “65 minutes of hammer operation.”

Elapsed time is 65 minutes, while recorded tool-use intervals total 15 + 20 = 35 minutes. The remaining 30
minutes are setup/pause. Keep both values because different exposures may continue during non-trigger
time. The log does not calculate a permissible vibration dose; actual assessment needs the relevant
exposure data and method.


  Label the supply-to-tool diagram, including hose, coupling, retainer and tool

  Label the supply-to-tool diagram, including hose, coupling, retainer and tool. Explain where stored
  energy remains after a trigger is released.

  Answer guidance.

  Compressed air remains in the system until the specified isolation/depressurization procedure is
  completed. Releasing a control is not proof of zero pressure.

  Observe: Reading, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  Calculate and check the result

  Recalculate the worked log and propose two columns that prevent elapsed-time and trigger-time
  confusion.

  Answer guidance.

  65 minutes elapsed; 35 minutes recorded operation. Keep start/end, action and duration, with
  exposure assessment recorded separately.

  Observe: Numeracy, Writing through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or
  saved work.

Safety working around and operating
propane tanks

Worked example · The assembly register
Fictional teaching sample: the cylinder and torch are identified, but the hose rating is unreadable and the
regulator model cannot be matched to the appliance. A learner proposes connecting them because the
threads fit.

The assembly has two unresolved compatibility/condition questions. Matching threads does not establish
pressure suitability or the correct sealing design. Keep the assembly out of operation; obtain the
appropriate component identification and manufacturer instructions, then have the supervised setup and
leak test completed. Record the unknown components rather than replacing unknown values with guesses.


  Trace the labelled propane assembly and state the function of each part

  Trace the labelled propane assembly and state the function of each part. Identify which parts must be
  matched to the actual appliance.

  Answer guidance.

  Cylinder/valve store and control the supply; connector joins the approved assembly; regulator where
  required controls pressure; hose conveys fuel; appliance uses it. Ratings, connections and
  configurations require the actual instructions.

  Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  Write a connection-and-leak-check preparation record for the worked case

  Write a connection-and-leak-check preparation record for the worked case. State the rejection
  conditions and the records required before use.

  Answer guidance.

  Unreadable hose identification or unverified regulator compatibility prevents acceptance. Use
  manufacturer-approved testing, never flame; stop/isolate/report a leak under the procedure.

  Observe: Writing, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.

Weather effects and precautions

Worked example · A break plan that cannot be used
Fictional schedule: a crew is assigned continuous handwork from 10:00 to 12:00. The written heat plan
requires planned cool-rest opportunities, but the relief person has been reassigned. A new worker begins
making confused replies.

The plan is not being implemented, and confusion is an emergency warning sign in this context. Stop the
affected work, summon emergency/first-aid help and follow the cooling response. Arrange coverage and
reassess the work before resuming. The two-hour scheduled block does not establish a safe exposure
interval; it reveals a mismatch between the plan and staffing.


  Explain and apply the idea

  List five factors beyond air temperature that influence heat risk, then match each to information the
  supervisor must consider.

  Answer guidance.

  Workload, radiant heat, humidity/air movement, clothing/PPE, acclimatization and individual factors
  are valid. Explain the relationship rather than treating a list as an exposure assessment.

  Observe: Reading, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  Mark the time span in the worked case and identify why the plan failed

  Mark the time span in the worked case and identify why the plan failed. Write the first emergency
  communication with location and observed condition.

  Answer guidance.

  10:00–12:00 is two hours; no safe limit is inferred. Relief removal prevents planned control. Message
  reports actual location, confusion/condition, people affected and known hazards, and summons help.

  Observe: Numeracy, Writing, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Safety and traffic control procedures

Worked example · The plan revision matters
Fictional documents: T-02 revision A places the facility entrance outside the work area. Revision B extends
the work beyond that entrance. The crew has B, but a traffic briefing still uses A.

The drawings describe different access conditions. Before work under the changed arrangement, resolve
the mismatch through the responsible traffic-control planner/supervisor and brief all affected roles. Record
the current approved revision and the entrance provisions. An extra cone added by an unassigned person
does not resolve the plan conflict.


  Explain and apply the idea

  Compare TCP and traffic-assistant training requirements using retained Part 18 sections 18.6.2–
  18.6.3. Identify what qualification this chapter does not issue.

  Answer guidance.

  TCP training includes the specified program and knowledge/practical assessment; traffic assistants
  have workplace training addressing their defined tasks. Neither role is conferred by this reading.

  Observe: Reading, Writing through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved
  work.




  Calculate and check the result

  Annotate the work-zone concept with questions about warning, transition, work area, public route and
  return to normal traffic. Explain why no spacing can be taken from it.

  Answer guidance.

  Concept identifies functions, not actual distances or approval. Obtain the road-authority plan/risk
  assessment and current requirements; address visibility, queues and vulnerable road users.

  Observe: Problem solving, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Plant site and gravel pit safety

Worked example · One stopped belt is not the whole system
Fictional process: feeder → crusher → conveyor → screen → stockpile. The conveyor stops and material
begins accumulating upstream. A visitor proposes stepping over it while an operator investigates.

A stopped component does not establish isolation or a safe crossing. Accumulation adds a stored-material
hazard and connected equipment may restart. Keep the visitor on the authorized route, report the condition
and use the site’s controlled shutdown/isolation response. The process map helps identify connected
sources; it does not replace the isolation plan.


  Explain and apply the idea

  Trace the process diagram and identify four different hazard mechanisms, including one that remains
  when motion stops.

  Answer guidance.

  Examples: crusher impact/crush, belt nip/entanglement, noise/dust, mobile plant and stored-material
  collapse. Stopped motion does not remove gravity, pressure or automatic restart.

  Observe: Reading, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  Write a visitor brief for the worked site using only authorized routes and role boundaries

  Write a visitor brief for the worked site using only authorized routes and role boundaries. Include the
  response to an unfamiliar alarm.

  Answer guidance.

  Brief identifies induction/contact, PPE/noise arrangement, routes, restricted areas and alarm
  instructions from the site. Learner seeks the actual emergency instruction rather than inventing an
  alarm meaning.

  Observe: Writing, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.

Light vehicle transporting procedures

Worked example · The trailer rating check
Fictional ratings-only exercise: trailer permitted gross mass 3,500 kg; measured empty trailer 900 kg;
proposed machine 2,300 kg; tools/ramps 420 kg. Ignore no other real requirement; this is one arithmetic

Worked example · Rest interval versus sleep opportunity
Fictional shift finishes at 20:00; the next begins at 06:00. Total travel uses 2 hours, meals/personal
preparation 1.5 hours and a required call 0.5 hour. Calculate the maximum remaining interval before other
interruptions.

There are 10 hours between shifts. Subtracting 2 + 1.5 + 0.5 leaves at most 6 hours available. That is an
opportunity estimate, not proof of six hours of sleep or a universal fitness threshold. The schedule needs
review through the workplace fatigue process, especially before safety-critical work or driving.
