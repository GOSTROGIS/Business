Study chapter · Outline H1



Milling and reclaiming equipment
Milling removes pavement to a controlled profile. Reclaiming processes existing pavement and underlying
material to form a designed layer. Understanding the material route helps a learner recognize the machine’s
working parts and the people who must stay clear of them.


  Milling equipment

  The function of milling equipment

  Major mechanical components

  Potential failures, symptoms and indicators of failure


LEARNING STEP 1




Milling equipment
Identify manufacturer, model, serial number, rotor configuration and fitted options before selecting
information. The CAT cold-planer parts guide illustrates assemblies, while the performance handbook
explains equipment families. Neither replaces the matching operation and maintenance manual (OMM). A
brochure can show a machine’s purpose without describing its safe operating sequence. Record which
document supports identification, which supports operation and which is specific to the training machine.

LEARNING STEP 2




The function of milling equipment
A cold planer uses a cutting rotor to remove a controlled depth, producing a milled profile and material for
collection. A reclaimer mixes a designed depth of existing layers; specified water, binder or stabilizing
agents may be introduced through a controlled system. Foamed asphalt is a particular binder-treatment
process, not simply any hot asphalt mixed with water. Reclaiming, grading, compaction and paving form a
coordinated sequence with design and testing requirements. Reuse can reduce virgin-material demand, but
mixture suitability and performance still need verification.

LEARNING STEP 3




Major mechanical components
Trace power from engine through the drive systems to the rotor and travel system. Identify cutting tools
and holders, rotor drum, cutting chamber, side plates, collecting and discharge conveyors, tracks or wheels,
lifting columns, water/dust-control system, operator station, grade sensors and guards. The cutting tools
fracture the surface; conveyors move the recovered material; grade controls influence the cut relative to a
reference. Call a component by its actual manual name and point to it from a safe position. A guard is part
of the machine’s protection, not an optional cover.

Potential failures, symptoms and indicators of failure
Unusual noise, vibration, smell, leaks, damaged tools, cracks or warning messages are observations
requiring action. State where and when they occur and whether conditions are worsening. Stop and secure
the machine when a hazard or serious fault is suspected, keep others clear and notify the responsible
person. Only authorized personnel diagnose or repair within their competence. Never search for a high-
pressure leak with a hand or reach into a stopped-looking conveyor. Record the defect and authorized
return-to-service decision.

Study chapter · Outline H2



Milling and reclaiming safety procedures
A milling plan must account for the cutting system, material discharge, truck movement, traffic and people
on the ground. Agree on boundaries and communication before the rotor or travel system moves.


  Interpret and follow job plan

  Awareness of machine-specific safety

  Traffic Control requirements

  Proper PPE

  Lockout procedures

  Secure equipment for safety


LEARNING STEP 1




Interpret and follow job plan
Read the path, pattern, depth, pass sequence, utilities, obstacles, discharge arrangement and planned
stopping points. Confirm the current revision and who can authorize a change. Walk the area only when
access is controlled. Resolve utility information and unidentified obstructions before cutting. Brief the plan
with ground crew, truck drivers and traffic-control personnel, using a drawing and read-back.

LEARNING STEP 2




Awareness of machine-specific safety
Recognize rotor and conveyor entanglement, ejected material, silica-containing dust, exhaust, noise, hot
surfaces, pressurized hydraulics, blind areas and raised components. Dust control is an engineered system
requiring inspection and effective operation; a visible spray does not prove adequate exposure control.
Block or support raised equipment using specified devices before access. Guard removal, clearing jams and
service require the applicable isolation procedure.

LEARNING STEP 3




Traffic Control requirements
The traffic-control plan and site assessment determine TCP positions, numbers, signs, devices and
communication. Milling traffic, public traffic and pedestrians can change relative positions as work
advances. Keep a defined route and reassess driveways, intersections and service access. Only trained and
authorized people perform assigned traffic-control roles. Stop if control or communication is lost.

LEARNING STEP 4




                                                                    ACCESS IS4S · Volume 5 · 12 / 51

Proper PPE
Select PPE through the hazard assessment and current requirements, including suitable footwear, head,
eye, hearing and high-visibility protection as applicable. Respiratory protection requires the employer’s
program, correct selection, training and fit testing where required. WorkSafeBC Part 8 assigns workers
specified basic clothing, general-purpose gloves, footwear and safety headgear, while employers provide
other required PPE; agreements may provide more. The employer must ensure required PPE is used and
maintained.

LEARNING STEP 5




Lockout procedures
Identify every hazardous energy source, including engine/fuel, electrical, hydraulic, gravity, rotating parts
and stored pressure. Use the machine-specific written lockout procedure: stop, isolate, secure, dissipate or
restrain stored energy and verify the safe state before work. A key removed or battery switch off does not
alone establish full isolation. Each worker’s protection follows the applicable lockout system.

LEARNING STEP 6




Secure equipment for safety
Park in the designated stable location, lower or support components as instructed, apply parking controls
and required chocks, and secure keys and access. Protect the public from the machine and residual
hazards. Remove or secure detachable items without leaving guards missing. Record defects and
communicate the machine’s status to the next shift.

Study chapter · Outline H3



Milling and reclaiming operator roles and
responsibilities
Milling is team work with distinct control responsibilities. Clear roles prevent two people giving
incompatible instructions and ensure the operator receives reliable information about the cut and
surrounding movement.


  The role of Milling Machine Operator

  The role of ground men

  Hand signals communication


LEARNING STEP 1




The role of Milling Machine Operator
The machine operator controls assigned travel and working functions from the operator station, monitors
warnings and machine response, and maintains awareness of the work area. “Top end” describes a crew
role, not permission to ignore ground observations. The operator must know who gives movement
instructions, what the stop signal means and when to stop for uncertainty. A ground guide cannot remove
the operator’s duty to operate safely.

LEARNING STEP 2




The role of ground men
Ground crew members may check cut depth/profile, manage assigned grade controls, observe the material
route and coordinate truck positioning from approved locations. Duties depend on the machine and work
plan. No person should measure beside moving cutting equipment or stand in a truck’s pinch zone to
improve visibility. Agree who reports depth, who communicates with trucks and who records quality. Rotate
or adjust work when fatigue, noise or visibility makes the assigned role ineffective.

LEARNING STEP 3




Hand signals communication
Before work, rehearse a small agreed set of movement and stop signals, radio wording and
acknowledgement. Use one designated movement signaller at a time, while anyone can give an emergency
stop. If a signal is unclear, conflicting or lost, stop and re-establish communication. Crane/hoisting work
has applicable standardized signal requirements; a crew-created milling signal must not replace them. Use
plain words with machine ID, direction and limits; avoid “over there” or an unexplained horn pattern.

Study chapter · Outline H4



Milling and reclaiming equipment
maintenance
Daily maintenance protects the next operator as well as the machine. Use the actual model’s maintenance
schedule and safe work instructions. Record what was checked, what was serviced and what remains out
of service.


  Service lubrication system (greasing)

  Service air intake system as required (air filters / pre cleaners)

  Drain fuel tank sump, water separator fuel filters if equipped

  Add fluids as required

  Inspect and clean components as required

  Housekeeping tasks


LEARNING STEP 1




Service lubrication system (greasing)
Identify grease points from the machine chart, lubricant specification, interval and required machine
position. Clean fittings before connection and load the grease gun according to its instructions. Use the
specified quantity or completion criterion; generic numbers of gun strokes and visible overflow are not
universal rules. Some bearings and centralized systems have different requirements. Do not reach past
guards or into a hazard to access a fitting. Collect excess lubricant and report damaged fittings or blocked
lines.

LEARNING STEP 2




Service air intake system as required (air filters / pre cleaners)
Check the intake restriction indicator and service the pre-cleaner as instructed. Keep dirt out of the clean
side of the system. Inspect seals and housings; a damaged seal can defeat a clean filter. Clean an element
only where the manufacturer permits it and with its method and limits; otherwise replace it. Reduced-
pressure air is not automatic permission to blow out every filter. Do not run without the required element.

LEARNING STEP 3




Drain fuel tank sump, water separator fuel filters if equipped
Where fitted, service the fuel sump/water separator using the specified safe conditions and collection
method. Identify water, debris or unusual contamination and report it. Keep ignition sources controlled and
avoid skin contact. Dispose of collected fluid through the approved route. Opening a high-pressure fuel line
is not a routine water-drain task.

LEARNING STEP 4

Add fluids as required
Identify each reservoir and correct fluid before adding anything. Check on the specified ground condition,
temperature and engine state. A two-sided dipstick may have different stopped/running scales; read the
marking and manual. Clean the fill area and tools, avoid mixing incompatible fluids and do not overfill.
Unexpected consumption or a repeated low level requires investigation, not endless topping up.

LEARNING STEP 5




Inspect and clean components as required
Inspect radiator and oil-cooler surfaces, plugs, dipsticks, battery condition, hoses and accessible guards.
Cooling surfaces can be damaged by inappropriate pressure or tools. Hot coolant and batteries have their
own hazards; use the safe procedure and authorized access. Record leak location and appearance without
touching a pressurized leak. Electrical and mechanical repairs belong to authorized personnel.

LEARNING STEP 6




Housekeeping tasks
Remove rubbish and loose items from safe access areas. Clean excess grease, steps and handholds, and
keep waste out of drains. Account for tools and restore guards before release. Enter meter reading, date,
