Study chapter · Outline H5



Markers, grades and stakes
A stake is a compact instruction that depends on its location, legend and reference. Read the whole mark
before acting. Protect survey control so the next crew inherits reliable information.


  Abbreviations used on stakes

  Interpret stakes


LEARNING STEP 1




Abbreviations used on stakes
Common abbreviations include BM or B/M—benchmark; C—cut; CB or C/B—catch basin; SMH—sanitary
manhole; EL/Elv—elevation; FG—finished grade; PL—property line; RW—right of way; SAN or S—sanitary;
Mon—monument; G—gas; PVC—polyvinyl chloride. Project legends can differ, so confirm the meaning and
units. A gas mark identifies a potential utility concern; it does not give complete location, depth or
clearance information. An elevation is a height relative to a stated datum, not a depth below the current
surface.

LEARNING STEP 2




Interpret stakes
Read station, side, offset, target/reference mark, cut/fill, grade and slope together. Station 0+125 means
125 m along the defined alignment in this metric example. An offset stake sits away from the point it
describes; preserve both the offset and direction. Property pins and survey monuments are protected
control, not disposable construction stakes. A temporary benchmark may use an assumed datum, while
other control is tied to a geodetic datum; do not combine them without an authorized relationship.

Cross-heads and reference marks can carry a level used to transfer information. Confirm which physical
mark is the reference before measuring. Locate work such as catch basins, manholes and headwalls from
the approved layout. Compare the milling limit and required profile with the marks before setting controls.
If a stake is disturbed, illegible or conflicts with the plan, protect the evidence and request verification. An
apparently small guess can propagate along an entire pass.

Operate milling and reclaiming equipment
Machine operation develops through supervised practice on the selected equipment. The sequence below
explains the decisions and observations required; the instructor uses the matching OMM for control
positions, settings and exact operating steps.


  Plan job

  Position the machine

  Engage teeth

  Move forward

  Make adjustments

  Control depth

  Set Controls


LEARNING STEP 1




Plan job
Review the training or job plan, permitted surface, depth, line, material discharge, weather, utilities and
exclusion areas. Complete pre-start checks and daily maintenance, identify emergency stopping controls,
and agree on communications. The instructor defines the permitted operating envelope and observes the
learner. A changed condition requires a new assessment before continuing.

LEARNING STEP 2




Position the machine
Position the machine on the approved approach with adequate support and clearance. Align the truck or
approved side-discharge arrangement without placing a person between moving equipment. Check the full
conveyor movement envelope, including overhead electrical hazards. Use the designated guide and stop if
visibility or acknowledgement is lost.

LEARNING STEP 3




Engage teeth
Engage and lower the cutting system only through the selected machine’s sequence, with guards and dust
controls effective and everyone clear. Rotor speed, depth and engagement conditions are model- and task-
specific. A rotating drum is not approached for a visual check or manual clearing. Unexpected impact or
vibration requires an appropriate stop and report.

LEARNING STEP 4

Move forward
Move along the verified line with smooth control and continuous observation. Painted lines, string
references or the previous cut may guide alignment when authorized. Keep the cutting and discharge
processes coordinated; do not focus so closely on a line that people, trucks or obstacles disappear from
attention.

LEARNING STEP 5




Make adjustments
Make deliberate, controlled adjustments after identifying the reason. Large repeated changes can create an
uneven profile and obscure the cause. Communicate changes to ground crew and allow the machine’s
response to be evaluated. If the machine behaves unexpectedly, stop for investigation instead of chasing
the error with more controls.

LEARNING STEP 6




Control depth
Set and verify cut depth against the approved reference. Check at safe, authorized locations and record
station, side and measured depth. Existing surface variation, sensor reference changes and machine
movement can influence the result. Average depth alone does not establish an acceptable profile
everywhere.

LEARNING STEP 7




Set Controls
Coordinate travel speed, cut depth, machine attitude and discharge capacity within the OMM and plan.
Monitor engine/load indicators, dust-control performance and truck availability. Finish with the
manufacturer’s shutdown sequence and a recorded handover. Competence includes stopping early when
the conditions exceed the plan, not only producing material.

Study chapter · Outline H7



Follow shut down procedures
Shutdown leaves the machine in a known safe state for people, maintenance and the next shift. A parked
machine can still contain heat, pressure and gravity hazards.


  Shut down procedures


LEARNING STEP 1




Shut down procedures
Choose the designated stable parking location with service access and adequate separation from traffic,
pedestrians, edges and drainage routes. Plan access for a possible failure to restart. Use level ground
where the machine procedure requires it, including accurate fluid checks. Complete the machine’s
stopping, cooling and shutdown sequence; do not invent a universal idle time.

Place the rotor, conveyor and raised components in their specified safe positions. Apply parking controls
and required chocks or blocking. Shutdown and maintenance lockout serve different purposes: if someone
will enter a hazardous area or service the machine, apply the full required isolation and verification. A
parked machine with the key removed is not automatically ready for service.

After the required safe state is established, walk around from safe access areas. Look for leaks, damage,
missing guards, debris and anything that changed during the shift. Do not open a hot pressurized system to
complete a routine check. Record defects, meter reading, remaining materials and unresolved work. Mark a
machine out of service when required and ensure the status reaches the next operator.

Secure keys, controls, access panels and detachable items according to the site plan. Consider public
access after the crew leaves and protect residual hazards. Confirm the final state with the instructor or
responsible person. A completed checklist identifies the actual machine, manual procedure and observer; a
ticked generic form alone is not evidence of a safe shutdown.

Study chapter · Outline H8



Transport milling and reclaiming equipment
Transport is a planned operation involving machine configuration, carrier capacity, route and load
securement. The person driving the carrier and the person moving the machine need a shared plan and
clearly assigned responsibilities.


  Roles and responsibilities

  Prepare machine for transport

  Loading and unloading procedures


LEARNING STEP 1




Roles and responsibilities
The carrier/driver checks legal loading, axle and vehicle ratings, route/permit requirements and cargo
securement within their role. The designated machine operator follows the OMM loading procedure. A
trained ground guide controls the agreed communication from a safe position. Do not assume a job title
automatically authorizes loading. Confirm who checks ramp suitability, machine mass/configuration,
attachments and final securement before movement.

LEARNING STEP 2




Prepare machine for transport
Prepare the machine in its specified transport configuration: conveyor, guards, attachments, locks and
loose items. Remove attachments only with the approved handling method and rated lifting/rigging
equipment where required. Know the actual mass, dimensions and centre-of-gravity implications of fitted
equipment. Use designated tie-down points; a convenient handrail or hydraulic line is not one. Secure
detached items as separate cargo. Retain guards needed for safe loading.

LEARNING STEP 3




Loading and unloading procedures
Inspect the carrier, ramps, approach and ground for capacity, support, alignment and traction. Establish a
controlled area and agreed stop signal. Follow the machine’s direction, speed and configuration
requirements when crossing changes in slope. Stop if a guide is lost or alignment becomes uncertain. After
positioning, apply parking/support requirements and the compliant securement system. National Safety
Code Standard 10 and applicable BC rules govern the arrangement; chain count alone does not prove
adequate restraint. At unloading, control the machine before releasing restraints in the authorized
sequence.
