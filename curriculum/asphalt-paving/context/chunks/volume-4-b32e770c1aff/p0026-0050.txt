Study chapter · Outline G2



Oil content
In asphalt quality work, “oil content” commonly refers to asphalt binder content. It does not mean engine
oil. Binder content affects the mixture, but its reported percentage is meaningless unless the mass basis
and test method are known.


  Define Section 502


LEARNING STEP 1




Define Section 502
Section 502.12 of the supplied 2006 specification evaluates actual asphalt content against the approved
content. Table 502-F expresses deviation in kilograms of binder per 100 kg of dry aggregate. This is a
different basis from binder as a percentage of total mixture. For example, 5 kg binder with 100 kg dry
aggregate is 5.00% on aggregate mass and 5/105 × 100 = 4.76% on total mixture mass. Never subtract
results reported on different bases.

The historical procedure uses the sample mean from the specified sub-lot samples. Preserve sample
identity, test method, lot and lift. Compute actual minus approved content, then use the magnitude of the
deviation where the table applies to either above or below. Follow the contract’s rounding and reporting
rules rather than rounding early to reach a favourable band.

In Table 502-F, a deviation of 0–0.15 earns +$0.50/t, 0.16–0.30 has no adjustment, and 0.31–0.35 gives −
$1/t. Larger deviations have larger deductions and different top/lower-lift rejection boundaries. The full
table and associated clauses govern; these are historical exercise values, not current project permission. A
top-lift deviation greater than 0.50 triggers the stated rejection/remedial provisions. An operator does not
fix a field result by independently changing the approved JMF.

Possible causes of an unexpected result include actual production variation, sampling problems, incorrect
identities, test issues or a mistaken reporting basis. Preserve the original evidence and refer the issue to
the quality lead. A single favourable retest is not automatic authority to discard an unfavourable result.

Study chapter · Outline G3



Application
Application rate describes how much asphalt mixture was placed over a measured area. It is a mass-per-
area quantity. It can help identify a quantity or thickness concern, but does not replace density, level or
compacted-thickness checks.


  Define Section 502


LEARNING STEP 1




Define Section 502
Section 502.14 of the 2006 specification determines application rate from the delivered tonnage assigned
to the lot and its covered area, with the specified treatment of entrance letdowns and intersections.
Reconcile weigh tickets, returned material, location and lift according to the contract. Counting an entire
truck twice or including a ticket from another lane changes the result even when the arithmetic is correct.

Use consistent units: 1 tonne = 1,000 kg. Actual application rate in kg/m² = accepted assigned mass in kg ÷
measured area in m². The ratio used by Table 502-H is actual rate ÷ specified rate × 100. This ratio is a
percentage of the specified rate, not a binder percentage and not percent density.

The dated table distinguishes bottom/single lifts from the top lift of multiple lifts. For the uncomplicated
96.0–103.9% band, both columns show +$0.50/t. Other bands include different deductions, restrictions on
payment for excess material and rejection/remedial provisions. Read the full row and correct lift column.
Do not treat over-application as a desirable way to earn payment: it can change grade, tie-ins and material
demand.

If a calculated rate seems wrong, check area boundaries, units, tickets, lift identity and authorized changes
first. Then compare with thickness and survey evidence. A rate alone cannot prove a uniform mat; a thick
patch and a thin patch can average to the specified rate.

Study chapter · Outline G4



Segregation
Segregation is separation within the mixture that produces an uneven distribution of aggregate sizes or
other mixture characteristics. A visibly coarse patch can be a clue, but a crew needs agreed inspection
criteria and a traceable location before deciding its cause or treatment.


  Define Section 502


LEARNING STEP 1




Define Section 502
Section 502.15 of the 2006 specification calls for a homogeneous mixture and uniform surface texture. Its
visual classification distinguishes slight, moderate and severe segregation using the amount of stone and
remaining binder/fine-aggregate matrix. The project’s agreed comparison areas or photographs support
consistent judgement. Temperature differences can also affect paving behaviour; thermal segregation and
aggregate-size segregation are related quality concerns but are not interchangeable descriptions.

Track where a pattern occurs: at truck changes, the end of loads, edges, behind a particular part of the
screed or at repeated stops. Material handling, stockpiles, truck loading/unloading, hopper management
and the feed system all deserve investigation. Do not claim a single cause from a photograph alone. Record
station, lane, lift, time, load and observed texture, then notify the responsible lead while the crew can still
investigate the process.

The historical top-lift tables 502-I-1 and I-2 address remediation and payment. They include different
remedies by severity and counts per lane kilometre. These dated repair descriptions are an exercise in
reading a contract, not a field instruction to apply slurry or overlay without approval. Current project
specifications and the authorized repair design govern actual work. A payment deduction does not
automatically remove the requirement for remediation.

Prevention is a coordinated task. Maintain the specified material-handling and paving process,
communicate supply changes early, and avoid scattering coarse material onto a finished surface. When a
defect is suspected, preserve evidence and obtain direction before an improvised repair hides the cause.

Study chapter · Outline G5



Density
Percent density compares measured in-place density with a defined reference density. The reference is
part of the result. Saying only “the density is 94%” leaves out information needed to interpret it.


  Define Section 502


LEARNING STEP 1




Define Section 502
Section 502.11 of the supplied 2006 document uses different references for Marshall and Superpave
mixtures. The Marshall expression divides in-place density by the density of the plant briquette. The
Superpave expression divides in-place density by maximum theoretical density determined by the
specified procedure. Use compatible units, the applicable method and representative results from the
correct lot.

A core and a nuclear gauge do not produce evidence through the same procedure. Follow the approved
sampling, testing, calibration/correlation and reporting requirements. Nuclear gauge operation requires the
applicable CNSC licensing, training and controls; reading this chapter does not authorize it. Record location,
lift, sample/gauge identification, method, density units and reference result. Avoid substituting a different
day’s reference because it produces a more favourable percentage.

The dated tables E-1 and E-2 use different acceptance/payment bands. For example, a Marshall lot average
of 97.6–98.0% has a +$0.50/t adjustment, while the Superpave +$0.50/t band is 93.0–93.9%. These
bands cannot be exchanged. Sub-lot limits, rejection provisions and the other acceptance requirements
also apply. A satisfactory lot mean can conceal an unacceptable individual area.

Compaction effort, temperature, mixture behaviour, support and rolling pattern affect the outcome. More
passes are not automatically better: they may be ineffective after cooling or damage an unstable mat. Use
the approved pattern and quality feedback to guide authorized changes.

Study chapter · Outline G6



Smoothness
Smoothness concerns the longitudinal profile experienced by a moving vehicle. It differs from crossfall,
compacted thickness and density. A mat can meet one of those requirements while failing another.


  Define Section 502


LEARNING STEP 1




Define Section 502
Section 502.16 of the dated 2006 specification measures longitudinal profile with a specified precision
rolling instrument and computes the International Roughness Index (IRI). IRI is commonly expressed in
m/km; lower values indicate a smoother profile under the model. It is not a direct measurement of crossfall
or a single bump’s height. Straightedge checks and localized-defect requirements answer different
questions and may apply in addition to IRI.

Identify the lane, direction, station limits, lift and applicable lot/sub-lot rules. The historical clauses treat
auxiliary lanes and short lengths specifically, and use the applicable Table 502-J or J-2 when adopted.
Acceptance limits and payment schedules depend on the road classification and contract. Do not import an
IRI threshold from another job. Preserve the instrument record and the quality lead’s interpretation.

Smoothness begins before the profiler arrives: prepared base, correct setup, steady paving speed and
material head, coordinated truck changes, careful joints and suitable rolling practice all matter. Abrupt
control changes, stops and poorly matched joints can leave profile defects. A roller cannot reliably erase
every paver setup error. Report an emerging pattern early enough for the lead to investigate the process.

The 2006 auxiliary-lane example prorates a qualifying adjustment by segment length relative to a 1 km lot.
This is a contract rule, not a general conversion from IRI to dollars. Repairs and remeasurement require
authorized procedures. Keep the original and subsequent profiles linked so the correction can be evaluated
transparently.
