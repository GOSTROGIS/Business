Study chapter · Outline F1



Drainage, grade and base
A pavement is a layered structure that must carry loads and manage water. A smooth new surface will not
correct an unstable foundation or a blocked drainage route. Reading the whole road helps the crew protect
access to homes, services and community spaces.


  Proper road drainage

  Sub grade

  Sub-base

  Base


LEARNING STEP 1




Proper road drainage
Surface water follows crossfall, longitudinal grade and low points toward an approved collection route.
Subsurface water needs its own drainage path. Inspect ditches, catch basins, culverts and outlets against
the drawings; a visible inlet is not proof of a functioning outlet. Sediment, fuel and uncured materials must
be kept out of drainage systems. Do not redirect water toward neighbouring land or a watercourse because
that route looks convenient. Report ponding, erosion and blocked drainage before covering the evidence.
Approved environmental controls and local access commitments belong in the work plan.

LEARNING STEP 2




Sub grade
Subgrade is the prepared soil foundation beneath the pavement structure. Soil type, moisture, density, frost
susceptibility and soft areas influence performance. Rutting under equipment, pumping water and
inconsistent support require evaluation; adding asphalt is not a substitute for correcting the cause.
Geotextiles can separate materials or provide filtration, depending on the specified product and design.
Geogrids have different functions. Check product identity, overlap, orientation and protection requirements
before placement. Damaged or wrongly installed fabric cannot be assumed effective because it is hidden.

LEARNING STEP 3




Sub-base
Sub-base distributes loading and can provide a working platform or drainage function. Its specified grading,
thickness, moisture and compaction requirements govern placement. Approved recycled material may be
suitable, but availability alone does not establish compliance. Place in controlled lifts, protect against

water can change how the layer behaves. Record the material source and test results so a later concern can

LEARNING STEP 4




Base
Base is the prepared layer directly supporting the asphalt in a typical flexible pavement. Crushed aggregate
from a quarry or pit, approved dredged or river-derived material, and recycled products each require the
project’s source authorization and material acceptance. Their names do not establish equal performance or
permission to extract them. Check surface elevation, crossfall, density, cleanliness and drainage. A thin low
spot in the base changes asphalt demand and may create a local thickness problem. Maintain the accepted
base until paving starts; traffic or rain can alter it after inspection.

Study chapter · Outline F2



Verify slopes, crowns and grades
Grade work turns a drawing into reliable elevations on the ground. Keep the benchmark, units, drawing
revision and sign convention visible in every calculation. Another person should be able to repeat your
result without guessing what a number means.


  Confirm plans/blueprints

  Define role of Surveyor

  Define role of Grade Person

  Typical calculations


LEARNING STEP 1




Confirm plans/blueprints
Read the title block, north arrow, legend, scale, revision and detail references before measuring. A plan is
the overhead view; a profile follows an alignment; a cross-section cuts across it. Profiles often exaggerate
the vertical scale, so the drawn angle is not the road’s actual slope. Use stated dimensions before scaling,
especially on resized screens or prints. One inch is exactly 25.4 mm and one foot exactly 0.3048 m.
Horizontal run is used for ordinary grade calculations; a tape stretched along a slope measures a longer
distance.

LEARNING STEP 2




Define role of Surveyor
The surveyor establishes or verifies control and supplies elevations, alignment and construction
information within their assigned role. Cut and fill at a profile station normally refer to the defined
centreline, not automatically to the entire cross-section. Pipe invert is the inside-bottom elevation. Depth to
invert and cover above the pipe crown are different quantities; calculating cover requires the appropriate
pipe dimensions. Quantity work combines accepted geometry with a justified allowance for settlement or
compaction. A material list includes identity, size, quantity, units and drawing reference, not just an
unexplained total.

LEARNING STEP 3




Define role of Grade Person
The grade person transfers authorized control to usable offset stakes, marks and machine references.
Protect the original control and record offsets so a stake displaced from the construction line is not
mistaken for the line itself. Confirm benchmark identity, datum, instrument setup and backsight. If marks
conflict or a stake moves, stop using it and obtain verification. Do not average conflicting elevations. Read
back instructions with station, side, offset, target elevation and cut/fill direction.

LEARNING STEP 4




Typical calculations
For differential levelling, height of instrument (HI) = known elevation + backsight; point elevation = HI −
foresight. For grade, rise/run × 100 gives percent when both use the same units. A 2% crossfall across a
3.5 m horizontal half-width gives 0.070 m or 70 mm drop. Use a spirit level, builder’s level, laser and
automatic slope control only with their setup, checking and operating instructions. Check calibration and an
independent reference. Instrument displays do not make an incorrect benchmark correct. Record
tolerances from the project rather than inventing a universal pass band.

Study chapter · Outline F3



Final grade preparation
Final grade preparation is the last opportunity to correct the support and geometry before the mat covers
them. Work from accepted levels, drainage and utility details. A neat edge and a clean base help the paving
crew place the intended thickness.


  Check and raise utilities

  Fine grading

  Boning crown/cross-fall

  Limit of asphalt layout


LEARNING STEP 1




Check and raise utilities
Locate utility structures using the project information and required locating process. Confirm casting type,
ownership, target elevation and approved adjustment detail. Raising a frame may require rings, approved
bedding, structural support and curing time; it is not simply lifting the lid. Protect openings, coordinate
access and traffic control, and prevent loose material entering the system. Only authorized workers make
the adjustment. Recheck finished elevation and crossfall around the structure so the paving joint does not
create a pond or abrupt bump.

LEARNING STEP 2




Fine grading
Fine grading removes high spots and fills low areas with the approved material and method. Check
moisture and compaction as required, remove contamination and protect the surface from rutting. Do not
conceal soft spots with a thin scatter of dry aggregate. Confirm that repairs are accepted and that the base
is ready for the specified prime or tack treatment, where applicable. Record the inspection location and
time because construction traffic and weather may change the surface after it was checked.

LEARNING STEP 3




Boning crown/cross-fall
Boning uses aligned sighting references to compare intermediate levels with a defined grade. All rods and
reference heights must be consistent, and the method needs a verified starting and finishing reference.
Check crown and crossfall with appropriate instruments and the drawing’s actual geometry. A crown is not
always centred, and curves, transitions, intersections and accessibility features may have different
requirements. Check machine slope calibration against an independent measure. Agreement between two
displays using the same wrong reference is not independent verification.

Limit of asphalt layout
Mark the finished mat limits from approved alignment, width, edge and tie-in details. Include tapers,
entrances, curb offsets, construction joints and the paving sequence. Distinguish the paver’s nominal
screed width from the specified finished width and any working allowance. Measure at identified stations
and record changes. Discuss public access and emergency routes before changing the work boundary. If
the layout conflicts with a utility or existing surface, request a resolved detail before placing material.

Study chapter · Outline G1



Gradation
Quality control connects the planned pavement with evidence from production and placement. The crew
needs to know which document governs, which tests apply and who can release or reject work. This
chapter uses the supplied BC Ministry of Transportation DB 2006 Section 502 for a dated contract-reading
exercise.


  MOT Specs

  The audit process

  Penalty/bonus

  Limit of asphalt layout


LEARNING STEP 1




MOT Specs
A quality management plan identifies materials, accepted mix design, sampling and testing, production
controls, placement checks, records and responsibilities. Quality control is the producer/contractor’s
process control; quality assurance provides the owner’s confidence and verification through the specified
system. Neither term means that a crew can self-approve a departure. In the 2006 example, a mix design
becomes the job mix formula (JMF) through the required written acceptance process. Identify the actual

LEARNING STEP 2




The audit process
An audit follows evidence: approved procedure, actual activity, dated record, result and corrective action. A
missing record and a failed result are different findings, and both require a response. Trace a sample from
station and lot through laboratory report to disposition. Check instrument identification and calibration
records. Record what was observed without inventing motives. Close a finding only when the responsible
authority accepts the evidence of correction. A tidy binder with untraceable sample numbers does not
demonstrate control.

LEARNING STEP 3




Penalty/bonus
Payment adjustments are contract calculations tied to defined lots, tests and acceptance rules. A bonus for
one property does not cancel rejection under another. The 2006 tables separately address binder content,
gradation, application rate, segregation, density and smoothness. Always state parameter, units, applicable
lift, table and edition. Crowns and crossfalls remain geometric requirements even where a payment table
does not express them. Learners calculate an isolated adjustment first, then check all other acceptance
conditions before discussing overall payment.

LEARNING STEP 4




Limit of asphalt layout
Define the paving limits, lift, station range, lane and measured area before assigning tickets or samples to a
lot. Mark intersections, letdowns and other geometry according to the contract’s measurement method. A
layout change can affect quantity, sample locations and the meaning of a payment calculation. For the
2006 gradation exercise, Table 502-G compares departures from the JMF on specified sieves; it is not a
width table. Keep layout verification and the appropriate quality-table lookup linked through the same lot
identifier.
