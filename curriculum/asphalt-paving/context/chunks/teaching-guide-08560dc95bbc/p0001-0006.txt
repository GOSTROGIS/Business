Teach the concept, observe the skill
The learner book is a complete classroom sequence with six illustrated concepts, worked teaching, twelve
activities and a revised explanation task. Use a selected section where it fits the learner's current trade work, or
deliver the sequence across several supported sessions. Agree time and access arrangements locally; these
activities are not a prescribed allocation of technical-training hours.

Read the diagram title, locate its terms, model one evidence-based explanation, allow an individual attempt, then
use a partner check. Have the source image available when a technical term or interpretation needs checking.
The full 834-page book is available in the MS-4 reader. The source is the seventh edition, published in 2007;
current contracts and machine-specific documentation determine actual applications.

The new SVGs simplify identified source figures. They deliberately omit dimensions, control settings and
operational directions where these are not part of the introductory concept. The picture-reading work supports
understanding before technical instruction; it does not authorize design, adjustment or operation.

Answer guidance
MS4-01 · Load path

Expected sequence: wheel load reaches the pavement surface; the pavement structure spreads and transfers the
load to the supporting subgrade. The subgrade matters because it supports the structure and carries the
transferred load. The diagram does not give required layer thickness, an allowable pressure or a numerical wheel
load. Accept a correctly ordered explanation through an accessible mode. If the learner attributes all support to
the surface alone, return to the lower part of the picture and trace the arrows.

MS4-02 · Model layers

50 + 120 + 180 = 350 mm = 0.350 m. A useful estimate is about 0.35 m; another check is (120 + 180) + 50 =
300 + 50 = 350 mm. The sketch labels surface, base and subbase above prepared subgrade. No subgrade
thickness was supplied. These are invented model dimensions and do not establish design adequacy, material
suitability or an actual project's required structure. Do not award full numeracy evidence for a copied total
without units and a check.

MS4-03 · Production types

Record A describes a batch process; Record B describes a continuous process. A dryer can be part of either type.
Drying/heating and combining aggregate with binder are common functions; separately weighing a batch versus
measuring a continuing feed distinguishes these descriptions. Do not accept “has a drum” as the whole
explanation. A drum/continuous facility can use a separate dryer and continuous mixer, as the cited source
explains.

MS4-04 · Saved process note

Observe the learner create or control the document, label both routes, save to an identifiable location, close and
reopen the saved file. Check the content after reopening. Record the actual tool actions and support. A paper
note or another person's file does not demonstrate this learner's Digital outcome.

MS4-05 · Component functions

 Term                      Expected meaning


 Hopper                    Receives the asphalt mixture.


 Slat conveyor             Carries mixture toward the rear of the paver.


 Augers                    Distribute mixture across the width ahead of the screed.


 Screed                    Places and shapes the mat.


The false statement confuses rearward conveying with lateral distribution. Accept accurate arrows and plain
language. The learner should not infer the location of a particular machine's switch from this functional diagram.

MS4-06 · Check-back and next source

Look for one accurate explanation, listener restatement, clarification and confirmation from each partner. The
visitor needs the identified model's applicable operator manual and responsible instructor before selecting a
control. The general drawing cannot establish the control layout, machine configuration, authorization or task
conditions. Record individual contributions; speed, accent, eye contact and confidence are not the criteria.

MS4-07 · Changed factors

The recorded factors are paving speed/forward motion and head of material. Missing facts can include measured
mat thickness, the time/location of the observation, temperature record, the earlier and later material-head
record, or a reliable description of the reported result. Because more than one factor changed and the record is
incomplete, it cannot isolate one cause. Do not turn this task into a machine-adjustment recommendation.

MS4-08 · Observation form

A sound form separates the reported speed change and material-head change from missing measurements. For
example: Observation: speed changed; source: supplied classroom note; time/version: not supplied, request it;
information needed: verified before/after record. “The mat failed because the speed was wrong” is an
unsupported diagnosis and should be revised. Keep the first and revised versions and the actual partner
feedback. Record Digital only if the learner performed the file actions.

MS4-09 · Passes and coverage

Point P has two passes. The record establishes one complete coverage, because every part of the width has at
least one crossing and some areas do not have a second. Two crossings of P do not establish equal treatment
across the full width. These invented facts interpret vocabulary; they do not specify an operating pattern.

MS4-10 · Incomplete log

An acceptable handover is: “The log records three crossings past the observer. It does not establish how much of
the mat width was covered.” Request the track/coverage record for the full width, including any untreated areas.
Do not silently equate an observer crossing count with the source's point-based pass definition or a complete
coverage count. The learner's partner should recover both the observation and the missing information.

MS4-11 · Develop and test an explanation

Keep two meaningfully different formats, a choice tied to the new learner's question, an actual trial and an
improvement supported by what happened. Both need accurate technical terms and the figure's source credit. A
copied attractive layout without alternatives or a reader trial does not demonstrate the intended creative process.
Each learner records their contribution. Do not require cultural or personal disclosure to make an example
relevant.

MS4-12 · Revise for the new reader

Check the revised explanation has at most 80 words, excluding the source note, and works in black and white
without colour carrying the only meaning. It answers what the term means and what the picture does not
establish. Keep the original, changed brief, revised version and test result. A revision should follow the changed
requirement or observed reader need; a learner need not invent an error if the first version already had a useful
feature. Confirm saved content after reopening when Digital is claimed.

Technical and local review
Before a technical-delivery claim, obtain qualified review of the simplified figures, term explanations, task
boundaries and relationship to the provider's training plan. Record the reviewer, date, findings and revisions;
none is pre-filled as approved. Invite locally directed examples and accessible teaching choices without inventing
Nation-specific knowledge or endorsement. Keep practical assessment separate from this conceptual classroom
evidence.
