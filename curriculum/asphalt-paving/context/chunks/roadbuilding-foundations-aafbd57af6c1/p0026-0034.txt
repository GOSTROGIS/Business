Calculate a quantity you can check
Worked example, P-01 revision B. Convert thickness first: 40 mm ÷ 1,000 = 0.040 m . Plan area is 50
m × 6 m = 300 m² . Geometric layer volume is 300 m² × 0.040 m = 12 m³ . Estimate: a 300 m² layer
one-tenth of a metre thick would occupy 30 m³; a 0.04 m layer is less than half as thick, so 12 m³ is plausible.
Units check as m × m × m = m³ .

The profile rises 100.50 − 100.00 = 0.50 m across 50 m of stated horizontal distance. For this drawing-
reading exercise, rise/run is 0.50 ÷ 50 × 100 = 1% . This centreline grade is not the transverse crown or
an acceptance limit. A real quantity may need a different geometry and an approved measurement method;
our rectangular model has stated simplifications.

Q-01 revision B · Fictional mass model and delivery records. For this exercise only, the instructor supplies a

specification. The planned model mass is 12 m³ × 2.00 t/m³ = 24.00 t . Three classroom records list
8.00 t, 8.00 t and 7.60 t. These are fictional record inputs, not a real delivery or placement measurement.

Turn a promising idea into a testable question
A trend is useful to investigate when it responds to a defined need. A product described as green, new or
innovative still needs evidence. Ask what problem it addresses, how it works, where it has been studied, what
limits apply and who would approve its use. Something new to a local project may have been used elsewhere
for decades. Publication date and location matter.

This unit compares material and design ideas without selecting a live road design. You will prepare a
recommendation for further investigation using complete evidence cards and a fictional project brief. A careful
recommendation can say that one option deserves a closer technical study while leaving design approval to
the appropriate professional.

Compare surface and reuse approaches
Trend card N1 · Paving stones. Modular paving uses individual units rather than one continuous asphalt layer.
Some systems are designed to manage water through joints and underlying layers; the word paver alone does
not establish permeability. Ask about the whole pavement system, loading, drainage, accessibility and
maintenance. A patterned appearance is not environmental evidence.

Trend card N2 · Porous asphalt. FHWA's April 2015 technical brief describes porous asphalt pavements with
stone reservoirs as systems intended to provide a surface and manage stormwater. Water moves through
interconnected surface voids into the underlying reservoir. This differs from an open-graded friction course on
a dense pavement, where water moves through the surface course and out toward the side. The brief
identifies potential clogging and the need for specialized maintenance, as well as site/design limitations. A
material name does not guarantee infiltration at an unsuitable site.

Trend card N3 · Recycling. Recovered asphalt may enter an approved plant or specialist in-place process.
Recovered concrete may be processed as aggregate. Potential reductions in new-material demand need to be
examined alongside processing, transport, suitability, maintenance and end-of-life. “Recycled” is a property
of the material pathway, not a complete environmental score.

LA6-01 · Improve three claims

Rewrite these statements so they are bounded and useful: “All paving stones absorb rain”; “Porous asphalt
never needs drainage planning”; “Any recycled material is automatically the lowest-impact choice.” For each,
identify a mechanism, a condition and one question for a qualified reviewer. Skills: Reading, Problem Solving,
Writing.



Ask what a material changes
Trend card N4 · Epoxy, colour and modified asphalt. Epoxy systems, coloured surfaces and modified binders
are families of products and applications. Colour can serve appearance or wayfinding, but it does not by itself
establish structural performance, skid properties, durability or accessibility. A modified binder may be
selected for a particular performance requirement. The full product description, intended use, test evidence,
compatible construction system and approved specification are needed before adoption. Do not turn a
product family into one universal recipe.

Trend card N5 · Lightweight fill and “floating roads.” Lightweight fill reduces the load imposed by an
embankment compared with a heavier fill arrangement. Expanded polystyrene, or EPS, geofoam is one
engineered lightweight-fill material. Its use beneath a road does not mean the road floats on open water. The
soil, loading, stability, protection, groundwater and installation conditions require geotechnical design. A
brand name or generic word such as foam is not a material specification. Pumice is another material
encountered in lightweight-fill discussions, with properties and availability that must be checked for the
actual source and use.

FHWA's April 2017 Ground Modification Methods, Volume I describes lightweight fills and EPS geofoam. It
explicitly states that the publication is not a standard, specification or regulation. Its chapter explains that
material properties vary and detailed design requires further testing and evaluation. The examples show that
lightweight-fill ideas are not all recent inventions.

LA6-02 · Read the cost boundary

Calculate the difference between EPS-block geofoam and pumice in N6. Name three reasons the table cannot
be used as a current B.C. quotation. Explain why a lightweight-fill cost table does not by itself establish an
approved pavement sub-base material. Explain why price ranking alone cannot establish technical suitability.
Keep units and the word historical in your conclusion. Skills: Numeracy, Reading, Problem Solving.



Separate an enhancement from a requirement
Trend card N7 · Patterned surfaces. Street-print or other patterned treatments can add a visual effect to a
pavement. A visual enhancement may serve a project purpose, but performance claims still need evidence.
Ask about the actual treatment, friction, wear, maintenance, access needs and compatibility with the
underlying system. A decorative pattern is not evidence of structural strengthening or permission to use a
cultural design. Any community-specific image or knowledge requires appropriate permission.

LA6-03 · Make a product enquiry

Choose N4 or N7 and draft five questions for a fictional supplier. Include intended use, product identity,
evidence, maintenance and approval/permission. Make one question specific enough that a vague marketing
answer would be recognizable. Keep the enquiry local; no message is sent. Skills: Writing, Communication,
Creativity and Innovation.



Apply the evidence to a defined case
Fictional project N8 · A learning-centre courtyard. A facility team is considering a 600 m² courtyard used by
pedestrians and occasional authorized service vehicles. The team wants to improve surface-water
management and readable wayfinding. No geotechnical investigation, infiltration test, approved loading
design, maintenance budget or cultural-design permission has been supplied. The project budget is not yet
set. A promoter offers porous asphalt, coloured asphalt or an EPS-supported pavement as three “green”
options. These options address different questions and are not interchangeable solutions.

LA6-04 · Compare options against the actual need

Make a table for the three offers. State the problem each might address, evidence provided in N1–N7, missing
project information and the reviewer needed. Select one or two ideas for further investigation, with reasons.
Do not approve construction or claim a total environmental winner. Each learner contributes an evidence-
based question before the group makes its shortlist. Skills: Problem Solving, Collaboration, Reading.

LA6-05 · Adapt to a new constraint

The fictional facility manager says there is no commitment to specialized pavement-cleaning equipment and
asks for a maintenance plan before further consideration. Revise your shortlist or investigation plan. Explain
why this new fact is relevant to porous asphalt and why it does not automatically approve another option.
Skills: Adaptability, Communication, Problem Solving.

LA6-06 · Build an evidence brief and test its clarity

Create a one-page brief containing the project question, one proposed investigation, a supported benefit, a
limitation, missing information and a next decision. Add source titles/dates from the research record and
mark predictions as predictions. Save and reopen the file if using Digital. Ask a partner to identify what you are
recommending and what you are leaving undecided; revise one unclear sentence. Skills: Writing, Digital,
Communication, Creativity and Innovation.

Keep the claims you improved, historical comparison, supplier questions, option table and revised brief. A
useful innovation record shows the question, evidence, trial or investigation needed and the limits of the
conclusion.

Read the word, find the evidence, explain the limit
To practise a term, write the trade word, point to its feature or source statement, and explain the function in
one plain sentence. Then say what the picture or statement cannot establish. Keep technical words intact
while making the explanation easier to follow. For example: “The asphalt course is the surface layer shown
here; this picture does not specify its required thickness.”

result; check; feedback; revision; and next practice. Mark the observed skill. A group product needs an
individual contribution record. An accurate calculation needs units and assumptions. A digital claim needs
actual tool use. A future application is a plan until it has been observed.
