Use this volume
This volume provides complete classroom readings, worked examples, named practice tasks and answer
guidance for paver operation. Follow the learning route in each chapter, study the labelled figures and
explain your reasoning. The separate answer book contains the worked keys and observation records.

Care for people, place and the next person using the work runs through the cases. Local First Nations, Métis
and Inuit learners and partners bring distinct knowledge and priorities. Participation does not require
sharing personal stories or cultural teachings, and no diagram claims to represent a Nation’s protocol.


    Classroom authoring is complete for the listed competencies. Qualified technical review, actual
    fleet/site matching, a local teaching pilot and observed practical qualification are separate
    requirements. The practical records are ready for an instructor to configure and use; they do not certify
    unobserved work.

    1. Identify the object, process or quantity shown.
    2. Read labels, units and the figure’s stated limits.
    3. Connect the image to the chapter explanation.
    4. Try the exercise and check against evidence.
    5. Explain the result to another person and revise if needed.

Cowork and Codex can help with permitted multi-file tasks and repeatable workflows when their tools are
available. Give a clear purpose, named sources, output formats, limits and checks. Ask for a findings table
with page/cell evidence; verify it against the source. Reopen DOCX, XLSX, HTML or PDF outputs. For study
audio/video, verify the script, captions, diagrams and actual playback. A generated result is not a technical
approval.



Keep the term with its meaning
AGGREGATE
Mineral particles forming the solid skeleton of the mixture or granular layer.

BINDER
The asphalt material that coats and binds the aggregate in the specified mixture.

COMPACTED THICKNESS
Layer thickness after the required compaction process; distinct from loose depth behind the
screed.

DENSITY REFERENCE
The specified comparison value used as the denominator of a percent-density calculation.

GRADATION
Distribution of aggregate particle sizes, commonly expressed as cumulative percent passing
specified sieves.

HEAD OF MATERIAL
The quantity/distribution of mix immediately in front of the screed.

JOB MIX FORMULA (JMF)
The accepted mixture proportions and properties under the applicable quality/contract process.

LOCKOUT
Isolation, securing and verification of hazardous energy for work requiring protection.

LOT / SUB-LOT
Defined portions of work used for sampling, testing and acceptance under the contract.

OMM
Operation and maintenance manual matched to the actual machine, serial range and configuration.

RAP
Reclaimed asphalt pavement: recovered material containing aggregate and aged binder.

SDS
Safety data sheet for the actual product; read with its label and workplace procedure.

SCREED
The paver assembly that forms the mat; its response depends on setup, material and movement.

STATION / OFFSET
Location along an alignment / distance and direction from a defined reference.

TCP
Traffic control person with the required training and assigned role.

TRACEABILITY
A record connecting material, location, time, sample, result and authorized decision.

SVG
Scalable vector graphics: useful for clear diagrams whose labels and geometry must be checked.

resources.

Asphalt placement process

Worked example · Convert a trial thickness relationship
A fictional trial produced 50 mm compacted thickness from 62 mm loose thickness. The same approved
trial relationship is used to estimate a 45 mm compacted target.

Loose/compacted factor = 62/50 = 1.24. Estimated loose thickness = 45 × 1.24 = 55.8 mm. This is an
exercise estimate tied to the stated trial, not a universal setting; confirm with actual placement and
compaction checks.


  Explain and apply the idea

  Put surface acceptance, treatment readiness, paver setup, placement and rolling in a useful sequence.

  Answer guidance.

  Accept and prepare the surface; apply/verify the specified treatment; inspect and set the
  paver/screed; place consistently; compact through the approved rolling plan. Communication and
  quality checks continue throughout.

  Observe: Reading, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  A rectangular 50 m × 3 m mat has 0.05 m compacted thickness

  A rectangular 50 m × 3 m mat has 0.05 m compacted thickness. Find compacted volume.

  Answer guidance.

  50 × 3 × 0.05 = 7.5 m³. Tonnage requires an appropriate density; loose thickness and delivered mass
  are different quantities.

  Observe: Numeracy through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved work.

Paving machine equipment and attachments

Worked example · Two sensor jobs
A diagram labels one sensor near the auger and another following a reference beside the machine.

The feed sensor can control the material supply in its assigned zone; a grade sensor follows a reference for
elevation control. Confirm the actual model and function from the manual. Similar-looking sensors do not
necessarily control the same system.


  Label ten parts on the supplied paver figure and state each function.

  Label ten parts on the supplied paver figure and state each function.

  Answer guidance.

  Use hopper, wings, push rollers, conveyors, augers, tracks/wheels, operator station, tow arms, screed
  plate and end gates or other correctly identified visible parts. Explain functions using the diagram and
  model scope.

  Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  Convert nominal 8 ft and 10 ft widths to metres

  Convert nominal 8 ft and 10 ft widths to metres. Do these establish the actual screed settings?

  Answer guidance.

  8 × 0.3048 = 2.4384 m; 10 × 0.3048 = 3.048 m. These conversions do not establish a particular
  machine’s working width or setting.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Paver work safety procedures

Worked example · A stopped auger
Material stops moving, and a worker reaches toward the auger to remove a lump.

Stop the approach and keep clear. A pause in automatic feed does not isolate the auger; it may restart. Use
the approved stopping, lockout, stored-energy and clearing procedure with authorized personnel.


  Name the safe response when the truck guide’s signal is lost.

  Name the safe response when the truck guide’s signal is lost.

  Answer guidance.

  Stop the affected movement, maintain safe positions and re-establish acknowledged communication
  before resuming.

  Observe: Communication, Collaboration through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.




  A revised layout moves the paver edge 0.4 m toward a public route

  A revised layout moves the paver edge 0.4 m toward a public route. What must be checked before
  proceeding?

  Answer guidance.

  Reassess the actual clearance, barriers, swept paths and access plan with the responsible lead/TCP
  team. The numerical shift alone does not establish acceptable separation.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving, Adaptability through the learner’s actual contribution,
  calculation, explanation or saved work.

Asphalt paver operator roles and
responsibilities

Worked example · Hopper level is not the whole story
The hopper contains mix, but the right side of the screed has a visibly reduced material head and texture
changes.

Report the side and location, coordinate with the screed operator and inspect the feed-system status from
safe positions. A full hopper does not prove uniform delivery across the screed. Use authorized
adjustments and record the response; do not reach into the feed system.


  Explain the difference between hopper contents and head of material.

  Explain the difference between hopper contents and head of material.

  Answer guidance.

  The hopper receives/stores incoming mix; conveyors and augers deliver/distribute it to the screed.
  Head refers to the material in front of the screed. One does not guarantee the other is consistent.

  Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  A 20 t load lasts 8 min at a steady fictional rate

  A 20 t load lasts 8 min at a steady fictional rate. Find t/min; why is it not a universal paver speed?

  Answer guidance.

  20/8 = 2.5 t/min. Travel speed also depends on width, thickness, density, supply and the approved
  process; mass rate alone is insufficient.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Paving machine maintenance

Worked example · A repeated low level
Hydraulic oil is low again after yesterday’s top-up. A fresh wet area is visible beneath the machine.

Record the repeated loss and safe visual observation, secure/hold the machine as required and notify the
responsible person. Do not treat repeated top-up as a repair or touch the suspected leak. Confirm
authorized correction before release.


  A fictional service interval is 10 operating hours

  A fictional service interval is 10 operating hours. Last service 1,420 h; current 1,429.5 h. How much
  remains?

  Answer guidance.

  9.5 h elapsed; 0.5 h remains under this exercise interval. Calendar, condition and other schedule
  requirements may make service due sooner.

  Observe: Numeracy, Reading through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or
  saved work.




  Explain why an unlabelled spray bottle cannot be accepted as a release agent.

  Explain why an unlabelled spray bottle cannot be accepted as a release agent.

  Answer guidance.

  Its identity, compatibility, hazards and approved use cannot be confirmed. Obtain the labelled
  approved product and SDS before use.

  Observe: Problem solving, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Markers, grades and stakes

Worked example · Offset versus edge
A stake marked 1.5 m RT OFFSET describes the approved right paving edge at station 0+060. A learner
treats the stake itself as the edge.

Use the project legend and stated offset direction to locate the actual edge; the stake is a reference away
from it. Verify the relation with the grade lead before setting the paver line. Do not shift the whole mat to
the stake.


  At one verified point, base EL is 101.250 m and finished EL is 101.300 m

  At one verified point, base EL is 101.250 m and finished EL is 101.300 m. Find the vertical difference.

  Answer guidance.

  0.050 m = 50 mm. It is the specified geometric difference at that point, not automatically the loose
  screed setting.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.




  Write a read-back containing station, side, offset and unresolved datum.

  Write a read-back containing station, side, offset and unresolved datum.

  Answer guidance.

  Example: “Station 0+060, right-side offset stake, marked 1.5 m. I need confirmation of the reference
  direction and datum before using its elevation.” Include the actual mark and ask the responsible
  person to confirm.

  Observe: Writing, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.

A learner completes a controlled flat-pad pass with simulated material. The next job involves a steep
access road and hot mix.

Record the demonstrated flat-pad skills. Plan the additional machine/site-specific preparation and

  Answer guidance.

  actual behaviour and help.

  specified quality are the criteria.

Follow shut down procedures

Worked example · An incomplete handover
The paver is parked correctly, but the operator noticed a leak and left without recording it.

The shutdown/handover is incomplete. Record the leak observation, location and machine status, notify
the responsible person and arrange the required hold/inspection. The next operator needs the information
before starting.


  Explain shutdown versus lockout.

  Explain shutdown versus lockout.

  Answer guidance.

  Shutdown ends operation in the prescribed state. Lockout isolates, secures and verifies hazardous
  energy for work requiring protection. A shutdown may be part of lockout but is not the whole process.

  Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  A ten-item assessment gives 17/20 but a required screed-support check failed

  A ten-item assessment gives 17/20 but a required screed-support check failed. State the result.

  Answer guidance.

  85% numerically, but not released: the safety-critical failure must be corrected and reassessed.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.
