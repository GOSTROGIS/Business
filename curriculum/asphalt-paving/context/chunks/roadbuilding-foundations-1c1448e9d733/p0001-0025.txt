Asphalt paving · Roadbuilding foundations
Learn the industry, read the work and explain a decision
Roadbuilding connects materials, people, places and decisions. This book starts with the industry and moves
through road history, paving technology, team responsibilities, road documents and emerging approaches.
You will read evidence, work through examples, make calculations, discuss choices and record what still
needs checking. The classroom cases give you the information needed to practise without using private
workplace records.

This is the 29 October 2026 classroom teaching edition. Qualified technical review and classroom pilot are
pending. Classroom completion records learning evidence; equipment training, workplace orientation and
authorization are separate requirements.

1 · Industry evidence and decisions
Read a number with its label
A statement about an industry needs a measure, geography and period. A count of people employed is
different from a count of employers. A forecast of openings over ten years is different from the number
employed in one year. Construction includes building work and civil engineering as well as paving. A broad
construction figure provides context; it cannot be renamed an asphalt-paving total.

reports the following. The 2024 values are attributed on the page to the Labour Force Survey. The openings

Compare environmental claims within a boundary
Asphalt and concrete are pavement materials, not complete environmental outcomes. Compare a defined
service, time period and system boundary. A material-production figure alone leaves transport, construction,
maintenance, use and end-of-life outside the answer. A reusable material is not automatically the option with
the lowest total impact. The amount recovered, replacement of new material, processing energy, transport
and required performance all matter.

2 · How roads changed
Ask what a road was built to do
Road history is a history of materials, movement, power and relationships with place. A route can serve travel,
trade, access to resources, settlement or military movement. Its purpose can change. A road that enables one
activity may also affect land, water, access and existing travel routes. A timeline should identify whose
account is being used and avoid treating one engineering tradition as the beginning of all movement.

In B.C., a classroom discussion of road history does not give permission to map or publish community-held
routes or knowledge. Use the public evidence cards below. Invite local knowledge only through the
appropriate relationships and agreed purpose. The lesson asks you to explain change, not to speak for a
community.



Evidence cards for a timeline
H1 · Via Appia. UNESCO describes the Appian Way as a major Roman road constructed and developed from
312 BCE to the fourth century CE. Its initial strategic purpose included military conquest. It later supported
connected settlements, agriculture and trade. Engineering works included the road and associated structures.
One road can have several purposes over time; “Roman” does not mean “the world's first road.”

H2 · Alaska Highway. Public Services and Procurement Canada's history, updated 16 December 2025,
explains that the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers built the road during the Second World War to provide land
access to Alaska. It identifies local First Nations' vital role as guides. The pioneer road was completed in
1942, and the permanent location in 1943. Responsibility for the Canadian portion transferred to National
Defence in 1946. Official civilian non-military use began in 1948. Further responsibility changes occurred in
1961, 1964 and 1992. These are different events, not competing dates for the same event.

H3 · Post-war highways. FHWA identifies the U.S. Federal-Aid Highway Act of 1956 as a major step in the
Interstate system. Its historical discussion connects the system with national planning and everyday
movement of goods and people. The 1950s were within the Cold War period. A date and a national-security
context do not prove that every new road had one motive; use evidence for the particular project.

H4 · Present resource roads. B.C.'s resource-road information describes a network used for resource
industries and access. A resource road is not necessarily paved, and a logging road is not necessarily a
corduroy road. Its owner, administrator, condition and access rules need to be established from the relevant
record. A present-day category does not describe every historical method used to build it.

LA2-01 · Build a timeline that preserves meaning

Place 312 BCE, 1942, 1943, 1946, 1948 and 1956 in chronological order. Label each event and its evidence
card. Identify two different meanings of “completed” in H2. Write one sentence explaining why a timeline
alone cannot show every social or environmental effect of roadbuilding. Skills: Reading, Writing, Problem
Solving.

Materials and methods solve different problems
An earth route uses the ground surface, sometimes shaped or improved. Stone and gravel add mineral
material. Cut or shaped stone can form a durable surface where the design, foundation and maintenance suit
the use. Wood has also been used: a corduroy road is a historical construction form using closely laid logs
across the travel direction, often associated with soft or wet ground. The log pattern explains the name. It is a
method, while logging road describes a road's use; the categories overlap in some histories but are not
synonyms.

Asphalt pavement combines aggregate with a bituminous binder. Concrete pavement uses a cement-based
binding system. These materials have different production and construction processes. Do not confuse
asphalt cement—the binder in an asphalt mixture—with Portland cement used in concrete. A road may have
several layers and may be repaired with a different material from its original surface. Appearance alone is not
enough to identify its entire construction.

Modern asphalt placement commonly involves delivery of mixture, controlled placement through a paver and
screed, and compaction using rollers, with associated preparation, checks and finishing. Mechanization
changes how material is moved and shaped; it does not remove the need for a suitable foundation, drainage,
specifications or skilled people. This unit describes the change in method. Operating settings belong to the
relevant supervised training and approved documents.

Worked example · Correct a category mistake. A museum draft says, “All logging roads were corduroy roads,
and modern roads replaced wood with asphalt.” The first clause confuses purpose with method. The second
implies one universal sequence. A better label is: “Corduroy construction used logs across a route. It was one
historical way of addressing difficult ground. Logging roads have used varied surfaces and construction
methods; modern asphalt paving is one distinct technology.”

LA2-02 · Match material, method and purpose

Create three columns headed material, method and purpose. Sort these terms: earth; cut stone; gravel;
timber logs; concrete; moving harvested timber; military access; asphalt mixture; closely laid crosswise logs;

cement and Portland cement. Explain why a purpose can be served by more than one material. Add one
question you would ask before classifying an unfamiliar road from a photograph. Skills: Reading,
Communication.

LA2-03 · Explain a road without inventing its history

Fictional archive label: “Photo R17, date not recorded. A line of logs crosses a wet-looking route. A truck
appears in the background. No owner, purpose or place is recorded.” Write a 50–80-word description
separating observation, reasonable interpretation and unknown information. A partner reads it and identifies
any unsupported claim. Revise one sentence. Skills: Writing, Collaboration, Adaptability.



Explain why demand changed
War can accelerate road construction, as H2 shows. Post-war planning and Cold War concerns formed part of
the setting for major highway development, while trade, industry, settlement and daily access also influenced
demand. The same road can support freight, recreation and local travel. “More roads” is not a complete
measure of benefit: maintenance, alternatives, land use and the people affected belong in the question.

3 · From materials to paving
Follow the material through the system
Paving technology connects material sources, manufacturing, transport and placement. Begin with the
function of each stage. A pit or quarry supplies mineral material; processing prepares aggregate to specified
characteristics; a plant combines selected materials; haul vehicles move the mixture; paving equipment

Know what the ingredients do
Aggregate is mineral material such as processed sand, gravel or crushed stone. A pit generally obtains
unconsolidated deposits; a quarry extracts rock that may need crushing and screening. Material availability
does not establish suitability: the required properties come from the approved design and specification. A
short haul distance alone cannot answer whether a source meets the project requirements.

Asphalt binder, also called asphalt cement in many documents, binds the aggregate in asphalt mixtures. It is
distinct from the Portland cement used in concrete. Additives and modified binders can change mixture
characteristics for a defined purpose, but their selection and proportions require the applicable approved
information. Fuel used to heat a production process is not automatically an ingredient in the pavement
mixture. A delivery label must identify the actual product rather than relying on the loose word oil.

Technology evidence card T1. EPA's Hot Mix Asphalt Plants description identifies sized aggregate, liquid
asphalt cement and potentially reclaimed asphalt pavement as components of hot-mix asphalt. It describes
batch and drum-mix production. Its technical account is used here for the process distinction; its historical
U.S. production totals and operating details are not current B.C. specifications.

LA3-01 · Explain the ingredients to a new learner

Write a three-sentence explanation that distinguishes aggregate, asphalt binder and fuel. Add a sentence
explaining the conditional purpose of an additive and why its use needs approved information. Distinguish a
pit from a quarry, then name one question about an aggregate source that proximity alone cannot answer.
Explain why “cement” needs context when comparing asphalt and concrete. Skills: Reading, Writing,
Communication.



Distinguish batch and drum production
A batch plant prepares identifiable batches: aggregate is proportioned and combined with binder in a mixer. A
drum-mix plant uses a continuous process in which material flows through the plant. Drum designs differ; do
not treat every drum layout as identical. The beginner distinction is batch quantities versus a continuing
material stream, not “one uses a drum and the other has no drum.” A batch plant can have a drying drum
before its mixer.

Fictional classroom production records. Plant B's teaching log lists batch 01, batch 02 and batch 03, each
with a separate record of ingredients and release. Plant D's teaching log records a continuous feed period and
time-stamped checks. These are deliberately simplified descriptions. Neither log provides an approved mix
design, production rate or operating instruction.

Worked example. “Plant B has a drying drum, so it must be a drum-mix plant” is an invalid inference. Ask
where and how the mixture is produced. B's separate batch records support a batch process in this case. The
physical presence of one part does not classify the whole system.

Treat product codes as information to verify
A product code can identify a grade system or a product, but it does not supply the whole application
instruction. A penetration-grade designation and a performance-grade designation are different ways of
describing binder characteristics. The numbers are not interchangeable merely because both appear beside
asphalt. The purchaser and operator need the approved product, governing specification, supplier record and
relevant safety information.

Fictional document-check case. An archive card contains the labels “85–100 Pen” and “PG 72–22,” and a
handwritten note says “altered oil.” A new practice request says only “send the usual oil.” No current
approved binder, project specification or supplier product sheet has been attached. These labels are for
recognition and clarification; the case does not recommend either grade for B.C. work. Do not infer a mixing
temperature, quantity or handling procedure from them.

LA3-03 · Write the clarification before the order

Make a four-item information request that would identify the intended product and its governing documents.
Explain why “usual oil” and an archive grade label are insufficient. Read your request to a partner, who checks
whether you have accidentally approved a product. Keep it as a classroom draft. Skills: Writing,
Communication, Problem Solving.



Separate the recycling routes
Reclaimed asphalt pavement (RAP) is asphalt pavement material recovered for possible reuse. A plant-
recycling route takes recovered material through suitable processing and an approved production process.
Reuse is conditional on the design and quality requirements. A claim that all recovered material can be added
without testing is unsupported.

Hot-in-place recycling treats an existing asphalt surface on the road using a coordinated equipment train. Its
broad purpose is to reuse existing material in place through a specialist process. It is different from hauling
recovered material to a plant. A milling machine's removal of material is also not proof that the removed
material has been successfully recycled. Track the actual route and evidence for reuse.

An asphalt train is a coordinated sequence of equipment and roles. In ordinary placement, haul, paver/screed
and compaction functions connect. A specialist hot-in-place train has its own process and equipment. The
word train does not mean every paving job uses the same machinery, controls or sequence.

LA3-04 · Trace two reuse pathways

Draw two labelled routes: recovered material sent to a plant, and a specialist hot-in-place process on the
existing road. Mark where the existing material is recovered or reused. Add one document or check needed
before calling either route approved. Explain why “milled” and “recycled into a new pavement” are different
claims. Skills: Reading, Creativity and Innovation, Problem Solving.

LA3-05 · Calculate a fictional material record

A classroom demonstration model uses 80 counters for new aggregate, 15 for recovered mixture and 5 for
binder, for 100 counters in total. Counters are a visual model, not a mix recipe; the recovered mixture already
contains its own materials. Find each category's share of the model and check the sum. Explain why adding
the five binder counters to the 15 recovered-mixture counters does not establish the real binder content.
Skills: Numeracy, Reading.

4 · Working in a paving team
Understand the role and the handover
A paving team coordinates people whose tasks depend on one another. The driver delivers material; the paver
operator manages the machine's placement function; the screed role attends to the screed's contribution to
the placed mat; rakers support hand placement and finishing; roller operators perform the assigned
compaction work; milling operators work with equipment that removes or profiles existing material. Job titles
and boundaries vary. A job title alone does not establish a person's training, authorization or responsibility on
a particular site.

Fictional team brief W1. At the Willow Reach training centre, a classroom team is preparing an explanation of

Treat health and safety as a shared system with defined duties
Health and safety includes planning, suitable equipment and systems, instruction, supervision, reporting and

must guide actual work.

Plan development without promising job security

5 · Read road documents and quantities
Identify the project before the specification
A project may be described by use—commercial, industrial or residential—or by jurisdiction and delivery, such
as a provincial highway project. These categories answer different questions and can overlap. A private
industrial site is different from a provincially administered highway, even if both use asphalt. Owners can
include provincial, federal, municipal or private organizations. A funder, designer, contractor and owner may
be different parties.

6 · Investigate a paving trend
Turn a promising idea into a testable question
A trend is useful to investigate when it responds to a defined need. A product described as green, new or
innovative still needs evidence. Ask what problem it addresses, how it works, where it has been studied, what
limits apply and who would approve its use. Something new to a local project may have been used elsewhere
for decades. Publication date and location matter.

This unit compares material and design ideas without selecting a live road design. You will prepare a
recommendation for further investigation using complete evidence cards and a fictional project brief. A careful
recommendation can say that one option deserves a closer technical study while leaving design approval to
the appropriate professional.



Compare surface and reuse approaches
Trend card N1 · Paving stones. Modular paving uses individual units rather than one continuous asphalt layer.
Some systems are designed to manage water through joints and underlying layers; the word paver alone does
not establish permeability. Ask about the whole pavement system, loading, drainage, accessibility and
maintenance. A patterned appearance is not environmental evidence.

Trend card N2 · Porous asphalt. FHWA's April 2015 technical brief describes porous asphalt pavements with
stone reservoirs as systems intended to provide a surface and manage stormwater. Water moves through
interconnected surface voids into the underlying reservoir. This differs from an open-graded friction course on
a dense pavement, where water moves through the surface course and out toward the side. The brief
identifies potential clogging and the need for specialized maintenance, as well as site/design limitations. A
material name does not guarantee infiltration at an unsuitable site.

Trend card N3 · Recycling. Recovered asphalt may enter an approved plant or specialist in-place process.
Recovered concrete may be processed as aggregate. Potential reductions in new-material demand need to be
examined alongside processing, transport, suitability, maintenance and end-of-life. “Recycled” is a property
of the material pathway, not a complete environmental score.

LA6-01 · Improve three claims

never needs drainage planning”; “Any recycled material is automatically the lowest-impact choice.” For each,
identify a mechanism, a condition and one question for a qualified reviewer. Skills: Reading, Problem Solving,
Writing.

Trend card N4 · Epoxy, colour and modified asphalt. Epoxy systems, coloured surfaces and modified binders

establish structural performance, skid properties, durability or accessibility. A modified binder may be

Trend card N5 · Lightweight fill and “floating roads.” Lightweight fill reduces the load imposed by an

lightweight-fill ideas are not all recent inventions.
