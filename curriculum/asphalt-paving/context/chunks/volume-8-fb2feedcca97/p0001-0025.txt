Compaction equipment
Compaction equipment changes the mixture’s density and surface through a planned application of load
and, where fitted, dynamic energy. Choose equipment to suit the mixture, support, production and finished
requirements.


  Types of Compaction Rollers

  Major components of compaction equipment

  Basic functions of compaction equipment

  Emerging technology


LEARNING STEP 1




Types of Compaction Rollers
Manufacturers such as Caterpillar, Ammann, BOMAG and Dynapac offer different roller families and
configurations. Compare drum width, operating mass, tire/drum arrangement, working modes, production
and access. A small utility roller and a highway tandem roller are not interchangeable simply because both
compact asphalt. Confirm the actual model, serial range and OMM. Equipment choice and the rolling plan
belong to the responsible crew/quality process.

LEARNING STEP 2




Major components of compaction equipment
A steel-drum roller may work statically or with fitted vibration/oscillation. A pneumatic-tired roller applies
load through tires; tire condition, pressure, temperature and ballast affect its action under the
manufacturer and project requirements. Do not invent tire pressures. A combination roller has both drum
and tire contact. Identify engine, travel/steering controls, articulation joint, drums/tires, water/spray
system, scrapers, vibration controls, ROPS and seat belt where fitted. Components and permitted modes
vary by model.

LEARNING STEP 3




Basic functions of compaction equipment
Breakdown rolling applies early compaction behind the paver within suitable mixture conditions.
Intermediate rolling develops required density and surface condition as planned. Finish rolling removes
marks and completes the surface without damaging it. One machine may perform more than one role
where the plan supports it; role names do not prescribe a universal machine count or pass sequence.

LEARNING STEP 4




Emerging technology
Oscillation applies a different dynamic action from conventional vertical vibration. Intelligent compaction,
mapping and temperature displays can help monitor coverage and conditions. Their displayed values are
not automatically the contract’s acceptance density. Confirm what each measurement represents and how
it is calibrated/validated. Technology supports operator judgement and quality testing; a colourful coverage
map does not prove every point meets specification.

Compaction work safety procedures
A roller repeatedly changes direction near people and other equipment. Safe operation depends on
visibility, ground support, communication and the machine’s protective systems throughout the pattern.


  Interpret job planning

  Machine-specific safety hazards

  Traffic control requirements

  PPE

  Lockout procedures

  Secure machine for safety


LEARNING STEP 1




Interpret job planning
Review the path, pattern, thickness, temperature conditions, crown, joints, obstacles and coordination of
the asphalt train. Identify edges, slopes, structures, traffic and public access. Confirm the current plan and
who authorizes a change. Walk the inspection route only under controlled conditions.

LEARNING STEP 2




Machine-specific safety hazards
Recognize blind areas, crush/articulation zones, hot surfaces, noise, fumes, hydraulic pressure, rollover
hazards and proximity to pavers/other rollers. Inspect and use the fitted ROPS and seat belt according to
the OMM and applicable rules; do not modify protective structures. A fixed three-metre spacing is not a
universal safe separation. Establish actual clearances from the machine instructions and site assessment,
including stopping distance and visibility.

LEARNING STEP 3




Traffic control requirements
Use the traffic-control plan, trained personnel, signs and communication arrangement. Keep the public
route separate from reversing/turning areas. Obtain acknowledgement before entering another machine’s
working zone. If a pedestrian, vehicle or unexpected obstacle enters, stop affected movement safely and
restore control before continuing.

LEARNING STEP 4

PPE
Wear the selected footwear, head, eye, hearing and high-visibility protection and any task-specific items.
Ensure the belt and operator position remain compatible with safe control. PPE does not replace the
protective structure, separation or visibility checks. Supply, use and maintenance responsibilities follow
current Part 8 and employer arrangements.

LEARNING STEP 5




Lockout procedures
Service and hazardous access require the actual machine’s lockout, stored-energy and support procedure.
The articulation joint can move and crush; use the prescribed steering-frame lock where required and only
in the specified conditions. A parked engine does not remove every hydraulic or gravity hazard. Verify the
safe state before work.

LEARNING STEP 6




Secure machine for safety
Park on the approved stable ground, apply the specified parking controls and required chocks, and secure
keys/access. Position away from unsupported edges and public routes. Record defects and protect people
from a machine that could roll or be moved by an unauthorized person. A battery/night switch is only one
part of the actual security/isolation arrangement.

Asphalt Compaction Roller operator roles
and responsibilities
The roller operator coordinates density development and surface finish with the paving train. A useful
rolling pattern is planned, checked and adjusted through evidence rather than repeated by habit.


  Communication techniques

  Effective rolling pattern

  Determine pattern to achieve ride planning

  Responsibilities of Compaction Roller operator


LEARNING STEP 1




Communication techniques
Agree machine roles, lane/area, direction-change locations, signal/radio channel and stop response. With
multiple rollers, share which area is complete, which still needs work and where testing or handwork is
occurring. Use location and pass/coverage terminology consistently. An unacknowledged instruction is not
a completed handover.

LEARNING STEP 2




Effective rolling pattern
A rolling pattern specifies sequence, overlap, operating mode, speed and repetitions for the actual mixture
and conditions. A pass and a coverage must be defined: one machine movement is not necessarily one
complete treatment of the full mat width. Establish the pattern with the approved trial/quality process and
record changes. Repeatedly rolling one strip while missing another creates uneven treatment even if the
total pass count looks high.

LEARNING STEP 3




Determine pattern to achieve ride planning
Plan for ride quality before the mat cools. Coordinate steady paving, joints, starts/stops and smooth
direction changes. Abrupt turns or stopping in a damaging condition can mark or displace the mat. The
finish roller cannot be assumed to repair every earlier profile defect. Use measured feedback and report
persistent marks or instability promptly.

Responsibilities of Compaction Roller operator
The operator monitors surroundings, mixture condition, spray/scraper performance, working mode and the
planned pattern. Avoid sharp cuts across the mat and do not allow vibration to continue while stationary
where prohibited by the machine/process instructions. Coordinate changes with the lead instead of adding
passes independently. Required density, smoothness and surface condition are separate checks; meeting
one does not establish the others.

Compaction Roller maintenance
Roller maintenance supports safe control and consistent contact with the mat. Water sprays, scrapers and
clean contact surfaces are process equipment as well as housekeeping items.


  Service lubrication systems (greasing)

  Service air intake system as required (air filters/pre cleaners)

  Drain fuel tank sump, water separator fuel filters is equipped

  Add fluids as required

  Inspect and clean components as required

  Housekeeping tasks


LEARNING STEP 1




Service lubrication systems (greasing)
Use the actual lubrication chart and fitted system. Confirm product, interval, quantity and safe position,
clean fittings and load the grease gun correctly. Articulation-area access requires the specified protection.
Do not transfer a grease quantity from a different roller or force a blocked fitting.

Service air intake system as required (air filters/pre cleaners)
Read intake restriction indicators and service the pre-cleaner/filter through the manufacturer’s method.
Inspect seals and prevent contamination of the clean side. Clean only when permitted; otherwise replace

Where fitted, drain the fuel sump/water separator under the prescribed conditions, collect the liquid and

Add fluids as required
Identify the correct fluid and reservoir, then use the specified level ground, temperature and engine state
for checking. Dual-scale dipsticks require the correct reading condition. Clean fill tools and avoid mixing,
overfill and dirt/water entry. Report repeated losses and leaks rather than treating top-up as a permanent
correction.

LEARNING STEP 5




Inspect and clean components as required
Inspect radiator/oil cooler, plugs, dipsticks, batteries, hoses, guards and accessible components. Protect
fins and electrical parts during cleaning. Hot coolant, pressurized hydraulics and battery hazards require
their actual procedures. Inspect safety systems and report defects for authorized repair. A maintenance
checklist does not authorize work beyond the learner’s training.

LEARNING STEP 6




Housekeeping tasks
Remove debris and excess grease from safe access areas. Maintain the spray tank, filters, nozzles and
approved water-treatment arrangements, and inspect drum/tire cleanliness, scrapers and fitted mats.
Blocked sprays can allow pickup and mark the surface. Prepare the water system for freezing conditions
using the model’s drain/winterization procedure; “drain all water” requires knowing every affected
component. Collect waste, restore guards and record service and defects.
