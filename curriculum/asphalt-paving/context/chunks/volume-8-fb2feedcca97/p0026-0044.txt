Study chapter · Outline L5



Operate Compaction Roller
Compaction operation combines smooth machine control with temperature, density and coverage
feedback. The instructor observes the actual machine and conditions; a written pattern alone does not
demonstrate operating skill.


  Plan work

  Test temperature

  Test density

  Maintain efficient patterns


LEARNING STEP 1




Plan work
Inspect the grade for edges, support, obstacles and limits. Confirm roller types/roles, approved settings
and pattern, water supply, communications and traffic controls. Complete pre-start checks and daily
maintenance. Start and monitor through the matching OMM. Practise forward–stop–reverse–stop on the
flat training area, parking controls, travel-mode changes and fitted vibration controls under supervision.
Weather, site conditions, grade and quality feedback can require an authorized plan change.

LEARNING STEP 2




Test temperature
Measure temperature with the specified instrument and method at safe locations. Infrared instruments
read a surface-related temperature and depend on emissivity, distance/spot size and conditions; a probe
measures through its contact method. Record location, time, method and units. Mixture-specific
compaction conditions govern; do not use a universal temperature window from memory. Cooling rate
varies with lift, weather, support and delays.

LEARNING STEP 3




Test density
Density testing provides feedback through the approved method and reference. A nuclear gauge such as
the Troxler 3430 requires licensed/trained use and applicable CNSC controls; observing it does not
authorize operation. Core testing has a different sampling process. Confirm the denominator for percent
density and the test location/lot. The test plan determines frequency. Results help the responsible lead
evaluate the pattern; a single reading does not prove complete acceptance.

LEARNING STEP 4




Maintain efficient patterns
Maintain the approved breakdown, intermediate and finish sequence with smooth direction changes and
controlled speed/mode. Demonstrate the assigned confined-edge and longitudinal-joint patterns under the

damaging stops. Do not interpret repeated forward/reverse movement as permission to stop with vibration
engaged. Finish with the model-specific shutdown and a clear record of actual treatment and unresolved
areas.

Study chapter · Outline L6



Follow shut down procedures
A roller can move unintentionally after the shift if parking and securement are incomplete. Shutdown also
prepares the machine for cleaning, staging, maintenance and transport.


  Shut-down procedures

  Clean equipment

  Ensure efficient staging

  Prepare for transport

  Secure machine for safety


LEARNING STEP 1




Shut-down procedures
Choose the designated stable parking position with service access, clear emergency/public routes and
suitable ground for checks. Follow the OMM stopping, cooling and shutdown sequence, apply parking
controls and required chocks, and inspect safely for leaks or damage. Use the battery/night switch only as
directed; it is not the whole lockout procedure. Record defects and the machine’s status.

LEARNING STEP 2




Clean equipment
Clean drums/tires, scrapers and spray equipment through the approved safe method. Control movement,
heat, pressure and cleaning-product hazards, and contain waste. Never reach toward a rotating drum or
articulation zone to remove pickup. Restore guards and account for tools before release.

LEARNING STEP 3




Ensure efficient staging
Coordinate staging with the foreperson so the next shift can access the roller without blocking other
equipment, deliveries or public routes. Identify the machine, location, readiness and pending service.
Efficient staging includes safe access for a machine that cannot restart.

LEARNING STEP 4




Prepare for transport
Prepare for transport using the actual OMM configuration and carrier plan. Assign a competent
operator/guide and carrier responsibilities, verify mass/dimensions, ramp/support suitability and
designated tie-down points. Use the applicable securement requirements and approved loading sequence.
The transport driver’s role does not remove the need for clear machine-control responsibilities.

LEARNING STEP 5




Secure machine for safety
Secure controls, keys, detachable items and access panels; protect the public and prevent roll-away
through the required parking/chocking arrangement. Consider residual heat and stored energy.
Communicate out-of-service status visibly through the site system. Recheck the final state before leaving; a
completed numerical checklist score cannot override a failed critical securement item.
