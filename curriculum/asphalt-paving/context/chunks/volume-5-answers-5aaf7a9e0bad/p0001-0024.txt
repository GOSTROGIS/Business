Use this volume
This volume provides complete classroom readings, worked examples, named practice tasks and answer
guidance for milling and reclaiming. Follow the learning route in each chapter, study the labelled figures
and explain your reasoning. The separate answer book contains the worked keys and observation records.

Care for people, place and the next person using the work runs through the cases. Local First Nations, Métis
and Inuit learners and partners bring distinct knowledge and priorities. Participation does not require
sharing personal stories or cultural teachings, and no diagram claims to represent a Nation’s protocol.


    Classroom authoring is complete for the listed competencies. Qualified technical review, actual
    fleet/site matching, a local teaching pilot and observed practical qualification are separate
    requirements. The practical records are ready for an instructor to configure and use; they do not certify
    unobserved work.




Read the picture, then test the idea
    1. Identify the object, process or quantity shown.
    2. Read labels, units and the figure’s stated limits.
    3. Connect the image to the chapter explanation.
    4. Try the exercise and check against evidence.
    5. Explain the result to another person and revise if needed.



Use AI as a checked working tool
Claude and ChatGPT can help explain provided documents and create supported files. Claude Code,
Cowork and Codex can help with permitted multi-file tasks and repeatable workflows when their tools are
available. Give a clear purpose, named sources, output formats, limits and checks. Ask for a findings table
with page/cell evidence; verify it against the source. Reopen DOCX, XLSX, HTML or PDF outputs. For study
audio/video, verify the script, captions, diagrams and actual playback. A generated result is not a technical
approval.



Keep the term with its meaning
AGGREGATE
Mineral particles forming the solid skeleton of the mixture or granular layer.

BINDER
The asphalt material that coats and binds the aggregate in the specified mixture.

COMPACTED THICKNESS
Layer thickness after the required compaction process; distinct from loose depth behind the
screed.

DENSITY REFERENCE
The specified comparison value used as the denominator of a percent-density calculation.

GRADATION
Distribution of aggregate particle sizes, commonly expressed as cumulative percent passing
specified sieves.

HEAD OF MATERIAL
The quantity/distribution of mix immediately in front of the screed.

JOB MIX FORMULA (JMF)
The accepted mixture proportions and properties under the applicable quality/contract process.

LOCKOUT
Isolation, securing and verification of hazardous energy for work requiring protection.

LOT / SUB-LOT
Defined portions of work used for sampling, testing and acceptance under the contract.

OMM
Operation and maintenance manual matched to the actual machine, serial range and configuration.

RAP
Reclaimed asphalt pavement: recovered material containing aggregate and aged binder.

SDS
Safety data sheet for the actual product; read with its label and workplace procedure.

SCREED
The paver assembly that forms the mat; its response depends on setup, material and movement.

STATION / OFFSET
Location along an alignment / distance and direction from a defined reference.

TCP
Traffic control person with the required training and assigned role.

TRACEABILITY
A record connecting material, location, time, sample, result and authorized decision.

SVG
Scalable vector graphics: useful for clear diagrams whose labels and geometry must be checked.

DOCX / XLSX
Editable document / spreadsheet workbook; verify actual content and formulas after creation.

PDF / HTML
Page-based reading copy / browser document; inspect completeness, access and linked
resources.

MP3 / MP4 / VTT
Audio file / video container / timed web captions; test playback and retain a transcript.

Milling and reclaiming equipment

Worked example · A noise with a location
A new rhythmic noise and vibration begin at the discharge conveyor after a load change. A learner suggests
increasing speed to clear it.

Report the change and location, stop safely under the machine procedure and protect the area. Have the
responsible person arrange inspection under the required isolation. Increasing speed is an unsupported
intervention and may worsen the fault.


  Match rotor, discharge conveyor, lifting column and grade sensor to their functions.

  Match rotor, discharge conveyor, lifting column and grade sensor to their functions.

  Answer guidance.

  Rotor cuts; discharge conveyor transfers recovered material; lifting column changes/supports machine
  elevation as designed; grade sensor reads a reference for control. Identify exact configurations on the
  selected machine.

  Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  A 90 m × 2 m strip is milled 0.04 m deep

  A 90 m × 2 m strip is milled 0.04 m deep. Find its geometric removed volume. Is this its loose truck
  volume?

  Answer guidance.

  90 × 2 × 0.04 = 7.2 m³ in place. Loose volume requires a supported swell/conversion factor; it is not
  automatically 7.2 m³.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Milling and reclaiming safety procedures

Worked example · A changed discharge route
The planned truck position is blocked by a delivery vehicle. Someone proposes swinging the conveyor over
a pedestrian route.

Stop the conflicting movements, preserve the pedestrian boundary and ask the lead to revise the
arrangement with traffic control. Check overhead and swept-path hazards. Resume only after the new plan
is communicated and controls are in place.


  Name four hazardous energies that can remain after engine shutdown.

  Name four hazardous energies that can remain after engine shutdown.

  Answer guidance.

  Gravity in raised components, hydraulic pressure, rotating inertia and electrical/stored energy may
  remain. The actual machine assessment determines all sources and controls.

  Observe: Reading, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  Write a read-back for a revised milling boundary at station 0+120.

  Write a read-back for a revised milling boundary at station 0+120.

  Answer guidance.

  State station, lane/side, revised limit, discharge route, protected public route, stop signal and person
  authorizing the change. Ask the lead to confirm the interpretation.

  Observe: Writing, Communication, Collaboration through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Worked example · Two people signal
One ground worker signals a truck forward while another signals stop. The operator sees both.

Stop the affected movement. Clarify the hazard and designated signaller, confirm the driver and operator
understand the revised instruction, and resume only when the area is clear and the communication
arrangement is restored.


  Assign operator, depth checker, truck coordinator and recorder duties for a simulated
  pass.

  Assign operator, depth checker, truck coordinator and recorder duties for a simulated pass.

  Answer guidance.

  A workable allocation names each person, safe position, information needed, communication channel,
  stop authority and relief arrangement. Roles may be combined only where the plan and workload
  allow.

  Observe: Collaboration, Writing through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or
  saved work.




  Depth readings at three authorized checks are 38, 41 and 40 mm against a fictional target
  40 mm

  Depth readings at three authorized checks are 38, 41 and 40 mm against a fictional target 40 mm.
  Find the mean and explain its limitation.

  Answer guidance.

  Mean = 119/3 = 39.67 mm. It does not prove every location meets the project tolerance; retain each
  reading and its location.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Worked example · The wrong grease assumption
A chart specifies a particular grease and service interval. A worker brings an unlabelled cartridge and
proposes ten strokes at every fitting.

Do not use the unlabelled product or generic stroke count. Confirm lubricant, points, quantities and safe
positions from the matching chart. Obtain the correct product and record any blocked or damaged fitting for
action.


  A machine meter reads 812 h; the last service was at 786 h and the fictional interval is 25
  h

  A machine meter reads 812 h; the last service was at 786 h and the fictional interval is 25 h. Is it due?

  Answer guidance.

  812 − 786 = 26 h; it is 1 h beyond the stated exercise interval. Notify the lead and follow the actual
  maintenance/operating restrictions.

  Observe: Numeracy, Reading through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or
  saved work.

  procedure, waste handling and person notified. Do not diagnose the source without evidence.

  Observe: Writing, Digital, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Markers, grades and stakes

Worked example · Read an offset
A fictional stake says STA 0+125, 2.0 m RT OFFSET, FG EL 102.400, C 0.060. The legend defines cut from
the marked reference to the required grade.

Locate station 125 m on the alignment and interpret the right-side offset using the project convention. The
stated cut is 60 mm from the defined reference, not automatically from the top of the stake. FG is 102.400
m in the stated datum. Confirm the target location and mark before using the value.


  Expand BM, CB, EL, FG, PL and PVC.

  Expand BM, CB, EL, FG, PL and PVC.

  Answer guidance.

  Benchmark; catch basin; elevation; finished grade; property line; polyvinyl chloride. Verify project-
  specific usage.

  Observe: Reading, Writing through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved
  work.




  At a verified point, existing EL is 102.455 m and required milled EL is 102.400 m

  At a verified point, existing EL is 102.455 m and required milled EL is 102.400 m. Find nominal cut
  depth.

  Answer guidance.

  102.455 − 102.400 = 0.055 m = 55 mm, subject to the approved milling plan and checks for utilities
  and layer limits.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Operate milling and reclaiming equipment

Worked example · Depth changes across a pass
The approved exercise cut is 40 mm. Safe checks show 40, 41 and then 54 mm near a changed reference
surface.

Record each location, notify the lead and hold the affected operation for verification of reference and
controls. The 54 mm result should not be concealed by an average. Resume with authorized settings after
the cause and required correction are resolved.


  Describe a safe supervised sequence for forward–stop–reverse–stop on a flat training
  pad.

  Describe a safe supervised sequence for forward–stop–reverse–stop on a flat training pad.

  Answer guidance.

  Precheck and brief; confirm clear route and signals; use the OMM travel controls; move smoothly;
  stop; recheck the reverse route and acknowledgement; reverse and stop; apply the specified
  parking/isolation controls. Exact controls are machine-specific.

  Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  A 75 m pass takes 15 min of actual travel

  A 75 m pass takes 15 min of actual travel. Find average travel speed in m/min; explain why this is not
  a recommended setting.

  Answer guidance.

  75/15 = 5 m/min. It is a recorded average, not a safe or productive setting for every material, depth,
  machine or site.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Follow shut down procedures

Worked example · Service access tomorrow
The nearest parking place is level but blocks an emergency access route. Another approved area provides
stable ground and service access.

Use the approved location that preserves emergency access. Follow the machine shutdown procedure,
secure components and parking controls, inspect safely and hand over the final state. Convenience does
not justify blocking the route.


  Name four items in a useful shutdown handover.

  Name four items in a useful shutdown handover.

  Answer guidance.

  Machine/serial and meter; parking location/state; defects and isolation/out-of-service status;
  responsible person and next required action. Include keys/access and any residual hazards as
  applicable.

  Observe: Writing, Collaboration through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or
  saved work.




  A checklist has ten equally weighted scored items and a learner earns 16/20

  A checklist has ten equally weighted scored items and a learner earns 16/20. A critical parking check
  failed. Is the learner released?

  Answer guidance.

  No. 16/20 is 80%, but every safety-critical item must pass and the unsafe condition must be
  corrected. The numerical threshold cannot compensate for a critical failure.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Transport milling and reclaiming equipment

Worked example · A mass record that excludes attachments
The transport plan lists a base machine mass of 19,800 kg. A fitted conveyor option and attachments add
1,250 kg.

Planned machine load is at least 21,050 kg before any other cargo. Confirm actual configuration,
carrier/ramp/axle ratings and route requirements. The base-machine figure is insufficient for release;
obtain the transport lead’s verified plan.


  A carrier’s fictional available payload is 22,000 kg

  A carrier’s fictional available payload is 22,000 kg. Machine plus attachments is 21,050 kg and
  separate equipment is 1,200 kg. Find the excess.

  Answer guidance.

  Total 22,250 kg, exceeding available payload by 250 kg. Hold and revise the load plan; also check axle
  distribution and all other limits.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.




  List information needed before selecting tie-downs.

  List information needed before selecting tie-downs.

  Answer guidance.

  Actual machine mass/configuration, designated attachment points, restraint arrangement, rated
  components and condition, applicable Standard 10/BC requirements, carrier points and manufacturer
  transport instructions.

  Observe: Reading, Writing through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or saved
  work.
