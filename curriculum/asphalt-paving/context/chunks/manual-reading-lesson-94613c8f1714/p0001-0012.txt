Read the right manual before using the
information
Read the identity. Find the evidence. Explain the limit.




A document has to fit the machine and the question
A manual is more than a collection of instructions. Its cover, publication number, model, serial range, revision and
contents tell you what it can answer. A complete engine manual answers engine questions. A fluids publication
answers a narrower family of questions. A machine manual connects those references to a particular machine.
The same manufacturer can publish all three. The largest file, highest revision number or newest-looking
filename is not automatically the correct choice.

This lesson uses the original manufacturer documents in this folder. Keep each document's title and identifier
beside your notes. Open the complete PDF when reading a section: a search result or extracted paragraph can
leave out a condition on the previous page. PDF viewer page numbers and printed page labels may differ. For
example, the LeeBoy contact-information page is PDF page 25, labelled 2-3 on the page. Record both when
helping another reader find it.

The work takes place with documents and fictional machine records. You will not operate, service or adjust
equipment. A match between a cover and a fictional serial number is a document-selection result. For actual
equipment, the responsible instructor or employer must confirm the machine's full identification, configuration,
current information and the work you are trained and authorized to do.


Prepare your reading space
Use a PDF reader and an approved practice folder, or begin with printed covers and contents pages. Enlarge the
view, use text-to-speech, a line guide or another agreed access method. A paper attempt can show Reading or
Numeracy. For Digital, take your own turn opening a file, finding a passage, saving a note and reopening it.
Record any navigation help separately from help that selects the answer.

Work privately or with a willing partner. Use the fictional IDs below; no actual equipment ownership, employee
details, account credentials or personal information is needed. Keep individual notes even when a pair develops
one useful index. The aim is a clear handover that another person can check.

MAN-01 · Read the identity before the contents

For OEM-01 through OEM-07, record manufacturer, exact title, document number or revision, date, equipment
scope and serial statement. If a cover does not state a serial range, write that it is not stated there. Do not invent
a range from another model. For the LeeBoy file, distinguish the revision suggested by its download filename
from information actually printed on the cover.

Then answer: Which two documents are complete machine manuals for the same model family but different
serial ranges? Which file covers engines rather than the whole machine? Which covers a particular subject
across Cat machines? Point to the words supporting each answer. Skills: Reading, Problem Solving.


MAN-02 · Find it, save the evidence and reopen it

On an actual device, open OEM-01. Locate Contact Information through the contents or search. Confirm the
printed page label and PDF viewer page. Read the paragraph about the machine nameplate and the separate
engine-nameplate reference. Create a note named manual-check-v1-[learning-ID] with the title, pack ID,
page labels and a one-sentence explanation of why machine and engine identification should not be confused.
Save, close and reopen the note from its known location.

See the equipment, then use the trade words
The trade outline asks learners to describe paving equipment in A1, then develops paving-machine components
in I2, screed functions and attachments in J1, and compaction equipment in L1. These four visual practices
introduce those words through actual manufacturer figures. They build document-reading evidence; the later
technical units still need their full instruction and practical assessment. The complete learning-task locations are
listed in the teaching guide.

Use this reading routine: look at the whole view → find a callout → read its name → explain its function →
check the complete page. A callout is the number or letter joined to part of a drawing. A legend connects the
callout to its meaning. Read both; the shape alone can mislead.

Use the trade words. A paver has a tractor and a screed. Here, tractor means the powered machine section
that carries material and pulls the screed. The hopper receives asphalt mixture. Conveyors carry that mixture
rearward; augers distribute it toward the screed. The screed shapes the placed layer and provides initial
compaction. A screed extension changes the working width; an end gate helps form the edge. These are
functions to understand before learning the actual machine's procedures.

Worked example. “Number 10 is a screed extension. I can locate it at the side of the screed. Its role relates to
paving width.” This connects a visible callout, a precise term and a function. “Number 10 is the yellow part” does
not identify a function and will not help someone reading a different view.

Find 7, 10, 11 and 12. Write or dictate one name-and-function sentence for each. Check against the full source
table, then explain one sentence to a partner using the picture. The listener points to the intended callout and
explains what they understood. If pointing is not an accessible action, identify the number and position by words
or an agreed communication method. Skills: Reading, Writing, Communication.

Open the larger figure · Read “Augers” and “Hopper and Conveyors” together

Read in everyday words. The hopper holds the delivered mixture. Conveyors move material towards the rear.
Augers move material across towards the screed. The screed works on the placed layer. Hopper, conveyor,
auger and screed name different components; they are not four words for the same thing.

Make four labelled cards using those terms. Add one short function to each. Arrange the cards to explain the
material route from receipt to the screed, then compare your explanation with the source page. Connect the two
parts that mainly move material with different direction words: rearward and across. A learner's draft says, “The
hopper compacts the finished mat.” Correct that sentence and explain which idea was mixed up.

Use the trade words. Drum and pneumatic (rubber tire) describe different contact types. Vibratory and static
describe different compaction actions: vibratory equipment adds vibration; static compaction uses load without
added vibration. Those words answer different questions. The drawing shows a tandem drum roller; it does not
show a pneumatic roller. The outline also names breakdown, intermediate and finish as phases in a
compaction train. A component picture alone does not select a roller, phase, pass count or machine setting for a
job.

On a device, open the full PDF, find Figure 4 and then page 15. Locate three of the selected callouts and record
the exact names, figure number and both page locations in your saved note. With a partner, swap the finder and
checker roles for another two callouts. Keep each person's contribution.

Now review a fictional draft label: “All compaction rollers are diesel powered, so this diagram shows a diesel
model.” Check the cover and page 12. Revise the label using the named engine's fuel type and the stated model.
Explain why neither a broad category nor a familiar picture establishes the engine specification. Save the
corrected version while keeping the first draft. Skills: Digital when performed, Reading, Collaboration,
Adaptability.

Worked example. Find B on the side view, then its row in the table. The metric value is 1,470 mm, which is
1.470 m because 1,000 mm = 1 m. Keep the letter and unit with the value. Writing only “1,470” loses what was
measured and in which unit. The imperial and metric values are published rounded measurements; do not expect
every pair to give an exact conversion match.

Use the metric column only for these checks:

     1. Find F and G on the upper view. Record both values and test whether 2 × G = F in the published table.
     2. Convert F from millimetres to metres. Explain why 692 m would be an unreasonable transcription of 692
        mm.
     3. Read H and I in the side view/table. Calculate their numerical difference in millimetres. Keep the dimension
        letters in your statement so another reader can check the comparison.
     4. A fictional room plan lists a doorway as 700 mm wide. Calculate the difference between that value and
        dimension F. Explain why this arithmetic alone cannot approve moving a machine through the doorway: the
        route, machine configuration, clearances and safe movement procedure have not been checked.

Show the source row, your calculation, unit and explanation. Ask a partner to trace one answer back to the
diagram rather than only checking the arithmetic. Skills: Numeracy, Reading, Writing, Communication.

MAN-03 · Count pages and compare ranges

Use the two contents pages. OEM-03 lists Maintenance on printed pages 23–32; OEM-04 lists it on 25–34. How
many pages does each listed range include? Show why subtracting the first page from the last page alone
misses one page. The complete PDFs contain 42 and 46 viewer pages respectively; find that difference and
explain why neither count establishes the amount of training required.

Next write the first and last integer serial values in the uncovered interval between OEM-04's stated upper
boundary and OEM-03's stated lower boundary. Count those possible values inclusively. Explain why that count
is not a count of machines manufactured. Skills: Numeracy, Reading.


MAN-04 · Write a verification note that preserves uncertainty

Write a 70–110-word note to the classroom coordinator covering records A, B and C. Include the selected manual
or unresolved status for each, the exact serial evidence, the meaning of the revision dates, and a next
confirmation step. Separate a cover match from authorization to work. Keep the note factual; do not claim that a
document is current for an actual machine when that has not been checked. Use a heading and a clear
requested action. Add this verification note to manual-check-v1-[learning-ID] , keeping the MAN-02
navigation note in the same file; on paper, keep both notes together as version 1. Skills: Writing, Reading.


MAN-05 · Resolve the incomplete match

For record C, compare these proposed responses:

     1. Choose Revision 10 because its number and date are higher.
     2. Choose the nearer serial boundary.
     3. Keep the selection unresolved and ask the responsible instructor or manufacturer support to identify the
        manual from the complete machine record.

State the problem, one consequence of choosing without evidence, two facts already known and two facts still
needed. Select a response with a reason. Draft the information request without sending it. Name the evidence
that would allow a later decision; do not report that confirmation as already received. Skills: Problem Solving,
Writing.

MAN-06 · Give a handover and check what was understood

Explain your decision about record C to a partner in about one minute. The listener restates the model,
unresolved range and next action in their own words. Invite a question. Correct a misunderstanding if one occurs,
or record what was accurately understood. Swap roles using record A. Keep a short note of the actual check-
back rather than writing that communication was successful without evidence. Signing, a communication aid or
another agreed method can carry the same information. Skills: Communication.


MAN-07 · Share the checking work without losing individual evidence

Work in pairs with OEM-01 and OEM-02. For the first document, one learner reads the cover and the other
verifies the contents/page route. Record the title, scope and one useful navigation anchor. Swap the roles for the
second document. Each learner keeps one check they performed and one question or correction they
contributed. Agree how the pair's note may be shared. Judge the accuracy and coordination of the work, not
speed, confidence or who speaks most. Skills: Collaboration, Reading.


Case B · A changed classroom record
The coordinator corrects practice record C's serial number from 105500 to 110500. The model remains
AR13HA. This is a fictional correction to the classroom record, not a new manufacturer publication. The reason
for the original entry error is not supplied. The two manuals and their stated ranges remain unchanged.


MAN-08 · Revise the decision and keep the earlier record

Create manual-check-v2-[learning-ID] , retaining v1. Identify what changed, what stays the same and which
selection is now supported by the cover. State what actual-machine checks still remain. Do not invent a cause for
the entry error. Reopen v2 and verify both the corrected serial and the reasoning; changing only the filename
would leave the wrong decision in place. If working on paper, date and label both versions. Record Digital only
when you performed the file actions. Skills: Adaptability; Digital when performed.


MAN-09 · Make a navigation aid and test it

Develop two possible designs for a one-page manual-finder: for example, an index arranged by equipment family
or a decision path that begins with model and serial scope. Choose one and explain why it would help another
learner. Include pack ID, document type, model/scope, a useful page route and an unresolved-status option. Use
words and visual structure that remain clear without colour. Avoid turning the aid into operating steps or a
replacement for the original manual.

Make a small usable version. Ask a willing reader to use it to find the CAT document that addresses engine
identification, then to explain whether it can supply the complete operating instructions for a LeeBoy paver.
Record the actual choice, explanation and any help. Revise one feature if the evidence suggests a need, or retain
it with a reason. Try a second question: find the Multiquip walk-behind roller contents page. State one limit on
what these two trials demonstrate. Skills: Creativity and Innovation, Communication, Problem Solving.
