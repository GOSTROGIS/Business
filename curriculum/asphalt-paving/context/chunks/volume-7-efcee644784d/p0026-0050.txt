Operate asphalt screed
Screed operation combines careful setup with measured feedback. The learner practises on the selected
machine under supervision, using its OMM for exact control actions and limits.


  Set-up screed

  Maintain proper thickness

  Check depth of mat


LEARNING STEP 1




Set-up screed
Inspect the screed and surroundings, identify the fitted heating system and complete daily maintenance.
Establish starting width, crown/contour, extension alignment and the trial-based loose-thickness setting.
Lower onto the specified starting supports through the safe procedure. Operate heating only as instructed;
protect against burns, fire and electrical hazards. The LeeBoy 8680 example identifies separate
main/extension/end-gate heat controls and warns against overheating. Do not transfer its settings to
another machine.

Check depth of mat
Measure loose depth using the approved probe/tool and safe access method. Identify the base interface
rather than pushing into soft support, record station/side and avoid contact with hot material or moving
equipment. A probe reading is not a core result or proof of final compacted thickness. Compare repeated
readings with the specified target and trial relationship, report departures and retain the record.

Study chapter · Outline K1



Raker roles and responsibilities
The handwork crew shapes and finishes areas the paver cannot complete on its own. A raker needs
judgement about material, grade, joints and timing, as well as awareness of every machine moving nearby.


  Typical raker duties


LEARNING STEP 1




Typical raker duties
Typical duties include preparing small areas, distributing mix around structures, finishing edges, correcting
authorized local irregularities and preparing longitudinal or transverse joints. A lute commonly has a long
handle and a broad head with straight and toothed edges; a rake, shovel, tamper and wheelbarrow serve
different purposes. Inspect handle attachment, head/brace condition and working edges before use.

Work around manholes and other appurtenances to the approved elevation and drainage detail. Remove
excess and square the end of a run through the planned method. Avoid scattering coarse material over a
finished mat or repeatedly disturbing a surface until the mixture cools. A paver or screed problem that
repeats needs communication and correction at its cause, not endless hand repair.

At joints, understand which face is prepared, which treatment is specified, what overlap/allowance is
intended and how compaction will occur. Longitudinal joints run generally along the paving direction;
transverse joints cross it. Their details and rolling sequence depend on the project and mixture. Do not infer
a universal joint allowance from a sketch.

Coordinate with the paver, screed and roller operators before entering an area. Keep tools and handles out
of traffic and machine paths. Preserve accessible routes and protect nearby drains. Careful handwork
serves the finished road and the people using it; communicate a concern early rather than hiding it under a
cosmetic finish.

LEARNING STEP 2




Raker-specific safety hazards
Recognize burns, heat strain, fumes, noise, dust, pressurized systems, blind areas, pinch zones and traffic.
Keep long handles inside the work area. Use manageable loads and a stable stance, move feet to turn
rather than twisting under load, and vary tasks/breaks through the work plan. Do not overreach to save a
step. Wet or unsuitable footwear can worsen hot-material exposure. Report fatigue, dizziness or damaged
equipment early.

LEARNING STEP 3




Traffic control requirements
Coordinate with trained traffic-control personnel and the designated equipment signallers. A raker does not
step into live traffic to retrieve a tool or direct a truck without the assigned role and controls. Obtain
acknowledgement before approaching a machine’s working area. If visibility or communication is lost, stop
the affected activity and move only through the established safe route.

Study chapter · Outline K3



Place asphalt hot mix
Hand placement aims for a consistent layer that can be compacted properly. Work promptly and
deliberately, using the approved preparation, joint and compaction methods for the actual repair or small
area.


  Common hand work crew duties

  Common techniques


LEARNING STEP 1




Common hand work crew duties
Prepare the road edge, driveway, intersection, trench patch or pothole according to the specified repair
detail. Confirm sound boundaries, support, drainage, depth and the required bond treatment. Apply the
specified tack/emulsion to prepared faces under its procedure; an inert simulation may be used in training.
Use shovels to place manageable amounts close to their final position. Coordinate delivery without entering
a truck’s uncontrolled discharge area.

A wheelbarrow can move small quantities where the route, load, footing and heat controls allow. Inspect
wheel, handles and tray, keep the load manageable and use the designated transfer arrangement. Spread
with the suitable toothed or straight lute edge for the material and task. Avoid excessive manipulation that
separates particles or cools the mix. Grade changes need controlled placement to limit uneven compaction,
not an arbitrary heap of loose material.
