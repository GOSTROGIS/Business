Paving screed and attachments
The screed forms the asphalt layer behind the tractor. Its geometry, material supply, temperature and
movement interact. Knowing the parts helps the crew discuss a mat problem without making several
unrelated adjustments at once.


  Screed categories

  Common attachments

  Principal functions


LEARNING STEP 1




Screed categories
A rigid or fixed-width screed uses its assembled width and approved extensions. A variable-width screed
changes working width through its designed extension system. Front- and rear-mounted extensions have
different arrangements and setup needs. Identify the actual main screed, extensions, plates, heating
system, vibration/tamper equipment and control options. Nominal paver size does not establish the fitted
screed’s full capability.

LEARNING STEP 2




Common attachments
Attachments can include bolt-on extensions, hydraulic extensions, end gates, cutoff shoes, curb-forming
equipment and grade/slope controls. A stringline, ski, averaging beam or other reference system supplies
different information; a laser or sonic sensor is not an interchangeable reference for every job. Confirm
compatibility, mounting, calibration and safe movement envelope. Narrowing a pass with a cutoff
attachment affects material distribution and the approved setup.

Screed work safety procedures
Screed work places people close to hot material, moving extensions and the roller path. Safe positions and
clear communication must remain effective as the paving width and site conditions change.


  Interpret job plan

  Machine-specific safety hazards

  Traffic-control requirements

  Proper PPE

  Lockout procedures

  Secure machine for safety


LEARNING STEP 1




Interpret job plan
Review path, pattern, thickness, temperature requirements, crown, joints, obstacles and access. Confirm
the current width and any planned changes before extending equipment. Agree on who controls each side,
how adjustments are communicated and how the paver/roller crews respond to a stop.

LEARNING STEP 2




Machine-specific safety hazards
Identify hot plates and mix, heater energy, fumes, noise, hydraulic pressure, extension pinch zones and
moving machinery. Keep feet and clothing clear of the mat and hot surfaces. Wet or unsuitable footwear
can worsen burn risk. Plan heat exposure, hydration and breaks through the site program. Do not step into
the roller path to inspect the edge; coordinate a safe observation opportunity.

LEARNING STEP 3




Traffic-control requirements
Use the traffic-control plan and assigned trained personnel. The required TCP arrangement depends on the
site assessment, not a fixed number in a classroom example. Keep long tools, cables and extension
movements inside the controlled work area. Reassess when the work passes an entrance or narrows a
public route.

LEARNING STEP 4




                                                                    ACCESS IS4S · Volume 7 · 12 / 55

=== ORIGINAL PDF PAGE 13 ===
Proper PPE
Use the selected footwear, head, eye, hearing, visibility and heat/chemical protection, with training in use
and limitations. Check fit and condition, including wet or contaminated clothing. Follow current Part 8
responsibilities and employer arrangements. PPE cannot protect a person who enters an uncontrolled
crush zone.

LEARNING STEP 5




Lockout procedures
Before service or hazardous access, apply the machine-specific isolation for electrical/heater, fuel where
fitted, hydraulic, gravity and stored energy. Support raised components using specified devices and verify
the safe state. Screed heating may be electric, propane or another system; identify the actual equipment
rather than applying a generic fuel-isolation instruction.

LEARNING STEP 6




Secure machine for safety
At parking or interruption, secure the screed and paver together using the required supports/locks, parking
controls and access protection. Prevent unintended extension movement and protect people from residual
heat. Report defects and confirm the status with the responsible operator.

Asphalt screed operator roles and
responsibilities
The screed operator helps turn the plan into a consistent mat. The role combines observation, measured
checks, timely communication and controlled adjustment.


  Plan work procedures

  Maintain mat thickness

  Communication techniques

  Problem solving strategies

  The importance of teamwork

  Importance of adhering to standards


LEARNING STEP 1




Plan work procedures
Read the accepted thickness, width, crown, joint and end-product requirements before setup. Identify
where measurements will be made, who records them and who authorizes changes. Confirm the drawing,
JMF and quality plan versions. Plan transitions and obstacles before they reach the screed.

LEARNING STEP 2




Maintain mat thickness
Monitor loose mat thickness and width using approved methods and the trial-based relationship to
compacted thickness. Material volume depends on area and thickness; delivery and feed must support the
intended rate. A thick area and thin area may average to the correct tonnage while both miss the geometry.
Keep checks tied to locations.

LEARNING STEP 3




Communication techniques
Communicate with the paver operator and handwork crew using short, specific messages: side, station,
observation, requested action and acknowledgement. Share changes in head of material, speed,
temperature or width. Do not issue several conflicting adjustments through different people.

LEARNING STEP 4

Problem solving strategies
Investigate a symptom before adjusting. Compare current conditions with the stable setup, identify a
plausible cause and make an authorized controlled change. Allow the response to develop over the
machine’s relevant travel distance, then measure again. Document what changed. Repeated large
corrections can produce waves and make the cause harder to identify.

LEARNING STEP 5




The importance of teamwork
The paver operator controls the steady supply/travel process; screed operators control assigned screed
functions; rakers manage authorized handwork; quality personnel provide measurements and disposition.
These roles depend on one another. Respectful challenge is useful when someone sees a discrepancy: ask
for the observation and check it together.

LEARNING STEP 6




Importance of adhering to standards
Meet the specified geometry and quantity without treating either excess or shortage as automatically
acceptable. Record departures and obtain authorized correction. End-product specifications also include
properties beyond screed control, such as density and smoothness. Do not promise full acceptance from a
loose-thickness check alone.

Study chapter · Outline J4



Screed maintenance
Screed maintenance protects plate condition, movement and the quality of the next mat. Use the fitted
screed’s service instructions and coordinate shared systems with the paver operator.


  Service lubrication system (greasing)

  Inspect and clean components as required

  Housekeeping tasks

  Maintain screed plate


LEARNING STEP 1




Service lubrication system (greasing)
Identify lubrication points, including thickness-control screws and extension mechanisms where specified.
Confirm lubricant, interval, quantity and safe position. Clean fittings and load the grease gun correctly.
Centralized systems and optional fittings change the route; do not assume grease at one point reaches
every component. Report damaged fittings and protect the work area from excess lubricant.

LEARNING STEP 2




Inspect and clean components as required
Inspect accessible components and the paver systems serving the screed, including hydraulic lines,
electrical/heating connections and cooling systems where applicable. Radiator, oil-cooler, battery, plug and
dipstick checks may belong to the shared tractor maintenance schedule rather than a separate screed
engine. Identify what is actually fitted. Use isolation, cooling and approved access; do not open hot
pressure systems or diagnose electrical faults beyond authorization.

LEARNING STEP 3




Housekeeping tasks
Remove rubbish, buildup and excess grease from safe access surfaces, controls, steps, decks and any fitted
cab/windows. Use compatible products and contain waste. Coordinate cleaning with the paver crew so no
one restarts or moves a shared system unexpectedly. Restore guards and account for tools.

LEARNING STEP 4




Maintain screed plate
Inspect the screed plate and extension plates for wear, damage, distortion and buildup. Check adjustment
and replacement criteria in the actual manual. A scuff or streak in the mat can indicate a
plate/setup/material problem; record the pattern and investigate rather than assuming more heat will fix it.
Plate replacement and alignment require authorized procedures, support and specified fasteners/settings.
Record the condition before and after service.

  Worked example · A recurring streak
  A streak returns along the same line after surface buildup is removed.

  Think it through.

  Record its position and persistence. Have authorized personnel inspect plate wear/flatness,
  extension alignment, heating and relevant setup under safe conditions. Do not continue scraping a
  moving or hot hazard area or guess a replacement tolerance.
