Use this volume
This volume provides complete classroom readings, worked examples, named practice tasks and answer
guidance for compaction and final handover. Follow the learning route in each chapter, study the labelled
figures and explain your reasoning. The separate answer book contains the worked keys and observation
records.

Care for people, place and the next person using the work runs through the cases. Local First Nations, Métis
and Inuit learners and partners bring distinct knowledge and priorities. Participation does not require
sharing personal stories or cultural teachings, and no diagram claims to represent a Nation’s protocol.


    Classroom authoring is complete for the listed competencies. Qualified technical review, actual
    fleet/site matching, a local teaching pilot and observed practical qualification are separate
    requirements. The practical records are ready for an instructor to configure and use; they do not certify
    unobserved work.

SCREED

Compaction equipment

Worked example · Choose by the task
A narrow access area has restricted turning space, while the main road has a high production rate.

Compare suitable machine dimensions, modes, capacity and support for each area, then coordinate the
planned roles and testing. Do not select only by the largest operating mass or assume one roller can
maintain both areas without a scheduling conflict.


  Explain and apply the idea

  Identify drum, scraper, spray bar, articulation joint, ROPS and seat belt on the supplied model figures.

  Answer guidance.

  Use the actual visible labels and explain: drum contacts/compacts; scraper manages buildup; spray
  system wets as designed; articulation steers; ROPS/seat belt form the fitted protective arrangement.
  Stay clear of articulation hazards.

  Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  A fictional effective coverage width is 1.4 m over a 70 m strip

  A fictional effective coverage width is 1.4 m over a 70 m strip. Find area for one full-length coverage
  strip.

  Answer guidance.

  1.4 × 70 = 98 m². Actual overlap, pass definitions and rolling pattern must be specified; this is
  geometry, not a density result.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Compaction work safety procedures

Worked example · A shortcut behind the roller
A worker steps behind a roller to shorten the route while the operator is preparing to reverse.

Stop the affected movement and re-establish the controlled pedestrian route and communication. Review
the blind-area conflict with the crew. High-visibility clothing does not authorize crossing an active reversing
path.


  Explain why “three metres is always safe” is an inadequate rule.

  Explain why “three metres is always safe” is an inadequate rule.

  Answer guidance.

  Safe separation depends on machine size, movement, visibility, stopping distance, ground conditions
  and the site plan. Use the actual assessment and instructions; a generic number can be unsafe.

  Observe: Problem solving, Reading through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  A route change reduces available clearance by 0.6 m

  A route change reduces available clearance by 0.6 m. What should be recorded?

  Answer guidance.

  Original and revised geometry, machine/movement envelope, affected people/routes, assessment and
  authorized decision. The subtraction alone cannot establish safe clearance.

  Observe: Numeracy, Writing through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or
  saved work.

Asphalt Compaction Roller operator roles
and responsibilities

Worked example · Pass count without coverage
Two operators each report six passes, but their paths overlap on one side and leave a strip untreated.

Reconstruct the actual path and coverage map, identify the missed strip and obtain an authorized response
while conditions allow. Define pass/coverage consistently for the next run. Twelve reported movements do
not establish uniform compaction.


  Draw a conceptual coverage map with three labelled strips and a legend for
  complete/needs check.

  Draw a conceptual coverage map with three labelled strips and a legend for complete/needs check.

  Answer guidance.

  Use patterns or labels as well as colour, identify direction and boundaries, and record the actual
  approved sequence separately. Do not present the sketch as a universal rolling pattern.

  Observe: Writing, Creativity and innovation through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.




  A training record has four strips, each requiring two planned coverages

  A training record has four strips, each requiring two planned coverages. How many strip-coverages are
  planned?

  Answer guidance.

  4 × 2 = 8 strip-coverages. This exercise count is not a recommended field pass requirement or proof of
  density.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Compaction Roller maintenance

Worked example · A dry stripe on the drum
A drum shows a persistent dry stripe and material begins to pick up in that area.

Report the pattern, stop/hold as required and inspect the spray/scraper system through the safe
procedure. A blocked nozzle or another fault is a possibility, not a confirmed diagnosis. Correct and verify
the authorized repair before continuing.


  A fictional 120 L tank contains 35 L

  A fictional 120 L tank contains 35 L. How much water reaches the stated 120 L level?

  Answer guidance.

  85 L, assuming those stated values and no other limits. Use the actual machine filling instructions and
  do not infer a recommended tank capacity.

  Observe: Numeracy, Reading through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or
  saved work.




  List four water-system items for a model-specific winterization record.

  List four water-system items for a model-specific winterization record.

  Answer guidance.

  Tank, pump, filters/lines and spray bars/nozzles as fitted, with each actual drain/protection step and
  verification. Use the complete OMM procedure.

  Observe: Writing, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.

Operate Compaction Roller

Worked example · Density feedback changes the plan
A trial strip’s recorded density increases over the first approved passes, then stops improving while the
mixture cools.

Report the measured results with temperature, location and reference. The quality/crew lead evaluates
whether settings, timing, equipment or another factor needs adjustment. More passes are not
automatically effective and may damage the mat. Preserve the trial record and authorized revised pattern.


  In-place density is 2.310 Mg/m³ and the required maximum-theoretical reference is 2.500
  Mg/m³

  In-place density is 2.310 Mg/m³ and the required maximum-theoretical reference is 2.500 Mg/m³.
  Calculate percent.

  Answer guidance.

  2.310/2.500 × 100 = 92.4%. Interpretation requires the applicable method, lot and contract limits; it
  is not a standalone acceptance decision.

  Observe: Numeracy, Reading through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation or
  saved work.




  An infrared reading and probe reading differ

  An infrared reading and probe reading differ. What should be checked before declaring either wrong?

  Answer guidance.

  Instrument identity/calibration, method, location, timing, surface versus internal measurement, units
  and specified procedure. Record both and seek the responsible person’s interpretation.

  Observe: Problem solving, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Follow shut down procedures

Worked example · Ready for the next shift
A roller is clean and fuelled but parked where a delivery truck must reverse around it beside a public
entrance.

Have the responsible lead arrange a safer approved staging location. Complete the movement and
shutdown under the actual procedure, preserve access and communicate the final location/status. “Ready”
includes safe placement, not only clean equipment.


  State the difference between staging and transport securement.

  State the difference between staging and transport securement.

  Answer guidance.

  Staging positions equipment safely for site access and the next task. Transport securement restrains
  the configured load on a carrier under manufacturer and applicable transport rules. Both require
  planned parking/movement controls, but their arrangements differ.

  Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  A ten-item checklist has 20 possible points

  A ten-item checklist has 20 possible points. A learner earns 18 but fails a critical roll-away control. Is
  the outcome satisfactory?

  Answer guidance.

  90% numerically, but not satisfactory for release. Correct and reassess the critical control; the 80%
  outline threshold does not permit an unsafe final state.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Read the rolling record and its limits

    This is a complete fictional classroom case. It does not describe an actual community, Nation,
    employer or project. Learners may use the supplied case without disclosing personal or cultural
    knowledge.


A fictional density record gives in-place 2.325 Mg/m³ and maximum theoretical 2.500 Mg/m³. A coverage
sketch marks strips A and B complete and C awaiting a check. A draft says “93% means the whole lot is
accepted” and “six passes always work.” No contract limit, sub-lot results or approved fixed pass count is
supplied.




  Reading · Check the facts
  Identify the controlling facts, missing information and one unsupported statement in the brief.

  Evidence and feedback: Use the exact case facts; distinguish observation, assumption and missing
  authority.




  Writing · Make the handover useful
  Draft a concise handover with location, quantities, status and the next responsible action.

  Evidence and feedback: The note preserves verified facts, units and unresolved holds without
  inventing a release.




  Numeracy · Calculate and check
  Show the case calculation with units and an independent reasonableness check.

  Evidence and feedback: Use the worked numerical key below; show the relationship and its practical
  limits.

Digital · Direct AI and verify the files
Use an available AI assistant to draft a findings table and editable record from this brief. Request
DOCX or a structured document, XLSX with formulas where useful, and a PDF/HTML reading copy.
Audit every claim and reopen the actual outputs.

Evidence and feedback: Record the product/capabilities, prompt, source facts, saved files and
checks. A chat response alone is not a created or verified file. Check formulas and preserve originals.




Problem solving · Investigate the discrepancy
Explain the discrepancy, what evidence is needed and who should authorize the next step.

Evidence and feedback: Separate the observed issue from a guessed cause; preserve evidence and
identify the correct decision role.




Communication · Send and check the message
Brief a partner, ask for a check-back and repair one misunderstanding if it occurs.

Evidence and feedback: Observe an actual exchange and confirmation of the essential facts; do not
infer communication from a shared file.




Collaboration · Review together
Agree reviewer/author roles, complete a review and record each person’s contribution.

Evidence and feedback: Both learners take an identifiable turn; feedback is respectful and tied to the
case evidence.

   Change card · Use after the first attempt

Verified case key
Percent density = 93.0% using the stated maximum-theoretical reference. It does not establish whole-lot

Change-response key

contribution and support. Use Not observed, Not yet demonstrated, Demonstrated with support, or
