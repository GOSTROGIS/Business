ACCESS · INDIGENOUS SKILLS 4 SUCCESS



      Know the tools. Prepare the work.
      Understand the tool. Read the requirement. Prepare the work.




  Asphalt work depends on small tools as well as large machines. A sound lute, a readable sling tag and the correct
  temperature measurement each support the crew's work. This guide connects the trade terms to real manufacturer
  information and the requirements that govern work in British Columbia.

  Start with the picture, say the part name, then locate it in the actual document. Work with a partner to explain what
  the tool does and what must be checked. Nobody needs to share personal or cultural knowledge to participate. Any
  local language, place knowledge or community protocol belongs under the direction of the people who choose to
  contribute it.

  Figure 1. ACCESS IS4S (2026). Handwork tool anatomy. Original schematic informed by Seymour Midwest's Light
  Lute/Lute Assembly Instructions, pp. 1–2, and Partner Resource Guide, source PDF pp. 18–47, 94–96 and 156–

  Lute. Locate the head, handle, grip, brace and fasteners. The outline names both toothed and straight lute edges.
  The manufacturer's assembly sheet identifies different head widths, handle sizes and brace arrangements; those
  parts are not automatically interchangeable. Use the actual parts list when matching a replacement.

  Asphalt rake. Locate the handle, head and teeth. Distinguish the rake from a concrete placer, scraper and seal-
  coating squeegee. Similar-looking tools can have different jobs. The actual catalogue shows the available head
  shapes and applications.

  Shovel. Locate the grip, shaft/socket and blade. Select the blade and handle for the material and task, then plan
  manageable loads, stable footing and movement with the crew. CCOHS's shovelling examples support ergonomic
  discussion; they are not a maximum load that every learner should lift.

  Wheelbarrow. Locate the tray, handles, frame, legs, axle and wheel. The catalogue identifies different tray
  capacities and wheel arrangements. Plan a stable route and controlled loading. A tray's volume does not establish
  a safe load for a particular person, slope or surface.

4 · Torch, propane and hot work
  The Tiger Torch 95-B/445 sheet and BC Forest Safety Council crew talk provide actual instructions and safety
  context. Read them with the CCOHS propane profile, the actual supplier SDS, applicable workplace fire precautions
  and the employer's hot-work arrangements.

  Identify the torch, valve, hose, regulator, cylinder and connection types using the actual equipment. The outline's
  phrase “Tiger torch” does not establish that any regulator, pressure, hose or fitting is interchangeable. Obtain the
  regulator and hose instructions for the selected assembly and confirm its condition and suitability before practical
  use.

  Use the illustrated source sheet for terminology and model identification. Any practical lighting, shutdown, leak
  response or storage instruction must match the actual equipment, qualified instruction and current site procedure.
  Keep torch work away from the classroom identification station.

Temperature. The ThermoWorks manual and field-of-view diagram distinguish the infrared measurement and
  supported probe functions. An infrared surface reading is not automatically the internal temperature required by a
  mix or test specification. Record the measurement method and location as well as the number and unit.

  Density. The Troxler 3430 manual identifies actual gauge components and procedures for that model. The CNSC
  portable-gauge article, complete online guide and two-page field guide add Canadian licensing, radiation
  protection, security, transport and document context. The provider must establish its licence arrangements and
  authorized people before handling a nuclear gauge. Use diagrams, records or an approved inert training device for
  classroom identification.

  Mat depth and sampling. J5 names a poker for depth checks; E2/E3/E4 require sampling and density evidence.
  The actual contract/test method must define the location, timing, equipment, handling, identification and
  acceptance criteria. Collect those procedures and the coring/sampling equipment manuals when the equipment is
  selected.

7 · Practise all nine Skills for Success
  Use a fictional community access-road work order. Learners help the crew prepare a safe, clear tool station before
  simulated work. Keep the context practical and respectful: care for one another, clear explanations and
  responsibility for the place where the work happens.


      Skill                                    Evidence learners can produce


      Reading                                  Locate an actual tool/model and the applicable requirement; retain its conditions.


      Writing                                  Complete a short identification/condition record with a precise uncertainty or missing
                                               item.


      Numeracy                                 Compare listed tool dimensions or read the correct measuring scale and unit. No
                                               invented safe working load calculation.


      Digital                                  Open the local source, find its original page and record the model/edition.


      Problem solving                          Choose what evidence is needed when a label, instrument method or specification is
                                               unclear.


      Communication                            Explain a tool's purpose and ask a clear question; use teach-back to check
                                               understanding.


      Collaboration                            Agree who identifies, checks and records; invite a partner to verify the evidence.


      Adaptability                             Revise the plan when the route, weather, equipment or task changes.


      Creativity and                           Design a clearer tool-board label or source card while keeping official safety symbols
      innovation                               unchanged.


  Keep classroom identification, technical instruction, supervised practice and authorized assessment as distinct
  records. The delivery gap register gives the exact next acquisitions and decisions.
