Signals/communication
Use agreed, deliberate signals that the intended driver can see through the appropriate mirror or
communication system. Obtain acknowledgement. A horn’s meaning must be agreed, not guessed. One
designated person directs the movement; anyone can call an emergency stop. Lost or conflicting signals
mean stop.

LEARNING STEP 8




Obstacles and overhead hazards
Scan the route and overhead envelope for utilities, structures, branches, attachments, other machines and
people. Account for the truck body, hopper wings and screed extensions as they move. Electrical approach
limits depend on the actual system and required controls; do not substitute a guessed distance. Escalate an
unresolved obstacle before entering its zone.

Paving machine maintenance
A clean, correctly serviced paver feeds material consistently and gives the crew safe access. Daily
maintenance follows the selected machine’s schedule and safe conditions, with defects recorded for the
next shift.


  Service lubrication system (greasing)

  Service air intake system as required (air filters/pre cleaners)

  Drain fuel tank sump, water separator fuel filters if equipped

  Add fluids as required

  Inspect and clean components as required

  Housekeeping tasks


LEARNING STEP 1




Service lubrication system (greasing)
Locate lubrication points on the actual chart, including fitted centralized systems. Confirm grease type,
interval, quantity and safe position. Clean fittings and use a properly loaded grease gun. The LeeBoy 8680
example includes optional systems that combine several points; do not assume every machine has them.
Record service and report damaged or blocked fittings instead of forcing lubricant through an unknown
obstruction.

does not authorize opening high-pressure fuel components. Repeated contamination requires
investigation.

Add fluids as required
Match fluid to the labelled system and specification. Check ground level, temperature and stopped/running
condition before interpreting the dipstick or sight gauge. Use the correct side of any dual-scale dipstick.
Clean fill points, avoid cross-contamination and overfilling, and record unusual consumption. Coolant,
hydraulic fluid, engine oil and release agent are not interchangeable.

LEARNING STEP 5




Inspect and clean components as required
Inspect radiator/oil cooler, accessible plugs, dipsticks, battery condition and hoses using safe access and
isolation. Protect cooling fins and electrical parts during cleaning. Never open a hot pressurized system or
touch a suspected hydraulic leak. Restore guards and refer repairs to authorized personnel.

LEARNING STEP 6




Housekeeping tasks
Clean hopper, conveyors, augers, screed decks, extensions, steps and commonly missed ledges using the
approved procedure. Coordinate with the screed crew, control heat and ignition sources, and prevent
contact with moving systems. Use a compatible release/cleaning product with its SDS and containment
requirements. “Biodegradable” does not mean harmless or permitted to drain onto the ground. Collect
waste, account for tools and document the final condition.

LEARNING STEP 2




Interpret stakes
Identify station, side, offset, reference mark, cut/fill, slope and target work. Preserve property pins,
benchmarks and protected monuments. Distinguish an assumed local datum from geodetic control. Cross-
heads and offset stakes transfer information away from the work area; they are not automatically the
paving edge. If a mark is moved or conflicts with the current drawing, stop using it and request verification.

LEARNING STEP 3




Fundamentals of paver operation
Follow a verified line with a stable sighting reference and smooth steering. Look sufficiently ahead while
maintaining awareness of trucks, crew and obstacles. Guide trucks into the planned straight approach
rather than forcing the paver out of alignment to receive them. Experience improves precision through
feedback, not by ignoring measured checks. Coordinate alignment with the screed operator’s width and
grade controls.

Operate asphalt paver
Paver operation is learned through observed, supervised control of the actual machine. In-school practical
work in the outline is on flat surfaces; workplace performance adds the approved grades, slopes and
conditions under competent supervision.


  Standard moves

  Maneuvering the paver

  Simulate paving laying down a mat using 5mm crush and sand mixture or similar compact quality

LEARNING STEP 1




Standard moves
Complete pre-start checks and daily maintenance, identify controls and warnings, and enter using the
designated access and three-point contact. Follow the OMM start and travel sequence. Practise forward–
stop–reverse–stop on the cleared flat training area, checking surroundings before each movement. Apply
the specified parking and transmission controls. Transmission neutral or a control lock is not a substitute
for maintenance lockout.

Study chapter · Outline I8



Follow shut down procedures
A controlled shutdown preserves the paver’s condition and leaves a clear handover. The process includes
parking, heat and energy controls, inspection and security—not simply switching off the engine.


  Shut-down procedures


LEARNING STEP 1




Shut-down procedures
Select the designated stable parking location with service access and clear public/emergency routes.
Consider the possibility that the machine will not restart. Use level ground where required for the
procedure and fluid checks. Coordinate completion of material handling and screed work before the final
stop.

Follow the matching OMM sequence for stopping working functions, positioning the screed and
attachments, parking controls, required cooling and engine shutdown. Do not transfer a cooldown time or
control sequence from a different model. Use specified supports, locks and chocks. A normal shutdown
does not by itself authorize maintenance access: apply the full lockout procedure when the next task
requires it.

Inspect from safe access areas for leaks, damage, buildup and missing guards. Keep away from hot or
pressurized systems and moving/unsupported parts. Record machine ID, meter, parking location, defects
and required service. Secure keys, panels and detachable items, then communicate the final status to the
responsible person and next shift.

The practical checklist uses the actual machine procedure. Safety-critical parking, support and energy-
control items must all pass. The outline’s 80% checklist threshold cannot compensate for an unsafe final
state. Repeat the required practice after feedback and record the new attempt separately.
