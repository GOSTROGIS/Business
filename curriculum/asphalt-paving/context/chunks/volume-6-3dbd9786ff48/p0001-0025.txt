Keep the term with its meaning
AGGREGATE
Mineral particles forming the solid skeleton of the mixture or granular layer.

BINDER
The asphalt material that coats and binds the aggregate in the specified mixture.

COMPACTED THICKNESS
Layer thickness after the required compaction process; distinct from loose depth behind the
screed.

Asphalt placement process
Asphalt placement links surface preparation, delivery, paver setup, screed control and rolling. A consistent
process helps produce the intended thickness, bond and surface while keeping the whole crew informed.


  Place prime and tack coats

  Where to position the Paver

  Ready the Paving Machine

  Lower Screed on to blocks

  Place Haul Truck Loads in Hopper

  Place the asphalt product

  Compact asphalt


LEARNING STEP 1




Place prime and tack coats
Prime and tack have different purposes. A prime treatment may prepare an approved absorbent granular
surface; tack promotes bonding between specified pavement surfaces or lifts. Follow the actual product,
surface preparation, application rate, curing/breaking and traffic restrictions in the project procedure.
Inspect the label and SDS and protect drainage and public routes. Do not substitute one treatment for the
other or pave over an unready surface because a truck has arrived.

LEARNING STEP 2




Where to position the Paver
Position the paver within the planned asphalt train: delivery, placement, handwork and
breakdown/intermediate/finish rolling. Confirm line, width, starting joint, access and truck approach.
Establish safe separation and communication using the site plan, machine instructions and risk
assessment. A diagram of the train explains roles, not universal spacing.

LEARNING STEP 3




Ready the Paving Machine
Complete inspection, fluid checks, guards, controls, alarms and the required warm-up. Select the
machine’s specified operating mode and engine conditions; “full power” is not a universal instruction for
every function. Check the fitted feed and grade-control systems and agree who controls each adjustment.
Resolve faults before production.

Lower Screed on to blocks
Set the screed on the specified starting support/block arrangement and establish initial thickness, crown
and extension alignment. Loose thickness must allow for the actual mixture’s compaction behaviour and
project trial results. A fixed 20% allowance is not universal. Keep hands and feet out of the lowering zone;
move or retrieve blocks only through the approved safe sequence.

LEARNING STEP 5




Place Haul Truck Loads in Hopper
Coordinate the haul truck through the designated signal system. Confirm alignment, clear the pinch zone
and control the transfer without striking or disturbing the paver. Hopper-wing operation and load level
follow the machine procedure and paving plan. Avoid uncontrolled emptying or repeated material surges.
Watch overhead hazards when a truck body rises.

Place the asphalt product
Maintain appropriate paving speed and a consistent head of material at the screed. Conveyors carry mix
rearward; augers distribute it across the width. The floating screed responds to several interacting factors,
so abrupt changes can affect thickness and texture. Plan longitudinal/transverse joints, overlap and tie-ins
using the accepted procedure. Record emerging defects and communicate before repeated adjustments.

LEARNING STEP 7




Compact asphalt
Rolling follows the approved pattern while the mixture is within its effective compaction conditions.
Breakdown establishes early compaction; intermediate rolling develops density as required; finish rolling
removes marks and completes the surface. Temperature, mixture, support, roller type and weather matter.
The paver and roller crews coordinate supply and pace; speed alone does not establish production quality.

Paving machine equipment and attachments
A paver consists of a tractor that receives and moves material and a screed that forms the mat. Equipment
choice depends on the work, access, production and required result—not only the nominal paving width.


  Types of paving machines

  Major components

  Attachments


LEARNING STEP 1




Types of paving machines
Pavers range from compact pathway or shoulder machines to highway-class equipment. Common nominal
categories include 8 ft and 10 ft machines, but actual minimum/maximum widths, extensions and transport
dimensions depend on the model. Compare work width, production, turning space, support, transport and
fitted options. Light, medium and highway duty describe broad applications rather than interchangeable
operating limits. Match the OMM to model, serial range and configuration.

Major components
Identify the tractor’s hopper and wings, push rollers/truck interface, slat conveyors or other fitted feed
system, augers, engine, hydraulics, undercarriage, operator station and grade/slope controls. Wheeled,
rubber-tracked and steel-tracked arrangements differ. Identify the screed’s tow arms/tow points, main
plate, extensions, end gates, heating and vibration/tamper equipment where fitted. Conveyors move
material toward the screed; augers spread it; the screed forms the layer. Do not confuse a feed sensor with
a grade sensor.

LEARNING STEP 3




Attachments
A pickup machine can collect a windrow placed by an appropriate delivery system and feed the paver. A
material-transfer vehicle or hopper insert may buffer supply or remix material, depending on design. These
can reduce certain handling problems but do not guarantee freedom from segregation or a universal
productivity increase. Belly-dump and end-dump arrangements require different work plans. Check
compatibility, added weight, clearance and operating instructions for every attachment.

Paver work safety procedures
Safe paver work depends on a shared plan and controlled movement around hot material and moving
machinery. The ground crew, truck driver and screed crew need the same understanding of the work
boundary.


  Interpret job plan

  Awareness of machine-specific safety

  Traffic control requirements

  Use proper PPE

  Lockout procedures

  Secure machine for safety


LEARNING STEP 1




Interpret job plan
Brief the path, paving pattern, thickness, crown, joints, obstacles, utilities and public access. Identify the
approved drawing and changes. Establish who directs truck movement, who controls the screed and how
an emergency stop is communicated. Protect entrances and services according to the actual access plan.

LEARNING STEP 2




Awareness of machine-specific safety
Recognize hopper-wing and truck-interface pinch points, conveyor/auger entanglement, hot mix and
heating systems, fumes, noise, hydraulic pressure, blind areas and moving rollers. Never enter a hopper or
reach into a feed system because motion has paused. Keep clear of the machine’s swept path and raised
components. Use specified blocking/support before work requiring access.

LEARNING STEP 3




Traffic control requirements
Use the traffic-control plan for signs, devices, trained personnel and communication. Public traffic control
and guiding a delivery truck are distinct roles. Reassess as the paver advances and access points change.
Stop affected movements when the route or signals become unclear; do not assume high-visibility clothing
makes a worker visible to every operator.

LEARNING STEP 4




                                                                    ACCESS IS4S · Volume 6 · 17 / 58

=== ORIGINAL PDF PAGE 18 ===
Use proper PPE
Use the required footwear, head, eye, hearing, visibility and heat/chemical protection selected for the task.
Check condition and fit. Respiratory protection needs the applicable program and controls. The employer
ensures required PPE is used and maintained; worker/employer supply responsibilities follow current Part
8 and any agreements. PPE does not remove a crush or entanglement hazard.

LEARNING STEP 5




Lockout procedures
Apply the machine-specific lockout procedure for service, clearing or hazardous access. Address
engine/electrical, hydraulic, gravity, heat and stored energy. Secure and verify the safe state before work. A
night switch, stopped engine or emergency-stop button alone is not proof of complete isolation.
Reinstatement follows the authorized procedure with people clear and guards restored.

LEARNING STEP 6




Secure machine for safety
Park at the designated stable location, position and support the screed/attachments as instructed, apply
parking controls and required chocks, and secure access. Record defects and communicate out-of-service
status. Protect people who may approach after the crew leaves.

Complete prechecks, appropriate warm-up and screed preparation. Heating prevents material sticking to
cold plates; overheating can damage the screed. Follow the fitted heating system’s procedure and verify
ready status. Set starting supports and thickness through the agreed screed setup; never use an assumed
generic block size.

LEARNING STEP 3




Follow the line
Follow the authorized reference, which may be stringline, a suitable edge, laser or other grade/alignment
system. Look ahead and make smooth steering changes. Check that the reference remains valid and
protected. Automatic controls can follow an incorrect or disturbed reference faithfully, so independent
checks remain necessary.

The transition process
Coordinate truck changes before the hopper starves or receives a sudden surge. The delivery rate, available
buffer, paver speed and planned stopping method must work together. Communicate delays early. Follow
the machine’s hopper-wing and feed procedure rather than repeatedly cycling wings as a universal habit.

LEARNING STEP 5




Maintain consistent “head”
A consistent head of material in front of the screed supports stable operation. It is the controlled
quantity/distribution at the screed, not simply a completely full hopper. Monitor feed sensors, conveyors
and augers as fitted. Changes in head, speed and mixture conditions can alter the mat. Coordinate
corrective action with the screed operator.

LEARNING STEP 6




Positioning of haul trucks
Guide haul trucks into the approved alignment using the designated signalling arrangement. Keep people
clear of the truck/paver interface and verify clearance before unloading. Avoid impacts that disturb the
paver. Raised bodies change overhead clearance and stability considerations; the driver follows the vehicle
procedure and site plan.
