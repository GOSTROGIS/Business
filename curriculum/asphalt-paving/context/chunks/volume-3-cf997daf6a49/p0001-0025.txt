Study chapter · Outline C1



Crushing process and paving aggregate
production
Aggregate production changes a natural or reclaimed feed into identified products. Each stage controls a
different feature: crushing changes particle size and shape; screening separates sizes; washing removes
specified unwanted fines; handling preserves the product. A plant diagram is useful because a defect
observed at the stockpile may have begun several stages earlier.

The paving crew normally receives a finished product, but understanding its route helps them ask precise
questions about segregation, contamination and supply. The material record should preserve source,
product, test identity and destination.


  Gravel pits, quarries

  Crushing equipment

  The screen

  Conveyors

  Feeders

  Explain stockpiling and handling

  Washing procedures

  Types of aggregate transportation


LEARNING STEP 1




Gravel pits, quarries
A gravel pit extracts deposits that can contain varying proportions of gravel, sand and fines. A quarry
extracts rock that commonly needs breaking and crushing. Deposits vary within a site, so one attractive
sample does not establish an entire source’s suitability. Source approval, extraction permission,
environmental controls and safe access are separate requirements. Dredged or recovered material likewise
needs its authorized source and product assessment.

LEARNING STEP 2




Crushing equipment
A jaw crusher reduces feed between crushing surfaces using compression. A cone crusher reduces
material between a moving and a fixed crushing surface. An impact crusher uses impact to break material.
Feed properties, desired product and the designed circuit influence selection and result. A learner
identifies function and flow, not an operating gap or speed from a generic sketch. Wear and inappropriate
feed can change shape, size distribution and equipment demand.

LEARNING STEP 3




The screen
A screen separates material through openings of stated sizes. Material that passes an opening follows one
route; retained material follows another, which may return to crushing. A torn, blinded or incorrectly
configured screen can change the product even when the crusher is operating normally. Read the
screen/product relationship and the inspection/test evidence together. A screen opening description alone
is not the full aggregate specification.

Conveyors
A conveyor carries material between stages or to storage. Fixed, transfer and stockpiling arrangements
have different purposes. Belt, idlers, pulleys, drive, guards and discharge point are parts to identify.
Transfer height, direction and handling can affect segregation and dust. The carrying route must be
distinguished from a safe pedestrian route; a stopped belt is not an authorized crossing or maintenance
condition.

LEARNING STEP 5




Feeders
A feeder controls how material enters a process. Its job is more than moving the largest possible amount: a
consistent, suitable feed helps the downstream circuit operate as intended. Irregular feed, bridging or
incompatible material can disturb processing. Changes are handled through the plant’s procedure; workers
do not enter a hopper or reach into a feeder to correct the flow.

LEARNING STEP 6




Explain stockpiling and handling
Stockpiles keep identified products separate before use. Place and recover material using the approved
handling arrangement to limit intermingling, segregation and contamination from the ground or adjacent
products. Large particles can roll farther during pile formation, creating a different gradation at different
locations. Maintain signs and records so a loader does not supply the wrong pile. Moisture and recovered-
material variability also matter to later plant control.

LEARNING STEP 7




Washing procedures
Washing separates unwanted fine material through a designed water-and-material process. It can improve
cleanliness for the intended product, but also creates wash water and collected fines that need permitted
management. Washing does not automatically make a product suitable for every use. Check the specified
test results and the site’s water/settling or recycling arrangement; uncontrolled discharge is not part of a
legitimate material-improvement process.

LEARNING STEP 8




Types of aggregate transportation
Trucks, rail and barges can move aggregate between source, storage and plant. The selected transport
method affects logistics and handling points, not the required product identity. Loads must remain
traceable and protected against unacceptable contamination or segregation. Receiving checks compare the
supplier, product, quantity and relevant test record with the order. A change of transport route or
transloading point may introduce a new handling risk that belongs in the supply plan.

Types and specifications of aggregates
An aggregate name is the beginning of a specification, not its complete meaning. The useful questions are:
What particles are present? How do they interact? How do water and loading affect them? Which measured
properties must the product meet? Gradation is the distribution of particle sizes; the properties of the
blend are different from the properties of a single stone.


  Aggregate processing

  Characteristics

  Common products


LEARNING STEP 1




Aggregate processing
Crushing, screening and washing perform different jobs. Crushing can alter size and particle shape;
screening separates sizes; washing removes specified fine material. A product may pass through more than
one stage. The process name does not prove the final result: test the identified product using the required
method and compare it with the applicable specification. A change in source, wear or handling can affect
results even when the nominal process remains the same.

LEARNING STEP 2




Characteristics
Permeability describes the ability of a material arrangement to transmit water. Load-bearing response
concerns how the structure supports loading. Resistance to shearing depends on particle interaction and
other conditions. Particle shape, texture, grading, fines and moisture can influence these behaviours. An
open-looking product is not automatically a designed drainage layer; the complete gradation,
contamination and drainage arrangement matter.

Liquid limit and plastic limit characterize consistency of the applicable fine-grained material under
specified tests. The plasticity index is liquid limit minus plastic limit. These are not moisture limits for
every whole aggregate or a visual test of a pile. Gradation is normally recorded as cumulative percent
passing identified sieves. Keep sieve size, units, test method and product/sample identity with the result.

LEARNING STEP 3




Common products
Pit run describes material taken from a deposit with limited processing; its properties can vary. Screened
road base, including a nominal '3-inch minus' description, needs the specified grading and quality limits.
Bedding sand is selected for the relevant pipe or bedding design. Crushed road mulch is a

Drain rock serves an intended drainage use with specified grading/cleanliness. Chips and driveway chips

Study chapter · Outline C3



Asphalt plant operation
Both drum and batch plants turn specified inputs into a controlled mixture, but they organize the flow
differently. Learn the path of aggregate, binder, recovered material where permitted, collected fines and
finished mixture. A material-flow drawing is not a pipe-and-instrumentation diagram: it explains
relationships without supplying the plant’s operating settings.


  The operation of a drum plant

  The operation of a batch plant


LEARNING STEP 1




The operation of a drum plant
In a continuous drum-mix arrangement, cold-feed bins supply identified aggregate fractions. Feeders and
conveyors proportion and carry material through the designed drying/mixing system. Binder and other
permitted inputs are introduced through controlled systems. Drum layouts differ, including where drying
and mixing occur; do not infer one universal arrangement from the word 'drum'. Moisture in the aggregate
affects drying demand and production control.

Finished mixture moves to the designed storage/surge system and loadout. A storage silo provides a
controlled material-handling stage; it does not remove the need to preserve mix identity, temperature
conditions and acceptable storage duration. The load record connects the product and time to the truck.
Dust collection, burner performance and recovered-fines control interact with production and
environmental requirements. A change in one feed must be managed through the approved mixture/plant
process.

LEARNING STEP 2




The operation of a batch plant
In a conventional batch arrangement, cold feeds send aggregate to drying/heating. A hot stone elevator
lifts heated aggregate to the screen deck, where fractions are separated into hot bins. Weigh bins measure
the selected fractions for a batch. The pug mill mixes the measured materials with the specified binder and
other permitted inputs. A slat conveyor or other designed transfer moves the product toward
storage/loadout.

The batch identity helps relate proportioning to a production record. It does not guarantee that every batch
meets the formula: incorrect bin contents, screen condition, scales, moisture assumptions or binder
measurement can affect the result. Quality control checks the process and tested product. Operators
follow the actual plant’s safe controls, interlocks and procedures; learners should be able to trace an input
and explain what evidence would reveal a change.

Study chapter · Outline C4



Asphalt products and uses
A road requirement has an issuer, edition, project adoption and decision authority. A contractor, designer,
owner and regulator may each issue documents, but their documents answer different questions. Read the
contract register before choosing a clause from a familiar book. The same surface material can appear on a
highway, a municipal street and private property under different project requirements.

LEARNING STEP 1




The key elements of the MMCD
MMCD means Master Municipal Construction Documents. The published contract documents provide
standardized municipal construction specifications, drawings and related contract material. A project may
adopt an edition and add supplementary conditions or specifications. Obtain the actual adopted documents
and changes; the title 'municipal work' does not establish every MMCD clause or edition. Record the
governing project reference, not a guessed requirement from another job.

LEARNING STEP 2




The role of the MOT
The provincial transportation ministry’s role is established by the project and applicable law/contract.
Distinguish contractor quality control from owner-side quality assurance, audit and acceptance activities
as defined in the contract. A contractor’s passing internal check is not automatically the owner’s
acceptance. Conversely, an audit sample does not remove the contractor’s duty to control its own process.
Current ministry naming can change; identify the actual issuer and authorized representative.

LEARNING STEP 3




The role of the DOT
DOT is commonly used for a Department of Transportation in other jurisdictions. It does not identify one
universal Canadian asphalt standard. The supplied 2006 B.C. Design Build Standard Specifications is a
specific historical document, not a generic federal transport regulation. When an old note says 'DOT
standard', identify its jurisdiction, full title, edition and contractual adoption before using it. This prevents a
familiar acronym from becoming an unsupported requirement.

Engineer Specs
An engineer’s project specifications translate the design into required materials, workmanship, inspection
and acceptance criteria. Such specifications are used in public as well as private work. Drawings and
specifications must be read together, using the project’s hierarchy and change process. A conflict is
referred to the authorized design/contract role; the crew does not choose whichever version gives the
easiest quantity.

LEARNING STEP 5




Role of Architect
An architect may coordinate the site and building interface, accessibility, appearance and other project
requirements through the design team. The actual division of responsibility is project-specific. Architectural
drawings can identify finishes or interfaces while civil drawings establish drainage and pavement details. A
worker must know which record and responsible designer address the question, and retain an authorized
response when the work changes.

LEARNING STEP 6




Other asphalt and paving materials
Paving blocks/stones, asphalt, concrete and permeable pavement systems have different structures,
construction methods and maintenance needs. 'Green road' is a claim or design objective that needs
defined performance and evidence. A substitution affects more than appearance: loading, drainage, access,
joints, durability and approved quantities may change. Compare the required service and obtain the
authorized design decision before substituting a product.

  Worked example · Resolve the document conflict
  Fictional register lists drawing P-04 revision C and specification SP-07 revision 2. A delivery note
  refers to an older project’s MMCD detail. The crew is asked to change the surface material at the
  entrance.

  Think it through.

  The delivery note does not establish the governing design. Identify the current project documents,
  describe the requested change and obtain the authorized design/contract response. Keep the reason
  and affected drawing/specification revisions together. Do not treat an acronym or a supplier
  recommendation as approval.
