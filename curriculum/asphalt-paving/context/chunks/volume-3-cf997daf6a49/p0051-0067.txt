LEARNING STEP 4




The process of taking mix temperature readings
Select the temperature instrument and method for the required measurement. An infrared device senses a
surface within its field of view; spot size, viewing conditions and surface properties affect the reading. A
probe measures at its specified contact/insertion location. Neither automatically represents the entire load.

Verify instrument identity, condition and required check/calibration status. Record the time, location,
product/load, units and method. If readings disagree, investigate the different measurement conditions
before averaging them. A Celsius/Fahrenheit conversion changes units, not where the temperature was
measured.

Study chapter · Outline E3



Determine haul truck samples
A truck sample connects the delivered mixture to a production and quality record. The truck, load ticket,
mix identifier, time and sampled location must remain traceable. Sampling should answer the planned
question; choosing only the easiest or coolest-looking portion can introduce bias. Safe access is
established before sampling, not improvised after a truck arrives.


  Mix sampling

  Quality control

  Bag samples


LEARNING STEP 1




Mix sampling
Confirm the intended material and sampling purpose: mixture identity, temperature, binder-
content/gradation testing or another specified property. Use the required sampling location, increments,
tools and sample size under the approved method. The arrangement must control vehicle movement, hot
material, access and other exposed people. Do not climb a load or reach into a discharge stream on the
strength of a classroom description.

Temperature and laboratory specimens may require different handling. The sampling record identifies
exactly what was taken, where, when and by whom. A result is compared with the correct formula and
contractual criterion, not simply with the last truck’s appearance.

LEARNING STEP 2




Quality control
Random selection reduces the opportunity to choose favourable loads or locations, while the approved
plan determines the sampling population and timing. Random does not mean unplanned or unsafe. The
testing agency/company personnel must have the required competence and authorization, and the
truck/paver crew must understand the hold and communication arrangement.

Keep quality-control sampling separate from owner acceptance/audit sampling where the contract
distinguishes them. A failed result is handled through the documented process, preserving the selected
sample and records. Replacing it with an unrecorded convenient sample defeats traceability and can hide a
production problem.

LEARNING STEP 3




Bag samples
Use the container or bag specified for the sample’s material, temperature, quantity and test. Identify the
load, mix, date/time, location, sampler, intended test and any required seal/backup identification. Protect
the sample from contamination and loss while following safe hot-material handling. An ordinary classroom
bag may be unsuitable for actual hot mix.

Study chapter · Outline E4



Determine compaction levels
Density relates mass to volume. Compaction changes the arrangement of particles and air voids, but the
measured density must be compared with the correct reference and method. Two instruments can report
numbers that look similar while representing different locations or measurement principles. Keep the
instrument, sample, units and denominator visible.


  Two methods to determine compaction


LEARNING STEP 1




Two methods to determine compaction
A nuclear density gauge uses a radioactive source and detector arrangement to infer a material property
through its calibrated method. The actual instrument, mode, calibration/standardization, surface
preparation and test method affect the result. In Canada, possession/use and transport require the
applicable CNSC licensing, training, security and radiation-safety arrangements. Reading the Troxler 3430
manual is useful instrument study; it does not authorize source handling or operation.

A core is a physical specimen removed at an identified location. The approved coring and laboratory
method establish sampling, handling, dimensions and density determination. Coring also requires
utility/location checks, traffic/access controls and safe equipment/water management, followed by the
specified repair of the hole. A damaged, contaminated or mislabelled core may not support the intended
result.

Gauge and core results are compared only through the specified procedure and any required correlation. A
percentage density must state its reference: maximum theoretical mixture density, laboratory compacted
reference or another defined basis. Do not compare percentages that use different denominators. The
result belongs to the sampled location and acceptance unit defined by the contract; one point does not
automatically represent an entire project.

Report original readings, checks, location and any limitation. A low result prompts the approved
investigation/disposition process. Additional rolling is not automatically appropriate after the material’s
workable window or when the cause is unknown.

  Worked example · The denominator changes the meaning
  Fictional in-place density is 2.34 t/m³. Reference A, maximum theoretical density, is 2.50 t/m³.
  Reference B, a compacted laboratory reference in a different teaching system, is 2.40 t/m³.

  Think it through.

  Against A: 2.34/2.50 × 100 = 93.6%. Against B: 2.34/2.40 × 100 = 97.5%. Both calculations use the
  same numerator; they answer different defined questions. Neither establishes acceptance without
  the applicable method and criterion. Never relabel 97.5% as percent of maximum theoretical density.
