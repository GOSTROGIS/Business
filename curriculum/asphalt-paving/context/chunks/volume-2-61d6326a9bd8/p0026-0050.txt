Study chapter · Outline B6



Weather effects and precautions
Weather affects the worker as well as the material. Hot mix and hot pavement add radiant heat to the work
environment; sun, humidity, air movement, workload and clothing affect how the body loses heat. A
forecast temperature alone cannot describe the exposure. Crew members also differ in acclimatization and
health needs, which should be supported without demanding personal medical details in front of the group.


  The effects of temperature on the body

  The importance of proper hydration

  Define effects of exposure to direct sun


LEARNING STEP 1




The effects of temperature on the body
The body produces heat during work and loses heat through exchanges with the surroundings, including
evaporation of sweat. Heavy work, radiant heat and clothing can make cooling more difficult. Heat strain
can develop even when the air temperature seems familiar. The work plan assesses conditions, provides
appropriate controls and adjusts work/rest and supervision arrangements as conditions change.

Recognize changes in behaviour, weakness, dizziness, headache, nausea or collapse and obtain help. Cold,
rain and wind can also affect dexterity, judgement, traction and health. A weather response includes
clothing, shelter, suitable work organization and an emergency plan. A new worker needs an acclimatization
arrangement rather than an assumption that determination will make them tolerate the exposure.

LEARNING STEP 2




The importance of proper hydration
Workers need ready access to appropriate drinking water and the opportunity to drink regularly under the
workplace heat plan. Hydration is one part of control; it does not replace reducing exposure, rest, cooling or
recognizing illness. Do not make a class-wide medical fluid prescription or assume a person with symptoms
only needs another drink.

Heat exhaustion and heat stroke require prompt response. The WorkSafeBC heat-stress guide treats heat
stroke as a medical emergency. Altered mental state, collapse or other severe signs require emergency
assistance: call the local emergency number, get the first-aid attendant and begin the plan’s safe cooling
response while waiting for help. Do not leave the person alone or give fluids to someone unable to swallow
safely. The trained response follows the workplace emergency and first-aid arrangements.

LEARNING STEP 3




Define effects of exposure to direct sun
Ultraviolet exposure can damage skin and eyes, including on days that do not feel extremely hot. The task
plan combines suitable clothing/head protection, shade, scheduling and appropriate sunscreen or other
protection under the guidance used by the workplace. Select protective items so they remain compatible

Study chapter · Outline B7



Safety and traffic control procedures
Traffic control is a planned system for road users, workers and equipment. Signs, devices, traffic control
persons (TCPs), traffic assistants and communication have defined roles. A paving learner must understand
that system even when they are not assigned to control traffic. At a community entrance, include
pedestrians, cyclists, mobility needs and emergency access in the planning questions.


  Traffic control procedures (TCP) Certification

  Review TCP training content

  Review TCP regulations


LEARNING STEP 1




Traffic control procedures (TCP) Certification
WorkSafeBC Part 18 defines traffic-control training and responsibilities. Section 18.6.2 addresses a traffic-
control-person training program, including knowledge and practical competency assessment; the employer
must meet the applicable qualification requirements. A classroom paving chapter does not issue that
qualification. Confirm the accepted training program and the actual person’s current evidence before
assigning TCP duties.

A traffic assistant has a different role and workplace training requirements under 18.6.3. Do not relabel an
unqualified person a TCP because the crew needs help. Course completion records, employer orientation
and site-specific instruction serve different purposes; all applicable requirements must be met.

Review TCP training content
Traffic-control training develops recognition of hazards, the required plan, setup and removal of devices,
safe positioning and escape routes, signs/signals, communication and responses to changing conditions.
The B.C. Traffic Management Manual for Work on Roadways provides the roadwork framework, used with

interpreting an individual sign. A diagram from a manual is a typical arrangement with conditions, not a
ready-made plan for every site. Conditions such as sight distance, road speed, intersections, queues,
pedestrians and lighting affect the actual plan.

Study chapter · Outline B8



Plant site and gravel pit safety
A plant or gravel pit is an interconnected worksite. Material moves through feeders, crushers, screens,
conveyors, stockpiles and vehicles; people may not be able to see the next process from where they stand.
A stoppage at one machine can leave others running or material stored above it. Begin with the site
induction, responsible supervisor and applicable jurisdiction. A mine/pit operation may be governed by
B.C.'s mining safety framework; other workplaces fall under the applicable WorkSafeBC requirements.


  Define noise hazards and precautions

  Site specific hazards


LEARNING STEP 1




Define noise hazards and precautions
Crushing, screening, engines and material impacts can create sustained or changing noise. Hearing
protection is selected within the site’s assessed program. It must remain compatible with communication
and other PPE; a person who cannot hear a warning needs a safe communication arrangement rather than
removing protection in the exposure area.

Review the site’s noise areas, permitted access, signs and protection requirements before entering.
Equipment condition and engineering controls influence noise as well as reliability. An unusual sound can
be a defect indicator, but approaching the source through a restricted area to investigate is not an
appropriate response for an unassigned learner.

LEARNING STEP 2




Site specific hazards
Crushing and entanglement hazards occur at feed openings, belt/pulley nip points, rotating shafts and
moving mechanisms. Guards do not authorize reaching through or around them. Isolation must account for
connected equipment and stored material/energy when cleaning or maintenance is assigned. A bridge of
material can collapse; an idle belt may still be connected to a starting sequence.

Vehicle/loader routes, stockpile faces and reversing areas require the site’s controls. Flying debris and dust
can extend beyond a machine footprint; assess the material, collection/suppression systems and exposure.
Stockpile instability, water edges and restricted plant structures also matter. Follow approved pedestrian
routes and do not climb a pile or conveyor to take a better look. Report missing guards, damaged dust
controls or access conflicts to the responsible person without entering the hazard.

Study chapter · Outline B9



Light vehicle transporting procedures
Transport preparation begins with a complete description: vehicle, trailer, load, mass, dimensions,
attachments, route and driver. Gross vehicle weight rating, actual loaded mass, axle rating, tow rating
and cargo-securement rating answer different questions. A small-looking load can exceed one
component’s limit even when the whole combination appears capable.

The 5,000 kg categories used in the learning tasks are a comparison exercise, not a single legal switch
between company and federal rules. Applicable B.C. vehicle, driver, inspection, cargo-securement and
permit requirements depend on the actual combination and activity. Company rules apply alongside the
law and cannot reduce its requirements.


  Procedures for transportation of vehicles under 5000 kg

  Procedures for transportation of vehicles over 5000 kg


LEARNING STEP 1




Procedures for transportation of vehicles under 5000 kg
For a light vehicle or combination, identify the manufacturer’s ratings, registration/licensing requirements,
trailer/hitch/brake/tyre/axle suitability and the actual loaded condition. Include people, fuel, tools and
attachments in the appropriate mass assessment. A nominal machine mass may exclude options or carried
material. Verify the route and permitted load dimensions before departure.

Cargo must be restrained by an arrangement meeting applicable requirements and the actual anchors and
tiedowns. Keep loose tools, ramps and attachments secure too. A company checklist helps organize the
checks, but a tick beside 'under 5,000 kg' cannot establish that the combination is legal or safe. The driver
and responsible loading personnel need a common load record.

LEARNING STEP 2




Procedures for transportation of vehicles over 5000 kg
Heavier transport adds capacity and planning questions but does not erase the same fundamentals.
Determine the relevant vehicle and cargo-securement rules, driver qualifications, required inspections and
any oversize/overweight permits or route restrictions. The B.C. cargo-securement information and National
Safety Code Standard 10 provide the applicable framework; special commodity/equipment provisions may
matter.

labelled capacity is not automatically the allowable restraint of the whole system: angle, method, anchor
and applicable rules matter. Loading/unloading is also a separate controlled operation, with suitable
ground, ramps, alignment, separation and assigned communication. The trip record must reflect the actual
loaded machine, not a previous similar delivery.

Study chapter · Outline B10



Complete fatigue awareness training
Fatigue is reduced physical or mental capacity associated with inadequate sleep, prolonged wakefulness,
demanding work or other factors. It can affect attention, reaction, memory and judgement before a person
recognizes the full change. A crew depends on accurate handovers and timely responses; fatigue therefore
belongs in work planning, not in a judgement about someone’s character.


  Define mental fatigue

  Define a work schedule that helps reduce fatigue

  Discuss available training


LEARNING STEP 1




Define mental fatigue
Sleep debt accumulates when sleep is less than a person’s need. Circadian rhythms influence alertness
across the day and night. A worker may have a scheduled rest interval but little opportunity to sleep after
commuting, meals and personal responsibilities. Extended wakefulness and successive demanding shifts
can compound the problem.

Signs may include slowed responses, difficulty concentrating, errors, irritability or difficulty staying awake.
These observations do not diagnose the cause. Respond through the workplace fitness-for-task and
support arrangements, with privacy and respect. Caffeine or willpower cannot be treated as a complete
replacement for sleep. A fatigued person may also be unsafe to drive home; plan a safe response rather
than simply sending them onto the road.

LEARNING STEP 2




Define a work schedule that helps reduce fatigue
A fatigue-aware schedule considers shift length, timing, consecutive duties, recovery opportunity, travel
and workload. The employer’s planning and response arrangements should make reporting possible and
provide a practical way to change the assignment when needed. A schedule can look adequate on paper
while overtime, an early call-out or a long commute removes the intended recovery.

Distinguish time off duty from sleep opportunity and actual sleep. A calculation can expose an obvious
planning conflict, but it cannot certify that an individual is fit. Ask whether the schedule remains workable
after a delay and whether the highest-consequence tasks have appropriate staffing and supervision. Record
the changed assignment and handover so the response does not create another gap.

LEARNING STEP 3




Discuss available training
Useful fatigue-awareness training explains causes, signs, work and driving effects, reporting, prevention
and the workplace response. CCOHS provides a retained evidence source for this study. Evaluate an
external course by its content, current evidence, instructor/provider suitability and relevance to the actual
work; a website name or completion badge alone does not establish effective controls.
