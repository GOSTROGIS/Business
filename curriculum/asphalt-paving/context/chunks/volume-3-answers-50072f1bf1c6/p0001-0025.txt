Use this volume
This volume provides complete classroom readings, worked examples, named practice tasks and answer
guidance for materials, plants and quality evidence. Follow the learning route in each chapter, study the
labelled figures and explain your reasoning. The separate answer book contains the worked keys and
observation records.

Care for people, place and the next person using the work runs through the cases. Local First Nations, Métis
and Inuit learners and partners bring distinct knowledge and priorities. Participation does not require
sharing personal stories or cultural teachings, and no diagram claims to represent a Nation’s protocol.


    Classroom authoring is complete for the listed competencies. Qualified technical review, actual
    fleet/site matching, a local teaching pilot and observed practical qualification are separate
    requirements. The practical records are ready for an instructor to configure and use; they do not certify
    unobserved work.




Read the picture, then test the idea
    1. Identify the object, process or quantity shown.
    2. Read labels, units and the figure’s stated limits.
    3. Connect the image to the chapter explanation.
    4. Try the exercise and check against evidence.
    5. Explain the result to another person and revise if needed.



Use AI as a checked working tool
Claude and ChatGPT can help explain provided documents and create supported files. Claude Code,
Cowork and Codex can help with permitted multi-file tasks and repeatable workflows when their tools are
available. Give a clear purpose, named sources, output formats, limits and checks. Ask for a findings table
with page/cell evidence; verify it against the source. Reopen DOCX, XLSX, HTML or PDF outputs. For study
audio/video, verify the script, captions, diagrams and actual playback. A generated result is not a technical
approval.

Crushing process and paving aggregate
production

Worked example · Find the process change
Fictional circuit: a screen sends its oversize back to a cone crusher. A damaged screen allows larger
particles into the finished-product pile. The delivery ticket still carries the correct product name.

The name on the ticket has not verified the physical product. Identify affected production and stockpile
records, isolate the questioned product through the quality process, inspect the screen safely and obtain
the specified testing. Do not blend the questioned pile into accepted material to hide the difference. The
process diagram points to where the change occurred and which records may be affected.


  Trace a product through feeder, jaw/cone/impact stage, screen, conveyor, stockpile and
  transport

  Trace a product through feeder, jaw/cone/impact stage, screen, conveyor, stockpile and transport.
  Explain which stages change material and which preserve or separate it.

  Answer guidance.

  Crushers reduce/break material; screens separate size fractions; washing removes specified fines;
  conveyors/transport move it; stockpile handling preserves identity and limits segregation. Feed
  controls input.

  Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  A fictional delivery uses 12 truckloads of 18 t

  A fictional delivery uses 12 truckloads of 18 t. Two loads are held after the screen fault is discovered.
  Calculate the recorded total and the quantity held, without calling the remainder accepted.

  Answer guidance.

  12 × 18 = 216 t recorded; 2 × 18 = 36 t held. The other 180 t still needs the applicable quality
  decision; arithmetic does not establish its conformity.

  Observe: Numeracy, Problem solving, Writing through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Types and specifications of aggregates

Worked example · Read a sieve result
Fictional dry sample mass is 1,000 g. The measured retained masses on three successive sieves are 150 g,
350 g and 300 g; the pan holds 200 g. Use these values only to practise the arithmetic.

The masses total 1,000 g. Cumulative retained percentages are 15%, 50% and 80%; cumulative passing
values are 85%, 50% and 20%. The pan fraction is 20%. Whether the product conforms cannot be decided
without sieve sizes, specified method, product identity and acceptance limits.


  Make a product selection table for bedding, drainage, asphalt mixture and concrete sand

  Make a product selection table for bedding, drainage, asphalt mixture and concrete sand. Name the
  information needed before accepting a substitution.

  Answer guidance.

  Use the intended product specification, grading/quality tests, source and application. Product
  nicknames or appearance alone are insufficient.

  Observe: Reading, Writing, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.




  Recalculate the example if 20 g is missing from the total

  Recalculate the example if 20 g is missing from the total. Then calculate PI for fictional LL = 32 and PL
  = 21. Explain the limits of both results.

  Answer guidance.

  A 980 g recovery triggers the specified mass-reconciliation check; do not silently normalize an
  unexplained loss. PI = 11 for the tested fine fraction; it does not determine whole-aggregate suitability
  alone.

  Observe: Numeracy, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Asphalt plant operation

Worked example · One product, two process records
Fictional batch card: 950 kg aggregate plus 50 kg binder, with no other mass included. Fictional continuous
record: 19,000 kg aggregate plus 1,000 kg binder over an interval. Compare binder mass percentage on a
total-mixture basis.

Batch total = 1,000 kg; binder = 5%. Continuous total = 20,000 kg; binder = 5%. The same arithmetic ratio
does not establish matching gradation, moisture correction, binder grade, temperature or conformity. The
records describe different production organizations; both require the approved formula and checks.


  Explain and apply the idea

  Trace both plant diagrams and explain hot elevator, screen deck, hot bin, weigh bin, pug mill, slat
  conveyor, silo and loadout.

  Answer guidance.

  Correctly distinguish the conventional batch path from continuous proportioning. The explanation
  includes material identity and rejects a single universal drum layout.

  Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  Recalculate the batch if binder is 52 kg while aggregate remains 950 kg

  Recalculate the batch if binder is 52 kg while aggregate remains 950 kg. State the denominator and
  why the result alone cannot approve the product.

  Answer guidance.

  52/(950+52) × 100 ≈ 5.19% by total mixture mass. Approval also requires the actual accepted
  formula, method and other properties.

  Observe: Numeracy, Writing, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Worked example · Resolve the document conflict
Fictional register lists drawing P-04 revision C and specification SP-07 revision 2. A delivery note refers to
an older project’s MMCD detail. The crew is asked to change the surface material at the entrance.

The delivery note does not establish the governing design. Identify the current project documents, describe

Environmentally friendly properties

Worked example · Recycled proportion is not a full impact score
Fictional material record: a 240 t proposed mix contains 48 t of approved RAP. A sales note calls it “20%
recycled, therefore 20% lower whole-life emissions.” No emissions or service-life data is supplied.

48/240 × 100 = 20% by mass in this record. That establishes the stated proportion only. Whole-life
emissions need comparable quantities, processing/production, transport, construction, maintenance and
end-of-life evidence. The proposed reduction is not demonstrated by the recycled percentage.


  Explain and apply the idea

  Rewrite the sales note into one supported statement and three questions needed for a whole-life
  comparison.

  Answer guidance.

  Supported: proposed mixture contains 20% RAP by the stated total mass. Ask for common
  function/service period, production/haul data and maintenance/end-of-life assumptions, among other
  relevant needs.

  Observe: Reading, Writing, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.




  Calculate the RAP mass for a second fictional 300 t mix at 15%

  Calculate the RAP mass for a second fictional 300 t mix at 15%. Explain why comparing 48 t with the
  second mass does not rank environmental performance.

  Answer guidance.

  300 × .15 = 45 t. Different total quantities and proportions do not supply equivalent-service impacts;
  no ranking follows.

  Observe: Numeracy, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Use of storage areas

Worked example · Containment is a working system
Fictional inspection record: the containment area is intact, but its drain is open and collected rainwater has
not been assessed for contamination. A delivery is due in 30 minutes.

The record does not establish effective containment for the transfer. Have the responsible person
implement the storage/transfer procedure, address the open drain and assess the collected liquid before
release or transfer. Delivery timing does not change the product or environmental requirements. Record the
condition and resolution rather than ticking “tank intact” as the whole inspection.


  Draw the difference between separate containment and an integral self-contained
  arrangement

  Draw the difference between separate containment and an integral self-contained arrangement.
  Identify five checks both still require.

  Answer guidance.

  Product identity/compatibility, condition, usable capacity, transfer/overfill controls, permitted location
  and emergency/waste arrangements are relevant. The teaching Type 1/2 labels are not legal
  certification.

  Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  Calculate and check the result

  Fictional cost exercise: virgin material supply costs $8,000; recovered feed costs $5,000 plus $2,400
  processing and $900 extra transport. Compare only these stated costs.

  Answer guidance.

  Recovered-route subtotal = $8,300, $300 higher. This bounded estimate does not include every
  production/performance/life-cycle factor and does not authorize the material.

  Observe: Numeracy, Writing, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Asphalt plant and placement emissions

Worked example · Two measurements, different questions
Fictional records show a compliant stack sample from last week and a worker complaint about fumes at
today’s loadout. The supervisor proposes closing the complaint using the stack result.

The records differ in location, time and purpose. The stack result does not resolve today’s worker exposure.
Apply the workplace reporting/assessment process, control the affected task as required and retain both
records with their identities. Investigate the actual loadout conditions and any process change without
inventing a concentration.


  Trace dirty gas, filtered gas and collected dust through the baghouse drawing

  Trace dirty gas, filtered gas and collected dust through the baghouse drawing. Explain one pollutant-
  control claim the drawing cannot support.

  Answer guidance.

  Correctly separate gas and solids paths. Fabric dust collection alone does not prove removal of all
  gases or compliance with every permit/exposure requirement.

  Observe: Reading, Communication through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.




  Create a jurisdiction-and-record table for a fictional plant inside Metro Vancouver and one
  outside

  Create a jurisdiction-and-record table for a fictional plant inside Metro Vancouver and one outside.
  Keep environmental and worker-exposure records in separate columns.

  Answer guidance.

  Inside: current regional air program/actual authorization; outside: applicability of provincial Asphalt
  Plant Regulation plus other relevant requirements. Workplace exposure control remains a separate
  question in either case.

  Observe: Writing, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation, explanation
  or saved work.
