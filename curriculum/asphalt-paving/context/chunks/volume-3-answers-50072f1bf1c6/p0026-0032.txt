Determine compaction levels

Worked example · The denominator changes the meaning
Fictional in-place density is 2.34 t/m³. Reference A, maximum theoretical density, is 2.50 t/m³. Reference
B, a compacted laboratory reference in a different teaching system, is 2.40 t/m³.

Against A: 2.34/2.50 × 100 = 93.6%. Against B: 2.34/2.40 × 100 = 97.5%. Both calculations use the same
numerator; they answer different defined questions. Neither establishes acceptance without the applicable
method and criterion. Never relabel 97.5% as percent of maximum theoretical density.


  Explain and apply the idea

  Compare gauge and core methods using columns for measurement principle, identification, checks,
  access and authorization.

  Answer guidance.

  Gauge: calibrated indirect measurement with radiation/licensing requirements. Core: physical
  specimen under approved sampling/lab method. Both need location, traceability, instrument/method
  checks and safe access.

  Observe: Reading, Writing, Problem solving through the learner’s actual contribution, calculation,
  explanation or saved work.

Check your understanding of density evidence
Five questions, four marks each: 20 marks total. Minimum 14/20 (70%), as required by the outline for this
knowledge check. This does not authorize gauge operation.


  Question 1 · Explain one difference between core testing and nuclear-gauge testing, and
  identify the Canadian authorization issue.

  A core is a physical sample tested through its approved method; a nuclear gauge uses radiation-based
  measurement under its procedure. Canadian operation requires the applicable CNSC licence, training
  and controls.

  Award one mark for each: Core method identified; Gauge method identified; Methods not treated as
  interchangeable; CNSC authorization/training identified.




  Question 2 · Calculate percent density for 2.30 Mg/m³ in place and 2.50 Mg/m³ maximum
  theoretical.

  2.30/2.50 × 100 = 92.0%; reference is maximum theoretical density.

  Award one mark for each: Correct denominator; Correct calculation; 92.0% with percent unit;
  Reference named.




  Question 3 · Name four traceability fields needed with a density result.

  Examples: location/station, lane/lift, lot/sample ID, date/time, method/instrument ID, density units
  and reference result.

  Award one mark for each: One correct field; Second distinct field; Third distinct field; Fourth distinct
  field.

Question 4 · A draft changes the reference density to improve the percentage. Explain the
  response.

  Preserve the original result, verify the required method and reference, reject an unsupported
  substitution and obtain the responsible quality lead’s documented disposition.

  Award one mark for each: Preserves evidence; Checks required reference; Rejects unsupported
  substitution; Escalates/documents decision.




  Question 5 · Plan a safe learning observation of core or gauge testing.

  Use the approved sampling/testing plan, controlled access and authorized personnel. Record
  observation separately from operation and keep method/location/result traceable.

  Award one mark for each: Approved method/plan; Controlled access; Authorized personnel;
  Observation/operation distinction.

Trace a material and its sample

    This is a complete fictional classroom case. It does not describe an actual community, Nation,
    employer or project. Learners may use the supplied case without disclosing personal or cultural
    knowledge.


A fictional 1,200 g aggregate sample has retained masses of 180 g, 420 g, 360 g and 240 g in the pan. Its
log says Mix M, lot 4, but the container label says lot 5. A draft report says “the sample is representative
because it is large” and “20% RAP means 20% lower emissions.” The project location is in Metro
Vancouver. No acceptance limits or permit number are supplied.




  Reading · Check the facts
  Identify the controlling facts, missing information and one unsupported statement in the brief.

  Evidence and feedback: Use the exact case facts; distinguish observation, assumption and missing
  authority.




  Writing · Make the handover useful
  Draft a concise handover with location, quantities, status and the next responsible action.

  Evidence and feedback: The note preserves verified facts, units and unresolved holds without
  inventing a release.




  Numeracy · Calculate and check
  Show the case calculation with units and an independent reasonableness check.

  Evidence and feedback: Use the worked numerical key below; show the relationship and its practical
  limits.

Digital · Direct AI and verify the files
Use an available AI assistant to draft a findings table and editable record from this brief. Request
DOCX or a structured document, XLSX with formulas where useful, and a PDF/HTML reading copy.
Audit every claim and reopen the actual outputs.

Evidence and feedback: Record the product/capabilities, prompt, source facts, saved files and
checks. A chat response alone is not a created or verified file. Check formulas and preserve originals.




Problem solving · Investigate the discrepancy
Explain the discrepancy, what evidence is needed and who should authorize the next step.

Evidence and feedback: Separate the observed issue from a guessed cause; preserve evidence and
identify the correct decision role.




Communication · Send and check the message
Brief a partner, ask for a check-back and repair one misunderstanding if it occurs.

Evidence and feedback: Observe an actual exchange and confirmation of the essential facts; do not
infer communication from a shared file.




Collaboration · Review together
Agree reviewer/author roles, complete a review and record each person’s contribution.

Evidence and feedback: Both learners take an identifiable turn; feedback is respectful and tied to the
case evidence.
