ACCESS IS4S classroom teaching • Three 60-minute sessions • 180 minutes

Pair with the learner workbook and supporting materials. The workbook supplies the readings, equipment cards,
data and questions; the separate change card is in the supporting materials. No unavailable provider worksheet
or live website is required to complete this opening sequence. A device and spreadsheet or accessible table tool
are needed to observe Digital; a later individual tool turn is required if devices are unavailable.



Scope and source status
The occupational anchor is the user-supplied Asphalt Paving/Laydown Technician Program Outline, Line A,
competency A1 Introduction to the paving industry and paving equipment, PDF page 22 / printed page 20,
footer 12/12. Its objective is understanding roads-construction history and the relationship between governing
bodies and contractors. Its nine learning tasks are nine occupational-content items, distinct from the nine Skills
for Success.

Learner question IDs A1-Q01 through A1-Q18 identify original workbook questions. They do not reproduce the
outline's learning-task numbers; use the coverage table below to connect them.

The supplied outline is a historical source, not evidence of current regulations or this ACCESS cohort's approved
syllabus. The packet includes later-dated sections; record page-specific footers rather than assigning one date to
all content. Teach EPS — End Product Specification from the supplied 2006 specifications, clause 502.03.01

Observable outcomes and A1 coverage
By the end, learners can explain a basic process and equipment functions, connect roads with community and
trade needs, read a small industry table and project brief, distinguish project roles, and identify what evidence an
environmental or requirements claim still needs. Assess these as introductory understanding with recorded
supports.

Answer key and acceptable responses
A1-Q01: Infrastructure is the shared physical systems supporting a community. Daily-life examples include
reaching school, services or recreation; trade examples include deliveries and goods transport. Accept other
sensible examples tied to the reading. Learner must locate relevant wording, not only repeat an instructor's
answer.

A1-Q02: Some routes began as paths formed by repeated travel; people cleared/shaped routes and added
stone/gravel or other materials; different road types serve different purposes and contexts. No dates are
required. Do not insist this is the history of every road or community.

A1-Q03: Pit/quarry → plant → haul → place → compact. Purposes: obtain/process mineral material; prepare
specified mixture; transport it; place it; compact it. Accept accurate paraphrases and note that real projects have
more steps.

A1-Q04: 1 E4; 2 E5; 3 E2; 4 E6; 5 E1; 6 E3. Paver places/shapes the mat; roller compacts. Answers should not
invent operating settings.

A1-Q05: A suitable question asks whether the material meets the project's specification, whether supply is
approved/permitted for the project, or what properties/availability need checking. Examples include gravel, sand
and crushed stone; source types include pits and quarries. The response must reject “nearby means suitable” as
an unsupported inference.

A1-Q06: Accept two accurately described types and plausible conditional uses. For example, a gravel road could
provide access to a work area; an asphalt-surfaced road could serve a town route. An ice road could support
seasonal access under responsible-authority controls; do not accept learner-created safe-ice thresholds.
User/access questions might concern a clear pedestrian route, access for a mobility device, delivery timing, bus
access or emergency access. The task asks questions, not a learner-designed traffic-management plan.

A1-Q07: Observe accurate explanation, listener restatement, a useful clarification and a confirmation.
Collaboration requires both contributions and a jointly agreed improvement; sitting in a pair alone is insufficient.
Accept different culturally appropriate listening behaviours; eye contact is not a criterion.

A1-Q08: A road connects people/places or supports movement; equipment performs a particular
production/placement task. Accept another accurate distinction and a genuine next vocabulary goal.

A1-Q09: Owner: fictional Town of Willow Reach. Contractor: fictional Pathway Paving Company. Contributors:
Town $60,000; Regional Access Fund $40,000; Community Route Fund $20,000. Total $60,000 + $40,000 +
$20,000 = $120,000 . Funding does not by itself make an organization the road owner or the contractor; the brief
assigns those roles separately. Do not generalize this fictional arrangement to all projects.

A1-Q10: EPS means End Product Specification in the supplied clause 502.03.01. The contractor controls
construction-process quality; the Ministry performs the specified quality-audit sampling/testing of the end
product for acceptance/rejection and payment adjustment. Accept accurate plain-language explanations of those
distinct responsibilities. The EPS verification entry must still ask which specification and edition the actual
contract adopts. For MMCD, accept a request for the full title, issuer, version and contractual applicability from an
authorized project document or responsible issuer. The funding entry should request the actual agreement,

Feedback rubric and evidence record
Use not observed / not yet demonstrated / demonstrated with support / demonstrated independently as local
feedback descriptions, not official SFS proficiency levels or trade competency grades. Use “not observed” when
evidence has not been collected; use “not yet demonstrated” when an observed attempt still needs practice.
Record application in a new situation separately as a transfer note. A learner can show different descriptions for
different skills. Record the support, the actual evidence and the next step; permit another attempt after feedback.

Accessibility and extension
    Offer large print, uncluttered copies, optional reading aloud/text-to-speech, glossary cards, manipulatives
    and calculator use. Record reading support so a read-aloud response is not reported as independent unaided
    reading. Check comprehension through the learner locating and using information with their chosen support.
    For Writing, allow keyboard, speech-to-text or a scribe with learner control over wording and revision.
    Preserve the learner's authored text and record the method. A purely oral answer without a text product does
    not demonstrate this writing outcome.
    For Communication, accept an accessible interactive mode, including AAC. Assess message,
    listening/interpretation and clarification rather than accent, speaking speed or eye contact.
