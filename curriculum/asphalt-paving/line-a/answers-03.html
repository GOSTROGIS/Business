Unit 3 · Answer and teaching guidance

Use 15 minutes for ingredients and the flow visual, 15 for plant comparison, 15 for product clarification, 20 for recycling routes, 10 for the counter model and 15 for revision/digital note. Prepare 100 counters or a labelled paper grid; count categories, not a real mixture. Provide an actual device turn for LA3-07 if Digital is assessed.

Task Expected evidence
LA3-01 Aggregate is mineral material; asphalt binder binds the asphalt mixture; process fuel supplies energy and must not be assumed to be an ingredient. “Cement” may refer to asphalt cement or Portland cement. An additive may alter a specified property; its purpose and suitability require the actual product and approved mixture requirements. A pit commonly supplies unconsolidated material; a quarry commonly extracts rock that is processed. Neither source name guarantees suitable aggregate. Source suitability needs specification/material-property evidence.
LA3-02 B indicates batches; D indicates a continuous drum-mix process in the simplified case. Shared stages include supplied materials, proportioning/mixing, storage or loading and haul. A drying drum alone does not distinguish the two.
LA3-03 Ask for full product name/grade, current approved design or project specification, supplier/product confirmation and relevant safety/handling information through the responsible person. Accept an explicit question about the archive note's meaning. No grade is approved by the task.
LA3-04 Plant route: recovery/processing → suitable plant input → approved mixture/use. Hot-in-place route: specialist coordinated process works on existing pavement in place. Both need applicable approved process, suitability and quality evidence. Removal is not proof of later reuse.
LA3-05 80%, 15%, 5%; sum 100%. The recovered-mixture category includes more than one constituent, so the model does not isolate total binder content. The counters are not mass or a production recipe.
LA3-06 Remove the unsupported plant-haul label and show the in-place route; retain the idea of recovering/reusing existing material. Identify equipment/process details as pending. Observe each learner's correction and handover.
LA3-07 The learner controls the wording, saves and retrieves a versioned file and explains the distinctions. Paper composition supports Writing but does not demonstrate save/reopen.

Occupational learning-task evidence

Learning task Activity Locally authored criterion
A3.1 · Drum and batch plants LA3-02 Distinguish process types without classifying by one visible component
A3.2 · Ingredients LA3-01, LA3-05 Explain aggregate/binder/additive roles and avoid treating fuel or mixed recovered material as a single known constituent
A3.3 · Resources LA3-01 Distinguish pit/quarry sources and suitability questions
A3.4 · Production liquids LA3-03 Recognize that grade systems/product labels need current application and supplier confirmation; no operational selection demonstrated
A3.5 · Recycling technology LA3-04, LA3-06 Distinguish plant reuse, recovery and in-place processes and identify needed evidence
A3.6 · Asphalt train / hot-in-place LA3-04, LA3-06, LA3-07 Explain a coordinated train and the distinction between placement and specialist in-place recycling

Record grade interpretation and specific plant/recycling practices as requiring qualified technical review. The classroom unit teaches the vocabulary and information decisions with complete cases; it does not establish temperatures, percentages, compatibility, settings or a machine procedure. For a retry, present a different component-based misclassification or an incomplete product request and observe the learner's reasoning.
