# Unit 2 · Answer and teaching guidance

Suggested 75 minutes: 10 for purpose and vocabulary, 15 for chronology, 15 for materials/categories, 15 for paired labels, 15 for independent caption and 5 for reflection. Supply H1–H4 and paper or accessible drawing tools. Model observation versus interpretation before using the photograph description. No image or private archive is required.

| Task | Expected evidence |
|---|---|
| LA2-01 | 312 BCE Via Appia development begins; 1942 Alaska pioneer road completed; 1943 permanent location completed; 1946 Canadian portion responsibility transferred; 1948 civilian non-military opening; 1956 U.S. highway legislation. “Pioneer road” and “permanent location” are different milestones. Chronology alone cannot establish consultation, effects or all perspectives. |
| LA2-02 | Materials: earth, cut stone, gravel, timber logs, concrete, asphalt mixture. Asphalt cement is the bituminous binder in asphalt; Portland cement is used in the cement-based binding system in concrete. Methods: closely laid crosswise logs; delivery/placement/compaction. Purposes: moving timber, military access, travel to a community facility. Accept an explanation that different designs/materials can serve the same purpose. A photo question might concern date, location, scale, layers, use or author. |
| LA2-03 | Observable: logs across a route; truck in background; wet-looking appearance in the supplied description. Possible corduroy construction is a supported interpretation. Logging purpose, owner, actual moisture condition, date and location are not established. The truck does not prove logging. |
| LA2-04 | H2 supports the specific wartime-access explanation. It does not support a universal war-only claim; H1 and H3 also identify trade and daily movement. `1948 − 1942 = 6 years`. H2 records work/responsibility milestones within that interval. |
| LA2-05 | One panel distinguishes timber/crosswise-log method from logging purpose. The other gives mixture delivery, paver/screed placement and roller compaction with preparation/checks acknowledged. Do not score drawing polish or accept an invented operating setting. |
| LA2-06 | A valid caption integrates a correct date with a defined event, distinguishes method/material/purpose, gives evidence for demand and names a limitation. It may discuss public-record limits without asking learners to supply cultural knowledge. |

**Model caption:** “The Alaska Highway's pioneer road was completed in 1942 as a wartime land-access project; its permanent location was completed in 1943. Roads can later support other uses, including civilian travel and trade. Material, method and purpose describe different things: timber logs are a material, corduroy is a construction form, and logging is a road use. Modern asphalt paving combines manufactured mixture, delivery, placement and compaction. These examples show changes in techniques and needs, but they do not establish a single history for every road or describe every community's experience. A local account needs its own evidence and appropriate permission.”

Offer a partly ordered timeline, a word bank or a dictated first draft when useful, recording the support. For a retry, change one event description or give a new category statement. A technical reviewer should check the introductory material descriptions and provider relevance; a local history contribution requires its own sourcing and permission, not instructor inference.
