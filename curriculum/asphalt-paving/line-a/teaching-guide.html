Unit 1 · Answer and teaching guidance

Use 10 minutes for source/measure vocabulary, 20 for “Report what the data can support” through “Retrieve and preserve a source record”, 25 for “Match a question to a document” through “Check the funding statement”, 25 for “Make the strongest supported comparison” through “Revise a claim when the scope changes”, and 10 for revision. Prepare I1–I5 on paper and an actual device turn for each learner. The public-source register and retained extracts make the lesson workable offline; online retrieval remains unobserved until performed.

Report what the data can support

Expected evidence and useful feedback: Employment is 246,750 people in B.C. construction in 2024. Full-time share is 12 percentage points above the all-industry figure. 87,280 is forecast construction openings over 2025–2035, not guaranteed paving jobs. I1b's categories sum to 427 statistical locations; 175 − 63 = 112 locations; 175 ÷ 427 × 100 = 40.983606…%, or 40.98%. The road-construction classification includes highways, streets and bridges. Locations are not unique companies or workers; size ranges must not be used to estimate total employees. Comparing this location count with all-construction employment mixes measure, scope and period.

Retrieve and preserve a source record

Expected evidence and useful feedback: A retained note identifies title, WorkBC/Province of B.C., URL, access date, metric/year and any change. Observe locating, saving and reopening independently of the accuracy of the paper answer.

Match a question to a document

Expected evidence and useful feedback: I2 supports selected orientation requirements. I3 explains the document system, but the actual signed contract and relevant amendments/special provisions are needed to establish its applicable edition. I4 identifies the public funders and contractor. I3b confirms End Product Specification in the 2006 clause; it does not establish every old note’s intended usage. Profit and a separate unexplained note still require further evidence. A register must not silently label a specification “law.”

Check the funding statement

Expected evidence and useful feedback: 215.19 + 385.58 = $600.77 million. Federal fraction 215.19 ÷ 600.77 × 100 = 35.819032…%, or 35.82%. The provincial share is 64.18% after rounding. The total exceeds $600 million by $0.77 million. Province is the recorded contracting authority; Canada and B.C. are reported funders; Kicking Horse Canyon Constructors is the awarded contractor. The source does not identify profit.

Make the strongest supported comparison

Expected evidence and useful feedback: Both recovery pathways are supported. No matched environmental winner is established. A valid grid marks missing quantities/designs, production factors, distances/modes, construction impacts, maintenance schedules, use-stage assumptions and recovery/disposal scenarios. The proposed common service is the 50 m × 6 m route for 20 years; that is a comparison brief, not a verified design.

Revise a claim when the scope changes

Expected evidence and useful feedback: A per-tonne factory result has a different unit and boundary from a whole-route life-cycle result. Different required quantities may change the comparison. A useful request specifies equal function, service period, approved design quantities, included stages, current geography-relevant data, maintenance and end-of-life assumptions, uncertainty and reviewer. Do not reward an invented emissions calculation.

Worked conclusion: “Both asphalt and concrete have documented material-recovery pathways. The supplied information does not establish which option has lower whole-life emissions for the same 50 m by 6 m route over 20 years. A comparison needs approved pavement quantities, production and transport data, construction and maintenance assumptions, and end-of-life treatment. A factory result per tonne cannot establish the proposed whole-route claim. We can request comparable evidence before recommending an environmental preference.”

Unit 2 · Answer and teaching guidance

Suggested 75 minutes: 10 for purpose and vocabulary, 15 for chronology, 15 for materials/categories, 15 for paired labels, 15 for independent caption and 5 for reflection. Supply H1–H4 and paper or accessible drawing tools. Model observation versus interpretation before using the photograph description. No image or private archive is required.

Build a timeline that preserves meaning

Expected evidence: 312 BCE Via Appia development begins; 1942 Alaska pioneer road completed; 1943 permanent location completed; 1946 Canadian portion responsibility transferred; 1948 civilian non-military opening; 1956 U.S. highway legislation. “Pioneer road” and “permanent location” are different milestones. Chronology alone cannot establish consultation, effects or all perspectives.

Match material, method and purpose

Expected evidence: Materials: earth, cut stone, gravel, timber logs, concrete, asphalt mixture. Asphalt cement is the bituminous binder in asphalt; Portland cement is used in the cement-based binding system in concrete. Methods: closely laid crosswise logs; delivery/placement/compaction. Purposes: moving timber, military access, travel to a community facility. Accept an explanation that different designs/materials can serve the same purpose. A photo question might concern date, location, scale, layers, use or author.

Explain a road without inventing its history

Expected evidence: Observable: logs across a route; truck in background; wet-looking appearance in the supplied description. Possible corduroy construction is a supported interpretation. Logging purpose, owner, actual moisture condition, date and location are not established. The truck does not prove logging.

Compare two explanations

Expected evidence: H2 supports the specific wartime-access explanation. It does not support a universal war-only claim; H1 and H3 also identify trade and daily movement. 1948 − 1942 = 6 years. H2 records work/responsibility milestones within that interval.

Connect a historical method to a modern sequence

Expected evidence: One panel distinguishes timber/crosswise-log method from logging purpose. The other gives mixture delivery, paver/screed placement and roller compaction with preparation/checks acknowledged. Do not score drawing polish or accept an invented operating setting.

Independent transfer: a careful public caption

Expected evidence: A valid caption integrates a correct date with a defined event, distinguishes method/material/purpose, gives evidence for demand and names a limitation. It may discuss public-record limits without asking learners to supply cultural knowledge.

Model caption: “The Alaska Highway's pioneer road was completed in 1942 as a wartime land-access project; its permanent location was completed in 1943. Roads can later support other uses, including civilian travel and trade. Material, method and purpose describe different things: timber logs are a material, corduroy is a construction form, and logging is a road use. Modern asphalt paving combines manufactured mixture, delivery, placement and compaction. These examples show changes in techniques and needs, but they do not establish a single history for every road or describe every community's experience. A local account needs its own evidence and appropriate permission.”

Unit 3 · Answer and teaching guidance

Use 15 minutes for ingredients and the flow visual, 15 for plant comparison, 15 for product clarification, 20 for recycling routes, 10 for the counter model and 15 for revision/digital note. Prepare 100 counters or a labelled paper grid; count categories, not a real mixture. Provide an actual device turn for “Make a retrievable technology note” if Digital is assessed.

Explain the ingredients to a new learner

Expected evidence: Asphalt cement and asphalt binder name the binding material in this introductory context. Aggregate is mineral material; asphalt binder binds the asphalt mixture; process fuel supplies energy and must not be assumed to be an ingredient. “Cement” may refer to asphalt cement or Portland cement. An additive may alter a specified property; its purpose and suitability require the actual product and approved mixture requirements. A pit commonly supplies unconsolidated material; a quarry commonly extracts rock that is processed. Neither source name guarantees suitable aggregate. Source suitability needs specification/material-property evidence.

Compare two process records

Expected evidence: B indicates batches; D indicates a continuous drum-mix process in the simplified case. Shared stages include supplied materials, proportioning/mixing, storage or loading and haul. A drying drum alone does not distinguish the two. Figure LA-4 shows separate weighed batches versus a continuing measured material stream; some continuous facilities use a separate dryer and continuous mixer.

Write the clarification before the order

Expected evidence: Ask for full product name/grade, current approved design or project specification, supplier/product confirmation and relevant safety/handling information through the responsible person. The undefined “Group A / C” and “altered oil” labels require an explicit clarification; do not infer a grade or product from them. No grade is approved by the task.

Trace two reuse pathways

Expected evidence: Plant route: recovery/processing → suitable plant input → approved mixture/use. Hot-in-place route: specialist coordinated process works on existing pavement in place. Both need applicable approved process, suitability and quality evidence. Removal is not proof of later reuse.

Calculate a fictional material record

Expected evidence: 80%, 15%, 5%; sum 100%. The recovered-mixture category includes more than one constituent, so the model does not isolate total binder content. The counters are not mass or a production recipe.

Respond to a changed process description

Expected evidence: Remove the unsupported plant-haul label and show the in-place route; retain the idea of recovering/reusing existing material. Identify equipment/process details as pending. Observe each learner's correction and handover.

Make a retrievable technology note

Expected evidence: The learner controls the wording, saves and retrieves a versioned file and explains the distinctions. Paper composition supports Writing but does not demonstrate save/reopen. Figure LA-3: 7 Hopper (holds supplied material); 10 Screed Extension (extends working width); 11 End Gate (helps form the mat edge); 12 High-Speed Rubber Tracks (travel component, not a speed instruction). Figure LA-5 route: hopper receives → slat conveyors move rearward → augers distribute across width → screed places/shapes the mat. Conveying rearward and distributing laterally are distinct functions. Check two selected labels against the photo. Retain model 8680, manual 1033428-SOP, printed p. 3-7 / PDF p. 43, and serial scope 365152 and above. The image does not establish operating settings, current condition or applicability to another machine.

Review the AI-assisted technology note and study media

The checked note must preserve the plant/process distinctions, component names, material route and unresolved product request. Inspect the actual DOCX/Markdown, PDF/HTML and SVG: a filename in a chat is not evidence of a created file. The audit must connect each technical claim to a supplied source and retain unknowns. Reject invented temperatures, mix proportions or machine settings.

The short recording must accurately distinguish hopper, slat conveyors, augers and screed; it includes a question, pause, answer and transcript. If video is created, check caption timing, readability and whether the picture supports the spoken explanation. Record the actual tool, saved formats, checks, corrections and partner’s explanation. An accurate script demonstrates script work; award recording evidence only after playback of an actual audio/video file. Observe each learner’s own file and audit actions.

Unit 4 · Answer and teaching guidance

Suggested 90 minutes: role mapping 15, safety case 20, licences 15, handover 10, schedule 15 and development/enquiry 15. Allow spoken planning before authored writing. Keep every role-play in the classroom; provide no simulated machine signals. Evaluate observable actions and source distinctions rather than confidence, accent or posture.

Make a role and dependency map

Expected evidence: Broad functions and meaningful dependencies: haul supplies mixture; paver places; screed helps control the placed layer; raker supports handwork/finishing; roller compacts; milling removes/profiles existing material. A handover identifies status or information, not an invented signal. W1 authorizes no site access or operation.

Choose the responsible next step

Expected evidence: Equipment-name knowledge is insufficient workplace-specific orientation. Learner asks for supervisor/contact, relevant hazard and emergency information, task instruction/demonstration and the appropriate communication arrangements. The employer's obligation is not transferred to the learner.

Separate four kinds of permission

Expected evidence: Admission: provider's current intake requirements. Licence: ICBC/applicable legal requirements for the actual vehicle/activity. Competence: appropriate training and assessment records. Assignment: responsible employer/site authorization. Class 5/7 in a provider prerequisite cannot authorize every truck or machine.

Repair a rushed handover

Expected evidence: Example: “The role map is ready for review, but licensing is still pending. We can print the classroom explanation with that question marked open and ask the responsible person before adding an authorization claim.” Observe the receiver's check-back and each contribution.

Check time and revise the plan

Expected evidence: 18:00–24:00 is 6 h; 00:00–02:00 is 2 h; elapsed 8 h. Subtract 0.5 h = 7.5 paid hours in the fictional record. Add Wednesday 6 h = 13.5 h. Questions may concern overtime, cancellation/travel pay, breaks, fatigue controls or actual schedule arrangements. No legal entitlement follows from these invented inputs.

Prepare a useful enquiry and growth plan

Expected evidence: Collective bargaining is negotiation between an employer and workers represented by a union about employment terms; the applicable parties, agreement and dates must be checked. Forecast demand and attendance do not guarantee hiring, continued work or qualification. Questions identify the actual agreement/terms and role-specific pathway; the goal includes a task, evidence, support, check date and responsible reviewer. A saved/reopened file is separate from a paper enquiry. Do not require sending, personal employment disclosure or joining any organization.

Unit 5 · Answer and teaching guidance

Use 120 minutes: project/document register 15, three-view vocabulary 25, modelled quantity 20, independent check 20, revision 25 and handover 15. Provide the complete P-01/Q-01/SP-01 text, calculator, blank paper and the cross-section visual. No physical measurements of the printed image are valid inputs. Offer unit cards and a partly completed quantity table when needed. Record support.

Sort use, ownership and authority

Expected evidence: Private facility access; Cedar Learning Society owner; Valley Surface Ltd. contractor; community fund contributor. Use/category does not by itself adopt a specification. Actual signed contract and authorized current documents determine applicability.

Choose the right view

Expected evidence: Plan: 50 m horizontal length/6 m width. Profile: elevation change along centreline. Section: 40 mm calculation-layer thickness. 100.50 m is an elevation relative to the illustrative datum, not a layer depth.

Describe the road form

Expected evidence: Crown raised transverse form; shoulder road edge area; open ditch drainage channel; curb/gutter contained edge/drainage arrangement; sidewalk pedestrian route; runoff surface-water movement; superelevation cross-slope arrangement on curves; grader equipment. Figure LA-6: granular base supports the asphalt surface; subbase is an additional supporting layer where specified; prepared subgrade is the prepared ground beneath the pavement structure. Other structures exist; the drawing supplies no P-01 design dimensions. Questions may concern outlet, base preparation, approved slope, services or required materials. Do not supply invented design answers.

Check the quantity and record

Expected evidence: Thickness 0.040 m; area 300 m²; volume 12 m³; model mass 24 t. Records 8 + 8 + 7.6 = 23.6 t, 0.4 t below the model quantity. Profile rise 0.50 m/50 m=1%. Neither record totals nor an invented factor establish actual placed quantity, thickness, density, acceptance or payment.

Recalculate after the revision

Expected evidence: Revision C area 360 m²; volume 14.4 m³; model mass 28.8 t. Increases 60 m², 2.4 m³, 4.8 t. Existing record total 23.6 t is 5.2 t below the revised model quantity. Profile rise 0.60 m/60 m=1%. Keep the old record and identify which revision each calculation uses.

Review and hand over

Expected evidence: Handover includes current revision C, 14.4 m³ geometric model, 28.8 t at fictional 2.00 t/m³, 23.6 t recorded, 5.2 t arithmetic difference, and the limits on a real order or acceptance decision. Each person demonstrates both checks over the two cases.

Model quantity note: “P-01 revision C shows a 60 m by 6 m rectangle with a 0.040 m calculation layer. Area is 360 m² and geometric volume 14.4 m³. At the supplied fictional 2.00 t/m³ factor the model mass is 28.8 t. The unchanged classroom records total 23.6 t, a 5.2 t difference. I checked units and recalculated from the new length. These model values do not establish an actual order, approved density or acceptance result.”

Unit 6 · Answer and teaching guidance

Use 90 minutes: claim reading 15, material/lightweight-fill discussion 15, cost calculation 10, supplier enquiry 10, option table 20, changed condition 10 and brief/check 10. Supply N1–N8 and the retained public-source excerpts. Learners need no product samples, site data or external accounts. Distinguish an evidence-supported mechanism from a project performance prediction.

Improve three claims

Expected evidence: Paving-stone permeability depends on the complete system; porous asphalt with a reservoir still needs site/drainage design and maintenance; recycling alone cannot establish total impact. Each revision names a condition and a question rather than simply adding “may” to a vague claim.

Read the cost boundary

Expected evidence: $1,145,000 − $633,000 = $512,000. Limits include historical 2016/2017 context, California/U.S. geography/currency context, project-specific quantities/design, missing installation/overhead/profit and no current B.C. supplier quotes. Technical suitability depends on site/design requirements, not the cheapest listed total. The example concerns lightweight fill; that role cannot be relabelled as approved pavement sub-base without design and material evidence.

Make a product enquiry

Expected evidence: Questions identify actual product/system, intended use, relevant tested performance, maintenance, compatibility and approval. Pattern questions include access/visibility and permission for any protected imagery. No universal epoxy, colour or pattern performance is accepted without evidence.

Compare options against the actual need

Expected evidence: Porous asphalt relates to water management, subject to site/loading/drainage/maintenance study. Coloured asphalt may relate to wayfinding; it does not answer infiltration or structural needs by colour alone. EPS geofoam relates to embankment/load conditions that have not been established for this courtyard. Accept different investigation shortlists when evidence and limits are explicit. Appropriate review may include qualified pavement/civil/geotechnical/design professionals and the facility's maintenance decision-maker.

Adapt to a new constraint

Expected evidence: Lack of a specialized-maintenance commitment is relevant to clogging/maintenance risk in the porous-asphalt reference. A reasonable revision makes maintenance feasibility a condition for investigation or postpones that option. It does not establish an alternative's suitability.

Build an evidence brief and test its clarity

Expected evidence: The brief recommends an investigation, with a known mechanism, a case-specific unknown, source date and next decision. Reader can distinguish a recommendation to study from permission to construct. Record actual file actions separately.

Model brief: “For the fictional 600 m² courtyard, investigate whether a porous-asphalt/stone-reservoir system could meet the surface-water and loading requirements. FHWA's2015 brief describes the mechanism and identifies clogging and specialized maintenance as considerations. We have no infiltration/geotechnical results or maintenance commitment, so we cannot recommend construction. First obtain site/design review and a feasible maintenance plan. Evaluate wayfinding separately: colour may help a communication purpose but does not establish drainage performance. Keep environmental comparisons within a defined life-cycle boundary and use project-specific information before ranking options.”

Pilot evidence
