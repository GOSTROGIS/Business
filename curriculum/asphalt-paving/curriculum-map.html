What is ready to teach and review

The Roadbuilding foundations learner book and teaching and answer guide contain six authored Line A classroom units, 37 activities, worked examples and separate answer guidance. The original 18-activity opening case remains available for introductory equipment, document, numeracy and teamwork practice. It uses explicitly fictional classroom data. The new industry-evidence unit adds dated public facts and teaches learners to distinguish those facts from invented practice inputs.

The whole-program status map records all 68 occupational competencies. Line A classroom units are authored; the 62 later competencies do not have complete technical units here. F2/F3 receive a foundation bridge in the road-document unit. Independent qualified technical review, local delivery agreement and classroom pilot remain pending. Authored activities and completed classroom work do not establish workplace competence or equipment endorsement.

Follow the nine A1 learning tasks

A1's nine trade learning tasks and the nine Skills for Success are different lists. They are connected through meaningful activities rather than matched one-to-one. A1 learning-task references below come from outline PDF 22 / printed 20.

A1 task Trade content, paraphrased Delivered treatment Application or review still needed
A1.1 Paving and equipment, from pit to finished product Opening reading, equipment/function cards and materials-flow sequence; A3 develops the process. Qualified review of vocabulary and examples; equipment-specific training remains separate.
A1.2 Basic history of how roads were created Opening introduction followed by the sourced historical work in A2. Review local relevance and invite any place-specific contribution through an appropriate local process.
A1.3 Roads as infrastructure Opening tasks identify services, movement and connections supported by roads; later cases connect these purposes to decisions. Choose suitable local examples with learners or partners.
A1.4 Provincial paving resources, including gravel and aggregates Opening vocabulary and the materials/process work in A3. Check any specific resource or product against an approved current source.
A1.5 Users of paving, recreation and trade Opening activities include multiple road users and purposes, including people who do not drive. Extend the context without requiring personal location disclosure.
A1.6 Relevant industry-size, economics and employer statistics The opening fictional table teaches calculation. LA1-01–02 use dated WorkBC construction data and Statistics Canada road-industry business-location counts with measure, geography and period. Statistical locations are not unique employers; construction and road-industry measures are not asphalt-only totals. Retain those limits and check changes before delivery.
A1.7 Other road types, including ice roads Opening reading compares broad contexts; A2 develops road-type and historical interpretation. Local or seasonal examples need suitable evidence; no travel-safety assessment is claimed.
A1.8 Regulations and funding The opening fictional brief introduces roles. LA1-03–04 interpret selected B.C. orientation provisions, highway-specification information and a reported project-funding record. Confirm actual contract/application requirements and resolve the historical outline's undefined “EPS” label without guessing.
A1.9 Environmental comparison of asphalt and concrete LA1-05–06 use evidenced recovery pathways and a defined fictional decision brief to test an unsupported whole-life claim. No matched current B.C. project life-cycle ranking is established; that requires equivalent designs, complete assumptions and qualified review.

Teach and observe the nine skills

Reading, Writing, Numeracy, Digital, Problem Solving, Communication, Collaboration, Adaptability, and Creativity and Innovation recur across the units. The teaching map, activity skill labels and answer-guide criteria identify observable work rather than assign official proficiency levels.

Learning context Skills in use Evidence to keep
Opening session 1 · Understand the industry Reading, Communication, Collaboration A source detail, explained equipment/function match, check-back and individual contribution.
Opening session 2 · Read and share the numbers Numeracy, Digital, Writing Checked method and units, saved table and an accurate audience-focused summary.
Opening session 3 · Respond to a changed brief Problem Solving, Adaptability, Creativity and Innovation A reasoned response, observable revision and tested explanatory aid.
Line A · Investigate, explain and revise All nine, selected according to each activity Dated source records, calculations, document registers, individual exchanges, actual digital actions, comparison criteria and revisions after feedback.

A group product needs an individual contribution record. A correct calculation needs its units and assumptions. A digital claim needs actual tool use. Record the task version, support, observed action, result and next practice.

Delivery time and technical authority

The six new units propose 555 minutes, or 9 hours 15 minutes, including supported foundational-skills practice. The opening case separately proposes three 60-minute sessions. An instructor should plan their combination deliberately rather than assume both allocations are mandatory technical-training hours.

The supplied November 2014 profile records 120 technical-training hours. The outline's 3% share gives 3.6 hours for all A1–A6, not for A1 alone. The IUOE Local 115 public page's statement of 140 accumulated hours was retrieved and verified as a published statement on 29 October 2026. Its relationship to the historical profile and the ACCESS delivery agreement remains a pathway question. The technical handoff keeps these records separate and provides review and pilot fields.

The research register records public evidence and limits. The specifications validation record and manual access-status record document the acquired editions, checks and remaining gaps. A download or link alone does not establish technical approval or project applicability.
