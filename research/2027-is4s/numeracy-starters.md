# IS4S: 11 worked numeracy starters

[Back to the research pack](README.md)

All quantities, schedules and records below are invented for learning. These are proposed preparation activities, not trade specifications, clinical guidance or credential assessments. Calculations use only the givens; real technical parameters and procedures come from the program's instructor. Each activity can be discussed aloud, drawn or completed with accessible calculation tools.

## 1. Welding — plan repeated blank lengths

- **Givens:** A paper planning task needs four blanks. Each requires 125 mm of usable length plus a fictional 3 mm planning allowance per blank. Available stock length: 600 mm. This allowance is a supplied exercise value, not a cutting standard.
- **Learner prompt:** How much total length should the plan allocate, and how much remains?
- **Worked answer:** One blank: 125 mm + 3 mm = 128 mm. Four blanks: 4 × 128 mm = **512 mm**. Remaining: 600 mm − 512 mm = **88 mm**.
- **Estimate/check:** Four usable lengths need 500 mm; four allowances add 12 mm, giving 512 mm. Check: 512 mm + 88 mm = 600 mm.
- **Common misconception:** Adding the 3 mm allowance only once gives 503 mm; “per blank” requires four allowances.
- **Scaffold / stretch:** Draw four labelled length bars. Stretch: six blanks require 768 mm, so the stated 600 mm is 168 mm short.
- **Next skill — Reading:** Underline the words that tell you how often to apply the allowance, then explain your interpretation to a partner.

## 2. Asphalt Paving — convert depth before finding volume

- **Givens:** A fictional rectangular classroom model represents an area 8 m long and 3 m wide, with a uniform depth of 50 mm. Ignore compaction, waste and all material properties for this exercise; 50 mm is not a recommended paving depth.
- **Learner prompt:** What geometric volume does the model represent, in cubic metres?
- **Worked answer:** Depth: 50 mm ÷ 1,000 = 0.05 m. Area: 8 m × 3 m = 24 m². Volume: 24 m² × 0.05 m = **1.2 m³**.
- **Estimate/check:** A depth of 0.05 m is one twentieth of a metre; 24 ÷ 20 = 1.2. Reverse check: 1.2 m³ ÷ 24 m² = 0.05 m.
- **Common misconception:** Multiplying by 50 without converting mm to m mixes units and makes the answer 1,000 times too large.
- **Scaffold / stretch:** Use a conversion strip showing 1,000 mm = 1 m. Stretch: at the same width and fictional depth, a 10 m length represents 1.5 m³.
- **Next skill — Digital:** Build a three-input spreadsheet with visible units and explain how you would detect an incorrectly entered depth.

## 3. Ironworker — reconcile an inventory record

- **Givens:** A fictional stores record lists 12 identical components, each with a supplied mass of 7.5 kg. These values are for inventory arithmetic only and provide no lifting or handling limit.
- **Learner prompt:** What total component mass belongs in the inventory record?
- **Worked answer:** 12 × 7.5 kg = (10 × 7.5 kg) + (2 × 7.5 kg) = 75 kg + 15 kg = **90 kg**.
- **Estimate/check:** Using 8 kg each gives 96 kg, so 90 kg is reasonable. Reverse check: 90 kg ÷ 12 = 7.5 kg each.
- **Common misconception:** Treating 7.5 kg as 7 kg and 5 g; the 0.5 kg is 500 g.
- **Scaffold / stretch:** Group the components into pairs, each totalling 15 kg. Stretch: if the count is corrected to 10, the total becomes 75 kg, a decrease of 15 kg.
- **Next skill — Collaboration:** One partner checks the count and another checks the supplied unit mass; agree on the corrected inventory entry and record who checked each input.

## 4. Carpentry — find a rectangle's diagonal

- **Givens:** A drawing shows a true rectangle with adjacent sides of 3 m and 4 m. The task is geometry practice; it does not establish construction tolerances or approval criteria.
- **Learner prompt:** What is the distance between opposite corners?
- **Worked answer:** The diagonal is the hypotenuse of a right triangle. d² = (3 m)² + (4 m)² = 9 m² + 16 m² = 25 m². Therefore d = √25 m² = **5 m**.
- **Estimate/check:** The diagonal must be longer than the 4 m side and shorter than the 7 m route along two sides. Also, 5² = 3² + 4².
- **Common misconception:** Adding 3 m + 4 m finds the two-side route, not the direct diagonal.
- **Scaffold / stretch:** Draw and label the right triangle inside the rectangle. Stretch: doubling both sides to 6 m and 8 m doubles the diagonal to 10 m.
- **Next skill — Communication:** Explain why a 7 m answer represents a different path, using the drawing rather than only naming a formula.

## 5. Piping — combine straight segment lengths

- **Givens:** A classroom diagram contains three separate straight segments: 0.80 m, 1,250 mm and 0.65 m. Add only these stated lengths; the exercise includes no fittings, offsets, joining allowances or code requirements.
- **Learner prompt:** What is their combined length in metres and millimetres?
- **Worked answer:** 1,250 mm = 1.25 m. Total: 0.80 m + 1.25 m + 0.65 m = **2.70 m**, or **2,700 mm**.
- **Estimate/check:** Round to 0.8 m + 1.3 m + 0.7 m = 2.8 m, close to 2.7 m. Separate check in millimetres: 800 + 1,250 + 650 = 2,700 mm.
- **Common misconception:** Moving the decimal only two places converts 1,250 mm to 12.5 m; millimetres to metres requires division by 1,000.
- **Scaffold / stretch:** Rewrite all three values in one unit before adding. Stretch: two identical sets total 5.40 m.
- **Next skill — Writing:** Produce a short material-length note with a clear heading, one consistent unit and the calculation used.

## 6. Tower Crane — standardize a fictional load record

- **Givens:** A classroom record has three mass entries: 750 kg, 0.45 metric tonnes and 125 kg. One metric tonne (t) equals 1,000 kg. This is record-conversion practice only; the record is incomplete for real lifting and establishes no crane capacity, safe load, rigging choice or lift authorization.
- **Learner prompt:** Rewrite the entries in kilograms and total them, then express the total in tonnes.
- **Worked answer:** 0.45 t × 1,000 kg/t = 450 kg. Total: 750 kg + 450 kg + 125 kg = **1,325 kg**. In tonnes: 1,325 kg ÷ 1,000 kg/t = **1.325 t**.
- **Estimate/check:** The first two entries total 1,200 kg; adding 125 kg gives a little over 1.3 t. Reverse check: 1.325 t × 1,000 kg/t = 1,325 kg.
- **Common misconception:** Reading 0.45 t as 45 kg produces a tenfold conversion error for that entry.
- **Scaffold / stretch:** Make a two-column “given unit / kilograms” table. Stretch: if the 125 kg entry was a duplicate record, the corrected total is 1,200 kg or 1.2 t.
- **Next skill — Problem solving:** Explain why a correct sum alone cannot answer whether any crane can lift these items; list the missing information you would refer to the qualified instructor.

## 7. Marine Mechanical Technician — calculate elapsed workshop time

- **Givens:** An invented workshop observation log starts at 09:40 and ends at 10:25 on the same day, with no breaks. No engine or equipment performance specification is implied.
- **Learner prompt:** How long did the observation last, in minutes and decimal hours?
- **Worked answer:** From 09:40 to 10:00 is 20 minutes; from 10:00 to 10:25 is 25 minutes. Total: 20 + 25 = **45 minutes**. Decimal hours: 45 ÷ 60 = **0.75 hours**.
- **Estimate/check:** The interval is less than an hour and equals three quarters of an hour. Check: 09:40 + 45 minutes = 10:25.
- **Common misconception:** Subtracting 9.40 from 10.25 as decimal numbers gives 0.85; clock minutes use groups of 60, not 100.
- **Scaffold / stretch:** Mark the interval on a clock or number line with 10:00 as a bridge. Stretch: an end time of 11:15 would give 95 minutes, or 1 hour 35 minutes.
- **Next skill — Adaptability:** Update the log when the supplied end time changes, and explain which part of your original calculation remains useful.

## 8. Security — read a shift schedule

- **Givens:** An invented same-day schedule runs from 14:30 to 22:00 and includes a 30-minute break from 18:00 to 18:30. This exercise asks for scheduled time excluding the break; it makes no claim about paid time, employment rules or required breaks.
- **Learner prompt:** How many hours and minutes remain after subtracting the listed break?
- **Worked answer:** 14:30 to 22:00 is 7 hours 30 minutes, or 450 minutes. Subtract 30 minutes: 450 − 30 = **420 minutes**, or **7 hours**.
- **Estimate/check:** 7.5 hours − 0.5 hours = 7 hours. Add back the break: 420 + 30 = 450 minutes.
- **Common misconception:** Writing 7 hours 30 minutes as 7.30 decimal hours; 30 minutes is 0.5 hours.
- **Scaffold / stretch:** Split the timeline at 18:00 and 18:30. Stretch: ending at 22:15 with the same break produces 7 hours 15 minutes excluding the break.
- **Next skill — Writing:** Write a factual two-sentence schedule note that distinguishes start/end times, the break and the calculated time, without adding any invented incident or personal information.

## 9. Metal Fabrication — calculate material utilization on paper

- **Givens:** A paper model of a rectangular sheet measures 120 cm × 80 cm. Six rectangles measuring 40 cm × 20 cm are placed on it in a three-column, two-row arrangement. Assume zero cutting loss for this paper exercise; these are not workshop cutting instructions.
- **Learner prompt:** What area do the six rectangles occupy, and what percentage of the sheet is that?
- **Worked answer:** Sheet area: 120 cm × 80 cm = 9,600 cm². One rectangle: 40 cm × 20 cm = 800 cm². Six rectangles: 6 × 800 cm² = **4,800 cm²**. Utilization: 4,800 ÷ 9,600 × 100% = **50%**. Unused area: **4,800 cm²**.
- **Estimate/check:** The layout occupies the full 120 cm width and 40 cm of the 80 cm height, exactly half the sheet. Used plus unused area: 4,800 + 4,800 = 9,600 cm².
- **Common misconception:** Adding length and width finds neither area nor utilization; area requires multiplication and square units.
- **Scaffold / stretch:** Draw the sheet as a 3 × 4 grid of equal rectangles and shade six cells. Stretch: nine cells use 7,200 cm², or 75%.
- **Next skill — Creativity and innovation:** Draw another arrangement of the six rectangles, then explain how you know it fits and has the same occupied area.

## 10. Educational Assistant — interpret a visual activity schedule

- **Givens:** An invented group activity schedule starts at 09:00: welcome 10 minutes, reading 15 minutes, making 20 minutes, break 15 minutes, in that order. It contains no real learner information and makes no recommendation about an individual's support needs.
- **Learner prompt:** What fraction of the one-hour schedule is reading, and when does making begin?
- **Worked answer:** Total: 10 + 15 + 20 + 15 = **60 minutes**. Reading fraction: 15/60 = **1/4**, or **25%**. Welcome ends 09:10; reading ends 09:25, so making begins at **09:25**. The full schedule ends at **10:00**.
- **Estimate/check:** Four 15-minute blocks fill an hour. Check the timeline: 09:25 + 20 minutes = 09:45, then + 15 minutes = 10:00.
- **Common misconception:** Confusing an activity's duration with its clock start time, or using 15/100 because an hour is mistaken for 100 minutes.
- **Scaffold / stretch:** Show four quarter-hour blocks beside the activity timeline. Stretch: transfer five minutes from making to reading; reading becomes 20/60 = 1/3 of the unchanged hour.
- **Next skill — Communication:** Explain the schedule using both words and a visual sequence, then invite a partner to show when the next activity begins.

## 11. Nursing — total a synthetic intake practice record

- **Givens:** A fictional arithmetic record lists 250 mL, 0.15 L and 100 mL, all marked as measured intake volumes in the exercise. There is no real patient, fluid target, medication or clinical decision. One litre equals 1,000 mL.
- **Learner prompt:** Rewrite all entries in millilitres and find the total in millilitres and litres.
- **Worked answer:** 0.15 L × 1,000 mL/L = 150 mL. Total: 250 mL + 150 mL + 100 mL = **500 mL**. In litres: 500 mL ÷ 1,000 mL/L = **0.5 L**.
- **Estimate/check:** 250 mL is one quarter of a litre; 150 mL + 100 mL adds another quarter, making half a litre. Independent unit check: 0.25 L + 0.15 L + 0.10 L = 0.50 L.
- **Common misconception:** Treating 0.15 L as 15 mL; it equals 150 mL. Do not infer clinical appropriateness from the total.
- **Scaffold / stretch:** Use a 1 L diagram divided into ten 100 mL sections. Stretch: an additional fictional 0.20 L entry brings the total to 700 mL or 0.7 L.
- **Next skill — Digital:** Enter the synthetic values into a practice table with separate value and unit fields; compare the result with the hand calculation before marking it checked.
