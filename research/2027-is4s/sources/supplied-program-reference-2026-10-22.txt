Program Planning References
Research checked: October 21, 2026. Internal reference for ACCESS staff: program context, scope, entry pathways, learning materials and participant-support considerations for ACCESS cohorts. The BCIT Nursing reference was added October 22, 2026.
How to use these references: ACCESS creates dedicated cohorts with partner institutions; participants in these cohorts are not in direct competition with the general applicant pool. These sections give staff occupational and training context. Public course pages describe standard programs and academic preparation; their competitive selection processes are not descriptions of ACCESS cohort arrangements. Dedicated seats do not imply waived academic, clinical, safety or professional requirements. Program duration, curriculum and credentials remain distinct from cohort scheduling and seat allocation.
Curriculum context: For designated trades, SkilledTradesBC Program Outlines set out competency outcomes, practical achievement criteria, assessment rules, facilities, equipment and instructor requirements. Program Profiles describe registration and completion pathways; standards notifications identify changes. The summaries below give staff an overview of this framework rather than a complete course syllabus. Source: SkilledTradesBC standards updates.
Source and book-list limits: Some provider pages display older intakes. For the BCIT trade sections, the catalogue was accessible but individual program pages could not be fully retrieved; their exact foundation admission thresholds remain unverified. The nursing section uses the BSN program page and a dated library booklist. No cohort-specific 2027 purchasing lists were verified. Historical references are labelled below; get instructor approval of titles, editions, access codes and quantities before buying. Source: BCIT entrance requirements. Fallback: BCIT trades catalogue (fallback).
Welding
Tentative cohort: January 25–March 19, 2027 | Trades | UAPIC.
Course and scope: The scheduled welding cohort runs for eight weeks. UAPICBC’s published 28-week Welding Foundation provides context for the broader training pathway. Its page displays a 2023 intake, so the course information is background rather than a 2027 intake notice. Course website: UAPICBC Welding Foundation.
Prerequisites: The published foundation minimum is Grade 10 completion, including English, Math and Science 10; Grade 12 or GED is recommended. These are published foundation requirements; assessments, equivalencies and cohort-specific entry arrangements are separate intake details. Source: UAPICBC welding entry requirements.
SkilledTradesBC coverage: Foundation competencies cover occupational safety, trade mathematics, measuring/layout, tools and rigging; cutting and gouging; welding processes; drawings/symbols; and metallurgy. The processes, joint types, welding positions and practical tests vary with the training level and course scope. Source: Welder program outline and profile.
Books: UAPICBC lists CWB textbooks as included, without titles or editions. Historical fallback: the April 2020 foundation outline lists the Welder Training Program Level C Package (CPUB230M). That reference section says it is under review and directs learners to their provider. This is not a verified 2027 shopping list. Sources: UAPICBC book information; Welder historical references.
Staff notes: Participant-support considerations include PPE and consumables, shop safety, respiratory protection, practical assessment and progression. CWB performance testing and course attendance are separate outcomes; the cohort’s assessment plan is the reference for any testing included.
Asphalt Paving
Tentative cohort: February 1–March 12, 2027 | Trades | Six-week program; provider not identified in the table.
Course and scope: The scheduled asphalt paving cohort runs for six weeks. IUOE Local 115’s four-week / 140-hour Asphalt Laydown Operator program provides a reference for the occupational training component. The schedule does not name a provider or break down the six-week curriculum. Course website: IUOE Asphalt Laydown Operator.
Prerequisites: IUOE lists a valid Class 5 or 7 driver’s licence, at least 70% on its Online Trades Assessment, an application, current résumé and ICBC driver’s abstract. Grade 12 is recommended. These describe IUOE’s published entry pathway; the schedule does not include cohort-specific eligibility criteria. Source: IUOE asphalt entry requirements.
SkilledTradesBC coverage: The trade outline covers paving principles, safe work around equipment, asphalt products/mixes, road structures, and milling, reclaiming, paving, screed, raking and compaction operations. The Program Profile sets out technical training, work-based hours, assessments and endorsement requirements; these extend beyond cohort attendance. Source: Asphalt profile and program outline.
Books: Historical references in the linked outline include Common Core and equipment-specific manuals, The Asphalt Handbook MS-4 (7th edition), and 2006 Design Build Specifications. Obtain current student materials, equipment manuals and applicable specifications; do not treat a historical specification as current regulatory guidance. Source: Asphalt historical reference list.
Staff notes: Staff considerations include the training site, equipment time per learner, PPE, transportation, winter-weather contingency and workplace-hour records. Training completion, trade certification and employment are separate stages in the participant’s pathway.
Ironworker
Tentative cohort: March 15–May 7, 2027 | Trades | BCIT.
Course and scope: The scheduled BCIT ironworker cohort runs for eight weeks. BCIT’s 23-week Ironworker Foundation is the broader training reference. Generalist and Reinforcing are distinct trade pathways with different curriculum and qualification requirements. Links: BCIT Ironworker course page; Ironworker training reference.
Prerequisites: Exact current BCIT academic thresholds were not verified. As an industry preparation reference—not a confirmed BCIT entry rule—the College of the BC Building Trades recommends English 12 (50%) or Communications 12 (67%), and Math 10. Ask BCIT for its current transcript, equivalency and assessment checklist. Source: BC Building Trades ironworker preparation.
SkilledTradesBC coverage: The Generalist pathway covers drawings/specifications, rigging/hoisting, structural-steel erection and connections, reinforcing concrete, and inspection of structures. The revised outline/profile took effect January 1, 2026; the current Red Seal exam aligns with the 2025 occupational standard. Reinforcing has a separate trade pathway. Sources: Ironworker Generalist resources; Generalist Program Outline.
Books: The September 2025 outline’s provider reference list includes Steel Erection Level 1 (BCIT), IPT’s Crane and Rigging Training Manual, CRSI Placing Reinforcing Bars Manual, RSIC Manual of Standard Practice and SSTC Structural Bolting Handbook. These are provider references, not a verified student purchase list; obtain required editions from BCIT. Source: Ironworker reference materials.
Staff notes: Staff considerations include reinforcing versus structural work, practical rigging, fall protection, shop/site access and progression to apprenticeship. Individualized support planning is relevant to physically demanding work and work at heights. Assessment and credit records support next-step referrals.
Carpentry
Tentative cohort: March 22–May 14, 2027 | Trades | BCIT.
Course and scope: The scheduled BCIT carpentry cohort runs for eight weeks. BCIT’s Carpentry Framing and Forming Foundation provides context for entry-level construction training; foundation training and apprentice technical training are separate pathways. Course website: BCIT Carpentry course page.
Prerequisites: Exact foundation English/math thresholds were not verified; request BCIT’s current academic/assessment checklist. For apprentice technical training, BCIT requires active SkilledTradesBC apprentice registration and specified eligible Canadian status categories. Those apprenticeship rules should not automatically be applied to a foundation or introductory cohort. Source: BCIT Carpentry apprentice registration.
SkilledTradesBC coverage: The Carpenter outline covers drawings/building codes, safe hand/power-tool use, measurement/layout, foundations and formwork, floors/walls/roofs, and doors, stairs and finishing across its training levels. The referenced outline is effective April 1, 2024. Full apprenticeship and trade certification extend beyond the scheduled cohort. Source: Carpenter program outline and profile.
Books: Verified Fall 2026 example: Carpentry, 5th Canadian edition, text + workbook + MindTap package, by Vogt, ISBN 9781779545794. BCIT lists it for Carpentry Foundation and selected other courses; it is not a confirmed 2027 requirement. SkilledTradesBC lists 2018 or 2024 BC Building Code for Level 1–3 exams; ask which edition/format the instructor requires. Sources: BCIT Carpentry book package; Carpenter exam resources.
Staff notes: Staff considerations include the construction project, tools/PPE, assessment evidence, work experience and progression credit. The instructor’s code edition and materials list provide the teaching/testing reference; older textbooks are background resources rather than substitutes for current safety or code requirements.
Piping
Tentative cohort: May 17–July 9, 2027 | Trades | UAPIC.
Course and scope: The scheduled piping cohort runs for eight weeks. UAPICBC’s 21-week Piping Foundation introduces Plumbing, Steamfitting and Sprinklerfitting and provides context for the broader pathway. The published Level 1 credit applies to that full foundation program; the schedule does not specify cohort credit. The provider page displays older intakes. Course website: UAPICBC Piping Foundation.
Prerequisites: UAPICBC’s published foundation minimum is Grade 10 completion, including English, Math and Science 10; Grade 12 or GED is recommended. These are published foundation requirements; assessments, equivalencies and cohort-specific entry arrangements are separate intake details. Source: UAPICBC piping entry requirements.
SkilledTradesBC coverage: Piping spans distinct trades and training levels. Plumbing covers drawings/specifications, pipe selection/measurement/shaping/joining, fixtures and systems, and leak testing. Steamfitting and sprinkler fitting each have separate trade outlines; “piping” is not a single credential. Source: Plumber program outline and profile.
Books: UAPICBC says books are included but does not publish titles/editions on this page. Request the actual module list and instructor-approved codes. The Plumber resource page identifies National Plumbing Code references and CSA B149.1 for certain later-level exams; these are level-dependent, not automatic purchases for this cohort. Sources: UAPICBC piping books; Plumber code-book guidance.
Staff notes: Staff considerations include pipe materials and jointing processes, practical hours, tools/PPE, testing and progression. Steamfitter/Pipefitter is already a compulsory-certification trade, so registration or recognized-training arrangements are relevant to any workplace component. Source: SkilledTradesBC compulsory-trade guidance.
Tower Crane
Tentative cohort: July 5–August 27, 2027 | Trades | Listed as “IOUE Maple Ridge”; verify the intended partner is IUOE Local 115.
Course and scope: The scheduled tower crane cohort runs for eight weeks. IUOE’s Maple Ridge pathway lists Level 1 at five weeks and Level 2 at four weeks, separate from the cohort dates. SkilledTradesBC’s current model has two levels; the provider’s FAQ still refers to three. Course website: IUOE Tower Crane Operator.
Prerequisites: IUOE lists a minimum 70% Online Trades Assessment result, application, résumé and ICBC driver’s abstract; Grade 12 is recommended and an English competency check may apply. Its apprenticeship training requires tower-crane apprentice registration and an employer sponsor. These requirements apply to IUOE’s apprenticeship route, rather than serving as a general entry checklist for introductory training. Source: IUOE tower crane entry requirements.
SkilledTradesBC coverage: The outline covers safety, crane components, rigging, hoisting calculations, inspections/maintenance, lift planning, operations, and assembly/disassembly/reconfiguration/transport across the training levels. The full pathway includes 315 technical-training hours, 2,685 hours of crane-related experience, exams and a practical assessment. BC Crane Safety registration is also required. Sources: Tower Crane trade profile; Tower Crane Program Outline.
Books: The outline implemented October 1, 2024 lists the WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation, current CSA Z248 Code for Tower Cranes, and ANSI/ASME B30.3, B30.9, B30.10 and B30.20. Recommended references include Donald E. Dickie’s Rigging Manual and Crane Handbook. Confirm which resources the school supplies/licenses and the approved editions before purchasing. Source: Tower Crane reference materials.
Critical 2027 planning flag: SkilledTradesBC compulsory certification takes effect July 5, 2027—the cohort’s tentative start date. To continue working in the trade, workers must meet the registered-apprentice, trade-qualifier or certified-journeyperson pathway. This is separate from existing crane-safety obligations. Learner registration status, supervision and workplace arrangements are key placement considerations for staff. Source: SkilledTradesBC July 2027 crane requirements.
Marine Mechanical Technician
Tentative cohort: July 19–September 10, 2027 | Trades | BCIT.
Course and scope: The scheduled BCIT marine mechanical cohort runs for eight weeks. BCIT’s Marine Mechanical Technician Foundation provides background on entry-level training. The May 2021 SkilledTradesBC outline describes the full foundation pathway as 720 hours / 24 weeks, distinct from the scheduled cohort and from apprenticeship training. Links: BCIT Marine Mechanical course page; Marine Mechanical Program Outline.
Prerequisites: Current BCIT academic minimums and assessment alternatives were not verified. Request the correct foundation-versus-apprentice checklist, any sponsor/registration requirements and workshop access requirements. Recommended preparation includes measurement, technical reading, basic electrical concepts and mechanical problem-solving; these are planning suggestions, not verified admission rules.
SkilledTradesBC coverage: The trade outline covers safe yard/marina practices, tools/documentation, vessel water and electrical systems, hydraulics, metalwork, engines and support systems, drive trains, ignition, fuel and controls. Competencies and practical assessments are organized by training level. The trade page lists 20 weeks of technical training over two years and 4,800 work-based hours for apprenticeship. Source: Marine Mechanical trade requirements.
Books: Historical recommended references in the May 2021 outline include Nigel Calder’s Marine Diesel Engines: Maintenance, Troubleshooting, and Repair and Boatowner’s Mechanical & Electrical Manual; Everett Collier’s The Boatowner’s Guide to Corrosion; and John F. Dagel’s Diesel Engine Repair. These are outline references, not a verified BCIT intake list. Confirm editions and equipment-specific service manuals with BCIT. Source: Marine Mechanical historical reading list.
Staff notes: Staff considerations include the campus, engines/propulsion systems used for hands-on work, tools/PPE, environmental handling and employer connections. This reference concerns marine mechanical training, distinct from a marine-engineering officer credential; cohort completion and full apprenticeship qualification are separate outcomes.
Security
Tentative cohort: September 14–November 6, 2027 | Programs | Provider to be determined.
Course and scope: JIBC Basic Security Training (BST), delivered by JIBC or an approved school, is the reference pathway for security-guard training. Its 40 hours of theory plus assessment are distinct from the longer scheduled cohort. The schedule does not describe the additional cohort content. Course website: JIBC Basic Security Training.
Prerequisites: JIBC requires good spoken/written English. BST can be completed before age 19, but a Security Worker Licence requires age 19. Security checks are not required for BST; a criminal record check is part of licensing. Training completion does not authorize work. Source: JIBC admission and licensing guidance.
Required coverage: The approved curriculum covers professionalism/ethics, legal studies, report writing and personal safety. JIBC requires at least 70% on the assessment, completed within six months after theory. This is a security-licensing pathway, not a SkilledTradesBC apprenticeship. Source: JIBC curriculum and assessment.
Books: A current or historical intake-specific purchasing list was not verified. Ask the approved provider for its authorized BST participant manual and online materials; avoid purchasing an unofficial exam-preparation package as a substitute. Confirm whether materials are included in tuition.
Staff notes: Staff considerations include separate licensing and exam costs, identification, laptop access, exam arrangements and learner support. JIBC now uses its transcript as proof of completion and stopped issuing BST certificates on December 15, 2025. Source: JIBC completion and exam instructions.
Metal Fabrication
Tentative cohort: September 14–November 6, 2027 | Trades | BCIT.
Course and scope: The scheduled BCIT metal fabrication cohort runs for eight weeks. BCIT’s Metal Fabricator Foundation provides context for the SkilledTradesBC Metal Fabricator (Fitter) pathway. Metal fabrication, welding and sheet metal are distinct trade qualifications. Course website: BCIT Metal Fabricator course page.
Prerequisites: Current BCIT foundation English/math minimums and assessment alternatives were not verified. Foundation admissions and registered-apprentice entry are separate pathways. Suggested preparatory work includes measurement, fractions, geometry, drawings and safe shop practices; these are not asserted admission thresholds.
SkilledTradesBC coverage: The trade outline covers drawings/specifications, layout and pattern development, cutting/shaping/forming, fitting/assembly, fabrication processes and associated equipment across the training levels. The full apprenticeship lists 23 weeks of technical training over three years, 4,500 work-based hours and examinations. An eight-week cohort alone does not complete that pathway. Sources: Metal Fabricator trade profile; Metal Fabricator Program Outline.
Books: The April 2019 outline’s provider list names Metal Fabrication – a Practical Guide, 3rd edition, R. L. O’Con and R. H. Carr (2010), and IPT’s Metal Trades & Welding Training Manual, R. G. Garby and B. J. Ashton (2007), plus WorkSafeBC regulations and a student materials package. Recommended: IPT’s Guide to Blueprint Interpretation, G. E. Jacobs (2004). These are historical references, not a verified BCIT purchasing list. Source: Metal Fabricator historical references.
Staff notes: Staff considerations include machines and processes used, consumables, ventilation/PPE, layout and fitting tolerances, and assessment projects. Fabrication course credit, separate welding qualifications and apprenticeship progression are distinct outcomes to track in participant records.

Request required readings for every course; do not buy an old behaviour-support text merely because it appears on a previous syllabus. Resource link: DACS 1258 reading requirements.
Staff notes: Staff considerations include school-district recognition, practicum access, screening, first aid/CPR expectations, confidentiality, personal-care training and accessibility. Cultural safety and Indigenous learner supports are central to participant support. Course completion, employer entry requirements and further credential study are distinct pathway considerations.
Nursing — BCIT
Cohort context: Programs | BCIT | Dedicated ACCESS cohort; start and end dates are TBD. The reference pathway below is the Bachelor of Science in Nursing (BSN). ACCESS cohort access is separate from competition for general-public seats; academic and professional requirements provide preparation and progression context.
Course and scope: BCIT’s standard BSN is a three-year, full-time degree with simulation and clinical placements, preparing registered nurses. This is the degree pathway, rather than a short specialty course. Course website: BCIT Bachelor of Science in Nursing.
Prerequisites — school subjects: The published baseline is English Studies 12 (67%) or equivalent, Chemistry 11 (73%) or equivalent, and one math option: Pre-Calculus 11 (73%) or 12 (67%); Foundations of Math 11 or 12 (73%); or MATH 0007 (73%), or equivalent. Source: BCIT BSN academic requirements.
Prerequisites — post-secondary: Eighteen credits, each with at least 67%: ENGL 1177 academic writing (3); BHSC 1200 anatomy/physiology (6, completed within three years); PSYC 1101 psychology (3); and six elective credits at 100-level or higher across at least two academic disciplines. Accepted equivalents may apply. Sources: BSN Academic Foundations; course-equivalency guide (April 2022).
Professional framework and coverage: Nursing uses British Columbia College of Nurses and Midwives (BCCNM) standards, rather than SkilledTradesBC trade outlines. Entry-level RN competencies span clinical care, professional accountability, communication, collaboration, coordination, leadership, advocacy, education and scholarship. Core learning includes assessment and clinical judgment, medication safety, infection prevention, evidence-informed care, documentation and care across the lifespan. B.C. priorities include Indigenous cultural safety and humility, trauma-informed practice, mental health and substance use, and team-based primary care. Source: BCCNM RN entry-level competencies (October 2026).
Books — dated reference list: BCIT Library’s page is labelled Spring 2026 and was last updated September 14, 2026; it is a useful reference, not an ACCESS-specific 2027 purchase list. Examples include Potter and Perry’s Canadian Fundamentals of Nursing, 7th ed. (2024), ISBN 9780323870672; Lewis’s Medical-Surgical Nursing in Canada, 5th ed. (2023), ISBN 9780323791588; and Clinical Nursing Skills and Techniques, 11th ed. (2025), ISBN 9780443107184. Source: BCIT BSN required booklist.
Books — additional learning areas: The same list includes Health Assessment for Nursing Practice, 8th ed. (2026), ISBN 9780443127472; Karch’s Focus on Nursing Pharmacology, 10th ed. (2026), ISBN 9781975261115; and Davis’s Canadian Drug Guide for Nurses, 20th ed. (2026), ISBN 9781719655743. It also covers communication, mental health, maternity/child care, community health and research. The term-specific instructor list governs editions, digital access and timing of purchases. Resource: Full nursing booklist and library holdings.
Clinical readiness: BCIT lists criminal-record screening, immunization review and CPR–BLS (HCP) before Term 1, with annual CPR renewal; respirator fit testing may also be required. Source: BSN clinical requirements. Health-clearance deadlines may differ from admissions deadlines. BCIT lists a separate $75 immunization-review fee effective November 1, 2026. Health records go through Student Health Services, not this shared intranet guide. Source: BCIT health-science student requirements.
Qualification pathway: Degree completion and permission to practise are separate steps. B.C. graduates apply to BCCNM for RN licensure; the school reports completion and graduates must pass the NCLEX-RN and satisfy the licensing requirements. Provisional registration is an optional, conditional route while awaiting the exam, not an automatic outcome of cohort participation. Source: BCCNM application process for B.C. graduates.
Staff notes: Support planning should address prerequisite upgrading, anatomy/physiology recency, study time, childcare, placement travel, equipment and digital access, accessibility, cultural safety and wellbeing. Track course completion, clinical readiness and licensing as separate milestones. Keep ACCESS seat-allocation arrangements separate from public applicant-ranking language, and use cohort records for dates, funded items and participant supports.
