# Make, measure, improve

## Paper-glider lab: an introduction to the nine skills

[Back to the research pack](../README.md)

**Proposed 60-minute pilot · Adult foundational learning · Instructor review before delivery**

**Purpose:** Follow a visual instruction, collect useful measurements, compare results and explain an improvement. Connect the work to reading a job sheet, recording a measurement and sharing a change with a team.

**Source connection:** The three supplied ACCESS Essential Skills lesson images use a delta-wing paper-plane task, instructor/learner activity columns, pre-assessment, hands-on work and reflection. This is a new adaptation. The fourth page and the original folding handout were not supplied. The glider below is a simple classroom model, not a reconstruction of that missing handout or an aerodynamics lesson.

**Focus for feedback:** Reading, Numeracy and Communication. Collect brief evidence for the other six skills across the sequence. The learner's method and improvement matter more than distance travelled.

### What you need

- Two sheets of ordinary paper per pair, a ruler or tape, pencils and the record sheet below.
- A clear, instructor-controlled testing lane with a marked start line and soft target zone. Use paper only; keep people out of the lane. Launch only on the facilitator's signal and retrieve together.
- One accessible device per pair with a spreadsheet or simple table tool. A calculator is welcome.
- A demonstrated model and enlarged numbered instructions. Offer a partner-assisted or facilitator-launched version so learners can direct, observe and measure without throwing.

If a device is unavailable, complete the record on paper and schedule the digital entry later. Mark Digital **not observed yet**, rather than treating a paper table as digital evidence. For a larger class, use several supervised lanes or extend the testing time.

### Make the simple model

The facilitator demonstrates each step, then checks that learners can identify the next step in the numbered instructions.

1. Place a rectangular sheet with a short edge at the top. Fold the left long edge to meet the right long edge. Crease, then unfold to show the centre line.
2. Fold the two top corners toward the centre line so their top edges meet at the line, making a pointed top.
3. Fold each new sloping outer edge toward the centre line to make a narrower point.
4. Fold the model in half along the centre line, keeping the folded layers on the outside.
5. Make one wing by folding a top sloping edge down toward the long bottom edge. Turn over and repeat on the other side.
6. Open the wings gently so the body can be held underneath. Compare the two wings. Point out any difference before testing.

Use the first model to practise following and clarifying instructions. If a fold is difficult, demonstrate again or use a prepared model while the learner directs the steps. Test the activity locally before teaching; the outcomes are following, measuring and improving, not a promised flight performance.

### Session plan

| Time | Instructor actions | Learner actions | Resources / evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 min | State the first goal: explore how far the model travels within the testing lane. Introduce the task. Ask where learners already measure, follow instructions or explain a process. Invite examples without requiring personal disclosure. | Name a familiar strategy and predict how far the model might travel. | A baseline prediction with a unit. |
| 5–10 min | Demonstrate the numbered folds and establish the launch/measurement routine. Model how to ask for clarification. | Read or track the steps, identify a confusing instruction and check understanding with a partner. | Numbered instructions; annotated or explained step. |
| 10–18 min | Support making and purposeful role sharing. | Make the model, write one prediction and agree launcher/director, measurer and recorder roles. A pair can combine roles and swap. | Model, prediction, role note. |
| 18–28 min | Supervise three baseline trials per model. Demonstrate measuring along the lane from the start line to the first landing point's position along the lane. | Estimate, measure and record three distances to the nearest 10 cm. Keep start position and launch routine consistent. | Three labelled baseline measurements. |
| 28–36 min | Model a table and a mean with the fictional data below. Explain the difference between an estimate and a measured result. | Enter their own results digitally, calculate a mean and independently check the total. | Saved table, units and calculation. |
| 36–44 min | Introduce the changed goal: aim for a clearly marked target zone rather than the longest flight. | Propose one model change, explain the reason and agree how to check it. Record the change before testing. | Change sketch and revised plan. |
| 44–52 min | Supervise three revised trials under the same measurement routine. | Test, record and compare. Discuss variation and whether the revision helped the new goal. | Revised data and a cautious evidence-based conclusion. |
| 52–60 min | Invite a short partner handover and exit response. Connect strategies to a selected program brief. | Explain a finding, ask/check one clarification, identify skill evidence and choose a next practice step. | Handover observation and exit note. |

### Worked example — invented data

The practice target is **170–210 cm inclusive** along the lane. Baseline trials are **120, 150 and 180 cm**. Their total is `120 + 150 + 180 = 450 cm`. The mean is `450 ÷ 3 = 150 cm`, or **1.50 m**. One of three trials is in the target zone.

After one change, fictional trials are **150, 180 and 210 cm**. Their total is `540 cm`; the mean is `540 ÷ 3 = 180 cm`, or **1.80 m**. Two of three trials are in the target zone. The mean increased by **30 cm**.

**Calculator steps:** `120 + 150 + 180 =`, then `÷ 3 =`. Repeat with the revised data. Clear between separate calculations as needed. These are ordinary operations, not verified model-specific key recordings.

**Spreadsheet steps:** Put the three baseline values in cells B2:B4 and revised values in C2:C4. In B5 enter `=SUM(B2:B4)` and in B6 enter `=AVERAGE(B2:B4)`; repeat for column C. Label the columns **cm** and check the spreadsheet against the hand calculation. Formula names may differ with the tool's language settings.

**Check:** Each mean must lie between the smallest and largest trial. Three attempts provide limited evidence: changes in launching and measurement can affect the results. A longer mean is not automatically better for a target-zone goal.

### Make the nine skills visible

| Skill | Task evidence | Instructor look-for |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | Numbered instruction identified and applied. | Learner finds the next step and uses the instruction to check the model. |
| Writing | Prediction, change note and short conclusion. | Another person can understand what was tried and observed. |
| Numeracy | Labelled measurements, total, mean and target count. | Consistent units, sensible method and a reasonableness check. |
| Digital | Table created and saved with checked values/formulas. | Learner can find the saved result and explain one entry or formula. |
| Problem solving | Explanation of inconsistent results or a misleading mean. | Learner considers evidence and identifies a useful next check. |
| Communication | Partner handover plus clarification/check-back. | Listener accurately restates the finding or asks a relevant question. |
| Collaboration | Agreed roles and shared checking. | Both learners contribute and use feedback constructively. |
| Adaptability | Plan revised after the goal changes. | Learner explains why the original strategy may no longer suit the target. |
| Creativity and innovation | One proposed modification, tried and evaluated. | Learner connects an idea to a purpose and reflects on the test. |

### Exit prompts and transfer

1. Point to one result you checked. How did you know it was reasonable?
2. Show where you used a skill other than Numeracy. What would an observer see?
3. What would you change or keep for the next attempt, and why?

Choose a transfer task: a **welding cut list**, **asphalt quantity table**, **security timeline**, **classroom activity schedule**, or **synthetic nursing volume record**. Ask which checking strategy carries over and which new source or instruction is needed. Use the corresponding [program brief](../programs/README.md) and [numeracy starter](../numeracy-starters.md).

### Before the pilot

Prepare a model and verify the folds, room layout and measurement routine; set a target suited to that space. Confirm device availability and supports. After the pilot, record actual timings, unclear instructions and learner feedback before expanding this into program-specific lessons. This lesson has been designed and its example arithmetic checked; it has not been classroom-tested.
