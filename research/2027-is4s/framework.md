# Nine skills, visible in the work

[Back to the research pack](README.md)

**Proposed teaching framework · 22 October 2026**

The current nine names below are also explicitly recorded in the Numeracy Hub's [brand guidance](https://github.com/GOSTROGIS/access-trades-numeracy/blob/7c0ceaab485e36aa45c596ac58e43bba63e4f824/brand/BRAND.md). The descriptions here are working teaching prompts, not quotations from or a validated assessment instrument issued by ESDC. Verification against the current official framework is queued in the [source register](sources/README.md).

| Skill | Ask the learner to… | Look for this evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | Find and use an instruction, requirement or relevant detail. | The detail is identified and correctly applied; the learner can point to its source. |
| Writing | Make a record another person can use. | A clear, accurate note, form or explanation with the necessary units, dates or context. |
| Numeracy | Estimate, select a method, calculate or measure, and check. | Working, labelled units, a result and a reasonableness check. |
| Digital | Use a digital tool to find, organize, create or share task information. | An accurately created/retrieved file, table or record; the learner explains a relevant tool choice. |
| Problem solving | Identify a discrepancy, consider options and justify a response. | A reasoned decision supported by task evidence rather than a guessed answer. |
| Communication | Explain, listen, clarify and check shared understanding. | A useful exchange and a check-back that reveals whether the message was understood. |
| Collaboration | Agree roles, coordinate contributions and give useful feedback. | Shared decisions and an identifiable contribution from each learner. |
| Adaptability | Respond to a changed condition and revise the approach. | A revised plan and an explanation of what changed and why. |
| Creativity and innovation | Generate, try and improve a useful idea within the constraints. | A proposed improvement, a small test or comparison, and reflection on the result. |

Document literacy remains central: learners read drawings, interpret tables, complete forms and navigate records. Integrate these tasks across Reading, Writing, Numeracy and Digital. Continuous learning runs through practice, reflection and transfer; it is not simply a new name for Adaptability. See the full [historical crosswalk](source-audit.md#historical-essential-skills-and-current-skills-for-success).

## A repeatable lesson pattern

Use the photographed lesson's instructor/learner/resource/time structure and the Hub's visible worked steps. A program can demonstrate all nine across a sequence; select two or three focus skills for detailed feedback in an individual short lesson.

| Stage | Instructor action | Learner action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Connect | Introduce a believable task and ask about relevant experience. | Describe a familiar strategy or ask a question; personal disclosure is optional. | Starting strategy and support preferences. |
| Read the task | Supply a short document or clearly labelled diagram. | Identify givens, constraints, units and the required output. | Annotated task or explained interpretation. |
| Estimate and model | Demonstrate one example, with method and tool steps visible. | Predict an answer or range, then follow and question the method. | Estimate and method choice. |
| Practise | Offer a new example and purposeful roles. | Solve, measure, record and check; explain to a partner. | Task record, practical result and check-back. |
| Change one condition | Introduce a stated variation. | Revise the plan, compare options and test an improvement. | Revised work and justification. |
| Reflect and transfer | Ask what helped and where the approach applies next. | Name the skill used and point to evidence; choose a next step. | A short reflection tied to actual work. |

**Learner-sheet template:** task → what you need → givens/diagram → estimate → method → worked example → your turn → check → changed condition → explain → next task.

**Instructor-sheet template:** purpose → provider outcome/source (pending until verified) → focus skills → materials → timed actions → anticipated misconceptions → worked answers → observation prompts → supports used → follow-up.

## Feedback and access

Use simple task-specific observations: **not observed yet**, **completed with support**, **completed independently**, and **explained or transferred to a new case**. These are local feedback descriptors, not official Skills for Success levels, admission decisions or trade certification.

Record what support was used. Demonstration, a glossary, enlarged diagrams, assistive technology, extra practice and calculator access can make a task accessible. If Writing is the assessment target, an oral response can show understanding but does not by itself demonstrate Writing. Likewise, paper-based work can preserve the activity when a device is unavailable, but Digital remains unobserved until the learner uses a digital tool.

Ask learners how they prefer to participate. Use relevant community and workplace contexts with ACCESS/community input; do not invent shared Indigenous traditions or require disclosure of family, cultural or traumatic experiences. Offer collaborative improvement goals and private feedback. Use fictional records for practice involving students, clients, patients or incidents.

## What the Numeracy Hub contributes

- Keep dimensions, units, drawings and calculator steps close to the question.
- Provide a worked example and a chance to try again with changed values.
- Show why an answer is reasonable, including common unit or decimal errors.
- Offer print-friendly work as well as digital practice.
- Start with approachable tasks and increase the reasoning demand deliberately. Use “Start”, “Practise” and “Extend” for this draft; formal complexity labels need a validated mapping.
- For later visual materials, carry forward warm cream, white task surfaces, red headings/actions and gold rules/highlights. Use the existing IS4S assets appropriately; do not add invented cultural motifs.

Technical and professional criteria come from the actual provider's current approved materials. Classroom arithmetic, communication practice and scenario work do not independently establish safe performance on equipment, permission to practise or qualification credit.
